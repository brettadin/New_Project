# 6.2 — Scientific Workspaces and Specialized Environments

## Purpose

The application should provide dedicated scientific environments that organize functionality around different astronomical disciplines and research activities.

These environments should provide focused access to relevant tools, datasets, references, workflows and visualizations while remaining part of one integrated application.

The objective is to maintain organized, specialized working environments without unnecessarily isolating scientific disciplines or restricting interdisciplinary research.

### Core Requirements

**1. Dedicated Scientific Workspaces**

- Provide dedicated environments for major scientific disciplines and research activities.
- Initial environments should reflect the application's established scientific priorities, including exoplanet science, planetary science, stellar astronomy, observational astronomy and other planned research areas.
- Each environment should expose functionality relevant to its scientific discipline.
- Provide access to appropriate scientific tools, datasets, references, analytical methods and visualizations.
- Accommodate discipline-specific requirements rather than forcing every environment into an identical structure.
- Allow new scientific disciplines and specialized environments to be introduced as the application expands.

**2. Focused Workspace Organization**

- Keep individual scientific environments organized around their intended research areas.
- Avoid accumulating unrelated tools, datasets and functionality within the same workspace.
- Ensure users can concentrate on relevant scientific activities without unnecessary distractions.
- Avoid overwhelming users with functionality they do not currently need.
- Maintain a manageable and understandable organization as each workspace develops.

**3. Shared Application Capabilities**

- Maintain access to common application capabilities across relevant scientific workspaces.
- Avoid unnecessarily duplicating tools and functionality between environments.
- Allow shared capabilities to be used where scientifically and functionally appropriate.
- Maintain consistency in how shared functionality operates.
- Allow specialized environments to present shared tools in ways appropriate to their particular scientific requirements.

**4. Integration Without Unnecessary Isolation**

- Scientific workspaces should remain part of one integrated application.
- Allow users to access related functionality across the application where appropriate.
- Support scientific workflows that involve multiple disciplines without requiring every discipline to operate within the same environment.
- Preserve the ability to move between scientific environments without unnecessarily losing research context.
- Avoid rigid boundaries that prevent useful scientific relationships or comparisons.

### Future Possibilities

**1. Shared or Interdisciplinary Workspace**

Consider introducing an optional shared workspace where users can bring together selected material from multiple scientific environments.

Potential capabilities include:

- Adding selected datasets, observations, analyses, models and results from different workspaces.
- Conducting comparative investigations involving multiple astronomical disciplines.
- Combining relevant tools and analytical capabilities from different environments.
- Maintaining references to the original datasets and their associated workspaces.
- Preserving provenance, metadata, processing history and project relationships.
- Allowing users to work with selected material without merging or restructuring the original scientific workspaces.

This could support investigations such as comparing Solar System planetary spectra with exoplanet spectra, or combining stellar characterization with exoplanet observations.

The shared workspace should complement specialized environments rather than replace them.

**2. Additional Workspace Customization**

- User-customizable workspace configurations.
- Specialized environments for particular research techniques rather than entire scientific disciplines.
- Personal workspaces combining selected tools from multiple scientific areas.
- Context-sensitive workspace configurations based on current research activities.
- Additional specialized environments as new scientific capabilities are developed.

### Workspace Organization Principles

- Scientific workspaces should remain distinct by default.
- Cross-disciplinary research should be possible without requiring all functionality to exist in one environment.
- Shared workspaces should be optional and should not replace specialized environments.
- Adding material to a shared workspace should preserve its relationship to its original workspace and project.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of datasets and research records.
- Users should be able to return to the original scientific environment without losing context.
- Workspace structures should remain adaptable as scientific capabilities and application requirements evolve.

### Design and Implementation Considerations

- The exact organization, layout and navigation of scientific workspaces will be determined during later UI/UX planning.
- Workspace architecture should accommodate additional scientific disciplines and functionality without requiring extensive restructuring.
- The degree of independence between workspaces and shared application capabilities should be investigated during architecture planning.
- The implementation of any shared interdisciplinary workspace should be evaluated against usability, data management, provenance and architectural considerations.
- Avoid prematurely committing to a particular workspace structure or interaction model.

### General Principle

Scientific workspaces should provide focused, organized environments for different areas of astronomical research while retaining the advantages of one integrated scientific platform.

The default experience should prioritize clarity and specialization. Interdisciplinary functionality should remain possible through appropriately designed shared capabilities and, potentially, an optional shared workspace.

The application should be able to expand its scientific scope without sacrificing workspace organization or usability.

### Implementation Status

Dedicated scientific workspaces and focused organization are **core requirements**.

An optional shared interdisciplinary workspace and additional workspace customization are **future possibilities** to investigate during research, architecture and UI/UX planning.
