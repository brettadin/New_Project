# 8.13 — Moderation and Responsible Community Conduct

## Purpose

Establishes how the application maintains a safe, constructive and scientifically responsible community environment.

Community participation should support scientific discussion, knowledge exchange, collaboration and independent investigation while discouraging harassment, abuse, deliberate misinformation, inappropriate content and other harmful behaviour.

Moderation should protect users and maintain the integrity of community spaces without unnecessarily restricting legitimate scientific disagreement, criticism or alternative interpretations.

The application should provide understandable community standards, reporting mechanisms, moderation procedures and appropriate accountability.

Moderation should be proportionate to the situation and distinguish between harmful conduct and legitimate differences in scientific opinion.

## 8.13.1 — Community Guidelines and Standards of Conduct

### Functional Requirements

- Establish clear standards for responsible community participation.
- Encourage respectful scientific discussion and constructive criticism.
- Discourage harassment, threats, intimidation, personal attacks and discriminatory behaviour.
- Prohibit deliberate impersonation, fraudulent representation and malicious misrepresentation.
- Address spam, disruptive behaviour and irrelevant promotional content.
- Establish expectations for appropriate use of community resources.
- Encourage accurate attribution and respect for intellectual ownership.
- Discourage deliberate presentation of fabricated data, references, observations or scientific evidence as genuine.
- Make community standards accessible to users.
- Apply standards consistently across community discussions, groups, shared research and other community environments.

### Design Guidance

Community guidelines should be understandable and practical rather than unnecessarily restrictive.

Users should be able to question scientific claims, challenge interpretations and disagree with other researchers without being treated as disruptive merely because their conclusions differ.

Standards should focus on conduct, honesty, accountability and responsible participation.

## 8.13.2 — Scientific Disagreement and Responsible Debate

### Functional Requirements

- Protect legitimate scientific disagreement and alternative interpretations.
- Allow users to challenge research methods, assumptions, conclusions and published findings.
- Support discussion of conflicting evidence, competing hypotheses and methodological limitations.
- Distinguish scientific criticism from personal attacks or harassment.
- Allow users to present evidence that challenges established or widely accepted interpretations.
- Avoid automatically treating minority or unconventional scientific positions as misconduct.
- Encourage supporting arguments with relevant observations, methods, publications and references.
- Allow disagreements to remain unresolved where available evidence is insufficient.
- Provide appropriate moderation when discussion becomes abusive or deliberately misleading.
- Preserve relevant discussion history when moderation actions are taken.

### Design Guidance

Scientific disagreement is an essential part of research and should not be suppressed simply to maintain consensus.

Moderation should distinguish between:

- Legitimate differences in scientific interpretation.
- Reasoned criticism of methods or conclusions.
- Unsupported scientific claims.
- Deliberate misrepresentation of evidence.
- Harassment, intimidation or abusive conduct.

The application should not treat disagreement with established scientific interpretations as misconduct by itself.

Where claims are scientifically questionable, the appropriate response should generally be evidence-based discussion, clarification and review rather than automatic removal.

## 8.13.3 — Reporting Inappropriate Content and Behaviour

### Functional Requirements

- Allow users to report inappropriate content and behaviour.
- Support reporting of:
  - Harassment and personal attacks.
  - Threats and intimidation.
  - Spam and disruptive behaviour.
  - Deliberate misinformation or fabricated scientific evidence.
  - Impersonation and fraudulent representation.
  - Copyright and attribution violations.
  - Inappropriate or harmful shared material.
  - Privacy violations and unauthorized disclosure.
  - Other violations of community guidelines.
- Allow users to provide an explanation or supporting information when submitting reports.
- Associate reports with the relevant user, discussion, resource or other community material.
- Provide appropriate acknowledgement of submitted reports.
- Support review and investigation of reported issues.
- Protect reporting users from unnecessary exposure.
- Discourage malicious or knowingly false reporting.
- Maintain appropriate records of reports and their outcomes.

### Design Guidance

Reporting should be accessible without requiring users to navigate complicated procedures.

Users should be able to identify the specific issue and provide relevant context.

Reporting mechanisms should distinguish between scientific disagreements, content-quality concerns and misconduct.

## 8.13.4 — Moderation Roles and Responsibilities

### Functional Requirements

- Support appropriate moderation responsibilities across community environments.
- Allow designated moderators or administrators to review reported content and conduct.
- Support moderation within individual groups and broader application-wide community spaces.
- Distinguish ordinary users, group moderators, group administrators and application-level moderators.
- Provide moderators with appropriate tools for handling violations.
- Ensure moderation authority is limited to its intended scope.
- Establish consistent standards for moderation decisions.
- Allow significant moderation actions to be documented.
- Provide appropriate oversight of moderation activity.
- Avoid granting group moderators unrestricted control over unrelated research projects or private user material.

### Design Guidance

Moderation should operate at appropriate levels.

For example, a group moderator may manage discussions and membership within their group without automatically receiving access to private research projects belonging to group members.

Application-wide moderation should address issues that extend beyond individual groups or require broader intervention.

## 8.13.5 — Moderation Actions and Enforcement

### Functional Requirements

- Support proportionate moderation actions according to the nature and severity of violations.
- Allow moderators to:
  - Issue warnings.
  - Request clarification or corrections.
  - Remove or restrict inappropriate content.
  - Temporarily restrict participation.
  - Suspend access to specific community environments.
  - Suspend or restrict accounts where justified.
  - Escalate serious incidents for further review.
- Distinguish temporary restrictions from permanent account actions.
- Consider the severity, context and history of behaviour.
- Communicate relevant moderation decisions to affected users.
- Explain the reason for significant moderation actions where appropriate.
- Avoid unnecessary removal of legitimate scientific discussion.
- Preserve relevant records of significant moderation decisions.
- Apply restrictions consistently and proportionately.

### Design Guidance

Moderation should not rely exclusively on automatic removal or permanent account restrictions.

Where appropriate, clarification, warnings or requests for correction may resolve less serious issues.

Serious threats, harassment, malicious activity or deliberate violations may require stronger intervention.

Moderation actions should be based on documented behaviour and applicable community standards rather than popularity, disagreement or personal relationships.

## 8.13.6 — Scientific Misinformation and Fabricated Material

### Functional Requirements

- Provide mechanisms for identifying and reporting deliberately fabricated or misrepresented scientific material.
- Address fabricated observations, measurements, datasets, publications, citations and research claims presented as genuine.
- Distinguish deliberate fabrication from honest mistakes, incomplete documentation and legitimate scientific uncertainty.
- Support correction or clarification of inaccurate community material.
- Allow relevant scientific references and supporting evidence to be provided during review.
- Preserve the distinction between observations, processed data, model predictions, hypotheses and simulated material.
- Avoid automatically treating scientifically unconventional claims as misinformation.
- Avoid presenting moderation decisions as proof that a scientific interpretation is correct.
- Preserve relevant correction and moderation history.
- Coordinate with scientific integrity and educational quality requirements in Sections 5 and 7.9.

### Design Guidance

Scientific misinformation requires careful evaluation.

The application should distinguish between:

- Deliberately fabricated or falsified scientific material.
- Incorrect information resulting from an honest mistake.
- Outdated scientific information.
- Unsupported interpretations.
- Preliminary or uncertain findings.
- Legitimate scientific disagreement.

Where an error is identified, correction and clear documentation may be more appropriate than removing the entire contribution.

Scientific moderation should not become a substitute for scientific review or validation.

## 8.13.7 — Copyright, Attribution and Ownership Violations

### Functional Requirements

- Allow users to report suspected copyright and attribution violations.
- Address unauthorized redistribution of restricted scientific publications, datasets, software and other material.
- Support reporting of plagiarism and misrepresentation of authorship.
- Preserve original creator and contributor attribution.
- Provide appropriate mechanisms for reviewing reported material.
- Support removal, restriction or correction where violations are established.
- Respect licensing and usage restrictions.
- Distinguish between legitimate quotation, reference, adaptation and unauthorized reproduction.
- Maintain records of significant decisions and corrections.
- Coordinate with ownership, licensing and attribution requirements in Sections 4 and 8.10.

### Design Guidance

Moderation should account for the different licensing and attribution requirements associated with scientific data, software, publications and educational resources.

Not every use of externally created material constitutes a violation.

Where a concern is raised, the relevant source, licensing terms, attribution and context should be considered before taking action.

## 8.13.8 — Appeals and Moderation Review

### Functional Requirements

- Provide appropriate mechanisms for users to challenge significant moderation decisions.
- Allow users to request reconsideration of removed material or participation restrictions.
- Support review by an appropriate moderator or administrator.
- Preserve the original decision and relevant reasoning.
- Record appeal outcomes and subsequent changes.
- Allow incorrect moderation decisions to be corrected.
- Distinguish between disagreements with scientific criticism and challenges to moderation conduct.
- Avoid allowing appeals to become a mechanism for repeated harassment or disruption.
- Communicate appeal outcomes where appropriate.

### Design Guidance

Moderation systems should allow reasonable correction of mistakes.

Users should be able to understand why an action occurred and how to request a review.

Appeal procedures should be proportionate to the significance of the moderation decision rather than imposing excessive administrative processes on minor issues.

## 8.13.9 — User Blocking and Personal Interaction Controls

### Functional Requirements

- Allow users to block or restrict interactions with other users.
- Support controls for unwanted direct communication.
- Allow users to manage interactions and notifications from blocked or restricted users.
- Prevent blocked users from using ordinary community interactions to circumvent restrictions where technically practical.
- Ensure blocking does not automatically alter ownership or scientific attribution.
- Preserve legitimate access permissions for shared research where appropriate.
- Provide mechanisms for reporting attempts to circumvent blocking.
- Allow users to review and modify their blocking preferences.

### Design Guidance

Users should have meaningful control over unwanted personal interactions without unintentionally disrupting unrelated scientific records.

Blocking should primarily affect communication and community interaction.

Existing research ownership, historical contributions and independently authorized project access should be handled separately.

## 8.13.10 — Moderation Transparency and Records

### Functional Requirements

- Maintain appropriate records of significant moderation actions.
- Record relevant information such as the action taken, reason, date and responsible moderation authority.
- Preserve relevant correction and appeal history.
- Allow appropriate oversight of moderation decisions.
- Communicate relevant changes to affected users.
- Protect private reports and moderation records from unauthorized access.
- Avoid exposing confidential reports or personal information unnecessarily.
- Distinguish moderation records from ordinary scientific discussion and research activity records.
- Support consistent moderation practices across community environments.

### Design Guidance

Moderation transparency should support accountability without unnecessarily exposing reporting users, affected individuals or confidential investigations.

The application should maintain enough information to understand significant moderation decisions and review their handling.

## 8.13.11 — Community Safety and Responsible Participation

### Functional Requirements

- Establish mechanisms for addressing threats, harassment, intimidation and other harmful conduct.
- Provide appropriate responses to privacy violations and unauthorized disclosure.
- Support escalation of serious safety or security incidents.
- Protect users from repeated abuse and targeted harassment.
- Establish expectations for responsible participation in collaborative research and community groups.
- Ensure community moderation respects privacy and access controls.
- Maintain appropriate procedures for serious or repeated violations.
- Avoid allowing community features to expose users to unnecessary risks.
- Review moderation practices as the community and application expand.

### Design Guidance

Community safety should be considered alongside privacy, security and responsible participation.

Moderation should protect users while preserving legitimate scientific exchange.

The application should avoid unnecessary collection or disclosure of personal information during moderation and incident handling.

## Future Possibilities

The following capabilities may be investigated as the community develops but are not initial implementation priorities.

### Advanced Moderation Infrastructure

- More extensive moderation dashboards.
- Dedicated moderation queues and case management.
- Advanced reporting and investigation tools.
- Moderation assistance for larger groups and communities.
- Additional moderator oversight and review systems.
- More extensive moderation analytics.

### Automated Moderation Assistance

- Automated detection of spam and abusive behaviour.
- Identification of potentially fabricated or misleading scientific material for human review.
- Automated detection of copyright and attribution concerns.
- Identification of repeated harassment or attempts to circumvent restrictions.
- Context-aware moderation recommendations.

Automated systems should assist moderation rather than independently determine scientific validity or make consequential decisions without appropriate safeguards.

### Advanced Community Governance

- More formal community moderation policies.
- Community-appointed moderation roles.
- Independent moderation review structures.
- Specialized moderation arrangements for research groups.
- Additional accountability and transparency mechanisms.

These capabilities should be investigated according to community size, moderation requirements, legal considerations and technical feasibility.

## Initial Implementation Priorities

The initial implementation should establish basic community standards, reporting and moderation mechanisms without requiring a sophisticated moderation organization.

Priority capabilities include:

- Clear community guidelines.
- Protection of legitimate scientific disagreement.
- Basic reporting of inappropriate content and behaviour.
- Reporting of harassment, spam, privacy violations and attribution concerns.
- Basic moderator and administrator permissions.
- Proportionate moderation actions.
- Communication of significant moderation decisions.
- Basic user blocking and interaction controls.
- Appropriate handling of fabricated scientific material.
- Basic moderation records.
- Coordination with existing privacy, ownership and scientific integrity requirements.

The initial system should prioritize clear standards, user protection and consistent handling of reported issues.

## Implementation Considerations

Moderation should integrate with the community, group, profile, discussion, research-sharing and privacy systems established throughout Section 8.

Particular attention should be given to:

- Distinguishing scientific disagreement from misconduct.
- Protecting legitimate criticism and alternative interpretations.
- Handling deliberately fabricated or misrepresented scientific material.
- Preserving attribution and ownership.
- Providing understandable reporting and appeals.
- Applying proportionate moderation actions.
- Protecting reporting users and confidential information.
- Maintaining consistent moderation standards across community environments.
- Avoiding excessive automation or unnecessary restrictions on scientific discussion.

Moderation should remain adaptable as community participation grows and new forms of scientific collaboration are introduced.

**General Principle:** The community should support open scientific discussion, constructive criticism and responsible collaboration while protecting users from abuse, deliberate misrepresentation and harmful conduct. Moderation must be fair, transparent and proportionate, preserving legitimate scientific disagreement while maintaining a safe and scientifically responsible environment.
