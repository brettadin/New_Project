

# 2.10 — Future Scientific Expansion

## Purpose

The application's scientific scope should remain adaptable as astronomy develops, new discoveries are made, observational capabilities advance and research priorities change.

The initial scientific priorities establish a practical foundation, but they should not permanently restrict the application to a fixed set of disciplines, astronomical objects or research questions.

Future expansion should allow the application to incorporate additional scientific subjects, emerging research areas and new interdisciplinary opportunities while preserving its overall identity as an astronomy-focused research environment.

Expansion should be deliberate, scientifically justified and consistent with the application's broader direction.

## 2.10.1 Scientific Scope Flexibility

### Core Requirements

- Maintain a scientific scope that can evolve as astronomical research develops.
- Allow additional scientific disciplines to be incorporated over time.
- Accommodate newly discovered astronomical objects and phenomena.
- Support emerging research questions and scientific approaches.
- Allow existing research areas to expand beyond their initial subject coverage.
- Avoid permanently restricting the application to its initial scientific priorities.
- Preserve existing scientific capabilities and research relationships as the scope expands.
- Maintain a clear distinction between the application's overall scientific scope and its current implementation.

### Design Guidance

The application's scientific scope should be broad enough to accommodate future developments without requiring every possible research area to be anticipated in advance.

Flexibility should not mean uncontrolled expansion. New areas should be evaluated according to their scientific relevance and compatibility with the application's purpose.

## 2.10.2 Expansion of Existing Scientific Disciplines

### Core Requirements

Allow existing scientific disciplines to develop beyond their initial research coverage.

Potential expansion includes:

- Additional areas of exoplanet science.
- More extensive planetary and Solar System research.
- Additional stellar research subjects.
- Broader observational astronomy.
- Expanded spectroscopy and astrochemistry.
- More extensive theoretical and computational astronomy.
- Additional astrophysical and cosmological research.
- New interdisciplinary research areas.

Allow existing disciplines to incorporate new objects, methods, scientific questions and specialized research subjects.

### Design Guidance

Expansion should not require the creation of an entirely separate scientific area whenever an existing discipline develops new research directions.

Where appropriate, new subjects should extend established areas while preserving clear scientific distinctions and organizational relationships.

## 2.10.3 Introduction of New Scientific Disciplines

### Core Requirements

- Allow new scientific disciplines to be considered for inclusion.
- Accommodate scientific fields that become relevant through new discoveries or research developments.
- Support emerging interdisciplinary fields.
- Allow specialized disciplines to be introduced without requiring them to become initial development priorities.
- Preserve connections between new disciplines and existing scientific areas.
- Avoid excluding relevant scientific fields solely because they were not anticipated in the original scope.

### Design Guidance

New scientific disciplines should be evaluated according to their relationship with astronomy and their contribution to the application's research environment.

A new discipline may initially be represented through its connections with existing research areas before receiving more extensive dedicated support.

## 2.10.4 Emerging Astronomical Objects and Phenomena

### Core Requirements

Allow the application to accommodate:

- Newly discovered astronomical objects.
- Newly recognized object classifications.
- Newly observed astronomical phenomena.
- Revised scientific classifications.
- Newly identified physical or chemical processes.
- Emerging areas of observational and theoretical research.
- New categories of astronomical systems and environments.

Preserve the ability to represent objects and phenomena that do not fit neatly into existing classifications.

### Design Guidance

Astronomical knowledge changes as observations improve and scientific interpretations develop.

The application should not rely on a permanently fixed object catalog or classification structure that prevents the inclusion of newly discovered or reclassified subjects.

Scientific classifications should remain revisable without unnecessarily disrupting existing research records.

## 2.10.5 New Observational Methods and Scientific Approaches

### Core Requirements

Accommodate future developments involving:

- New observational facilities and instruments.
- Additional wavelength regions or observational techniques.
- Improved measurement capabilities.
- New forms of astronomical data.
- Advances in spectroscopy, photometry and imaging.
- New theoretical and computational methods.
- Improved scientific models and simulation approaches.
- Emerging forms of multi-instrument and multi-messenger astronomy.

Allow new methods and observational approaches to contribute to existing or newly introduced scientific disciplines.

### Design Guidance

Advances in observational and analytical capabilities may introduce research opportunities that are not practical or available during initial development.

The application should remain open to these developments without assuming that every new instrument, method or scientific approach requires immediate integration.

## 2.10.6 Expansion of Interdisciplinary Research

### Core Requirements

- Support new combinations of existing scientific disciplines.
- Accommodate research questions that cross established scientific boundaries.
- Allow additional scientific fields to contribute to existing investigations.
- Preserve relationships between disciplines, objects, observations and research results.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication when new interdisciplinary research areas are introduced.
- Maintain methodological compatibility and scientific integrity as research becomes more integrated.

### Design Guidance

Interdisciplinary research may develop in ways that cannot be fully anticipated during initial planning.

The application should support the evolution of these relationships without requiring users to reorganize their existing research whenever new scientific connections become relevant.

## 2.10.7 Evaluation of Scientific Expansion

### Core Requirements

Evaluate proposed scientific expansion according to relevant considerations, including:

- Scientific relevance to astronomy.
- Compatibility with the application's overall purpose.
- Potential value to intended users.
- Relationship with existing scientific disciplines.
- Availability and reliability of relevant scientific data.
- Availability of established methods and scientific references.
- Requirements for specialized tools or expertise.
- Development complexity and resource requirements.
- Potential effects on existing capabilities and research workflows.
- Opportunities for interdisciplinary research.
- Long-term maintenance and sustainability.

### Design Guidance

Scientific relevance should remain the primary consideration when evaluating new research areas.

However, scientific relevance alone does not automatically justify immediate implementation.

A proposed expansion may be scientifically valuable while requiring additional research, specialized expertise, external resources or substantial development effort.

The application should distinguish between recognizing a subject within its broader scientific scope and prioritizing its implementation.

## 2.10.8 Incremental Scientific Expansion

### Core Requirements

- Introduce new scientific areas progressively.
- Allow research and validation before committing to substantial development.
- Expand existing disciplines without unnecessarily disrupting established capabilities.
- Preserve existing scientific data, research relationships and project continuity.
- Evaluate new capabilities within their intended scientific context.
- Allow additional research areas to remain exploratory until their requirements are sufficiently understood.
- Avoid treating every proposed expansion as an immediate development commitment.

### Design Guidance

Scientific expansion should follow the application's broader incremental development philosophy.

A new research area may progress through stages of consideration, investigation, planning, validation and eventual implementation.

The level of support provided should reflect scientific maturity, available resources and demonstrated research value.

## 2.10.9 Preservation of Scientific Consistency

### Core Requirements

- Maintain consistent scientific standards as the application expands.
- Preserve established requirements for uncertainty, provenance, reproducibility and transparency.
- Ensure new disciplines follow appropriate scientific conventions.
- Avoid allowing new capabilities to compromise existing research functionality.
- Preserve compatibility with relevant existing data and research structures.
- Evaluate the scientific implications of changes to established classifications or methods.
- Maintain clear distinctions between established scientific knowledge, emerging research and speculative interpretation.

### Design Guidance

Expansion should strengthen the application rather than introduce inconsistent scientific practices.

Different disciplines may require different methods, conventions and standards of evidence. Consistency should therefore mean maintaining common principles of scientific integrity rather than forcing every discipline to follow identical analytical procedures.

## 2.10.10 Long-Term Scientific Development

### Core Requirements

- Maintain a long-term view of potential scientific expansion.
- Allow scientific priorities to evolve in response to research developments and user needs.
- Reassess the relevance of existing scientific areas periodically.
- Accommodate emerging observational facilities, discoveries and research methods.
- Preserve flexibility for future scientific disciplines and interdisciplinary developments.
- Avoid unnecessary restrictions based on initial implementation decisions.
- Maintain alignment between scientific expansion and the application's overall identity.

### Design Guidance

The application's scientific direction should be allowed to mature alongside astronomy itself.

Long-term planning should provide direction without treating the current scientific scope as a permanent limit or requiring speculative commitments to every possible future capability.

## General Principles

Future scientific expansion should:

- Preserve the application's broad astronomy-focused identity.
- Allow existing disciplines to develop beyond their initial priorities.
- Accommodate new scientific disciplines, objects and phenomena.
- Remain open to emerging observational and theoretical approaches.
- Encourage interdisciplinary research.
- Evaluate expansion according to scientific relevance and practical feasibility.
- Introduce new areas incrementally.
- Preserve scientific integrity and consistency.
- Distinguish broad scientific scope from current implementation.
- Avoid unnecessary restrictions while maintaining a coherent development direction.

## General Principle

The application should evolve alongside astronomical research, remaining scientifically ambitious and adaptable without losing its identity, compromising its standards or attempting to implement every possible research area simultaneously.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes the principles governing future expansion of the application's scientific scope.

It does not commit the application to implementing every discipline, object category, observational method or research capability identified throughout Section 2.

Actual development priorities, capability requirements, resource considerations and implementation decisions are addressed elsewhere in the Foundations documentation.

Scientific expansion should remain an ongoing process guided by research value, feasibility, user needs and the application's long-term purpose.
