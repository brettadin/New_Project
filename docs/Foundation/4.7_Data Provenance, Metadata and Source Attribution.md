# 4.7 — Data Provenance, Metadata and Source Attribution

## Purpose

The application should preserve information identifying the origins of scientific data and the metadata needed to understand its original scientific context.

Astronomical research relies on observations, measurements, catalogues, reference datasets and publications produced by many different organizations, instruments and researchers. Understanding where this information originated is essential to evaluating, interpreting and appropriately citing scientific material.

The application should retain available source information and original metadata when data is discovered, retrieved, imported or incorporated into research.

It should also support appropriate attribution of original data providers, creators, observatories, instruments, publications and other contributors.

Source information and metadata should remain distinguishable from application-generated descriptions, user-supplied corrections and subsequent scientific interpretations.

## 4.7.1 Original Source Identification

### Core Requirements

- Preserve the original source of externally retrieved and user-provided scientific data wherever that information is available.
- Identify the archive, database, catalogue, observatory, institution, publication, instrument or other source associated with a resource.
- Retain relevant dataset, observation, catalogue and resource identifiers.
- Preserve original filenames and source-specific naming conventions where appropriate.
- Retain available source references, URLs and persistent identifiers.
- Identify the organization or provider responsible for distributing the resource.
- Distinguish the original creator or producer of data from the organization distributing or hosting it.
- Support multiple source references when a resource has several relevant origins or contributors.
- Identify when original source information is missing, incomplete or uncertain.
- Avoid inventing or automatically assigning source identities that cannot be established.

### Design Guidance

A scientific resource may have several distinct source relationships.

For example, an astronomical observation may have been collected by a particular telescope, produced using a specific instrument, processed by a research team and distributed through an external archive.

These relationships should remain distinguishable rather than being represented by a single generic provider name.

The application should preserve the source information available from the original resource while allowing additional information to be supplied when necessary.

## 4.7.2 Observation and Instrument Metadata

### Core Requirements

Preserve relevant observational and instrumental metadata associated with scientific data.

Depending on the resource, this may include:

- Astronomical object or target identifiers.
- Observation identifiers.
- Observation dates and times.
- Observing programs or project identifiers.
- Telescope and observatory information.
- Instrument and detector information.
- Observational technique or measurement method.
- Wavelength range or observational band.
- Spectral, spatial or temporal resolution.
- Exposure duration and observing conditions.
- Coordinates and reference frames.
- Available calibration information.
- Measurement units and conventions.
- Associated uncertainty and quality information.
- Relevant processing or reduction information supplied by the source.

Maintain relationships between metadata and the specific observations or data products it describes.

Preserve the original metadata where technically possible, including source-specific fields that may not have an immediate use within the application.

### Design Guidance

Observational metadata provides important context for interpreting astronomical measurements.

Two datasets may contain similar numerical values while representing observations made using different instruments, observing conditions or measurement conventions.

The application should retain this context rather than reducing scientific data to numerical measurements alone.

Not every resource will provide the same metadata. The application should preserve what is available and clearly identify important information that is missing.

## 4.7.3 Original Metadata Preservation

### Core Requirements

- Preserve original metadata during retrieval, import, conversion and subsequent use.
- Maintain relationships between metadata and the data products it describes.
- Retain source-specific metadata even when the application cannot interpret every field.
- Avoid discarding metadata solely because it does not fit a standardized internal representation.
- Preserve original field names and values where practical.
- Distinguish original metadata from application-generated metadata.
- Identify metadata that cannot be retained or interpreted.
- Support access to original metadata alongside any normalized or reorganized representation.
- Avoid silently replacing original metadata with inferred or user-supplied information.

### Design Guidance

Astronomical archives and scientific instruments may provide specialized metadata that does not map directly to a common structure.

The application should preserve this information even when it cannot immediately make use of it.

Standardized metadata representations may improve interoperability, but they should not require the loss of original source information.

Where the application creates a normalized representation, users should still be able to identify and inspect the original values.

## 4.7.4 Metadata Interpretation and Supplementation

### Core Requirements

Allow additional metadata to be interpreted, supplied or corrected when the original information is incomplete or requires clarification.

Support:

- Supplying missing metadata.
- Correcting identifiable metadata errors.
- Associating alternative object names and identifiers.
- Clarifying ambiguous measurement descriptions.
- Specifying units or conventions when they are not provided.
- Associating observations with known instruments or facilities.
- Adding relevant observation or research context.
- Recording explanations for significant corrections.

Maintain a distinction between:

- Original source metadata.
- Application-generated interpretations.
- User-supplied information.
- User corrections to existing metadata.
- Information inferred from external references.

Preserve original values when metadata is corrected or supplemented.

Allow users to inspect the origin of additional information and identify unresolved ambiguity.

### Design Guidance

Imported files may contain incomplete headers, inconsistent field names or insufficient descriptions of their scientific contents.

Users should be able to supply additional context without losing the original information.

For example, a user may identify the instrument associated with an older observational file when that information was not included in its metadata.

The application should record this as user-supplied information rather than presenting it as though it had been confirmed by the original source.

## 4.7.5 Dataset Identifiers and Persistent References

### Core Requirements

- Preserve identifiers assigned by original data providers.
- Support multiple identifiers for a resource where appropriate.
- Retain observation identifiers, catalogue identifiers, dataset identifiers and publication identifiers.
- Support persistent identifiers where available.
- Preserve references to original archive records and resource descriptions.
- Distinguish identifiers assigned by external providers from internal application identifiers.
- Maintain relationships between alternate identifiers referring to the same resource where this can be established reliably.
- Identify when an external identifier is unavailable or cannot be verified.
- Avoid replacing original identifiers with application-specific identifiers without retaining the original reference.

### Design Guidance

Astronomical resources may be identified through multiple naming systems.

An observation may have an archive identifier, an observing-program identifier, an instrument-specific identifier and an associated publication reference.

These identifiers should remain available because different archives and scientific resources may use different conventions.

Internal application identifiers may be useful for organizing research materials, but they should not replace the identifiers needed to locate and cite original resources.

## 4.7.6 Scientific Citations and Bibliographic References

### Core Requirements

- Preserve recommended citation information supplied by external data providers.
- Support references to scientific publications associated with observations and datasets.
- Retain available bibliographic information, including authors, publication titles, publication dates, journals and persistent identifiers.
- Distinguish dataset citations from publication citations.
- Support attribution of relevant observatories, instruments, facilities, archives and data providers.
- Allow users to associate multiple publications with a dataset or investigation.
- Preserve citations when resources are incorporated into projects or exported.
- Support commonly used scientific citation styles where practical.
- Support appropriate citation export formats.
- Identify missing or incomplete bibliographic information.
- Avoid inventing citation details or presenting unverified references as complete.

**Automatic citation and bibliographic retrieval**

Where external providers or bibliographic services make citation information available, the application should:

- Attempt to automatically retrieve recommended dataset citations from the original data source.
- Retrieve available bibliographic information for associated scientific publications.
- Use available resource identifiers, persistent identifiers, archive records and other relevant metadata to locate corresponding citation information.
- Support retrieval through appropriate external provider interfaces, APIs, metadata services and bibliographic resources.
- Retrieve available citation components, such as authors, titles, publication dates, journals, dataset identifiers, publication identifiers and persistent references.
- Preserve the source from which citation information was obtained.
- Maintain a relationship between retrieved citation information and the specific dataset, observation, publication or resource it describes.
- Distinguish automatically retrieved information from manually entered, user-corrected or otherwise supplemented bibliographic information.
- Allow users to inspect automatically retrieved citations and correct or supplement incomplete information.
- Identify when citation information cannot be retrieved, verified or matched confidently.
- Avoid automatically treating a related publication as the required dataset citation.
- Avoid assuming that an associated publication citation satisfies an archive's recommended dataset attribution requirements.

### Design Guidance

Scientific data and the publications describing it may require separate attribution.

For example, a dataset may have a recommended archive citation while also being discussed in one or more scientific publications.

The application should retain these relationships without assuming that citing the associated publication automatically satisfies the provider's dataset attribution requirements.

Automatic retrieval should reduce the amount of bibliographic information users must enter manually, particularly when working with resources that already provide structured citation metadata.

However, automatically retrieved citation information should not be treated as verified merely because it was returned by an external service.

The application should establish which resource the citation describes, preserve its retrieval source and allow users to inspect ambiguous or incomplete matches.

Citation information should be accessible when users inspect resources and when they prepare research outputs.

Detailed reference-library organization, literature discovery and broader citation-management functionality are addressed in Section 7.6.

## 4.7.7 Attribution of Original Creators and Contributors

### Core Requirements

- Preserve available information identifying original data creators and contributors.
- Distinguish data creators, observing teams, instrument teams, processing teams, archives and distributing organizations where possible.
- Retain authorship and contributor information supplied by original sources.
- Support attribution of scientific reference resources and external databases.
- Allow users to supplement incomplete contributor information.
- Preserve attribution when data is incorporated into derived research materials.
- Avoid presenting the user who imported a dataset as its original creator unless this is established.
- Maintain relevant attribution information when research materials are shared or exported.
- Identify when contributor information is unavailable or uncertain.

### Design Guidance

Scientific resources may involve contributions from multiple researchers and organizations.

An observation may be collected by one team, processed by another and distributed through a separate archive.

The application should preserve these distinctions where the information is available.

Attribution should reflect the source's documented contribution rather than relying on assumptions about ownership or authorship.

## 4.7.8 Metadata and Source Information for User-Provided Data

### Core Requirements

- Preserve source information and metadata contained in user-provided files.
- Allow users to supply additional source details when importing files.
- Support recording the original instrument, institution, observation or research activity associated with a file.
- Preserve available creator and contributor information.
- Distinguish the person importing a file from its original creator where known.
- Allow users to associate publications and external references with imported data.
- Retain original metadata when additional information is supplied.
- Identify metadata that cannot be established reliably.
- Preserve source information when user-provided data is subsequently used in research.

### Design Guidance

Not all scientific data originates from public astronomical archives.

Users may import measurements from laboratory instruments, personal observations, institutional collections, previous research projects or collaborators.

The application should accommodate these sources without assuming that all imported data has a formal archive record or publication citation.

Where information is unavailable, users should be able to provide it voluntarily and have its origin clearly identified.

## 4.7.9 Source Information Across Research Activities

### Core Requirements

- Preserve source information when datasets are used in different scientific workspaces.
- Maintain metadata and attribution when research materials are incorporated into projects.
- Preserve original source identifiers when data is copied, referenced or reorganized.
- Retain relevant citations when datasets contribute to analyses and research outputs.
- Support source attribution for materials shared with other users, subject to applicable access and licensing conditions.
- Preserve relevant source information during project duplication and export.
- Identify when source information cannot be retained during a transfer.
- Maintain relationships between resources and their original source records.

### Design Guidance

Source information should remain associated with scientific materials throughout their use in the application.

Moving a dataset between workspaces or incorporating it into a project should not require users to reconstruct its original source information.

Likewise, research outputs should retain access to relevant source references so that users can identify and cite the underlying scientific materials.

## 4.7.10 Incomplete, Conflicting and Uncertain Source Information

### Core Requirements

- Identify missing or incomplete source information.
- Distinguish information that is unavailable from information that has not yet been investigated.
- Communicate uncertainty about source identities and metadata interpretations.
- Preserve conflicting source information rather than silently choosing one version.
- Allow users to investigate and supplement incomplete information.
- Record significant corrections and their origins.
- Avoid fabricating source identifiers, observation details, bibliographic information or authorship.
- Identify when incomplete source information may affect attribution or scientific interpretation.
- Preserve unresolved questions where source information cannot be verified.

### Design Guidance

Historical datasets, converted files and older scientific publications may contain incomplete or inconsistent source information.

The application should preserve what is known and make uncertainty visible.

For example, a dataset may contain an observation identifier but lack the instrument details needed to identify its original observing configuration.

The application should not fill this gap with an unsupported assumption merely to create a complete-looking metadata record.

## General Principles

Data provenance, metadata and source attribution should:

- Preserve the origins of scientific data and external resources.
- Retain original observational and instrumental metadata.
- Preserve source-specific metadata and identifiers.
- Distinguish original information from user-supplied or application-generated interpretations.
- Support metadata correction and supplementation without losing original values.
- Preserve dataset and publication citations.
- Support automatic retrieval of available citation and bibliographic information from external sources.
- Distinguish retrieved, user-supplied, corrected and unverified citation information.
- Recognize original creators, contributors, facilities and data providers.
- Accommodate user-provided research materials.
- Maintain source information across workspaces, projects and exports.
- Communicate incomplete, conflicting and uncertain information.
- Avoid inventing or presenting unverified source information as fact.

## General Principle

Every scientific resource should retain the information needed to understand where it originated, what it represents and who or what contributed to its creation or distribution.

Provenance, metadata and attribution should remain connected to scientific materials throughout their use in the application, supporting responsible citation, scientific transparency and informed research.
