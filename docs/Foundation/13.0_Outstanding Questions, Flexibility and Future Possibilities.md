# 13. Outstanding Questions, Flexibility and Future Possibilities

This section establishes how the application preserves unresolved questions, accommodates future scientific and technical opportunities, and remains adaptable as its requirements and development direction evolve.

It provides a framework for managing uncertainty and long-term possibilities without allowing speculative ideas to become accidental requirements or unnecessarily expand the active development scope.

The central principle is to maintain an ambitious and flexible vision while ensuring that development remains deliberate, evidence-based and manageable.

### 13.1 — Outstanding Questions and Unresolved Decisions

Establishes how unresolved scientific, technical, architectural and functional questions are identified, classified and managed. Distinguishes blocking decisions from matters that can be deferred, establishes independent investigation and user approval boundaries, and preserves outstanding questions in project documentation.

### 13.2 — Future Scientific Capabilities and Disciplines

Preserves opportunities to expand beyond the application's initial scientific priorities. Covers additional astronomical disciplines, advanced research capabilities, deeper functionality within existing disciplines, cross-disciplinary investigations and emerging scientific methods. Requires scientific and technical evaluation before future capabilities become development commitments.

### 13.3 — Future Technical and Application Possibilities

Identifies potential expansions to the application beyond individual scientific disciplines. Covers advanced functionality, automation, intelligent assistance, computational infrastructure, data resources, customization, user-created capabilities, collaboration, community infrastructure and platform deployment. Emphasizes practical value, maintainability and responsible expansion.

### 13.4 — Adaptability and Evolving Requirements

Establishes how the application adapts to changing scientific knowledge, user requirements, technological developments and practical experience. Addresses architectural flexibility, changing development scope, compatibility, retiring or replacing functionality, and preservation of historical research and scientific continuity.

### 13.5 — Evaluation and Prioritization of Future Opportunities

Defines a consistent approach to evaluating future capabilities and improvements. Considers scientific value, practical usefulness, strategic relevance, technical feasibility, development effort, dependencies, risks, maintenance and alternatives. Distinguishes immediate needs, development priorities, deferred opportunities and rejected proposals while maintaining the Improvement Register and Master Development Roadmap.

### 13.6 — Long-Term Vision and Development Flexibility

Establishes the application's broader long-term ambitions and the principles guiding its continued evolution. Preserves opportunities for scientific and technical expansion, emphasizes sustainability and maintainability, and ensures that the application can respond to future developments without becoming constrained by its initial implementation or a fixed feature list.

## Overall Principles

- Preserve an open and adaptable long-term scientific vision.
- Keep unresolved questions visible and appropriately prioritized.
- Distinguish potential opportunities from approved requirements and development commitments.
- Encourage scientific and technological expansion when justified.
- Evaluate opportunities according to evidence, practical value, feasibility and sustainability.
- Adapt to changing scientific knowledge and user needs without unnecessary redesign.
- Preserve historical decisions, research results, data and development continuity.
- Maintain clear development priorities and user approval boundaries.
- Avoid uncontrolled scope expansion and unnecessary complexity.
- Treat the application's future as an evolving direction rather than a predetermined final feature list.

**General principle:** The application should remain ambitious, scientifically credible and adaptable while preserving the discipline and practical direction necessary for sustainable development. Future possibilities should remain open to investigation without compromising current priorities or the integrity of existing research.

## Implementation Boundary

Section 13 establishes principles for managing unresolved questions, future opportunities and long-term adaptability. It does not independently authorize new functionality, architectural changes, technology adoption or expansion of the approved development scope.

Outstanding questions, improvement opportunities and approved development work must remain distinguishable and be maintained in their appropriate records, particularly the Master Development Roadmap and Improvement Register.

Significant changes to requirements, priorities, architecture or development direction remain subject to the approval procedures established in Section 10.
