# 7.6 — Scientific Literature and References

## 7.6.1 Scientific Literature Discovery

The application should provide ways for users to discover scientific literature relevant to their interests, research questions, astronomical objects and investigations.

Literature discovery should support both broad exploration of scientific subjects and targeted searches for specific research questions.

Relevant literature may include:

- Peer-reviewed research papers.
- Review articles and scientific surveys.
- Preprints and manuscripts.
- Scientific conference proceedings.
- Academic theses and dissertations.
- Scientific books and textbooks.
- Observational and instrumental papers.
- Research papers describing scientific methods, models and computational techniques.
- Publications associated with astronomical datasets, catalogues and archives.
- Historical scientific publications where relevant.

Discovery should cover the application's scientific priorities, including exoplanet science, planetary science, stellar astronomy, observational astronomy, spectroscopy, astrochemistry, atmospheric modelling and other relevant disciplines as the application expands.

Users should be able to discover literature through several approaches:

- Searching by astronomical object, such as a planet, star, exoplanet or planetary system.
- Searching by scientific subject or research area.
- Searching by specific research question or scientific concept.
- Searching by molecular species, atomic transitions, atmospheric components or spectral features.
- Searching by observational method, instrument, wavelength range or dataset.
- Searching by author, publication, journal, DOI or other bibliographic identifier.
- Exploring publications associated with particular datasets, models, methods or scientific resources.
- Discovering related publications through citations and references.

Search results should provide enough information to assess relevance without requiring users to open every publication individually.

Where available, this should include publication title, authors, publication date, journal or source, abstract, DOI or other identifier, publication type and access information.

The application should distinguish between different publication types and their respective roles in scientific research.

## 7.6.2 External Literature Databases and Scientific Sources

The application should investigate and integrate appropriate external literature databases and publication resources.

Potential sources include:

- NASA Astrophysics Data System (ADS).
- arXiv.
- Crossref.
- OpenAlex.
- Semantic Scholar.
- Publisher and journal platforms.
- University and institutional repositories.
- Other established scientific publication databases.

These are examples for investigation, not a requirement to integrate every service.

The AI development partner should independently research suitable literature sources and evaluate:

- Scientific coverage and relevance.
- Publication and metadata quality.
- Search functionality and available filters.
- API availability and integration requirements.
- Accessibility of abstracts, full texts and bibliographic information.
- Access restrictions and subscription requirements.
- Licensing and permitted usage.
- Reliability, maintenance and long-term availability.
- Duplicate records and overlapping database coverage.
- Suitability for the application's scientific disciplines.

The application should use reliable sources and avoid unnecessary duplication of external services.

Where several sources provide overlapping information, the application should investigate how to consolidate results while preserving the original source and publication identifiers.

The availability of a publication in a search result should not imply that its full text is freely accessible or that the application has permission to download, store or redistribute it.

## 7.6.3 Literature Search, Filtering and Organization

Literature search should support progressively more specific discovery without making the initial search process unnecessarily complicated.

Users should be able to refine results using relevant criteria, including:

- Scientific discipline or subject.
- Astronomical object or system.
- Publication date or date range.
- Author.
- Journal or publication source.
- Publication type.
- Research method or observational technique.
- Instrument or observatory.
- Wavelength range.
- Molecular species or other scientific entities.
- Availability of full text.
- Citation relationships.
- Other relevant scientific metadata.

Search criteria should be appropriate to the subject being investigated. Not every filter needs to be available for every type of search.

Where possible, users should be able to combine search terms and filters, refine existing results and explore related publications without repeatedly starting new searches.

Search results should be organized in a readable and useful manner.

Potential sorting options include:

- Relevance to the search.
- Publication date.
- Citation information where available.
- Author or publication source.
- Other useful scientific or bibliographic criteria.

Ranking and sorting should not imply scientific validity. Citation counts, publication prominence and search ranking are indicators of visibility or relevance, not proof that a scientific conclusion is correct.

## 7.6.4 Publication Information and Literature Inspection

Users should be able to inspect useful publication information directly within the application.

Where available, publication records should include:

- Full title.
- Author list.
- Publication date.
- Journal, conference or repository.
- Abstract.
- DOI or other persistent identifier.
- Publication type and status.
- Keywords and scientific subjects.
- Associated astronomical objects.
- Relevant datasets, instruments or observational facilities.
- References and citation relationships.
- Full-text access information.
- Source database and retrieval information.

The application should make it straightforward to open the original publication through its official source.

Where full text is available through a permitted source, the application may provide an integrated reading or inspection experience.

Where full text is unavailable, users should still be able to access the bibliographic information and follow the available links to the publisher, repository or institutional source.

The application should clearly distinguish between:

- Full publication text.
- Abstracts.
- Extracted publication metadata.
- Search result summaries.
- AI-generated summaries or explanations.
- User-created notes and interpretations.

These should never be presented as interchangeable.

## 7.6.5 Literature Access and Full-Text Handling

The application should accommodate different forms of publication access.

This may include:

- Open-access publications.
- Publicly available preprints.
- Institutional repository copies.
- Publisher-hosted publications.
- Subscription-based publications.
- Publications accessible through institutional or personal subscriptions.
- Historical publications available through archival services.

The application should identify access restrictions where possible and provide appropriate routes to the original publication.

Users should not be required to access all literature through a single integrated reader. External links and access through existing institutional or publisher services should remain supported.

Where users provide their own publication files, the application should investigate support for common document formats, particularly PDF.

User-provided publications should be handled separately from publications retrieved through external services.

The application should preserve relevant information about the source and access status of imported publications.

It should not assume that a publication may be redistributed or shared simply because a user can access it.

Full-text storage, extraction, indexing and sharing must respect applicable copyright, licensing and access restrictions.

## 7.6.6 Literature Connections to Astronomical Objects and Scientific Research

Scientific publications should connect naturally to the rest of the application.

Where relevant information is available, publications should be associated with:

- Astronomical objects and planetary systems.
- Stars and stellar systems.
- Exoplanets and their host stars.
- Solar System planets and natural satellites.
- Observations and observational datasets.
- Instruments and observatories.
- Spectral measurements and reference data.
- Molecular and atomic species.
- Atmospheric models and retrieval methods.
- Scientific analyses and research projects.
- Other publications and scientific resources.

For example, users investigating an exoplanet should be able to discover relevant publications concerning its discovery, orbital properties, atmospheric observations, spectral analysis, characterization and subsequent research.

Users investigating a spectrum should be able to find relevant publications describing the observed object, instrument, spectral features, molecular identifications and applicable analytical methods.

Literature connections should help users understand how individual publications relate to broader scientific investigations.

These relationships should be recorded where possible rather than relying exclusively on manually maintained links.

The application should distinguish between a publication that directly reports an observation, one that reanalyses existing data, one that presents a theoretical model and one that discusses or reviews previous research.

## 7.6.7 Literature Organization Within Research Projects

Users should be able to organize scientific literature within their research projects.

This should support:

- Associating publications with individual projects.
- Grouping papers by research subject or question.
- Saving publications for later reading.
- Organizing literature into user-defined categories or collections.
- Linking papers to specific astronomical objects.
- Connecting publications to datasets, analyses, models and results.
- Recording relevant publication details and identifiers.
- Maintaining literature lists for individual investigations.
- Revisiting previously collected references.

Literature organization should remain flexible.

Users may organize papers by object, research question, scientific method, discipline or another structure appropriate to their investigation.

The application should avoid unnecessary duplication of publication records when the same paper is relevant to multiple projects.

Where a publication is associated with several projects, its bibliographic identity and source information should remain consistent while project-specific notes and relationships remain independently manageable.

Literature collections should be included in project export and portability where technically and legally appropriate.

## 7.6.8 Personal Literature Libraries and Reading Management

The application should provide users with a way to maintain an organized personal collection of scientific publications and references.

Potential functionality includes:

- Saving publications to a personal library.
- Creating folders, categories or collections.
- Adding tags and keywords.
- Marking papers as unread, reading, read or requiring further investigation.
- Saving reading progress where supported.
- Adding personal notes and annotations.
- Recording important findings or questions.
- Linking publications to projects and research topics.
- Searching and filtering saved literature.
- Identifying duplicate publications.
- Exporting bibliographic records.

Users should be able to distinguish between publications they have saved for general interest and those actively associated with research projects.

Personal notes, annotations and interpretations must remain distinct from the original publication content.

The application should preserve publication identifiers and source information so that saved literature can be located again even if external database records or links change.

More advanced reading management, citation organization and reference-library functionality can be expanded as the application develops.

## 7.6.9 Citation Management and Bibliographic References

The application should provide reliable citation and reference management.

Users should be able to create and maintain bibliographic references associated with projects, analyses, reports and other scientific material.

Relevant capabilities should include:

- Retrieving bibliographic metadata from external databases.
- Importing existing references.
- Adding references manually.
- Editing incomplete or incorrect metadata.
- Associating citations with relevant projects and research outputs.
- Generating formatted bibliographies.
- Supporting commonly used academic citation styles.
- Exporting references in established bibliographic formats.
- Identifying duplicate records.
- Preserving DOI and other persistent identifiers.
- Maintaining links to original publications.

Citation formatting should be generated from structured bibliographic information wherever possible.

Users should be able to inspect and correct retrieved metadata. Corrections should not silently replace the original source information without preserving the distinction between retrieved and user-modified details.

The application should support established reference formats where practical, including BibTeX and RIS.

Citation information should be traceable to its source.

The application should also distinguish between:

- Publications cited as scientific evidence.
- Publications describing methods or analytical procedures.
- Publications associated with datasets or instruments.
- General educational and background references.
- Further reading that has not necessarily been used to support a particular conclusion.

This distinction will help users maintain accurate and meaningful scientific references.

## 7.6.10 Citation Relationships and Research Development

The application should support exploration of relationships between scientific publications.

Where external databases provide the necessary information, users should be able to explore:

- Publications cited by a particular paper.
- Later publications citing that paper.
- Related publications by the same authors.
- Papers addressing similar research questions.
- Publications using related observational datasets or methods.
- Research that supports, challenges or revises earlier findings.
- The development of a scientific subject over time.

Citation relationships should help users understand how scientific knowledge develops.

The application should not assume that a paper cited by another publication is necessarily endorsed by it. Citations can be used to support, criticize, compare or provide historical context.

Where practical, the application should help users identify important differences between publications, including changes in observational evidence, analytical methods, model assumptions and scientific interpretation.

More advanced literature relationship exploration may be considered as a future capability.

## 7.6.11 Literature Provenance, Publication Status and Scientific Context

Scientific literature should be represented with appropriate source information and publication context.

The application should distinguish between publication states where relevant, including:

- Peer-reviewed publications.
- Preprints.
- Accepted manuscripts.
- Published conference proceedings.
- Theses and dissertations.
- Review articles.
- Retracted publications.
- Corrected or updated publications.

Publication status should be based on reliable source information wherever possible.

The application should not treat a preprint as equivalent to a peer-reviewed publication without making the distinction clear.

Where a publication has been retracted, corrected or otherwise subject to an important publication-status change, the application should communicate this information when it is available.

Users should be able to identify the source database, retrieval date and relevant publication identifiers.

Literature records should preserve bibliographic provenance, including the origin of retrieved metadata and any subsequent user corrections.

The application should not silently replace an older publication record with a newer version or discard the historical relationship between them.

## 7.6.12 Literature Evaluation and Scientific Interpretation

The application should help users assess scientific publications critically without presenting automated judgments as definitive assessments of scientific validity.

Where appropriate, educational guidance should help users consider:

- The research question and scientific objective.
- The observational or theoretical basis of the work.
- The methods and assumptions used.
- The quality and limitations of the underlying data.
- Statistical treatment and uncertainty.
- Instrumental and observational limitations.
- The strength of the evidence supporting the conclusions.
- Alternative explanations and interpretations.
- Whether the findings have been independently reproduced.
- How subsequent research has developed or challenged the conclusions.

The application should distinguish between the existence of a publication and the strength of its scientific evidence.

Publication status, journal reputation, citation counts and author prominence should not be treated as substitutes for evaluating the actual methodology and evidence.

Where several publications reach different conclusions, the application should help users identify the reasons for disagreement, including differences in datasets, methods, assumptions, observational conditions and interpretation.

The goal is to support informed scientific reading and critical evaluation rather than automatically assigning a publication a simplistic credibility score.

## 7.6.13 Literature Summaries and Research Assistance

The application may provide tools to help users understand and navigate scientific publications.

Potential assistance includes:

- Summaries of publication abstracts.
- Explanations of research objectives and findings.
- Identification of methods, datasets and instruments discussed.
- Explanations of unfamiliar scientific terminology.
- Clarification of mathematical and physical concepts.
- Identification of assumptions and stated limitations.
- Summaries of relevant sections.
- Identification of important equations or analytical methods.
- Comparison of related publications.
- Identification of questions or subjects for further investigation.

Where AI-assisted functionality is used, generated summaries and interpretations must be clearly identified as AI-generated.

They must not be presented as quotations or statements made directly by the publication unless they accurately reproduce the cited source.

AI-generated explanations should provide traceable references to the relevant publication sections or passages where technically possible.

The application must not fabricate publication details, authors, citations, findings, equations or scientific claims.

Summaries should preserve important qualifications, uncertainties and limitations rather than reducing complex scientific findings to misleading conclusions.

Users should be able to access the original publication and verify the information independently.

AI-assisted literature analysis should complement, not replace, reading and critical evaluation of the original scientific work.

This functionality should be developed consistently with the scientific integrity requirements in Section 5 and the future AI-assisted capabilities discussed in Section 5.7.

## 7.6.14 Literature Search History and Research Continuity

The application should support continuity across literature discovery and research activities.

Where appropriate, users should be able to:

- Revisit previous searches.
- Access previously inspected publications.
- Return to saved search results.
- Maintain collections of literature associated with ongoing investigations.
- Identify which publications informed particular analyses or decisions.
- Record why a publication was considered relevant.
- Revisit literature when new observations or research findings become available.

Search history and saved literature should be organized so users can continue investigations without unnecessarily repeating previous discovery work.

Where an external database changes, a publication becomes unavailable or bibliographic information is updated, the application should preserve existing records and communicate relevant changes.

Historical research records should not be silently rewritten because an external publication record or database has changed.

Literature relationships should integrate with the wider project history, provenance and reproducibility systems.

## Future Possibilities

The following capabilities may be considered as the application develops:

- More advanced literature discovery and semantic search.
- Research-topic monitoring and optional publication alerts.
- Notifications when new papers appear concerning selected objects, subjects or research questions.
- Advanced citation-network exploration and visualization.
- Research trend and publication-history analysis.
- Comparison of competing scientific interpretations across publications.
- More extensive publication reading and annotation tools.
- Integration with additional scientific literature databases.
- Advanced AI-assisted paper analysis and cross-publication synthesis.
- User-defined literature collections and reading workflows.
- Collaborative reading lists and shared bibliographies.
- Community-curated publication collections.
- More advanced connections between literature, observations, datasets, models and research projects.

These capabilities should be evaluated according to scientific value, technical feasibility, data access, licensing and integration requirements.

## Initial Implementation Scope

Initial literature capabilities should prioritize:

- Integration with a small number of reliable scientific literature sources.
- Searching by subject, astronomical object, author and publication identifier.
- Useful publication metadata and abstract retrieval.
- Direct links to original publications.
- Basic filtering and sorting.
- Identification of publication types and access availability.
- Saving and organizing literature.
- Associating publications with research projects and astronomical objects.
- Basic citation and bibliographic management.
- Import and export of established reference formats.
- Preservation of publication identifiers and source information.
- Basic distinction between preprints, peer-reviewed publications and other publication types.
- Clear identification of inaccessible or restricted full text.

More advanced AI-assisted literature analysis, citation-network exploration and research monitoring should be considered after the underlying literature discovery and reference-management capabilities are established.

## Implementation Considerations

- External literature sources should be researched and evaluated before integration.
- Publication metadata should be traceable to its source.
- Duplicate publications and overlapping database records should be handled carefully.
- Full-text access, storage, extraction and redistribution must respect licensing and copyright restrictions.
- External publication records should not be treated as permanently available.
- Users should be able to correct incomplete or incorrect bibliographic information.
- User modifications should remain distinguishable from original source metadata.
- Preprints, peer-reviewed publications, reviews and other publication types should be clearly distinguished.
- Retractions and corrections should be communicated when reliable information is available.
- Citation counts and publication prominence must not be presented as proof of scientific validity.
- AI-generated summaries must remain traceable, clearly identified and open to verification against the original publication.
- Literature records should integrate with the application's project management, provenance, knowledge management and reproducibility systems.
- The literature system should expand as additional scientific disciplines and research capabilities are introduced.

## General Principle

The application should provide an integrated environment for discovering, accessing, understanding and organizing scientific literature.

Scientific publications should connect naturally to astronomical objects, observations, datasets, analytical methods, models and research projects.

The system should support users ranging from beginners learning to navigate scientific literature to experienced researchers conducting detailed literature reviews.

It should encourage critical evaluation of scientific evidence, preserve accurate bibliographic information and maintain traceable relationships between publications and research outputs.

The objective is not simply to provide a search engine for papers, but to make scientific literature an accessible and integrated part of the research environment while preserving the importance of original sources, scientific context and independent evaluation.
