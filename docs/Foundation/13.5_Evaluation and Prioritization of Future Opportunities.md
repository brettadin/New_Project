# 13.5 — Evaluation and Prioritization of Future Opportunities

## Purpose

Establish a consistent approach to evaluating and prioritizing potential future capabilities, improvements and development opportunities.

The application should remain open to new ideas and scientific developments. However, potential opportunities must be assessed according to their actual value, feasibility, dependencies and long-term implications.

An interesting idea should not automatically become a development requirement or implementation commitment.

## 13.5.1 Identification of Future Opportunities

**Core requirements**

- Identify potential scientific, technical, functional and usability improvements.
- Consider opportunities arising from scientific research, technological developments and user feedback.
- Identify capabilities that could extend existing functionality or introduce new research possibilities.
- Consider opportunities for improving shared infrastructure and cross-disciplinary workflows.
- Identify potentially useful external resources, software and integrations.
- Preserve worthwhile ideas that are not currently practical to implement.
- Distinguish new opportunities from existing approved requirements.
- Record significant opportunities in the appropriate development documentation.

Potential opportunities may originate from independent research, implementation experience, community contributions, external developments or previously deferred ideas.

## 13.5.2 Evaluation Criteria

Evaluate significant opportunities using consistent criteria.

**Core evaluation criteria**

1. **Scientific value:** Does the opportunity improve scientific capability, accuracy, understanding or research possibilities?
2. **Practical usefulness:** Does it address a genuine user need or improve an existing workflow?
3. **Strategic relevance:** Does it support the application's overall purpose and long-term direction?
4. **Technical feasibility:** Can it be implemented reliably with available or reasonably obtainable technologies?
5. **Development effort:** What time, complexity and resources would be required?
6. **Dependencies:** Does it rely on unfinished infrastructure, external resources or other capabilities?
7. **Compatibility:** Will it integrate appropriately with existing functionality, platforms and scientific workflows?
8. **Maintenance:** What additional long-term maintenance and support would it introduce?
9. **Security and privacy:** Does it introduce additional risks or external data exposure?
10. **Licensing and cost:** Are there financial, licensing or operational restrictions?
11. **Expansion value:** Does it establish foundations or opportunities that support additional future capabilities?
12. **Alternatives:** Can the same objective be achieved more simply using existing functionality or established tools?

Not every criterion will carry equal importance for every opportunity. Evaluation should reflect the nature and significance of the proposed change.

## 13.5.3 Scientific and Technical Value

**Core requirements**

- Prioritize opportunities that provide meaningful scientific or practical benefits.
- Evaluate whether a proposed capability improves existing methods or enables worthwhile new investigations.
- Consider the reliability and maturity of supporting scientific methods.
- Assess whether the technology is sufficiently established for its intended application.
- Identify opportunities that could improve accuracy, validation, reproducibility or data quality.
- Consider whether existing capabilities already satisfy the intended objective.
- Avoid adopting features solely because they are technically interesting or fashionable.
- Distinguish scientifically established improvements from experimental possibilities.

Scientific credibility and practical usefulness should take precedence over novelty alone.

## 13.5.4 Development Cost, Complexity and Risk

**Core requirements**

- Estimate implementation complexity and required development effort.
- Identify architectural changes and additional dependencies.
- Assess computational, storage and infrastructure requirements.
- Consider licensing, subscription and ongoing operational costs.
- Identify potential effects on existing functionality.
- Evaluate security, privacy and data integrity risks.
- Consider long-term maintenance and compatibility.
- Identify whether an opportunity can be introduced incrementally.
- Compare expected benefits against implementation and maintenance burdens.

**Design guidance**

A capability with substantial scientific value may justify significant complexity. However, complexity should be understood and justified rather than introduced without considering simpler alternatives.

## 13.5.5 Prioritization and Opportunity Classification

Classify significant opportunities according to their current development relevance.

**Suggested classifications**

- **Immediate attention:** A necessary correction, significant risk or important opportunity that should be addressed promptly.
- **High priority:** A valuable capability or improvement that should be considered for an upcoming development stage.
- **Medium priority:** Worthwhile functionality that should follow more important foundational work.
- **Low priority:** Useful but nonessential enhancements that can remain in the opportunity register.
- **Deferred:** An opportunity that may be valuable but currently depends on unavailable resources, unfinished infrastructure or unsuitable conditions.
- **Exploratory:** An idea requiring additional investigation before its value or feasibility can be established.
- **Rejected:** An opportunity determined to be unsuitable or unjustified.

These classifications describe current priority rather than permanent decisions.

An opportunity may be reassessed when requirements, scientific knowledge, technology or available resources change.

## 13.5.6 Relationship to Existing Requirements and Development Plans

**Core requirements**

- Determine whether an opportunity addresses an existing requirement or introduces new functionality.
- Identify its relationship to current development stages and dependencies.
- Consider whether it should be incorporated into an existing capability or treated as a separate addition.
- Identify potential duplication or conflicts with approved requirements.
- Assess effects on the Master Development Roadmap.
- Preserve the original application direction when evaluating unrelated opportunities.
- Obtain appropriate approval before introducing consequential scope or architectural changes.
- Avoid allowing speculative opportunities to displace higher-priority approved work without justification.

The Master Development Roadmap remains the authoritative source for approved development priorities and commitments.

The Improvement Register established in Section 10.8 should preserve evaluated opportunities and their status.

## 13.5.7 Recommendations and Decision-Making

**Core requirements**

For significant opportunities, the AI development partner should provide a recommendation that explains:

- What the opportunity involves.
- Why it may be valuable.
- What problem or need it addresses.
- Expected scientific or practical benefits.
- Estimated development effort and complexity.
- Dependencies and prerequisites.
- Potential risks and maintenance implications.
- Relevant alternatives.
- Recommended priority.
- Whether additional research or user approval is necessary.

The AI should make ordinary recommendations independently rather than asking the user to evaluate technical details they may not be equipped to assess.

Consequential decisions must follow the approval procedures established in Section 10.

## 13.5.8 Reassessment of Deferred Opportunities

**Core requirements**

- Preserve worthwhile deferred opportunities.
- Record why they were deferred and what conditions would justify reconsideration.
- Reassess them when relevant technologies, resources or dependencies become available.
- Consider changes in scientific understanding and user requirements.
- Identify when an opportunity is no longer relevant or has been superseded.
- Preserve the reasoning behind rejection or deferral.
- Avoid repeatedly investigating opportunities without meaningful new information.

Deferred opportunities should remain accessible without becoming a permanent backlog of obligations.

## 13.5.9 Documentation and Opportunity Management

**Core requirements**

- Record significant opportunities in the Improvement Register.
- Include their purpose, origin, expected benefits, dependencies, complexity, risks and priority.
- Record evaluation findings and recommendations.
- Distinguish proposed, investigated, recommended, approved, deferred, rejected and implemented opportunities.
- Link approved opportunities to the Master Development Roadmap.
- Preserve decision history and reasons for changes in status.
- Close implemented opportunities after appropriate verification.
- Review the register periodically to remove duplication and identify outdated possibilities without losing important historical context.

The Improvement Register and Master Development Roadmap serve different purposes and must remain distinct.

## General Principles

- Encourage useful innovation without uncontrolled feature expansion.
- Evaluate opportunities according to scientific and practical value.
- Consider development effort, dependencies, maintenance and risk.
- Prefer improvements that meaningfully strengthen existing capabilities.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication and complexity.
- Preserve promising ideas that are not yet practical.
- Reassess opportunities when circumstances change.
- Distinguish recommendations from approved development commitments.
- Maintain the Improvement Register and Master Development Roadmap as separate authoritative records.

**General principle:** Future opportunities should be encouraged, investigated and evaluated carefully. The application should expand through deliberate, evidence-based decisions rather than accumulating features simply because they are possible or interesting.
