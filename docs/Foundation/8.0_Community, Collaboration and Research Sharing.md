# 8.0 — Community, Collaboration and Research Sharing

## Section Purpose

This section establishes the application's community environment and the capabilities through which users can share scientific work, collaborate on investigations, exchange research resources and participate in a wider astronomy research community.

The community should support users with different levels of scientific knowledge and experience, including beginners, amateur astronomers, students, educators, independent researchers and professional scientists.

It should accommodate both independent research and collaborative scientific activities, allowing users to share selected research materials, contribute to investigations, exchange analytical methods and participate in scientific discussion.

Community functionality should remain integrated with the application's scientific workspaces and research environments while also providing broader opportunities for interaction and resource discovery.

The section establishes requirements for community participation, research identity, research sharing, collaborative projects, data and resource exchange, workflow sharing, scientific discussion, review, attribution, privacy, research networking and responsible community conduct.

## Section Overview

Section 8 addresses thirteen interconnected areas:

1. **Community Environment and Participation:** Establishes the overall community environment, intended participants and forms of interaction.
2. **User Profiles and Research Identity:** Defines user profiles, scientific interests, areas of expertise and research contributions.
3. **Research Sharing and Publication:** Establishes how users can share research, findings, analyses and scientific material.
4. **Collaborative Research Projects:** Defines how multiple users can participate in shared investigations and collaborative research activities.
5. **Data, Observations and Resource Sharing:** Establishes how users can share datasets, observations, spectra, photometry, models and other scientific resources.
6. **Analysis and Workflow Sharing:** Defines how users can share analytical procedures, processing workflows, models and reproducible investigations.
7. **Scientific Discussion and Peer Feedback:** Establishes environments for discussing findings, exchanging interpretations, questioning conclusions and providing feedback.
8. **Scientific Review and Reproducibility:** Defines how users can independently examine, reproduce and evaluate shared research and scientific methods.
9. **Groups and Research Communities:** Establishes organized groups around scientific disciplines, astronomical objects, research interests and collaborative activities.
10. **Attribution, Ownership and Research Recognition:** Defines how authorship, contributions, attribution and ownership of shared work should be preserved and recognized.
11. **Privacy, Permissions and Access Control:** Establishes how access to research material and community functionality should be controlled.
12. **Community Discovery and Research Networking:** Defines how users can discover researchers, projects, datasets, discussions and relevant scientific work.
13. **Moderation and Responsible Community Conduct:** Establishes requirements for addressing misconduct, misinformation, inappropriate content and community standards.

## General Principles

Community and collaboration functionality should:

- Accommodate users with different levels of scientific knowledge, experience and formal qualifications.
- Allow users to participate without requiring formal academic credentials.
- Preserve user control over private research and unpublished work.
- Require explicit authorization before research materials are shared with others.
- Support both dedicated community environments and interaction within relevant scientific workspaces.
- Preserve research context, scientific relationships, provenance and attribution when material is shared or transferred.
- Support independent analyses of shared datasets and resources without unintentionally altering the original material.
- Distinguish informal discussion, community feedback, independent reproduction, scientific review and scientific validation.
- Avoid treating popularity, agreement or activity levels as evidence of scientific reliability.
- Preserve authorship, ownership and contributor attribution.
- Respect applicable licensing, access restrictions and usage permissions.
- Maintain appropriate privacy, security and moderation controls.
- Support reproducible research and independent examination of shared scientific work.
- Allow community capabilities to expand as the application develops without requiring every proposed capability in the initial implementation.

## Implementation Boundary

This section establishes the intended community and collaboration capabilities without requiring every proposed feature to be available in the initial application release.

Initial implementation should prioritize reliable foundations for user-controlled sharing, research attribution, privacy, scientific discussion, resource exchange and basic collaboration.

More advanced collaborative research environments, community-maintained resources, extensive research networking, independent review systems and publication integrations may be introduced as technical feasibility, scientific value and practical demand justify them.

The implementation of community features must remain consistent with the application's scientific integrity, security, ownership, licensing and reproducibility requirements.

## General Principle

The community should function as an integrated environment for scientific exchange and collaboration, allowing users to share research, contribute to investigations, examine scientific work and develop connections with other researchers.

Community participation should encourage scientific openness and collaboration without compromising research ownership, privacy, scientific integrity, attribution or user control.
