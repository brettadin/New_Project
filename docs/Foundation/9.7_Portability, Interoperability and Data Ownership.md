# 9.7 — Portability, Interoperability and Data Ownership

## Purpose

Establishes the requirements for moving, exchanging, importing, exporting and maintaining access to scientific data and research materials across different platforms, applications and storage environments.

The application should allow users to retain control over their research materials and avoid unnecessary dependence on proprietary formats, individual service providers or a particular deployment environment.

Portability and interoperability are particularly important for long-term scientific research, where datasets, analyses and project records may need to be transferred, shared, archived or revisited using different software and computing environments.

The system should preserve scientific meaning, metadata, provenance, configurations and relationships when research materials are moved or exchanged.

The objective is to make research materials accessible and usable beyond the immediate application environment wherever technically and legally possible.

## 9.7.1 — Data Ownership and User Control

### Functional Requirements

- Users retain ownership and control of their original research materials and user-created content, subject to applicable licensing and legal restrictions.
- Distinguish ownership from access, storage, processing, sharing and redistribution rights.
- Do not make continued access to user-owned research unnecessarily dependent on application availability or a particular service provider.
- Allow users to retrieve and export their research materials.
- Avoid unnecessary restrictions on moving data between compatible applications and environments.
- Preserve ownership and attribution information when research materials are transferred.
- Clearly communicate restrictions affecting data access, export or redistribution.
- Do not assume that externally retrieved or publicly accessible data can be freely redistributed.
- Support appropriate removal of user data in accordance with applicable retention, legal and collaborative requirements.
- Preserve user control over locally stored and remotely stored research material.

### Design Guidance

The application should function as a scientific research environment rather than a system that traps users' work within a proprietary ecosystem.

Users should be able to retain their original data, research outputs and important project records even if they stop using the application.

Ownership, access permissions and licensing should remain distinct concepts.

## 9.7.2 — Data Import and Export Standards

### Functional Requirements

- Support importing and exporting commonly used scientific data formats.
- Accommodate structured datasets, scientific files, metadata, documents, configurations and research records.
- Preserve relevant information when converting between compatible formats.
- Identify information that cannot be represented in the destination format.
- Communicate potential data loss or transformation during conversion.
- Preserve original files where practical.
- Support batch import and export where appropriate.
- Maintain relevant scientific units, conventions and metadata.
- Document format requirements and compatibility limitations.
- Evaluate established scientific data standards before introducing custom formats.

### Potential Format Categories

Examples to investigate include:

- Scientific tables and numerical data.
- Spectroscopic and photometric datasets.
- Astronomical observational data formats.
- Scientific images and instrument products.
- Structured metadata formats.
- Research project and analysis records.
- Scientific configuration and workflow files.
- Documentation and reference formats.

Potential examples include CSV, TXT, FITS, VOTable, JSON, XML, HDF5, Parquet, Markdown, PDF, BibTeX and RIS.

These examples are not a requirement to support every format immediately. Format priorities should be determined by scientific relevance, user requirements and technical feasibility.

### Design Guidance

Prefer established and widely supported scientific formats wherever practical.

Custom formats may be necessary for preserving complex application-specific structures, but should not unnecessarily replace standard formats for ordinary scientific data.

## 9.7.3 — Complete Project Portability

### Functional Requirements

- Support exporting complete research projects.
- Support importing projects into compatible application environments.
- Preserve project organization and user-defined structures.
- Preserve associated datasets, observations, analyses, models and visualizations.
- Preserve configurations, parameters, documentation and references.
- Maintain relationships between original data, processed data, derived results and analyses.
- Preserve relevant project history and activity records.
- Identify external dependencies that are not included in an export.
- Identify licensing or redistribution restrictions affecting project components.
- Support exporting selected project components where appropriate.
- Validate imported project structures and identify missing or incompatible components.
- Preserve project ownership and attribution information.

### Design Guidance

A project export should aim to preserve the scientific investigation as a coherent unit rather than simply collect its associated files.

Where complete portability is not possible, the application should identify what is missing and explain its significance.

Project portability should integrate with the requirements in Sections 5.5 and 6.4.

## 9.7.4 — Cross-Platform Compatibility and Migration

### Functional Requirements

- Preserve compatible research data and project structures across supported application platforms.
- Support transferring research materials between web and desktop environments.
- Avoid unnecessary platform-specific restrictions on scientific data formats.
- Maintain compatibility of important scientific methods and configurations where practical.
- Identify platform-specific dependencies and limitations.
- Support migration between compatible environments.
- Preserve research history and provenance during migration.
- Avoid silently changing scientific results because of platform differences.
- Document compatibility limitations and required adjustments.
- Evaluate cross-platform functionality when introducing new architectural components.

### Design Guidance

The preferred long-term hybrid direction requires a shared foundation for scientific data and research structures.

A project created in one environment should remain usable in another supported environment wherever its dependencies permit.

Platform-specific differences may exist in storage, computation, file access and available resources. These should be documented rather than hidden.

## 9.7.5 — Scientific Data Interoperability

### Functional Requirements

- Support exchanging scientific datasets with compatible external applications and research tools.
- Preserve relevant units, coordinate systems, wavelength conventions, time systems and measurement information.
- Maintain scientific metadata and provenance.
- Support appropriate conversion between compatible data structures.
- Identify incompatible or ambiguous information.
- Communicate information loss or assumptions introduced during conversion.
- Preserve original values and source information where transformations occur.
- Support relevant astronomical and scientific data standards.
- Allow exported data to be interpreted outside the application wherever practical.
- Avoid proprietary data structures when established alternatives are suitable.

### Design Guidance

Interoperability must preserve scientific meaning rather than simply allow files to be opened by another application.

For example, converting a spectral dataset should preserve its wavelength or frequency convention, units, uncertainty information and relevant observational metadata.

A technically successful file conversion does not automatically guarantee scientific compatibility.

## 9.7.6 — Analysis, Workflow and Configuration Portability

### Functional Requirements

- Support exporting and importing analytical configurations and workflows.
- Preserve methods, parameters, processing sequences and relevant assumptions.
- Include necessary input requirements and compatibility information.
- Identify dependencies on external libraries, software and scientific resources.
- Preserve relationships between analytical stages and their outputs.
- Support transferring reusable analysis procedures between compatible environments.
- Identify unavailable or incompatible methods during import.
- Preserve software and dependency version information.
- Distinguish between a successfully imported configuration and one that has been validated in the receiving environment.
- Support independent modification of imported workflows without altering their original source.

### Design Guidance

Scientific reproducibility requires more than transferring numerical datasets.

The application should preserve the methodological information needed to understand and, where possible, reproduce an investigation.

Imported workflows may require adjustments because of differences in software versions, available resources, datasets or computational environments.

These differences should be identified and documented.

## 9.7.7 — External Application Interoperability

### Functional Requirements

- Support exchanging appropriate research materials with established scientific applications.
- Investigate integrations with relevant astronomy, spectroscopy, numerical analysis, visualization and computational tools.
- Support standard file formats and interfaces where available.
- Allow external applications to receive compatible data and produce results that can be imported.
- Preserve source and processing information when exchanging data.
- Identify limitations when external applications cannot preserve all relevant metadata or configurations.
- Avoid unnecessary dependence on proprietary external software.
- Document supported interoperability methods and known limitations.

### Design Guidance

The application should complement the wider scientific software ecosystem.

Users should not be unnecessarily restricted to tools built directly into the application.

Where external software provides useful or established functionality, the application should investigate appropriate ways to exchange information with it.

Full bidirectional integration is not required for every external application. File-based exchange may be sufficient in many cases.

## 9.7.8 — Long-Term Data Accessibility and Archival

### Functional Requirements

- Support preserving important research materials over extended periods.
- Allow projects and scientific datasets to be archived.
- Preserve original files, metadata, provenance and relevant analysis information.
- Maintain sufficient documentation to understand archived research.
- Identify dependencies that may become unavailable or outdated.
- Support retrieval and restoration of archived projects.
- Avoid relying exclusively on temporary external services for long-term research preservation.
- Consider established archival formats and practices.
- Preserve historical versions of important research materials.
- Communicate limitations affecting long-term accessibility or reproducibility.

### Design Guidance

Scientific research may remain relevant for years or decades.

Storage formats, software dependencies and external services may change during that period.

The application should therefore consider long-term accessibility when selecting data formats, storage mechanisms and export structures.

Archival copies should remain understandable and usable outside the application's immediate runtime environment wherever practical.

## 9.7.9 — Proprietary Formats and Vendor Lock-In

### Functional Requirements

- Avoid unnecessary dependence on proprietary formats for essential scientific data.
- Prefer established open standards when they adequately support requirements.
- Clearly document unavoidable application-specific formats.
- Support exporting application-specific data into appropriate external formats where possible.
- Preserve sufficient information to reconstruct important project structures.
- Avoid restricting access to user-owned research through proprietary storage arrangements.
- Evaluate the long-term accessibility of formats and dependencies.
- Consider alternatives when a proprietary dependency creates substantial portability risks.

### Design Guidance

Application-specific formats may be appropriate for preserving complex project structures, analytical configurations and relationships.

However, these formats should complement rather than unnecessarily replace established scientific standards.

Where proprietary structures are required, users should have access to appropriate export mechanisms and documentation.

## 9.7.10 — Portability Validation and Compatibility Testing

### Functional Requirements

- Validate exported research materials where practical.
- Check imported projects for missing files and dependencies.
- Verify that important metadata and relationships survive transfers.
- Identify information lost during format conversion.
- Test compatibility across supported platforms.
- Compare relevant scientific outputs before and after migration.
- Distinguish expected computational differences from unintended changes.
- Preserve original material when migration or conversion fails.
- Record significant portability and compatibility problems.
- Maintain documentation of tested formats and environments.

### Design Guidance

Portability should be tested rather than assumed.

A successful export operation does not necessarily mean that every scientific relationship, configuration or dependency has been preserved.

Testing should prioritize scientific integrity, completeness and practical usability.

## Future Possibilities

The following capabilities may be investigated as the application develops but are not initial implementation priorities.

- Advanced project exchange standards.
- More extensive integration with external scientific applications.
- Automated cross-platform project conversion.
- Advanced archival and preservation systems.
- Interoperability with institutional research infrastructures.
- Automated format migration and compatibility assessment.
- More extensive scientific data standard support.
- Community-developed format adapters.
- Advanced portable computational environments.
- Long-term archival packages containing complete research environments.

These capabilities should be introduced according to scientific requirements, user demand and technical feasibility.

## Initial Implementation Priorities

The initial architecture should prioritize:

- User ownership and access to original research materials.
- Import and export of relevant scientific data formats.
- Preservation of metadata, units and provenance.
- Complete project export and import foundations.
- Compatibility between the eventual web and desktop environments.
- Separation of original, processed and derived data.
- Export of important analysis configurations and research records.
- Support for established scientific file formats.
- Clear identification of missing dependencies and conversion limitations.
- Avoidance of unnecessary proprietary restrictions.
- Basic portability and compatibility testing.
- Preservation of historical research information.

Complete interoperability with every external scientific application is not an initial requirement.

## Implementation Considerations

Portability and interoperability decisions should be informed by research into:

- Established astronomical and scientific data standards.
- Scientific file formats and metadata conventions.
- Project and analysis serialization.
- Cross-platform data compatibility.
- External application interfaces.
- Data conversion and validation.
- Long-term archival practices.
- Licensing and redistribution restrictions.
- Proprietary format risks.
- Reproducible computational environments.

The AI development partner should prioritize formats and interoperability mechanisms according to actual scientific workflows and the application's hybrid platform direction.

Particular attention should be given to preserving scientific meaning, provenance and research relationships rather than simply achieving technical file compatibility.

Portability should be considered throughout architecture and data-management development, not postponed until the application is complete.

**General Principle:** Users should retain meaningful ownership and control over their scientific research materials. The application should support practical movement and exchange of data, projects and analyses across platforms and external scientific tools while preserving scientific meaning, provenance, reproducibility and long-term accessibility. Avoid unnecessary proprietary restrictions and ensure that research remains accessible beyond the application itself wherever technically and legally possible.
