# 8.2 — User Profiles and Research Identity

## Purpose

Establishes how users are represented within the application, how they can communicate their scientific interests and experience, and how their contributions and research activities are associated with their identity.

The application should support a range of user identities, from beginners and amateur astronomers to students, independent researchers, educators and professional scientists.

User profiles should facilitate scientific networking, collaboration, attribution and discovery without making social participation a requirement for using the application.

The system should accommodate both users who wish to establish a professional research identity and those who prefer to participate privately or under a pseudonym.

## 8.2.1 — User Profile Information

Users should have an individual profile associated with their application account.

Profiles may contain:

- Username or display name.
- Profile image or avatar.
- Optional biography or personal description.
- Scientific interests.
- Areas of research.
- Educational background.
- Professional experience.
- Institutional or organizational affiliations.
- Skills and areas of expertise.
- Public research activities and contributions.
- Optional external links and professional profiles.

Users should be able to maintain their profiles without being required to provide extensive personal or professional information.

The application should distinguish between information required for account functionality and information voluntarily displayed on a public or shared profile.

## 8.2.2 — Scientific Interests and Research Areas

Users should be able to identify the scientific subjects and research areas that interest them.

Examples include:

- Exoplanet science.
- Planetary astronomy.
- Stellar astronomy.
- Observational astronomy.
- Spectroscopy and astrochemistry.
- Atmospheric characterization and modelling.
- Photometry and transit analysis.
- Theoretical and computational astronomy.
- Galactic astronomy.
- Cosmology.
- Astrophysics and related scientific disciplines.

Users may also identify more specific interests, including:

- Particular astronomical objects or planetary systems.
- Specific research questions.
- Scientific instruments and observational techniques.
- Analytical methods and computational approaches.
- Particular areas of physics, chemistry or mathematics.

These interests may help users discover relevant discussions, research material, groups and potential collaborators.

Interest categories should remain expandable as the application's scientific scope develops.

## 8.2.3 — Experience, Qualifications and Expertise

Users should be able to optionally describe their scientific background, experience and areas of expertise.

Potential information includes:

- Educational background and qualifications.
- Academic degrees and certifications.
- Professional experience.
- Research experience.
- Institutional or organizational affiliations.
- Scientific and technical skills.
- Areas of specialization.
- Previous or ongoing research activities.

The application should accommodate users without formal scientific qualifications or professional research experience.

Qualifications and expertise should not be mandatory requirements for participation.

The application should distinguish between:

- Information supplied directly by the user.
- Qualifications or affiliations that have been independently verified.
- Experience or expertise described by the user but not formally assessed.

A user's stated experience, qualifications or affiliation should not automatically be treated as independently verified scientific authority.

## 8.2.4 — Research Activities and Contributions

Profiles should be capable of presenting an overview of a user's publicly shared scientific and community contributions.

Potential contributions include:

- Shared research projects.
- Published or publicly shared analyses.
- Observations and datasets.
- Scientific discussions and interpretations.
- Educational resources and tutorials.
- Analytical workflows and processing procedures.
- Models, simulations and visualizations.
- Contributions to collaborative investigations.
- Community-created scientific resources.

Where appropriate, contributions should link back to their associated projects, datasets, analyses or discussions.

The application should distinguish between original contributions, collaborative contributions, adapted material and independently reproduced research.

Private projects, unpublished observations and other restricted material must not appear on profiles unless the user has explicitly authorized their disclosure.

## 8.2.5 — User Identity, Pseudonyms and Attribution

The application must support both identifiable and pseudonymous participation.

Users should be able to participate using a username or pseudonym without being required to disclose their real identity.

Requirements include:

- Real names should be optional.
- Academic qualifications and professional affiliations should be optional.
- Users may choose a public display name different from their account credentials.
- Users may participate in discussions, share research and contribute to collaborative projects under a pseudonym.
- Contributions should remain consistently associated with the relevant user account.
- Attribution should be preserved across discussions, shared resources, research contributions and collaborative projects.
- Users should be able to change their public display information without losing the association between their account and historical contributions.
- The application should distinguish account identity, publicly displayed identity and independently verified credentials where applicable.

Pseudonymous participation should not prevent users from receiving recognition for their contributions or participating in meaningful scientific collaboration.

The application should not require users to reveal their real identity simply to participate in ordinary community activities.

Where a particular collaboration, publication or external scientific process requires identifiable authorship, the relevant requirements should be communicated separately rather than imposed across the entire community.

## 8.2.6 — Profile Visibility and Privacy

Users should have control over which profile information is visible to others.

Potential visibility arrangements include:

- Private information visible only to the account holder.
- Information visible to selected collaborators or groups.
- Publicly visible profile information.
- Publicly visible research contributions while personal information remains private.

Users should be able to determine whether information such as the following is displayed:

- Real name.
- Profile image.
- Biography.
- Scientific interests.
- Qualifications and affiliations.
- Research history.
- Public contributions.
- External profile links.

The application should avoid exposing personal information through profile pages, community interactions or shared research material without appropriate authorization.

Profile visibility should remain consistent with the application's broader privacy and permission systems.

Users should be able to review and modify their visibility preferences as needed.

## 8.2.7 — Research Networking and Connections

The application should support optional networking between users with related scientific interests, research activities or expertise.

Potential functionality includes:

- Discovering users with similar scientific interests.
- Finding researchers working on related astronomical objects or research questions.
- Following other users or contributors.
- Establishing connections with potential collaborators.
- Discovering users participating in particular research areas or groups.
- Identifying contributors to shared scientific resources.
- Finding users with relevant technical or scientific experience.

Networking should facilitate scientific interaction rather than encourage popularity-based social activity.

Users should retain control over their connections and the visibility of their networking activity.

Following or connecting with another user should not automatically grant access to that user's private projects, datasets or other restricted material.

## 8.2.8 — Profile Customization and Management

Users should be able to create, maintain and update their profiles as their interests and research activities change.

Potential functionality includes:

- Editing profile information.
- Adding or removing scientific interests.
- Updating qualifications and experience.
- Managing public contributions.
- Changing display names and profile images.
- Managing profile visibility.
- Adding or removing external links.
- Reviewing public profile information.
- Managing networking preferences.

Profile customization should remain straightforward and should not require users to maintain information that is irrelevant to their participation.

The application should preserve attribution and historical contribution relationships when profile information changes.

## Overall Principles

- Support both professional and informal scientific identities.
- Allow pseudonymous participation without requiring disclosure of real-world identity.
- Make personal, educational and professional information optional wherever possible.
- Provide meaningful ways to communicate scientific interests and expertise.
- Associate contributions with their creators while preserving authorship and attribution.
- Distinguish user-provided information from independently verified qualifications.
- Allow users to control profile visibility and personal information.
- Support scientific networking without making social participation mandatory.
- Avoid popularity-based measures of scientific credibility or authority.
- Maintain profile flexibility as the application and its community expand.

**General Principle:** User profiles should provide a flexible scientific identity system that supports participation, attribution, research discovery and collaboration. Users should be able to establish a professional identity, participate informally or remain pseudonymous while retaining control over their personal information and public contributions.
