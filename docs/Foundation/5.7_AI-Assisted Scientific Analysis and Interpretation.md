# 5.7 — AI-Assisted Scientific Analysis and Interpretation

## Purpose

Explore the possibility of introducing AI-assisted scientific research capabilities at some distant point in the application's future.

AI could potentially assist users with scientific data exploration, interpretation, hypothesis generation, literature research and analytical investigation.

However, these capabilities are strictly optional future possibilities. They are not requirements for the application, are not part of the initial implementation and should not be treated as an expected stage of development.

The application must remain a fully useful scientific research environment without AI-assisted scientific analysis or interpretation.

Any consideration of these capabilities should occur only after the core application, scientific workspaces, analytical methods, data management and reproducibility systems have been sufficiently developed.

AI-assisted science should remain among the lowest development priorities and should only be reconsidered if the application eventually has the platform, infrastructure, technical resources and financial support necessary to implement it responsibly.

## Potential Future Capabilities

The following are examples of capabilities that could be investigated if AI-assisted scientific research is ever considered. They are exploratory possibilities, not promised functionality or implementation requirements.

### 1. Scientific Data Interpretation

Potential applications include:

- Assisting with the interpretation of spectra, photometric observations, light curves and other scientific datasets.
- Helping identify potentially significant features, patterns and anomalies.
- Explaining possible physical or chemical interpretations.
- Identifying areas that may warrant further investigation.
- Distinguishing observational evidence from generated interpretations and hypotheses.

### 2. Hypothesis Generation

Potential applications include:

- Suggesting possible explanations for observed phenomena.
- Identifying alternative hypotheses and competing interpretations.
- Suggesting relationships between observed properties and known scientific processes.
- Helping users develop research questions.
- Identifying additional evidence that could help distinguish competing explanations.

Generated hypotheses must never be presented as established scientific findings.

### 3. Research and Analysis Recommendations

Potential applications include:

- Suggesting analytical methods based on a dataset and research objective.
- Recommending additional processing, modelling or comparisons where appropriate.
- Identifying potentially useful reference databases and scientific literature.
- Suggesting further observations that could help distinguish competing explanations.
- Explaining the reasoning and limitations behind recommendations.

### 4. Scientific Literature Assistance

Potential applications include:

- Helping locate and summarize relevant scientific publications.
- Identifying relationships between research findings.
- Assisting with comparisons of published results and methodologies.
- Identifying disagreements, uncertainties and gaps in existing research.
- Helping users navigate unfamiliar scientific subjects.

Original publications and references must remain accessible, and generated summaries must be distinguishable from published findings.

### 5. Cross-Disciplinary Research Assistance

Potential applications include:

- Identifying potentially useful connections between different astronomical disciplines.
- Suggesting comparisons between Solar System objects, exoplanets, stars and other astronomical systems.
- Helping users combine observations, models and reference information from different scientific areas.
- Identifying when interdisciplinary comparisons may be inappropriate because of differences in methodology, data quality or physical conditions.

### 6. Educational and Explanatory Assistance

Potential applications include:

- Explaining analytical results and scientific concepts at different levels of complexity.
- Helping users understand equations, physical processes, assumptions and methodologies.
- Providing contextual explanations of graphs, measurements and modelling results.
- Supporting users as they learn to interpret scientific evidence independently.

### 7. Research Investigation Assistance

Potential applications include:

- Helping users organize research questions and investigate possible explanations.
- Identifying missing evidence, unresolved questions and potential methodological problems.
- Suggesting investigation pathways and relevant resources.
- Helping compare different analytical approaches and their outcomes.

## Scientific Integrity and Safeguards

If AI-assisted scientific capabilities are ever considered for implementation, they must follow the scientific integrity requirements established throughout Section 5.

In particular:

- AI must not fabricate observations, measurements, citations, publications or scientific evidence.
- AI-generated interpretations and hypotheses must remain distinguishable from verified scientific findings.
- Recommendations must not be presented as scientifically established conclusions.
- Significant interpretations should be traceable to supporting observations, datasets, methods and references.
- Uncertainty, assumptions and limitations must be communicated.
- Alternative explanations should be considered where scientifically relevant.
- AI must acknowledge when available evidence is insufficient.
- Users must be able to inspect, challenge and reject AI-generated suggestions.
- AI must not silently modify original datasets or independently alter established research conclusions.
- AI-generated content must not automatically become part of a project's verified scientific record.
- Significant AI contributions must remain identifiable and subject to appropriate verification.
- AI capabilities must not replace established scientific methods or become necessary for essential application functionality.

These safeguards establish conditions for any possible future implementation. They do not create a requirement to develop AI-assisted scientific capabilities.

## Future Research and Feasibility Considerations

If AI-assisted scientific research is ever reconsidered, an initial feasibility investigation should establish:

- Whether particular scientific tasks would genuinely benefit from AI assistance.
- Whether established non-AI methods would be more reliable, appropriate or economical.
- Whether the application's eventual platform can support the necessary capabilities.
- Suitable AI models, tools and scientific integrations.
- Accuracy, reproducibility and scientific validation requirements.
- Computational requirements, infrastructure and operating costs.
- Availability of appropriate hardware or external services.
- Privacy, data security and external service dependencies.
- Long-term maintenance and support requirements.
- The appropriate balance between AI assistance and user control.
- Whether the expected scientific benefit justifies the financial and technical investment.

An investigation should not automatically lead to implementation. It may conclude that AI-assisted scientific capabilities are unnecessary, impractical, too expensive or less reliable than established alternatives.

## Priority and Implementation Status

<badge color="secondary">Distant future possibility only</badge>

AI-assisted scientific analysis and interpretation are not required application capabilities.

Their consideration is explicitly deferred until the application has reached a sufficiently mature state and the necessary platform, infrastructure, technical expertise and funding are available.

If ever reconsidered, this area should be treated as a final, low-priority development possibility rather than an assumed part of the development roadmap.

There is no requirement to:

- Implement AI-assisted scientific capabilities during initial development.
- Reserve development resources or funding for them.
- Select the application architecture around anticipated AI functionality.
- Introduce AI dependencies into essential scientific workflows.
- Develop AI models or specialized scientific AI systems.
- Establish a dedicated AI-assisted science platform.

Any future implementation would require a separate feasibility assessment, scientific justification, resource evaluation and explicit development decision.

## General Principle

The application should prioritize reliable scientific capabilities, established analytical methods, reproducibility, data integrity and user control.

AI-assisted scientific research may eventually offer additional opportunities, but it should never become a prerequisite for the application's usefulness or scientific value.

The application should first succeed as a scientific research environment without relying on AI to perform its essential functions.
