# 1.3 — Integrated Scientific Research Environment

## Purpose

Establish the application as an integrated scientific environment in which research tools, data resources, knowledge, projects and supporting activities work together wherever scientifically and practically appropriate.

The application should not simply collect unrelated scientific tools under one interface. It should provide meaningful connections between research activities, allowing users to move between related tasks while preserving context, relevant information and research continuity.

Scientific capabilities remain the central purpose of the environment. Education, project management, data discovery, collaboration and community functionality should complement these capabilities and make scientific investigation more accessible, organized and productive.

## 1.3.1 Integration of Scientific Capabilities

**Core requirements**

- Provide access to scientific tools and capabilities through a coherent application environment.
- Allow related scientific capabilities to exchange or reference compatible information.
- Support workflows that involve multiple analytical methods or scientific disciplines.
- Preserve relevant dataset metadata, units, provenance and processing history when information moves between compatible capabilities.
- Allow scientific tools to operate independently when integration is unnecessary or unsuitable.
- Avoid requiring users to repeatedly import, locate or configure the same information when it is already available and compatible.
- Make relationships between tools and their inputs and outputs understandable.
- Ensure integration does not compromise the scientific validity or independence of individual analytical methods.

**Design guidance**

Integration should be based on scientific and functional relationships, not simply on the desire to connect every available feature.

For example, observational data may be examined through multiple compatible analytical tools, with each tool retaining its own methodological requirements and producing independently traceable results.

## 1.3.2 Connection Between Research Materials

The application should maintain meaningful relationships between the scientific materials involved in an investigation.

**Core requirements**

- Support relationships between astronomical objects, observations, datasets, analyses, models, visualizations and research results.
- Allow users to navigate between related research materials.
- Preserve source information and provenance when materials are reused.
- Distinguish original observations and datasets from processed or derived materials.
- Allow multiple investigations to reference shared scientific resources.
- Preserve the relationship between analytical results and the data and methods used to produce them.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of information while allowing independent research activities to maintain their own results and configurations.
- Make unavailable, modified or outdated resources identifiable where they affect research continuity.

**Design guidance**

The application should distinguish between how users organize their work and how scientific materials are related.

A user should be able to access the same observation through different relevant research activities without creating unnecessary copies or losing its original scientific context.

## 1.3.3 Continuity Across Research Activities

The application should allow users to move between different stages of scientific investigation without unnecessarily losing their progress or context.

**Core requirements**

- Support continuity between data discovery, retrieval, examination, analysis, modelling and visualization.
- Allow users to revisit earlier stages of an investigation.
- Preserve relevant selections, configurations and analytical records.
- Allow research activities to produce materials that can be used in subsequent compatible activities.
- Support investigations that develop over multiple sessions.
- Allow users to return to previous results and continue or revise their work.
- Avoid forcing users into a single predetermined sequence of research activities.
- Preserve sufficient context to understand how research materials and results were produced.

**Design guidance**

Scientific research is not always linear. Users may revisit previous observations, compare alternative methods, introduce new data or change research questions.

The application should accommodate these patterns without requiring users to restart investigations or lose historical results.

## 1.3.4 Integration of Supporting Functions

Supporting functions should be accessible within the broader research environment and connected to scientific activities where appropriate.

**Core requirements**

- Integrate relevant educational and reference resources with scientific tools and research activities.
- Connect project organization with the scientific materials and results belonging to an investigation.
- Make relevant data discovery resources accessible from appropriate research contexts.
- Support collaboration and community interaction without unnecessarily separating them from the scientific work they concern.
- Allow users to access relevant discussions, documentation and learning materials in context.
- Preserve appropriate distinctions between scientific results, educational explanations, community contributions and user-generated content.
- Ensure supporting functions enhance research rather than obstructing scientific workflows.
- Allow supporting functionality to remain independently accessible when a dedicated environment is more suitable.

**Design guidance**

Supporting functions should be integrated according to their relevance.

For example, an educational explanation may be available alongside a scientific operation, while a discussion concerning a particular astronomical object may be accessible from that object's research context.

Not every supporting function needs to appear in every scientific workspace.

## 1.3.5 Shared Application Resources and Services

The application should provide common resources and services that can support multiple scientific areas without unnecessarily duplicating functionality.

**Core requirements**

- Identify functionality that is useful across multiple scientific disciplines.
- Provide shared resources where common functionality can be maintained consistently.
- Support appropriate reuse of data access, project management, visualization, documentation and other common services.
- Allow specialized scientific areas to retain their own methods and requirements.
- Avoid creating multiple inconsistent implementations of the same shared capability without a justified reason.
- Ensure shared services respect the requirements and compatibility constraints of the scientific capabilities using them.
- Preserve appropriate access controls and project boundaries when shared resources are used.
- Allow shared functionality to evolve without unnecessarily disrupting dependent research activities.

**Design guidance**

Shared functionality should reduce duplication and improve consistency, but it should not force unrelated scientific capabilities into an unsuitable common design.

## 1.3.6 External Scientific Software and Resources

The application should be capable of incorporating appropriate external scientific resources and software into its wider research environment.

**Core requirements**

- Support access to relevant external databases, archives, catalogues, literature and scientific resources.
- Evaluate opportunities to integrate established scientific software and computational tools.
- Distinguish between functionality provided directly by the application and functionality supplied by external services or software.
- Preserve source attribution, licensing information and relevant provenance.
- Communicate external dependencies and limitations where they affect research activities.
- Avoid unnecessary dependence on external services for essential functionality where practical alternatives exist.
- Evaluate integrations according to scientific suitability, reliability, compatibility, maintenance requirements and practical value.
- Ensure external integrations do not silently compromise data integrity or user control.

**Design guidance**

External software and resources should extend the application's capabilities where appropriate. Their inclusion should be based on demonstrated usefulness rather than integration for its own sake.

The application should remain a coherent research environment even when some capabilities depend on external tools or services.

## 1.3.7 Consistency Without Unnecessary Uniformity

The application should provide a recognizable and consistent experience while accommodating the different requirements of its scientific disciplines and supporting environments.

**Core requirements**

- Maintain consistent general navigation, terminology and interaction principles.
- Establish common expectations for handling scientific data, metadata, provenance and research records.
- Allow specialized scientific areas to use appropriate tools, methods and workflows.
- Avoid imposing identical analytical procedures on scientifically different disciplines.
- Support consistent presentation of shared information while preserving discipline-specific requirements.
- Make transitions between related application areas understandable.
- Allow specialized capabilities to develop without fragmenting the overall application experience.

**Design guidance**

A unified application does not require every scientific area to look or function identically.

Consistency should exist where it improves usability and research continuity. Differences should remain where they reflect genuine scientific or functional requirements.

## 1.3.8 Integration Quality and Practical Value

Integration should be evaluated according to whether it provides a meaningful improvement to scientific work.

**Core requirements**

- Evaluate proposed integrations according to their scientific and practical usefulness.
- Identify the research activities and information that an integration is intended to connect.
- Consider compatibility, reliability, maintenance requirements and potential limitations.
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies and excessive complexity.
- Ensure integrations do not duplicate existing functionality without a clear justification.
- Preserve the ability to use appropriate scientific capabilities independently.
- Reassess integrations when their underlying resources, methods or dependencies change.

**Design guidance**

A feature should not be considered successfully integrated merely because it is accessible from the same application.

Meaningful integration should improve access to information, reduce unnecessary repetition, preserve research context or enable scientifically useful workflows.

## General Principles

- The application should operate as one coherent scientific research environment.
- Scientific capabilities should remain the central focus of integration.
- Related tools and research materials should connect where scientifically and practically appropriate.
- Research context, provenance and continuity must be preserved.
- Supporting functions should strengthen the research environment without obstructing it.
- Shared functionality should improve consistency without imposing unsuitable uniformity.
- External resources and software should be integrated selectively and transparently.
- Scientific disciplines should retain their specialized methods and requirements.
- Integration should be evaluated according to practical and scientific value rather than feature count.
- Users should retain appropriate control over their research materials and workflows.

**General principle:** The application should bring scientific tools, data, knowledge and supporting activities together through meaningful relationships, creating a unified environment for research without sacrificing the specialized capabilities, methodological independence or scientific integrity of its individual components.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes the principles governing integration across the application.

It does not prescribe specific workspace arrangements, software architecture, communication mechanisms, data structures, APIs or technical integration methods.

These decisions should be developed through the relevant scientific, functional and technical requirements in subsequent sections and evaluated during the appropriate research and planning stages.
