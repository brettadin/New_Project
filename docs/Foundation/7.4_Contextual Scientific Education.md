## 7.4 — Contextual Scientific Education

This subsection establishes how educational material should be integrated throughout the application rather than existing exclusively in a separate learning library.

The application should allow users to learn about scientific concepts, methods, terminology and processes while actively using its scientific tools and capabilities.

Educational assistance should be relevant to the user's current workspace, activity, dataset or workflow. It should make scientific information accessible without unnecessarily interrupting research or overwhelming the interface.

The objective is to make education an integrated part of the application, allowing users to develop their scientific understanding alongside practical research activities.

### 7.4.1 Context-Sensitive Scientific Explanations

The application should provide educational explanations relevant to the user's current context.

Potential capabilities include:

- Explanations of scientific concepts encountered within a particular workspace.
- Definitions of unfamiliar terminology appearing in tools, datasets, graphs and analytical outputs.
- Explanations of scientific measurements, parameters, units and object properties.
- Background information relevant to the current research activity.
- Explanations of why particular scientific processes or analytical methods are being used.
- Identification of relevant physical, chemical and mathematical relationships.
- Connections between the current activity and related scientific concepts.
- Links to more comprehensive explanations in the scientific knowledge library.

Explanations should be contextually relevant rather than presenting generic educational information unrelated to the user's current activity.

Users should be able to access additional information when needed without being forced to interrupt their workflow.

### 7.4.2 Explanations of Scientific Methods and Tools

Scientific tools and analytical methods should provide accessible explanations of their purpose, operation and scientific foundations.

Potential information should include:

- What the tool or method does.
- Which scientific problems it is intended to address.
- Why the method is useful.
- When it is appropriate to use.
- What inputs, datasets or prerequisites are required.
- What parameters and settings control its behaviour.
- How different parameter values affect the results.
- What outputs are produced and how they should be interpreted.
- Which scientific assumptions and approximations are involved.
- What limitations, uncertainties or potential sources of error exist.
- Which alternative methods may be appropriate.

Where relevant, explanations should connect practical tool usage to the underlying physics, chemistry, mathematics and established scientific methodologies.

Educational descriptions must remain consistent with the actual implementation of the corresponding functionality. The application should not describe capabilities, methods or validation that have not actually been established.

### 7.4.3 Contextual Mathematical Assistance

The application should help users understand mathematical concepts and equations encountered throughout its scientific capabilities.

Potential capabilities include:

- Explanations of equations used in scientific calculations and analytical methods.
- Definitions of mathematical symbols, variables and units.
- Explanations of the physical meaning of mathematical relationships.
- Step-by-step explanations of calculations where useful.
- Explanations of how equations are derived or applied.
- Connections between mathematical expressions and observable scientific behaviour.
- Explanations of relevant assumptions, approximations and conditions.
- Links to foundational mathematical concepts and more advanced material.

Where appropriate, users should be able to move between a mathematical expression, its individual components and an explanation of its scientific significance.

For example, when examining an equation related to orbital motion, users should be able to understand what each variable represents, how the quantities relate and what changing individual parameters means physically.

Mathematical assistance should accommodate different levels of understanding without unnecessarily simplifying the underlying science.

### 7.4.4 Scientific Terminology and Reference Assistance

Scientific terminology should be accessible throughout the application.

Potential capabilities include:

- Definitions of scientific terminology and specialist vocabulary.
- Explanations of abbreviations, symbols and scientific notation.
- Definitions of physical quantities, measurements and units.
- Explanations of terminology specific to different astronomical disciplines.
- Connections between related scientific concepts.
- Clarification of terms that have different meanings in different scientific contexts.
- Links to relevant scientific references and more detailed explanations.

Where appropriate, users should be able to access definitions directly from terminology encountered in the interface, educational material, datasets and scientific outputs.

Definitions should be scientifically accurate and sufficiently detailed to prevent misleading oversimplifications.

The application should distinguish between introductory explanations and more precise specialist definitions where necessary.

### 7.4.5 Learning Within Scientific Workflows

Educational assistance should be integrated into practical scientific workflows.

Users should be able to understand the scientific purpose of individual operations and how they contribute to a larger investigation.

Potential capabilities include:

- Explanations of why individual workflow stages are necessary.
- Descriptions of what happens during data processing, analysis and modelling.
- Explanations of inputs, outputs and intermediate results.
- Clarification of why particular methods or processing operations may be appropriate.
- Explanations of how analytical decisions affect subsequent results.
- Identification of common mistakes and their scientific consequences.
- Explanations of data compatibility requirements and limitations.
- Connections between individual workflow stages and their underlying scientific principles.
- Links to relevant educational resources and methodological references.

Educational explanations should be available without requiring users to leave their current activity.

Where possible, users should be able to explore an explanation, return to their work and continue from the same point.

The application should also allow users to investigate the scientific reasoning behind a workflow rather than simply following instructions without understanding them.

### 7.4.6 Different Levels of Explanation

Contextual educational material should accommodate users with different levels of scientific knowledge.

Where appropriate, explanations should be available at several levels:

- **Basic:** A straightforward introduction explaining what something means and why it matters.
- **Intermediate:** A more detailed explanation of the scientific principles, practical applications and relevant relationships.
- **Advanced:** Mathematical foundations, physical principles, methodological details, assumptions and limitations.
- **Specialist:** More comprehensive theoretical and technical explanations, including relevant scientific literature and research methodologies.

Users should be able to access deeper explanations when needed without making the initial interface unnecessarily complicated.

The selected explanation level should not restrict access to more advanced information or imply that complex scientific material must always be simplified.

Different topics may require different levels of detail depending on their scientific complexity.

### 7.4.7 Connections to Further Learning

Contextual explanations should connect to the broader educational system established in Section 7.

Where relevant, users should be able to move from an explanation into:

- Detailed scientific reference material in Section 7.1.
- Related courses, modules and structured learning paths in Section 7.2.
- Interactive demonstrations and practical exercises in Section 7.3.
- Related scientific concepts and subjects.
- Relevant equations, methodologies and scientific references.
- Original research publications and external educational resources.
- Relevant documentation for the tools and capabilities being used.

These connections should allow users to progress naturally from a brief explanation to more comprehensive study.

For example, a user examining an atmospheric spectrum might access a brief explanation of absorption features, proceed to a more detailed explanation of molecular spectroscopy, explore an interactive demonstration and then access relevant scientific literature.

The application should help users discover these relationships without requiring them to search manually through the entire educational library.

### 7.4.8 Future Possibilities

- More advanced context-aware educational recommendations.
- Interactive scientific explanations embedded directly into analytical workflows.
- Explanations that adapt to the user's selected learning level.
- AI-assisted explanations of unfamiliar scientific concepts and methodologies.
- More advanced mathematical assistance and equation exploration.
- Contextual connections between different scientific disciplines.
- Recommendations for related subjects and additional learning material.
- Explanations that respond to the user's current analytical activity and selected data.
- More advanced integration between educational content, scientific tools and research workflows.

Any AI-assisted educational functionality should remain consistent with the scientific integrity requirements established elsewhere in the project. Explanations should not fabricate scientific information, references or certainty, and should identify relevant assumptions and limitations.

### Initial Implementation Scope

The initial implementation should establish useful contextual educational assistance without requiring a sophisticated adaptive learning or AI-based educational system.

Initial priorities should include:

- Contextual explanations of scientific terminology and concepts.
- Accessible descriptions of scientific tools and analytical methods.
- Explanations of important parameters, measurements and units.
- Basic mathematical and equation explanations where relevant.
- Links to the scientific knowledge library.
- Connections between educational explanations and relevant courses or practical exercises.
- Contextual help that does not unnecessarily interrupt scientific workflows.
- Explanations that accurately reflect the application's actual capabilities and methodologies.

More advanced adaptive explanations, personalized recommendations and AI-assisted educational capabilities can be introduced later as the application develops.

### Implementation Considerations

Contextual education should build upon the scientific knowledge repository and educational resources established in Sections 7.1–7.3.

Educational explanations should be maintained alongside the corresponding scientific tools and methodologies so that they remain consistent with changes to the application.

The application should avoid creating disconnected explanations that become outdated as scientific functionality evolves.

The level of detail and presentation should be appropriate to the context. Brief explanations should remain accessible, while more comprehensive material should be available when users want to investigate further.

Educational assistance should also remain distinguishable from operational instructions, scientific recommendations and formal analytical results.

### General Principle

Scientific education should be integrated throughout the application rather than confined to a dedicated learning area. Users should be able to understand scientific concepts, terminology, mathematical relationships, analytical methods and workflow decisions while actively conducting research. Contextual assistance should make complex science more approachable without compromising scientific depth, accuracy or user independence.
