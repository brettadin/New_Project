# 13.4 — Adaptability and Evolving Requirements

## Purpose

Establish principles for adapting the application's requirements, functionality, architecture and development direction as new information and changing circumstances emerge.

The application should not be treated as a permanently fixed design. Scientific knowledge, research practices, technologies and user needs will evolve, and the application must be capable of responding appropriately.

Adaptability must be balanced with stability, scientific integrity, maintainability and preservation of existing research.

## 13.4.1 Evolving Scientific Requirements

**Core requirements**

- Allow scientific requirements to evolve as new research and scientific understanding become available.
- Reassess existing capabilities when established methodologies, observations or scientific interpretations change.
- Allow new scientific disciplines, methods and research objectives to be incorporated where appropriate.
- Identify when existing functionality no longer adequately supports current scientific practice.
- Evaluate whether scientific developments require new functionality, revised methods or updated educational material.
- Preserve historical scientific results and the conditions under which they were produced.
- Distinguish updated scientific understanding from changes to the original observational evidence.
- Avoid silently replacing historical conclusions or analytical results with newer interpretations.

**Design guidance**

Scientific knowledge should be allowed to progress without making historical research unintelligible or invalidating previously documented work without explanation.

## 13.4.2 Evolving User Requirements and Research Needs

**Core requirements**

- Allow users to introduce new research objectives and functional requirements.
- Consider feedback from different types of users, including beginners, students, educators, amateur astronomers and experienced researchers.
- Identify recurring usability problems and unmet scientific needs.
- Reassess existing functionality when practical use reveals shortcomings.
- Allow research workflows and organizational approaches to evolve.
- Consider changing user preferences without assuming that all users have identical needs.
- Distinguish individual preferences from requirements that should affect the wider application.
- Preserve user control over consequential changes to application behaviour and research organization.

**Design guidance**

User feedback should inform development, but individual requests should be evaluated against scientific value, practicality and the wider application vision.

## 13.4.3 Adaptation to Technological Developments

**Core requirements**

- Maintain awareness of relevant developments in programming technologies, scientific software, computational methods and infrastructure.
- Evaluate new technologies when they offer meaningful improvements or capabilities.
- Reassess existing dependencies when they become outdated, unreliable or unsuitable.
- Identify opportunities to improve performance, compatibility, security and maintainability.
- Consider new standards, formats and interoperability requirements.
- Evaluate migration requirements and potential consequences before replacing established technologies.
- Avoid adopting new technologies solely because they are newer or more popular.
- Preserve existing functionality and research continuity during necessary technical changes.

**Design principle**

The application should remain technologically adaptable without becoming dependent on continuous replacement of functioning systems.

## 13.4.4 Architectural and Functional Adaptability

**Core requirements**

- Preserve an architecture capable of accommodating additional scientific workspaces and shared capabilities.
- Allow existing functionality to be extended where appropriate.
- Support the introduction of new scientific methods, data types and external resources.
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies between unrelated capabilities.
- Allow specialized functionality to develop without requiring unrelated areas to be redesigned.
- Reassess architectural limitations when they materially restrict necessary expansion.
- Identify when existing functionality should be consolidated, replaced or retired.
- Preserve compatibility and established workflows wherever practical.
- Document significant architectural and functional changes.

**Design guidance**

Adaptability should result from sensible architecture and maintainable development practices rather than attempting to anticipate every possible future requirement.

## 13.4.5 Changing Requirements and Development Scope

**Core requirements**

- Allow approved requirements and development priorities to be revised when justified.
- Identify the reasons for proposed changes.
- Assess their effects on existing requirements, functionality, architecture and development plans.
- Identify additional dependencies, risks and implementation effort.
- Distinguish corrections to existing requirements from new functionality and broader scope changes.
- Preserve previous versions and the reasoning behind consequential changes.
- Obtain appropriate approval before making significant changes to application direction or approved scope.
- Update authoritative documentation and the Master Development Roadmap when changes are approved.

A new idea or recommendation must not automatically become an implementation commitment.

## 13.4.6 Managing Existing Functionality and Compatibility

**Core requirements**

- Preserve existing functionality when introducing new capabilities wherever practical.
- Identify potential compatibility problems before significant changes.
- Consider the effects of updates on existing projects, datasets, analyses, configurations and workflows.
- Identify when data structures, formats or dependencies require migration.
- Preserve original data and historical analytical results.
- Document changes that affect existing functionality or user workflows.
- Provide appropriate migration or recovery procedures where necessary.
- Communicate limitations and compatibility changes to users.
- Avoid silently removing established functionality or changing scientific behaviour.

Where existing functionality must be replaced, preserve relevant historical information and provide a clear explanation of the change.

## 13.4.7 Retiring and Replacing Functionality

**Core requirements**

- Allow outdated, redundant or unsuitable functionality to be reconsidered.
- Evaluate whether existing capabilities remain scientifically and technically appropriate.
- Identify alternatives before recommending removal or replacement.
- Assess the impact on dependent tools, workflows, projects and external integrations.
- Preserve historical records and research results associated with retired functionality.
- Identify migration requirements and compatibility limitations.
- Provide appropriate warnings when functionality is deprecated or no longer supported.
- Avoid removing functionality without considering its existing users and dependencies.

**Design guidance**

Not every feature needs to remain permanently available. However, removing or replacing functionality must be deliberate, documented and considerate of existing research.

## 13.4.8 Long-Term Adaptability and Scientific Continuity

**Core requirements**

- Preserve the ability to continue long-term investigations as application capabilities evolve.
- Maintain access to historical datasets, analyses, configurations and project records.
- Preserve provenance and relationships between original data and subsequent research.
- Consider the effects of software, database and external resource changes on reproducibility.
- Identify outdated dependencies and unavailable resources that affect historical investigations.
- Avoid silently substituting newer methods, datasets or software versions into previous analyses.
- Allow historical work to be revisited and reassessed using newer methods where appropriate.
- Preserve the distinction between original results and subsequent revisions or reproductions.

The application should support scientific progress without sacrificing the historical context needed to understand previous research.

## 13.4.9 Review and Reassessment

**Core requirements**

- Periodically evaluate whether the application remains aligned with its scientific objectives and user needs.
- Reassess significant architectural and technical decisions when new evidence warrants it.
- Identify limitations, outdated assumptions and unnecessary complexity.
- Evaluate whether existing capabilities remain useful and maintainable.
- Review deferred opportunities when relevant dependencies or circumstances change.
- Preserve previous decisions and their reasoning.
- Document significant reassessments and resulting recommendations.
- Avoid unnecessary redesign or repeated reconsideration of decisions without meaningful new evidence.

These reviews should complement the scientific and technological review procedures established in Section 10.8.

## General Principles

- Allow scientific knowledge and application requirements to evolve.
- Preserve stability and continuity while introducing improvements.
- Consider user feedback without allowing uncontrolled scope expansion.
- Adapt to technological developments when meaningful benefits justify change.
- Maintain architectural flexibility without overengineering.
- Protect existing functionality, data and historical research.
- Document consequential changes and preserve previous decisions.
- Reassess outdated functionality and dependencies responsibly.
- Avoid unnecessary redesign and continuous refinement without meaningful benefit.

**General principle:** The application should evolve alongside scientific knowledge, technology and user needs while preserving the stability, scientific integrity and research continuity necessary for long-term use.
