# 8.12 — Community Discovery and Research Networking

## Purpose

Establishes how users discover relevant scientific communities, researchers, research projects, discussions, resources and potential collaborators throughout the application.

The application should make it possible for users to find people and research activities relevant to their scientific interests without relying exclusively on existing personal connections or institutional networks.

Discovery should accommodate different experience levels, scientific disciplines, research objectives and participation preferences.

The system should connect relevant community and research material while respecting privacy, access permissions, attribution and scientific context.

Community discovery should prioritize meaningful scientific connections rather than maximizing engagement, popularity or indiscriminate content exposure.

## 8.12.1 — Community and Research Discovery

### Functional Requirements

- Provide ways to discover relevant community activities and scientific resources.
- Support discovery of:
  - Scientific groups and communities.
  - Individual researchers and contributors.
  - Research projects and investigations.
  - Scientific discussions and questions.
  - Shared datasets and observations.
  - Analytical methods and workflows.
  - Models and simulations.
  - Educational resources.
  - Publications and scientific references.
  - Collaborative research opportunities.
- Allow discovery across different scientific disciplines and research subjects.
- Support both broad exploration and searches for specific research interests.
- Organize results into understandable categories.
- Respect privacy and access permissions when presenting discovery results.
- Avoid exposing private projects, discussions or personal information.
- Provide relevant context about discovered material before users access or reuse it.

### Design Guidance

Discovery should help users navigate the wider scientific community and identify useful research opportunities.

It should not become an indiscriminate social feed containing unrelated discussions, resources and activities.

Users should be able to explore broadly while retaining control over what they search for and follow.

## 8.12.2 — Researcher and Contributor Discovery

### Functional Requirements

- Allow users to discover other researchers and contributors.
- Support discovery based on:
  - Scientific disciplines.
  - Research interests.
  - Astronomical objects and systems.
  - Research questions.
  - Analytical methods.
  - Scientific publications.
  - Shared projects and contributions.
  - Educational and technical interests.
- Allow users to inspect publicly available profile information and contributions.
- Provide links to relevant research, discussions, resources and projects.
- Support discovery of contributors with relevant scientific or technical experience.
- Allow users to identify potential collaborators.
- Respect individual profile visibility and privacy settings.
- Avoid treating self-reported qualifications as independently verified credentials.
- Do not expose private research activities through researcher discovery.

### Design Guidance

Researcher discovery should help users identify people with relevant knowledge, interests or contributions.

Professional credentials and institutional affiliations may be useful information, but they should not be mandatory requirements for participation or collaboration.

Users should be discoverable through their scientific contributions and interests where they choose to make that information public.

## 8.12.3 — Scientific Interest and Subject Discovery

### Functional Requirements

- Support discovery organized around scientific subjects and research interests.
- Include broad disciplines and specialized research topics.
- Support discovery of astronomical objects and systems.
- Allow users to explore connections between related scientific subjects.
- Identify relevant discussions, groups, researchers, projects and resources.
- Support interdisciplinary discovery.
- Allow users to explore topics beyond their existing declared interests.
- Provide contextual information explaining why particular subjects or resources are related.

Potential discovery subjects include:

- Exoplanetary science.
- Planetary science.
- Stellar astronomy.
- Spectroscopy.
- Astrochemistry.
- Atmospheric characterization.
- Transit photometry.
- Radiative transfer.
- Observational astronomy.
- Computational and theoretical astronomy.
- Galactic astronomy.
- Cosmology.

### Design Guidance

Scientific discovery should not be restricted to rigid disciplinary categories.

For example, someone researching exoplanet atmospheres may benefit from discovering work involving laboratory spectroscopy, Solar System atmospheric observations, molecular databases or radiative-transfer modelling.

The application should support meaningful connections between related subjects without presenting every possible connection as equally relevant.

## 8.12.4 — Research Project and Investigation Discovery

### Functional Requirements

- Allow users to discover publicly shared research projects and investigations.
- Support discovery based on scientific subject, object, research question, methods and available resources.
- Present relevant project descriptions and research objectives.
- Identify whether research is ongoing, completed, preliminary or archived where this information is available.
- Provide access to publicly shared research material according to its permissions.
- Allow users to inspect associated datasets, analyses, models, visualizations and references.
- Distinguish between original research, collaborative work and adapted material.
- Support discovery of research involving multiple scientific disciplines.
- Respect project ownership and access restrictions.
- Avoid presenting private or restricted projects as publicly accessible.

### Design Guidance

Research discovery should help users understand what an investigation involves before deciding whether to follow, discuss, reproduce or collaborate on it.

The application should distinguish between discovering a project and receiving permission to access its underlying research material.

## 8.12.5 — Scientific Discussion and Knowledge Discovery

### Functional Requirements

- Allow users to discover relevant scientific discussions and questions.
- Support searching and browsing discussions by subject, object, method and research area.
- Identify discussions associated with specific datasets, observations, analyses, publications and projects.
- Allow users to discover discussions involving alternative interpretations, scientific questions and methodological problems.
- Support discovery of useful historical discussions.
- Preserve links between discussions and their original scientific context.
- Provide relevant references and associated resources where available.
- Distinguish between current discussions and historical material.
- Respect discussion visibility and participation permissions.

### Design Guidance

Scientific discussions should remain useful beyond their original participants and publication dates.

A discussion about a particular spectral feature, for example, may be valuable to future researchers investigating the same molecule or wavelength region.

Discovery should help users find relevant previous discussions rather than repeatedly creating disconnected conversations around the same scientific questions.

## 8.12.6 — Scientific Resource and Data Discovery

### Functional Requirements

- Allow users to discover community-shared scientific resources.
- Support discovery of datasets, observations, reference material, workflows, models, simulations, visualizations and educational resources.
- Allow filtering by relevant scientific characteristics.
- Present useful metadata, provenance, attribution, licensing and quality information.
- Identify whether resources are original, externally sourced, community-contributed, processed, derived or simulated.
- Provide relevant compatibility information where available.
- Allow users to inspect resources before accessing or incorporating them into research.
- Distinguish community contributions from established external scientific archives and reference databases.
- Respect ownership and access permissions.
- Connect discovered resources to associated projects, discussions and contributors.

### Design Guidance

Discovery should help users identify potentially useful scientific material without implying that discovery establishes scientific reliability.

Users should be able to evaluate a resource's origin, documentation, compatibility and limitations before using it.

These capabilities should complement the external data discovery systems established in Section 4 and the community resource-sharing requirements in Section 8.5.

## 8.12.7 — Collaboration and Research Networking

### Functional Requirements

- Help users identify potential research collaborators.
- Support discovery of people with relevant scientific interests, methods, experience and contributions.
- Allow users to discover research groups and collaborative projects.
- Support connections between researchers working on related objects, datasets or scientific questions.
- Allow users to express interest in potential collaboration.
- Provide appropriate ways to initiate contact or request participation.
- Respect user communication and profile preferences.
- Ensure discovering a researcher does not automatically grant access to their private research.
- Avoid assuming that shared interests automatically establish willingness to collaborate.
- Support interdisciplinary research connections.

### Design Guidance

Networking should facilitate meaningful scientific collaboration rather than operate primarily as a general social networking feature.

Users should be able to identify potential collaborators based on relevant scientific interests and work without being pressured into participation.

Any contact or collaboration request should remain under the recipient's control.

## 8.12.8 — Search, Filtering and Discovery Controls

### Functional Requirements

- Provide searchable community and research content.
- Support filtering by content category and relevant scientific characteristics.
- Allow users to narrow discovery results progressively.
- Support searches involving multiple related scientific subjects.
- Provide useful sorting options where appropriate.
- Preserve access permissions during search and filtering.
- Make result categories and search scope understandable.
- Support discovery across different scientific workspaces and community environments.
- Avoid overwhelming users with unrelated results.
- Allow users to refine searches without losing their original context.

### Design Guidance

Discovery should support both simple searches and more specialized scientific exploration.

Potential search categories include researchers, groups, projects, discussions, datasets, observations, publications, workflows and educational material.

The application should avoid making users search through unrelated categories when they already know what type of resource they want.

## 8.12.9 — Recommendations and Related Research

### Functional Requirements

- Allow the application to recommend potentially relevant researchers, groups, projects, discussions, datasets and educational resources.
- Base recommendations on relevant scientific context and user-selected interests where appropriate.
- Identify relationships between scientific subjects, objects, methods and research activities.
- Explain why a recommendation may be relevant.
- Allow users to dismiss or ignore recommendations.
- Provide appropriate controls over recommendation preferences.
- Avoid automatically sharing user information or research activity to generate recommendations without authorization.
- Avoid presenting recommendations as endorsements of scientific accuracy.
- Distinguish scientific relevance from popularity.
- Avoid allowing popularity or engagement metrics to dominate scientific discovery.

### Design Guidance

Recommendations should help users discover relevant scientific material they might otherwise overlook.

For example, a user researching an exoplanet atmosphere might receive suggestions for:

- Related molecular reference data.
- Relevant laboratory spectroscopy.
- Similar atmospheric investigations.
- Alternative analytical methods.
- Related observational datasets.
- Researchers working on comparable problems.

Recommendations should remain optional, explainable and scientifically contextual.

## 8.12.10 — Following Researchers, Groups and Research Activities

### Functional Requirements

- Allow users to follow researchers, groups and publicly shared research activities where appropriate.
- Allow users to manage their followed interests and connections.
- Provide access to relevant updates from followed subjects.
- Support following specific astronomical objects, research topics or investigations where useful.
- Allow users to unfollow or modify their preferences.
- Keep following separate from project membership and access permissions.
- Do not automatically grant access to private material through following.
- Avoid overwhelming users with excessive notifications.
- Allow users to control which types of updates they receive.

### Design Guidance

Following should provide a lightweight way to maintain awareness of relevant scientific activity.

It should not automatically subscribe users to every discussion, project update or community notification associated with a followed researcher or group.

Users should remain in control of their information feeds and notification preferences.

## 8.12.11 — Discovery Quality and Scientific Context

### Functional Requirements

- Present sufficient information for users to evaluate discovered material.
- Preserve source attribution, provenance and relevant documentation.
- Distinguish between established scientific resources and community contributions.
- Identify relevant review, reproduction and validation information where available.
- Avoid equating popularity, activity or visibility with scientific credibility.
- Communicate uncertainty and limitations where relevant.
- Avoid presenting recommendations as verified scientific conclusions.
- Preserve distinctions between observations, interpretations, hypotheses, models and validated results.
- Support discovery of less prominent but potentially valuable scientific contributions.
- Allow users to evaluate material independently.

### Design Guidance

Discovery systems should prioritize scientific relevance and useful context rather than engagement metrics.

A highly viewed discussion or frequently downloaded dataset should not automatically be considered more scientifically reliable than a less prominent resource.

The application should provide information that helps users make their own assessments.

- Advanced interdisciplinary collaborator matching.
- Institutional and university research networks.
- Research opportunity discovery.
- Advanced collaboration recommendations.

### Advanced Scientific Discovery

- Discovery based on relationships between datasets, methods and research findings.
- Cross-disciplinary research recommendations.
- Advanced object and system research networks.
- Research trend and activity discovery.
- Discovery of emerging scientific questions.
- More extensive connections to external literature and research repositories.

### Personalized Discovery

- Saved discovery preferences.
- Advanced recommendation controls.
- Customizable research feeds.
- Saved searches and discovery collections.
- Personalized subject monitoring.
- More advanced relevance filtering.

### Community Research Infrastructure

- Discovery of large community observational initiatives.
- Community-maintained research directories.
- Specialized scientific collaboration networks.
- Advanced research opportunity matching.

These capabilities should be evaluated according to scientific usefulness, privacy, technical feasibility and maintenance requirements.

## Initial Implementation Priorities

The initial implementation should establish practical discovery and networking capabilities without requiring a sophisticated recommendation or social networking platform.

Priority capabilities include:

- Searchable public groups and communities.
- Discovery of researchers and contributors through public profiles.
- Searchable public research projects.
- Discovery of scientific discussions and questions.
- Discovery of shared datasets and scientific resources.
- Filtering by relevant scientific subjects and categories.
- Links between researchers, projects, resources and discussions.
- Basic discovery of potential collaborators.
- Optional following of relevant researchers and groups.
- Basic recommendation capabilities where practical.
- Respect for privacy and access permissions.
- Clear distinction between scientific relevance and scientific validation.

The initial system should prioritize useful search, categorization and relationships between scientific material over complex personalization.

## Implementation Considerations

Community discovery should integrate with the application's existing search, profile, group, project, discussion, resource and literature systems.

Particular attention should be given to:

- Connecting related scientific material across application environments.
- Supporting both broad exploration and focused research searches.
- Preserving scientific context and provenance.
- Respecting privacy and access permissions.
- Avoiding duplicate or disconnected discovery systems.
- Distinguishing scientific relevance from popularity.
- Providing understandable recommendation explanations.
- Supporting interdisciplinary discovery.
- Avoiding unnecessary social features within scientific workspaces.

The discovery system should be designed to expand as the application's scientific capabilities and community resources grow.

**General Principle:** Community discovery and research networking should help users find relevant scientific knowledge, researchers, investigations, resources and collaboration opportunities. Discovery should prioritize scientific relevance, useful relationships and transparency while respecting privacy, access permissions and independent scientific judgment.
