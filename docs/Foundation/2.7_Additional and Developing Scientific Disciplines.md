

# 2.7 — Additional and Developing Scientific Disciplines

## Purpose

The application's scientific scope should extend beyond its initial priority disciplines. Astronomy is a broad and interconnected field, and future research needs may introduce additional scientific subjects, methods and areas of investigation.

The application should therefore accommodate a wider range of astronomical disciplines as it develops, including areas that may initially receive limited or no dedicated functionality.

These disciplines should be considered part of the application's broader scientific direction where they meaningfully contribute to astronomical research. Their inclusion should not require immediate implementation or imply that every possible area of astronomy must eventually become a major development priority.

## 2.7.1 Theoretical and Computational Astronomy

### Core Requirements

Recognize theoretical and computational astronomy as important areas of scientific research.

Relevant subjects include:

- Mathematical descriptions of astronomical phenomena.
- Theoretical models of astronomical objects and systems.
- Numerical methods and computational simulations.
- Predictions of observable astronomical behaviour.
- Mathematical analysis of physical processes.
- Computational investigation of planetary and stellar systems.
- Model comparisons with observational evidence.
- Theoretical investigations of astronomical formation and evolution.

### Design Guidance

Theoretical and computational research should complement observational astronomy rather than be treated as an entirely separate scientific environment.

The application should accommodate research involving mathematical reasoning, scientific models and computational predictions while preserving distinctions between theoretical results and observational evidence.

## 2.7.2 Galactic Astronomy

### Core Requirements

Include galactic astronomy within the broader scientific scope.

Relevant subjects include:

- The structure and composition of the Milky Way.
- Stellar populations within the Galaxy.
- Galactic structure and morphology.
- Galactic formation and evolution.
- Interstellar matter and environments.
- Star formation within galaxies.
- Galactic dynamics and gravitational structure.
- The distribution of gas, dust and stars.
- The central region of the Milky Way.
- Relationships between galaxies and their surrounding environments.

### Design Guidance

Galactic astronomy provides a broader context for understanding stars, stellar populations, interstellar environments and the formation of astronomical systems.

It should connect naturally with stellar astronomy, observational astronomy, astrophysics and other relevant disciplines.

## 2.7.3 Extragalactic Astronomy

### Core Requirements

Recognize the study of astronomical objects and phenomena beyond the Milky Way.

Relevant subjects include:

- Other galaxies and their classifications.
- Galaxy structure and morphology.
- Galaxy formation and evolution.
- Interactions and mergers between galaxies.
- Galaxy groups and clusters.
- Active galactic nuclei.
- Quasars and other luminous extragalactic objects.
- Intergalactic environments.
- Large-scale galaxy distributions.
- Relationships between galactic properties and cosmic environments.

### Design Guidance

Extragalactic astronomy should remain within the broader scientific scope without requiring immediate support for every type of galaxy, observation or research method.

Research should preserve relevant spatial, observational and cosmological context.

## 2.7.4 Cosmology

### Core Requirements

Include cosmology as a potential scientific research area.

Relevant subjects include:

- The origin and evolution of the Universe.
- Large-scale structure.
- Cosmic expansion.
- The cosmic microwave background.
- Dark matter and dark energy.
- Cosmic distance measurements.
- Galaxy formation within cosmological models.
- Early-Universe conditions.
- Large-scale cosmic evolution.
- Observational evidence relevant to cosmological theories.

### Design Guidance

Cosmology introduces scientific questions and scales that differ substantially from individual-object astronomy.

Its inclusion should allow future expansion into large-scale astronomical research while recognizing the specialized observational, mathematical and computational requirements of the discipline.

## 2.7.5 Astrophysics and High-Energy Astronomy

### Core Requirements

Recognize astrophysics as a broad scientific area connecting astronomical observations with the physical processes governing astronomical objects and phenomena.

Relevant subjects include:

- High-energy astrophysical processes.
- Accretion and accretion disks.
- Relativistic astrophysics.
- Compact objects.
- Neutron stars and pulsars.
- Black holes.
- Supernovae and explosive stellar phenomena.
- Gamma-ray bursts.
- Cosmic rays.
- High-energy radiation and particle interactions.
- Extreme gravitational, magnetic and thermal environments.

### Design Guidance

Astrophysics overlaps extensively with stellar astronomy, planetary science, galactic astronomy and observational astronomy.

High-energy research may require specialized data, physical models and observational methods. These requirements should be considered when evaluating future development rather than assumed to be covered by existing capabilities.

## 2.7.6 Solar Physics and Heliophysics

### Core Requirements

Recognize solar physics and heliophysics as related scientific disciplines that extend beyond the study of the Sun's basic stellar properties.

Relevant subjects include:

- Solar magnetic activity.
- Solar atmospheric structure and dynamics.
- Solar flares and coronal mass ejections.
- Solar wind.
- Heliospheric structure.
- Solar-terrestrial interactions.
- Planetary magnetospheres.
- Space weather.
- Charged-particle environments.
- Interactions between solar activity and planetary systems.

### Design Guidance

Solar physics and heliophysics connect stellar astronomy with planetary science and space physics.

The application should accommodate these relationships while preserving the distinction between research into the Sun as a star and research into its influence throughout the Solar System.

## 2.7.7 Astrobiology

### Core Requirements

Include astrobiology as a potential interdisciplinary research area.

Relevant subjects include:

- Conditions associated with the origin and development of life.
- Habitability of planetary environments.
- Planetary and exoplanetary atmospheric conditions.
- Prebiotic chemistry.
- Chemical processes relevant to biological systems.
- Potential biosignatures.
- Environments that may support microbial or other forms of life.
- Comparisons between terrestrial environments and extraterrestrial conditions.
- Scientific investigation of possible extraterrestrial biological evidence.

### Design Guidance

Astrobiology should be approached as a scientific discipline involving astronomy, planetary science, chemistry, biology, geology and atmospheric science.

Research involving habitability and potential biosignatures must maintain appropriate scientific caution. Potentially habitable conditions, chemical indicators and confirmed biological evidence must remain distinct.

## 2.7.8 Astrochemistry and Interstellar Chemistry

### Core Requirements

Recognize astrochemistry as a potential scientific area connecting astronomical environments with chemical processes.

Relevant subjects include:

- Chemical composition of astronomical environments.
- Molecular species in interstellar space.
- Molecular clouds and star-forming regions.
- Chemical processes in circumstellar environments.
- Molecular formation and destruction.
- Gas-phase and surface chemical reactions.
- Chemistry within planetary and protoplanetary environments.
- Dust grains and their chemical interactions.
- Chemical evolution of astronomical environments.
- Relationships between chemical composition and physical conditions.

### Design Guidance

Astrochemistry provides important connections between astronomy, spectroscopy, molecular science, chemistry and planetary research.

Its inclusion should allow future expansion into chemical investigations of astronomical environments without restricting chemical research to planetary or exoplanetary atmospheres.

## 2.7.9 Other Specialized and Emerging Disciplines

### Core Requirements

Allow additional specialized or developing areas of astronomy to be considered as the application evolves.

Potential areas include:

- Time-domain and transient astronomy.
- Gravitational-wave astronomy.
- Neutrino astronomy.
- Solar system small-body research.
- Planetary geophysics.
- Space physics.
- Interstellar medium research.
- Stellar population studies.
- Relativistic astrophysics.
- Multimessenger astronomy.

The application should not treat the disciplines listed in this subsection as an exhaustive inventory of all possible future research areas.

### Design Guidance

New scientific fields and research approaches may emerge as observational facilities, scientific methods and research priorities develop.

The application's scientific scope should remain sufficiently flexible to accommodate relevant developments without requiring a complete restructuring of its existing scientific organization.

## 2.7.10 Relationships Between Developing Disciplines

### Core Requirements

- Allow additional scientific disciplines to connect with the application's initial priority areas.
- Recognize that some research subjects naturally belong to multiple disciplines.
- Support interdisciplinary investigations involving several scientific fields.
- Preserve relevant scientific context when research crosses disciplinary boundaries.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of scientific concepts, data and research materials.
- Allow new disciplines to become more prominent as application capabilities and user needs develop.

### Design Guidance

Scientific disciplines should not be treated as isolated categories.

For example:

- Astrochemistry may contribute to exoplanet atmospheric research and interstellar medium studies.
- Astrobiology may connect planetary science, exoplanet science, chemistry and atmospheric science.
- Galactic astronomy may connect stellar populations, stellar evolution and interstellar chemistry.
- High-energy astrophysics may connect compact-object research with observational astronomy and theoretical physics.
- Gravitational-wave astronomy may contribute to the study of compact objects and other astrophysical phenomena.

The application should accommodate these relationships without requiring each investigation to belong exclusively to one discipline.

## General Principles

Additional and developing scientific disciplines should:

- Remain within the application's broader astronomy-focused scope where scientifically relevant.
- Complement rather than displace the initial priority disciplines.
- Accommodate theoretical, computational and observational research.
- Allow expansion into additional astronomical scales and research subjects.
- Support interdisciplinary research.
- Preserve scientific distinctions between disciplines and research methods.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of existing subject areas.
- Be evaluated according to scientific relevance, user needs and development feasibility.
- Remain adaptable to future developments in astronomy.

## General Principle

The application's scientific scope should be broad enough to support the evolving nature of astronomical research without requiring every scientific discipline to receive equal priority or immediate implementation.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection identifies additional scientific disciplines that may be accommodated as the application develops.

Their inclusion establishes potential scientific scope, not a commitment to implement every discipline, research method, specialized dataset or analytical capability.

Future development should evaluate additional disciplines according to their scientific relevance, compatibility with the application's direction, available resources and practical feasibility.

Specific capabilities, workflows, data requirements and implementation priorities are addressed elsewhere in the Foundations documentation.
