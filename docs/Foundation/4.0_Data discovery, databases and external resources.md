# 4.0 — Data Discovery, Databases and External Resources

## Section Purpose

The application should provide an integrated environment for discovering, evaluating, accessing and working with astronomical data, scientific databases, reference resources and relevant external services.

Astronomical research depends on scientific information distributed across observatories, archives, research institutions, public databases, scientific publications and specialized repositories. These resources differ in their scientific purposes, data formats, accessibility, reliability, licensing conditions and methods of retrieval.

The application should make these resources easier to discover and incorporate into research workflows while preserving their origins, metadata, limitations, scientific context and applicable usage conditions.

It should support both direct access to external resources and the use of locally available materials. Users should be able to investigate resources, retrieve selected information, organize exploratory work and incorporate appropriate materials into persistent research projects without unnecessary duplication or loss of provenance.

This section establishes the requirements for scientific data discovery, external resource integration, data retrieval and import, data lineage, provenance, quality evaluation, local storage, offline access and responsible resource use.

### 4.12 — Resource Evaluation, Expansion and User Contributions

Covers resource catalogues, scientific suitability and credibility, resource classification, catalogue maintenance, user-suggested resources, evaluation and prioritization of suggestions, integration planning, maintenance reviews, resource deprecation and decision records.

## General Section Principles

The application's data discovery and external resource environment should:

- Provide an integrated environment for discovering relevant astronomical data and scientific resources.
- Support multiple archives, catalogues, databases, reference collections and specialized scientific repositories.
- Accommodate different scientific data types, formats, standards and representations.
- Allow users to search, evaluate, retrieve, import and investigate scientific resources through appropriate workflows.
- Support exploratory research without requiring immediate creation of a persistent project.
- Allow selected exploratory materials to be transferred into projects while preserving their metadata, provenance and relevant relationships.
- Support selective and progressive retrieval when working with large datasets.
- Preserve original data and distinguish it from derived or transformed representations.
- Maintain traceable relationships between original resources, processing operations, analyses and research outputs.
- Preserve source information, metadata, citations, bibliographic references and attribution.
- Communicate data quality, completeness, uncertainty, limitations and availability.
- Distinguish scientific data quality from search relevance and external provider reliability.
- Support local reuse of retrieved resources, configurable retention and appropriate offline access.
- Protect saved research and project dependencies from unintended removal or replacement.
- Integrate with external scientific software, services and computational resources where justified.
- Respect licensing, access permissions, privacy, attribution and provider requirements.
- Remain extensible as new scientific resources, formats, services and research requirements emerge.

## Section Boundaries

This section establishes the requirements for discovering, evaluating, retrieving, importing, storing and responsibly accessing external scientific data and resources.

It does not define the detailed scientific analysis performed on retrieved data, which is addressed in Section 3.

General data examination, preparation and scientific compatibility requirements are established in Section 3.2. Search relevance and result presentation are addressed in Section 4.3, while scientific data formats and standards are addressed in Section 4.4.

Data retrieval, import and acquisition are addressed in Section 4.5. Data lineage, processing history and traceability are addressed in Section 4.6. Source provenance, metadata, citations and attribution are addressed in Section 4.7.

Data quality, reliability and availability are addressed in Section 4.8. Local storage, caching, retention and offline resource access are addressed in Section 4.9.

External software, services and API integration are addressed in Section 4.10. Licensing, access restrictions, privacy and responsible resource use are addressed in Section 4.11. Evaluation, expansion and maintenance of supported resources are addressed in Section 4.12.

Project organization, persistent research materials and project-level resource relationships are addressed in Section 6. Broader platform storage architecture, connectivity, offline operation and synchronization are addressed in Section 9.

Scientific validation, uncertainty, analytical safeguards and reproducibility requirements are further established in Section 5.

## General Principle

The application should connect researchers with the scientific data and external resources they need while preserving the information required to understand, evaluate, retrieve, reuse and responsibly incorporate those resources into scientific research.

Data discovery and resource management should function as integrated parts of the research environment rather than as disconnected search, download or file-management utilities.
