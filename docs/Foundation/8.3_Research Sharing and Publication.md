# 8.3 — Research Sharing and Publication

## Purpose

Establishes how users can share scientific research, findings, observations and investigations with other users, selected groups and the wider scientific community.

The application should support sharing research at different stages of development, from preliminary observations and ongoing investigations to completed analyses and established findings.

Sharing capabilities should accommodate private collaboration, restricted access and public dissemination while preserving research ownership, attribution, scientific context, provenance and reproducibility.

The application should make it straightforward to communicate scientific work without requiring users to complete formal publication processes or turn the platform into a dedicated academic publishing system.

## 8.3.1 — Sharing Scientific Research

Users should be able to share scientific work produced or organized within the application.

Potential shared material includes:

- Research findings and conclusions.
- Scientific investigations and research questions.
- Astronomical observations and measurements.
- Spectroscopic and photometric analyses.
- Atmospheric characterization and modelling results.
- Graphs, visualizations and comparisons.
- Scientific interpretations and hypotheses.
- Research summaries and supporting explanations.
- Links to associated projects, datasets and references.

Shared research should provide enough context for other users to understand what was investigated, how the work was conducted and what conclusions or observations were produced.

The application should support sharing individual results as well as more comprehensive research investigations.

## 8.3.2 — Research Presentation and Organization

The application should provide ways to present shared research in an organized and understandable format.

Research presentations may include:

- Research titles and summaries.
- Scientific objectives and research questions.
- Background and relevant scientific context.
- Observational data and supporting evidence.
- Methods and analytical procedures.
- Results and visualizations.
- Scientific interpretations and conclusions.
- Uncertainties, assumptions and limitations.
- References and citations.
- Links to associated research material.

The presentation should distinguish between observations, processed measurements, model predictions, interpretations and conclusions.

Users should be able to communicate their work clearly without being required to follow a formal academic manuscript structure.

More advanced presentation formats may be considered as the application develops.

## 8.3.3 — Sharing Unpublished and Ongoing Research

Users should be able to share research before an investigation is complete or formally published.

This may include:

- Preliminary observations.
- Ongoing data collection.
- Early analytical results.
- Research questions and objectives.
- Developing hypotheses.
- Initial model outputs.
- Incomplete investigations.
- Results requiring further validation or investigation.

The application should allow users to communicate the current status of their research.

Where appropriate, shared material should identify whether it represents preliminary findings, ongoing work, completed analysis, inconclusive results or findings requiring further investigation.

Unpublished and preliminary research should not automatically be presented as established scientific conclusions.

Users should retain control over whether unfinished work is shared and with whom.

## 8.3.4 — Research Visibility and Access

Users should be able to control the visibility and accessibility of their shared research.

Potential sharing arrangements include:

- Private research accessible only to the owner.
- Research shared with selected individuals.
- Research shared with invited collaborators.
- Research accessible to selected groups.
- Research made publicly accessible to the application community.
- Research made available for broader external access where supported.

Sharing should require explicit user authorization.

Users should be able to review and modify access permissions where appropriate, including revoking access when technically possible.

Publicly sharing research should not automatically make every associated dataset, project document or personal detail publicly accessible.

The application should preserve the distinction between sharing a particular research result and granting access to the complete underlying investigation.

These requirements should remain consistent with the privacy and permission systems established elsewhere in the project.

## 8.3.5 — Research Documentation and Supporting Material

Shared research should support the inclusion of relevant scientific documentation and supporting material.

This may include:

- Original observational datasets.
- Processed and derived datasets.
- Data provenance and metadata.
- Processing histories.
- Analytical methods and parameters.
- Model configurations and assumptions.
- Software and dependency information where relevant.
- Graphs, tables and visualizations.
- Scientific notes and explanations.
- References and citations.
- Uncertainty assessments and quality reports.
- Reproducibility information.

Users should be able to choose which supporting materials accompany shared research.

Where material is omitted, the application should avoid implying that the complete investigation or all supporting evidence has been provided.

Shared research should retain relevant relationships between its results and underlying data, methods and analytical procedures.

Scientific provenance, licensing and reproducibility requirements should remain consistent with Sections 4 and 5.

## 8.3.6 — Research Versions, Updates and Corrections

The application should support updates and revisions to previously shared research.

Users may need to:

- Correct errors.
- Add new observations.
- Incorporate additional datasets.
- Update analytical methods.
- Revise model configurations.
- Modify interpretations.
- Add further references.
- Replace preliminary conclusions with better-supported findings.

Significant revisions should preserve appropriate version history.

Requirements include:

- Preserve previous versions where scientifically or historically relevant.
- Record significant changes and their dates.
- Maintain authorship and contributor attribution.
- Allow users to identify the current version.
- Provide access to previous versions where appropriate.
- Distinguish corrections, additional results and changes in scientific interpretation.
- Preserve relationships between versions and their supporting research material.

Previously shared research should not be silently overwritten in ways that remove important historical information or make earlier findings impossible to inspect.

Where a correction materially changes an interpretation or conclusion, the nature of the change should be communicated clearly.

## 8.3.7 — Research Citations and External Publication Links

Shared research should support references to relevant scientific literature, datasets, external publications and other supporting resources.

Potential functionality includes:

- Bibliographic references.
- DOI identifiers.
- Links to scientific publications.
- Links to external datasets and archives.
- References to instruments and observational facilities.
- Links to related research.
- Citations of methods, models and software.
- Attribution of previously published findings.

Where possible, references should retain their identifying information and source details.

The application should distinguish between a user's own research contributions and previously published or externally sourced material.

External publication links should not imply that shared research has itself undergone peer review or formal publication.

Citation and attribution requirements should remain consistent with Sections 4.7, 5.5 and 7.6.

## 8.3.8 — Research Export and External Dissemination

Users should be able to export shared research material for use outside the application.

Potential export material includes:

- Research summaries and documentation.
- Graphs and scientific visualizations.
- Numerical datasets and tables.
- Processing and analysis configurations.
- Supporting references and citations.
- Relevant metadata and provenance.
- Reproducibility information.
- Complete research packages where technically and legally possible.

Export should preserve relevant scientific context and distinguish original data from processed, derived and model-generated material.

The application should support appropriate formats for further scientific analysis, documentation and external communication.

Users should not be restricted to sharing or accessing their research exclusively through the application.

More advanced external dissemination and publication integrations may be investigated as the platform develops.

## Overall Principles

- Allow users to share both completed and ongoing scientific research.
- Support private, restricted and public sharing arrangements.
- Preserve user ownership, authorship and attribution.
- Allow preliminary findings and incomplete investigations to be shared with appropriate status information.
- Provide structured ways to present research and its supporting evidence.
- Preserve relationships between research results, datasets, methods, models and references.
- Maintain scientific provenance, uncertainty and reproducibility information.
- Preserve historical versions and significant corrections.
- Support export and external use of research material.
- Avoid implying that shared or publicly accessible research is automatically peer-reviewed, validated or scientifically established.
- Keep research sharing accessible without requiring a formal publication workflow.

## Future Possibilities

The following capabilities may be investigated as the application develops but are not initial priorities:

- More advanced scientific research presentation pages.
- Dedicated manuscript preparation and formatting.
- Journal-specific publication templates.
- DOI and persistent research identifier integration.
- Connections to external scientific repositories.
- Integration with academic publication services.
- Research submission workflows.
- More advanced publication and dissemination tools.
- Public research collections and curated investigations.

Formal scientific publishing should remain an optional future direction rather than a central responsibility of the initial application.

## Implementation Considerations

Research sharing should build upon the application's existing project, data management, provenance, permissions, reproducibility and export capabilities.

The application should avoid creating separate systems for information already managed elsewhere.

Particular attention should be given to:

- Maintaining consistent ownership and access controls.
- Preserving scientific metadata and provenance.
- Respecting external data licensing and redistribution restrictions.
- Maintaining attribution across collaborative contributions.
- Supporting incomplete and evolving research.
- Preserving historical versions and corrections.
- Ensuring shared research remains understandable and inspectable outside its original project context.

The extent of research presentation and external publication functionality should be determined by practical requirements and future development priorities.

**General Principle:** Research sharing should make scientific work accessible, understandable and reusable while preserving its ownership, provenance, scientific context and limitations. The application should facilitate the exchange and dissemination of research without requiring users to adopt formal academic publishing processes or compromising their control over unpublished work.
