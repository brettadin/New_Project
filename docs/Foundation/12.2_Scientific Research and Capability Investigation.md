# 12.2 — Scientific Research and Capability Investigation

## Purpose

Establish a systematic approach to researching and understanding the scientific disciplines, methodologies and capabilities the application intends to support.

The AI development partner must investigate the scientific foundations of proposed functionality before recommending or implementing it.

Research should establish what a capability does, the scientific principles behind it, how it is used, what information it requires, how its results should be interpreted and what limitations affect its reliability.

## 12.2.1 Scientific Capability Investigation

**Core requirements**

- Investigate each major scientific capability identified in the application requirements.
- Understand the scientific problem the capability is intended to address.
- Identify its relevant disciplines, research applications and practical use cases.
- Investigate established scientific approaches and alternative methodologies.
- Identify the relationships between the capability and other application functionality.
- Determine what scientific knowledge users need to understand its operation and results.
- Identify existing scientific software, databases and resources that may support the capability.
- Assess scientific maturity and distinguish established methods from experimental or emerging approaches.

Research should prioritize the application's initial scientific scope while identifying dependencies and opportunities relevant to future expansion.

## 12.2.2 Scientific Principles and Mathematical Foundations

**Core requirements**

For each significant scientific capability, investigate its underlying scientific foundations.

This includes, where relevant:

- Fundamental physical and chemical principles.
- Relevant mathematical relationships and equations.
- Variables, parameters, constants and units.
- Physical assumptions and applicable conditions.
- Derivations and theoretical foundations where necessary.
- Approximations and empirical relationships.
- Numerical and computational methods.
- Statistical methods and uncertainty.
- Relationships between theoretical predictions and observational measurements.

The AI must understand the scientific reasoning behind a method rather than treating it as an unexplained computational procedure.

**Important considerations**

- Identify the conditions under which a method or equation is valid.
- Distinguish fundamental relationships from approximations and model-dependent formulations.
- Investigate alternative formulations where relevant.
- Identify assumptions that may not hold for every dataset or scientific application.
- Preserve mathematical and scientific accuracy when translating methods into software requirements.

## 12.2.3 Scientific Methodologies and Analytical Procedures

**Core requirements**

Investigate established scientific methodologies and how they are applied in practice.

Research should identify:

- The purpose of each method.
- Required inputs and prerequisites.
- Processing and analytical stages.
- Parameters and configuration requirements.
- Expected outputs and intermediate results.
- Assumptions and limitations.
- Sources of uncertainty and potential error.
- Appropriate applications and unsuitable use cases.
- Established alternatives and their trade-offs.
- Methods used to assess or validate results.

Where multiple approaches exist, compare their scientific suitability rather than automatically selecting the most familiar or easiest to implement.

The AI should identify when different methods are complementary, interchangeable or scientifically inappropriate for the same task.

## 12.2.4 Observational Data and Instrumentation

**Core requirements**

Investigate the observations, instruments and scientific datasets required to support application capabilities.

Research should consider:

- Types of astronomical observations.
- Relevant instruments and observing facilities.
- Instrument characteristics and performance limitations.
- Observation conditions and acquisition procedures.
- Measurement conventions and units.
- Spectral, photometric, imaging and time-series data.
- Calibration and preprocessing requirements.
- Resolution, sensitivity, coverage and sampling.
- Metadata and uncertainty information.
- Instrument-specific artifacts and systematic effects.
- Differences between observational measurements and theoretical or simulated data.

Investigate how observational data is acquired, processed, documented and interpreted.

Do not assume datasets from different instruments or facilities are directly interchangeable. Identify compatibility requirements and scientifically appropriate comparison methods.

## 12.2.5 Scientific Data Requirements and Reference Resources

**Core requirements**

For each significant capability, identify the data and external resources needed to support its operation.

Investigate:

- Required observational datasets.
- Relevant scientific archives and databases.
- Laboratory measurements and reference libraries.
- Molecular, atomic and other spectral reference resources.
- Published measurements and benchmark datasets.
- Synthetic spectra and theoretical model grids.
- Instrument specifications and calibration references.
- Scientific catalogues and object parameters.
- Required metadata, identifiers and provenance.
- Data formats, units, conventions and compatibility requirements.

Evaluate whether resources are authoritative, accessible, sufficiently documented and appropriate for their intended scientific use.

Identify whether a capability can operate with locally imported data, retrieved external data or both.

## 12.2.6 Complete Scientific Workflows

**Core requirements**

Investigate complete scientific workflows rather than isolated analytical operations.

For each major workflow, identify:

- The scientific objective.
- Required observations and reference resources.
- Data inspection and quality assessment.
- Necessary calibration and preprocessing.
- Analytical or modelling stages.
- Intermediate datasets and results.
- Relevant parameters and assumptions.
- Visualization and interpretation.
- Uncertainty and quality assessment.
- Validation and comparison with established results.
- Expected outputs and documentation.
- Potential failure points and alternative approaches.

Understand the relationships and dependencies between workflow stages.

Identify which stages are essential, optional or dependent on particular datasets and scientific conditions.

Workflows should accommodate legitimate variations in scientific practice rather than impose one universal sequence where alternatives are appropriate.

## 12.2.7 Scientific Validation and Benchmarking

**Core requirements**

Investigate how proposed scientific methods and capabilities can be evaluated for correctness and reliability.

Research should identify:

- Published methodologies and established equations.
- Reference datasets and benchmark cases.
- Known observational results.
- Published detections, non-detections and control cases where relevant.
- Analytical solutions and expected numerical behaviour.
- Independent software implementations where useful.
- Known limitations and failure conditions.
- Appropriate statistical and numerical comparison methods.
- Relevant uncertainty and tolerance requirements.

Distinguish between successful software execution, correct implementation of a method and scientific validation of its results.

Where suitable reference cases are unavailable, document the limitation rather than claiming validation without evidence.

## 12.2.8 Scientific Limitations and Interpretation

**Core requirements**

Investigate the limitations affecting each scientific capability and its results.

Consider:

- Measurement uncertainty and observational noise.
- Instrument limitations and systematic effects.
- Incomplete or insufficient data.
- Ambiguous measurements and overlapping signals.
- Model assumptions and approximations.
- Sensitivity to input parameters.
- Alternative scientific interpretations.
- Computational limitations.
- Detection limits and confidence.
- Conditions under which results cannot support a reliable conclusion.

Identify how these limitations should be communicated to users.

The application must not present a possible identification, model prediction or statistical association as a confirmed scientific finding without appropriate evidence.

## 12.2.9 Cross-Disciplinary Scientific Relationships

**Core requirements**

- Investigate relationships between the application's scientific disciplines.
- Identify opportunities to combine observations, methods, models and reference resources.
- Understand how results from one discipline may support investigations in another.
- Identify shared scientific principles and analytical techniques.
- Evaluate compatibility requirements when combining data from different sources or disciplines.
- Identify where specialized methods must remain distinct.
- Avoid forcing integration where scientific assumptions or requirements are incompatible.

Examples include:

- Comparing Solar System and exoplanet atmospheres.
- Using stellar characterization to support exoplanet investigations.
- Combining spectroscopy with photometric observations.
- Comparing observational measurements with theoretical models.
- Connecting laboratory molecular measurements with astronomical spectral features.

Cross-disciplinary integration should be based on scientific value and methodological compatibility.

## 12.2.10 Scientific Research Documentation

**Core requirements**

- Preserve significant scientific research findings in the appropriate documentation locations.
- Record supporting publications, references and authoritative resources.
- Document methods, equations, assumptions and limitations.
- Identify the scientific capabilities and requirements supported by each investigation.
- Distinguish established knowledge from developing research and unresolved questions.
- Preserve alternative methodologies and relevant comparisons.
- Identify areas requiring further investigation.
- Update existing research records when new findings materially change previous conclusions.
- Maintain relationships between scientific research, application requirements and implementation decisions.

Research documentation should be sufficiently detailed to support future implementation, scientific review and educational material development.

## General Principles

- Understand the science before implementing scientific functionality.
- Investigate complete scientific workflows rather than isolated features.
- Prefer established and appropriately validated methodologies.
- Understand required data, instrumentation, references and assumptions.
- Investigate alternatives and identify their scientific trade-offs.
- Preserve uncertainty, limitations and unresolved questions.
- Evaluate scientific compatibility before combining methods or datasets.
- Maintain traceability between scientific research and application requirements.
- Continue scientific investigation as the application develops.

**General principle:** Every significant scientific capability must be supported by a genuine understanding of its scientific foundations, practical methodologies, data requirements, workflows and limitations. The application should be designed around scientifically defensible capabilities rather than implementing features whose underlying science has not been adequately investigated.
