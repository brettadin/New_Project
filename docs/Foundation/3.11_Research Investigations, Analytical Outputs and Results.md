# 3.11 — Research Investigations, Analytical Outputs and Results

## Purpose

The application should provide a coherent framework for organizing scientific investigations and connecting their research objectives, analytical activities, outputs, findings and interpretations.

Scientific research involves more than performing individual calculations or operating specialized analytical tools. Researchers need to organize related activities around scientific questions, examine the outputs of individual analyses, compare alternative approaches, evaluate evidence and develop conclusions supported by their observations and methods.

The application should allow individual scientific activities to contribute to broader investigations while preserving the relationships between their inputs, methods, outputs and interpretations.

Investigations should accommodate exploratory research, hypothesis-driven studies, observational programmes, comparative research and other scientific approaches.

The system should support investigations that develop progressively, change direction, incorporate additional evidence or produce multiple alternative analytical outcomes.

This subsection concerns the scientific organization and use of investigations, analytical outputs and results. Persistent project organization is addressed in Section 6.4, detailed analysis reproducibility and traceability in Section 5.5, and scientific visualization and presentation in Section 3.10.

## 3.11.1 Research Investigations and Scientific Questions

### Core Requirements

- Allow users to organize related scientific activities around a research question, objective or investigation.
- Support investigations focused on individual astronomical objects, complete systems, scientific phenomena or broader research questions.
- Accommodate investigations involving multiple objects, observations, datasets, scientific disciplines and analytical methods.
- Allow investigations to begin with an existing observation, dataset, published result, theoretical question or exploratory examination.
- Support investigations with clearly defined objectives as well as exploratory research whose direction develops over time.
- Allow users to record relevant research questions, objectives, hypotheses and initial assumptions.
- Allow users to revise research questions and objectives as new evidence becomes available.
- Preserve the relationship between an investigation and its contributing scientific activities.
- Allow multiple related investigations to contribute to a broader research project.
- Avoid requiring every scientific activity to belong to a formally defined investigation.

### Design Guidance

An investigation should represent a coherent scientific inquiry rather than simply a collection of files or a sequence of operations.

For example, an investigation might examine whether a particular molecular feature is consistent with an exoplanet atmosphere, compare atmospheric properties across several planets, or evaluate the orbital characteristics of a planetary system.

Investigations should remain flexible. An initial research question may lead to several related analyses, alternative explanations or additional questions.

The application should support this development without forcing users to establish every objective or analytical step in advance.

## 3.11.2 Investigation Organization and Scope

### Core Requirements

- Allow investigations to contain multiple related analytical activities.
- Support investigations that use different scientific capabilities and workspaces.
- Allow users to organize activities according to their research objectives.
- Support investigations that involve multiple astronomical objects and datasets.
- Allow related analyses to be grouped by scientific question, analytical method, observational source or other meaningful criteria.
- Allow users to identify the scope and intended purpose of an investigation.
- Support both small, focused investigations and broader research studies.
- Allow investigations to be divided into related analytical questions or work areas where appropriate.
- Preserve relationships between related and dependent activities.
- Allow investigation organization to change without breaking scientific relationships.

### Design Guidance

The application should distinguish between the scientific organization of an investigation and the physical organization of its project materials.

An investigation may involve datasets, analyses and results located in different scientific workspaces or organizational categories.

Users should be able to organize their research according to the scientific relationships that matter to them without being constrained by a fixed folder structure or mandatory workflow.

Persistent storage, project organization and project-level continuity are addressed in Section 6.4.

## 3.11.3 Analytical Activities and Analysis Instances

### Core Requirements

Allow individual scientific operations to be represented as identifiable analytical activities within an investigation.

An analytical activity may include:

- Data examination.
- Data preparation or processing.
- Measurements and calculations.
- Spectral analysis.
- Photometric and time-series analysis.
- Image analysis.
- Orbital calculations.
- Scientific modelling and simulation.
- Statistical analysis.
- Comparative analysis.
- Other supported scientific operations.

For each activity, preserve relevant information about:

- Its scientific purpose.
- The datasets and other inputs used.
- The analytical method applied.
- Relevant configurations and parameters.
- The outputs produced.
- Its relationship to the wider investigation.
- Its status and subsequent use.

Allow users to create multiple analytical activities using the same original observations.

Allow alternative methods or configurations to be applied to the same inputs without unintentionally replacing previous outputs.

### Design Guidance

An analytical activity should represent a particular application of a scientific method, not merely the existence of a tool or capability.

For example, a user might perform several spectral analyses on the same observation using different wavelength regions, normalization approaches or model configurations.

These should remain distinguishable so that users can compare their outcomes and understand how the different analytical approaches contributed to the investigation.

Detailed execution records, parameter histories and reproducibility requirements are established in Section 5.5.

## 3.11.4 Relationships Between Inputs, Analyses and Outputs

### Core Requirements

- Maintain relationships between original observations, imported datasets and subsequent analytical activities.
- Identify which inputs contributed to each analytical output.
- Preserve relationships between intermediate outputs and subsequent analyses that use them.
- Support analytical activities that depend on the outputs of multiple earlier operations.
- Allow one dataset or analytical output to contribute to multiple investigations.
- Allow multiple analytical activities to use the same underlying source data independently.
- Preserve relationships when outputs are reorganized or reused.
- Identify missing, unavailable or incompatible dependencies where relevant.
- Distinguish original observations from prepared datasets and derived outputs.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of large underlying resources.

### Design Guidance

Scientific investigations frequently involve chains of related operations.

For example, an observational spectrum may be calibrated, normalized, examined for spectral features, compared with reference data and subsequently used in an atmospheric model.

Each operation should remain connected to its relevant inputs and outputs.

The application should support these relationships without requiring users to duplicate the original dataset at every analytical stage.

Where an output is reused in another analysis, the relationship between the original source and the subsequent activity should remain understandable.

## 3.11.5 Analytical Outputs and Scientific Artifacts

### Core Requirements

Allow scientific operations to produce identifiable outputs that can be inspected, saved, compared and reused.

Potential analytical outputs include:

- Prepared and transformed datasets.
- Extracted measurements.
- Spectral measurements and identified features.
- Photometric measurements and light curves.
- Image measurements and source catalogues.
- Orbital parameters and calculated quantities.
- Model outputs and predictions.
- Parameter estimates.
- Statistical results.
- Comparative measurements.
- Residuals and diagnostic information.
- Scientific graphs and visualizations.
- Tables and structured numerical results.
- Research notes and documented findings.
- Other outputs produced by supported scientific capabilities.

Preserve the type and scientific meaning of each output.

Allow users to identify the analysis that produced an output and the inputs on which it depends.

Support outputs containing multiple related components where appropriate.

Allow compatible outputs to be used as inputs to subsequent scientific activities.

### Design Guidance

An analytical output should remain a distinct scientific resource rather than existing only as a temporary display inside the tool that produced it.

For example, a spectral fitting operation may produce fitted parameters, a model spectrum, residuals and diagnostic measurements.

These outputs should remain identifiable and available for further examination even when the original analytical interface is closed.

The application should distinguish between the underlying analytical output and its graphical or tabular presentation.

Scientific visualization and export requirements are established in Section 3.10.

## 3.11.6 Intermediate, Unfinished and Completed Analyses

### Core Requirements

Support analytical activities at different stages of completion.

Relevant states may include:

- Not started.
- In progress.
- Awaiting required information.
- Awaiting review.
- Completed.
- Completed with limitations.
- Unsuccessful.
- Inconclusive.
- Superseded.
- Abandoned.

Allow users to retain partially completed analyses and return to them later where technically possible.

Preserve useful intermediate outputs when an analysis has not yet produced a final result.

Distinguish between an operation that failed technically and an analysis that completed successfully but produced inconclusive scientific findings.

Allow users to record why an analysis was paused, abandoned, unsuccessful or superseded where appropriate.

Do not automatically classify unfinished analyses as failed or completed analyses as scientifically conclusive.

### Design Guidance

Scientific work does not always produce an immediate or definitive result.

An analysis may be technically complete while the available evidence remains insufficient to support an interpretation. Another analysis may remain unfinished because additional observations or metadata are required.

The application should represent these situations accurately rather than reducing all analytical activity to a simple completed or incomplete state.

Detailed execution and reproducibility statuses should remain coordinated with Section 5.5.

## 3.11.7 Result Examination and Interpretation

### Core Requirements

Allow users to examine analytical outputs and develop scientific interpretations based on their results.

Support activities such as:

- Inspecting measurements and derived quantities.
- Examining model outputs and predictions.
- Reviewing statistical and analytical results.
- Comparing outputs from different methods.
- Examining residuals and diagnostic information.
- Identifying patterns, relationships and unusual observations.
- Comparing results with reference information and published findings.
- Considering alternative explanations.
- Recording interpretations and relevant limitations.
- Identifying questions that require further investigation.

Allow users to associate interpretations with the specific outputs and evidence on which they are based.

Distinguish direct measurements from calculated quantities, model-dependent estimates and scientific interpretations.

Allow interpretations to be revised when additional evidence or improved methods become available.

### Design Guidance

Analytical outputs do not automatically constitute scientific conclusions.

A numerical measurement, fitted parameter or model prediction must be interpreted in the context of its assumptions, uncertainty and supporting evidence.

For example, identifying a spectral feature that resembles a molecular reference spectrum does not, by itself, establish the presence of that molecule in an astronomical atmosphere.

The application should allow users to develop interpretations while preserving the evidence and limitations supporting them.

Scientific integrity and uncertainty requirements are established in Section 5.

## 3.11.8 Findings, Conclusions and Scientific Claims

### Core Requirements

Allow users to document findings and conclusions associated with an investigation.

Support the recording of:

- Observed patterns and relationships.
- Measurements and calculated quantities.
- Supported scientific findings.
- Proposed interpretations.
- Alternative explanations.
- Hypotheses and their evaluation.
- Unresolved questions.
- Limitations and qualifications.
- Negative or inconclusive findings.
- Conclusions supported by the available evidence.

Allow findings to reference the observations, datasets, analyses, models and outputs that support them.

Distinguish between:

- Directly observed findings.
- Results derived through analytical methods.
- Model-dependent interpretations.
- Tentative or provisional hypotheses.
- Conclusions supported by multiple sources of evidence.
- Unresolved or inconclusive outcomes.

Allow users to revise or supersede previous interpretations without silently erasing historical conclusions.

### Design Guidance

Scientific conclusions should be supported by identifiable evidence and should communicate the degree of certainty justified by that evidence.

The application should not automatically convert analytical outputs into established findings or treat a user's interpretation as independently validated.

Where evidence supports multiple possible explanations, the application should allow those alternatives to remain documented.

Negative and inconclusive results should remain valid research outcomes rather than being treated as unimportant simply because they do not establish the original hypothesis.

## 3.11.9 Alternative Analyses and Competing Interpretations

### Core Requirements

- Allow users to perform alternative analyses using the same underlying observations.
- Support comparison of different methods, configurations and assumptions.
- Allow competing model outputs and interpretations to be retained.
- Preserve the identity and context of each alternative.
- Allow users to examine why different approaches produce different outcomes.
- Support investigation of conflicting measurements and results.
- Allow alternative analytical approaches to contribute independently to an investigation.
- Avoid automatically replacing one interpretation with another.
- Allow users to document which approaches they consider more suitable and why.

### Design Guidance

Scientific research often involves more than one plausible analytical approach or interpretation.

For example, different model assumptions may produce different estimates of an atmospheric parameter, or alternative processing methods may affect the appearance of a spectral feature.

The application should preserve these alternatives so users can evaluate them rather than forcing an early choice or hiding disagreement.

Alternative analyses should remain traceable to their inputs, methods and configurations.

## 3.11.10 Reuse of Analytical Outputs

### Core Requirements

Allow compatible analytical outputs to be reused in subsequent investigations and scientific activities.

Relevant capabilities include:

- Using prepared datasets in later analyses.
- Reusing measurements and calculated parameters.
- Applying model outputs to comparative investigations.
- Using results from one scientific method to constrain another.
- Reusing analytical outputs across related research questions.
- Referencing results from other investigations where access permissions allow.
- Maintaining relationships between original outputs and their subsequent uses.
- Checking compatibility before applying an output to another scientific operation.
- Identifying when an output is outdated, incomplete or unsuitable for reuse.

Allow users to select which outputs are reused rather than requiring an entire analysis to be transferred.

### Design Guidance

Reusing a result should not imply that the original analysis has been repeated or that the result remains valid under every new research context.

The application should identify the origin of reused outputs and communicate relevant assumptions, limitations and compatibility requirements.

Where a reused output depends on an earlier model, configuration or dataset, that dependency should remain identifiable.

## 3.11.11 Investigation Progress and Research Status

### Core Requirements

Allow users to understand the current state and progress of an investigation.

Potential investigation statuses include:

- Planned.
- Active.
- Paused.
- Awaiting observations or additional information.
- Under analysis.
- Under evaluation.
- Completed.
- Inconclusive.
- Archived.

Allow users to change investigation status as the research develops.

Support identification of:

- Completed analytical activities.
- Unfinished work.
- Outstanding research questions.
- Missing or required observations.
- Analyses awaiting review.
- Results requiring further examination.
- Dependencies preventing subsequent work.
- Areas where additional investigation may be useful.

Allow users to record significant research decisions and changes in direction.

### Design Guidance

Investigation status should provide a useful overview of research progress without imposing a rigid sequence of required stages.

A completed investigation may still contain unresolved questions, and an investigation marked as paused may contain valid and reusable results.

Investigation status should remain distinct from the execution status of individual analytical operations and the lifecycle status of the containing project.

Project lifecycle requirements are established in Section 6.4, while analysis execution and reproducibility records are addressed in Section 5.5.

## 3.11.12 Research Notes and Scientific Documentation

### Core Requirements

Allow users to associate scientific notes and documentation with investigations, analytical activities and results.

Potential documentation includes:

- Research questions and objectives.
- Initial hypotheses and assumptions.
- Methodological decisions.
- Observational context.
- Analytical notes.
- Interpretations of results.
- Alternative explanations.
- Identified limitations.
- Relevant literature and references.
- Outstanding questions.
- Significant changes in research direction.
- Explanations of completed or abandoned approaches.

Allow notes to reference relevant datasets, analyses, outputs and findings.

Support revision of documentation while retaining appropriate historical context.

Distinguish informal research notes from structured analytical records and formal scientific conclusions.

### Design Guidance

Research documentation should help users understand why particular analytical activities were performed and how their results were interpreted.

Notes should complement the structured analysis and reproducibility records rather than replace them.

The application should allow users to document reasoning and scientific context without requiring every thought or exploratory action to become a formal research record.

## 3.11.13 Results from Multiple Analytical Activities

### Core Requirements

Support investigations in which multiple analytical activities contribute to a broader scientific result.

Relevant capabilities include:

- Combining complementary measurements.
- Comparing outputs from different analytical methods.
- Connecting observational and model-derived results.
- Relating results from different scientific disciplines.
- Examining dependencies between intermediate and final outputs.
- Identifying which analyses contribute to a particular finding.
- Preserving the individual contribution of each analytical activity.
- Allowing users to inspect the evidence supporting an integrated interpretation.
- Supporting alternative combinations of results where scientifically appropriate.

### Design Guidance

A broader scientific result may depend on multiple observations and analytical methods.

For example, an exoplanet characterization investigation may combine stellar characterization, transit measurements, radial velocity constraints, atmospheric spectroscopy and model-derived estimates.

The application should allow users to examine how each contribution supports the broader interpretation without hiding the individual results or their uncertainties.

Detailed scientific compatibility and evidence-integration requirements are established in Section 3.9.

## 3.11.14 Result Comparison and Evaluation

### Core Requirements

Allow users to compare analytical outputs and results produced within or across investigations.

Support comparisons involving:

- Different observations of the same object.
- Alternative processing and analytical methods.
- Different model configurations.
- Results produced at different times.
- Independent analyses of shared source data.
- Published reference values and findings.
- Results from different astronomical objects or systems.
- Previously recorded and newly obtained results.

Allow users to examine differences, agreements, inconsistencies and changes.

Preserve the source and method associated with each compared result.

Support identification of results that may require further investigation, validation or revision.

### Design Guidance

Result comparison should help users understand how findings depend on observations, methods, assumptions and analytical choices.

A difference between two results does not necessarily indicate that one is incorrect. It may reflect different observational coverage, processing methods, model assumptions or uncertainty.

The application should preserve these distinctions and allow users to investigate discrepancies.

Scientific validation and acceptance criteria are established in Sections 5.4 and 5.6.

## 3.11.15 Research Outputs and External Use

### Core Requirements

Allow users to make appropriate analytical outputs and documented findings available for further scientific work.

Support the preparation of outputs for:

- Subsequent analysis.
- Comparative investigations.
- Research documentation.
- Scientific visualization.
- Reporting and presentation.
- External scientific software.
- Project export and archival.
- Appropriate collaboration and sharing where permissions allow.

Allow users to select individual outputs or related groups of outputs for export or reuse.

Preserve relevant scientific context, units, metadata, provenance and relationships.

Distinguish export of an analytical result from export of its underlying data and from export of a complete reproducible analysis package.

Respect applicable access permissions and licensing restrictions.

### Design Guidance

Different research activities require different types of output.

A user may need to export a numerical table, reuse a fitted spectrum in another analysis, include a visualization in a report or preserve a complete analytical workflow for independent reproduction.

These should remain distinct operations with clear descriptions of what information is included.

Detailed visualization export is addressed in Section 3.10. Portable analysis packages and reproducibility requirements are established in Section 5.5. Project export and portability are addressed in Section 6.4.

## 3.11.16 Historical Results and Research Continuity

### Core Requirements

- Preserve previous analytical outputs and documented findings.
- Allow users to revisit earlier stages of an investigation.
- Maintain relationships between historical results and the observations and methods that produced them.
- Support revision of previous analyses without silently replacing historical outputs.
- Identify results that have been superseded or affected by updated information.
- Allow users to compare previous and revised interpretations.
- Preserve negative, inconclusive and unsuccessful research outcomes where scientifically relevant.
- Maintain access to historical results when an investigation changes direction or reaches completion.
- Preserve relevant dependencies and limitations affecting historical work.

### Design Guidance

Scientific understanding evolves as new observations become available and analytical methods improve.

The application should allow researchers to revise interpretations and produce updated results without erasing the history of how earlier conclusions were reached.

A newer result should not automatically invalidate an older result. The application should preserve the distinction between historical findings, revised interpretations and currently preferred results.

Project-level continuity and historical preservation are addressed in Section 6.4. Detailed versioning and reproducibility requirements are addressed in Section 5.5.

## 3.11.17 Future Research and Analytical Capabilities

### Core Requirements

The application should remain extensible to additional investigation and analytical-output capabilities as scientific requirements develop.

Potential future capabilities include:

- More advanced investigation planning and tracking.
- Structured research-question and hypothesis management.
- More extensive analytical dependency visualization.
- Advanced result comparison and evaluation.
- Reusable investigation structures.
- More extensive integration between independent research activities.
- Advanced scientific documentation and reporting.
- More extensive support for multidisciplinary investigations.
- Automated identification of potentially outdated or incomplete results.

### Design Guidance

Additional research-management functionality should be introduced where it provides meaningful scientific value.

The application should avoid duplicating project management, workflow automation, reproducibility or reporting systems that are established elsewhere.

The investigation framework should remain sufficiently flexible to accommodate future scientific methods without requiring unnecessary complexity in the initial implementation.

## General Principles

Research investigations, analytical outputs and results should:

- Organize related scientific activities around meaningful research questions and objectives.
- Accommodate exploratory, hypothesis-driven and comparative investigations.
- Support flexible and non-linear research development.
- Preserve relationships between observations, datasets, analytical activities and outputs.
- Treat analytical outputs as identifiable and reusable scientific resources.
- Distinguish intermediate, unfinished, completed, unsuccessful and inconclusive analyses.
- Support evidence-based interpretations and documented findings.
- Preserve alternative analytical approaches and competing interpretations.
- Allow compatible outputs to contribute to subsequent research activities.
- Support investigation progress and outstanding research questions.
- Preserve research notes and relevant scientific context.
- Connect results from multiple analytical activities.
- Support result comparison and evaluation.
- Preserve historical findings and research continuity.
- Remain integrated with project management, reproducibility, visualization and comparative analysis capabilities.

## General Principle

Scientific investigations should provide a coherent context in which individual analytical activities, their outputs and resulting interpretations contribute to broader research questions. The application should preserve these relationships, support alternative approaches and allow scientific results to be examined, reused, revised and developed as new evidence becomes available.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes the intended scientific organization and use of investigations, analytical activities, outputs and results.

It does not prescribe specific database structures, analysis engines, workflow technologies, reporting systems or implementation technologies.

The distinction between investigations and persistent project organization should be maintained:

- Section 3.11 establishes how scientific inquiries, analyses, outputs and findings relate to one another.
- Section 6.4 establishes how persistent research projects organize and preserve their materials, configurations, history and continuity.
- Section 5.5 establishes detailed analysis-level reproducibility, traceability and portable analysis requirements.

The scientific capability framework and general research workflows are established in Section 3.1. Comparative and integrated analysis is addressed in Section 3.9. Scientific visualization and presentation are addressed in Section 3.10.

Scientific interpretation, uncertainty, validation and acceptance criteria are addressed in Section 5.
