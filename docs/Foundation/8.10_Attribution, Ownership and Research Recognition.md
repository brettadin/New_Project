# 8.10 — Attribution, Ownership and Research Recognition

## Purpose

Establishes how the application recognizes scientific contributions, preserves ownership and attribution, and ensures that users receive appropriate recognition for their research and community contributions.

The application should distinguish between ownership, authorship, attribution and individual contributions.

Recognition should extend beyond traditional research publications to include observations, data collection, processing, analytical methods, modelling, software development, scientific interpretation, education, independent reproduction and community contributions.

The system should preserve original authorship and subsequent contributions when research material is shared, adapted, modified or incorporated into other investigations.

Ownership, attribution and usage permissions should remain understandable and consistent across the application's research, project, educational and community environments.

## 8.10.1 — Research Ownership and Intellectual Contributions

The application should preserve the ownership and origin of scientific material contributed by users and external sources.

Potential material includes:

- Original observations and datasets.
- Processed and derived scientific data.
- Research findings and interpretations.
- Analytical methods and workflows.
- Mathematical and computational models.
- Scientific software and custom tools.
- Educational resources and documentation.
- Visualizations and scientific presentations.
- Research reports and publications.
- Other original scientific contributions.

The application should distinguish between ownership of original material and the right to access, use, modify or redistribute it.

Using another researcher's dataset, workflow or analytical method should not automatically transfer ownership of that material.

Likewise, contributing to a collaborative investigation should not automatically establish ownership of every component or result.

Ownership and usage rights should respect applicable licensing arrangements, agreements and legal restrictions.

The application should avoid making unsupported assumptions about ownership where multiple contributors, external sources or third-party rights are involved.

## 8.10.2 — Authorship and Contributor Attribution

The application should preserve authorship and identify individual contributions to scientific work.

Relevant attribution information may include:

- Original creator or author.
- Additional contributors.
- Individual contribution descriptions.
- Dates of creation and contribution.
- Relevant affiliations where provided.
- Associated research projects.
- Supporting publications and references.
- Relationships to original and derived material.

The application should support multiple contributors without assuming that every participant made the same type or level of contribution.

For collaborative investigations, individual contributions should remain identifiable even when results are presented collectively.

The application should not automatically assume that every project member is an author of every research output.

Users should be able to identify their contributions and distinguish original authorship from subsequent participation or assistance.

## 8.10.3 — Attribution of Shared and External Resources

The application should preserve attribution to external scientific sources and resources used in research.

Potential sources include:

- Astronomical observations and datasets.
- Scientific archives and databases.
- Published research papers.
- Laboratory measurements and reference spectra.
- Molecular and atomic databases.
- Scientific software and libraries.
- External models and analytical tools.
- Educational and technical resources.

Where relevant, attribution should include:

- Original source or creator.
- Dataset or resource identifier.
- Publication or retrieval information.
- Relevant citations.
- Instrument or facility information.
- Applicable licensing and usage restrictions.

When external material is incorporated into a user's investigation, its original source should remain identifiable.

The application should distinguish between original user contributions, externally sourced material and subsequently derived results.

Attribution should remain traceable when material is transferred between workspaces, projects, analyses and exported research packages.

These requirements should remain consistent with the data provenance, metadata and source attribution requirements established in Section 4.7.

## 8.10.4 — Collaborative Research Recognition

The application should recognize contributions from multiple researchers participating in shared investigations.

Contributions may involve different scientific disciplines, research stages and responsibilities.

For example, a collaborative exoplanet investigation might involve:

- One researcher characterizing the host star.
- Another processing transit observations.
- Another developing atmospheric models.
- Another interpreting the resulting atmospheric composition.
- Additional contributors reviewing or reproducing the analysis.

The application should preserve the contributions of each participant and their relationship to the resulting research.

Potential functionality includes:

- Individual contributor records.
- Contribution descriptions.
- Links to specific datasets and analyses.
- Attribution of research outputs.
- Historical contribution records.
- Recognition of independent and collaborative work.

The application should distinguish between project membership, research participation and authorship of particular outputs.

Contribution recognition should remain flexible enough to accommodate different research structures and interdisciplinary collaboration.

## 8.10.5 — Recognition of Community Contributions

Recognition should extend beyond conventional research authorship and formal scientific publications.

The application should acknowledge different forms of valuable scientific and community participation.

Potential contributions include:

- Original astronomical observations.
- Data collection and monitoring.
- Data processing and calibration.
- Analytical methods and workflows.
- Mathematical and computational modelling.
- Scientific interpretation.
- Software and custom tools.
- Educational resources and explanations.
- Scientific discussions and knowledge exchange.
- Independent reproduction.
- Scientific review and validation.
- Community-maintained resources.
- Documentation and technical contributions.

The application should allow contributions to be identified and appropriately attributed even when they do not result in formal publications.

Different contributions should remain distinguishable rather than being reduced to a single general activity measure.

Recognition should reflect documented contributions rather than popularity, activity levels or arbitrary rankings.

For example, a user contributing an important observational dataset or independently reproducing a significant scientific result should receive appropriate recognition regardless of their overall community activity.

Popularity, follower counts, downloads and discussion activity should not be treated as measures of scientific value or credibility.

## 8.10.6 — Attribution of Adapted and Derived Work

The application should preserve attribution when users adapt, modify or build upon existing scientific material.

Potential examples include:

- Modifying another user's processing workflow.
- Adapting a published analytical method.
- Creating a derived dataset from shared observations.
- Extending a community-developed algorithm.
- Modifying a scientific model.
- Reproducing and extending another researcher's analysis.
- Adapting educational resources or documentation.

The system should preserve relationships between original material and subsequent contributions.

Where appropriate, attribution should identify:

- Original creator.
- Source material.
- Subsequent contributors.
- Nature of modifications.
- Relevant versions.
- Supporting references.
- Applicable licensing conditions.

Users should receive recognition for their own contributions without removing or obscuring the original creator's attribution.

The application should distinguish between original work, direct adaptations, independently developed alternatives and derived scientific results.

Modifications should not silently replace the original material.

## 8.10.7 — Licensing and Usage Permissions

The application should communicate relevant licensing and usage restrictions for research material shared or incorporated into investigations.

Users should be able to understand whether material can be:

- Viewed.
- Downloaded.
- Used in scientific analysis.
- Modified.
- Incorporated into other research.
- Redistributed.
- Included in derivative work.
- Shared publicly.

The application should preserve relevant licensing information when material is imported, shared, adapted or exported.

Potential licensing considerations include:

- Original creator permissions.
- Third-party dataset restrictions.
- Software and library licences.
- Publication copyright.
- Commercial and non-commercial usage.
- Redistribution and derivative-work restrictions.
- Attribution requirements.

Public accessibility should not automatically be interpreted as permission to redistribute or modify material.

Where licensing is unclear or unavailable, the application should communicate that uncertainty rather than assuming unrestricted usage.

These requirements should build upon the licensing and usage safeguards established in Section 4.11.

## 8.10.8 — Research Identity and Public Recognition

The application should connect scientific contributions to user profiles and research identities.

Users should be able to associate their contributions with their chosen public identity.

This should accommodate:

- Real names.
- Pseudonyms.
- Usernames.
- Professional research identities.
- Optional institutional affiliations.
- External researcher profiles.
- Verified credentials where supported.

Users should retain control over which identity information is publicly displayed.

The application should distinguish between account identity, public display identity and independently verified credentials.

Pseudonymous users should still be able to receive consistent attribution for their contributions without publicly revealing their real identities.

Changes to public profile information should not silently remove historical attribution from previous contributions.

These requirements should remain consistent with the identity and privacy provisions established in Section 8.2.

## 8.10.9 — Attribution History and Contribution Records

The application should maintain historical records of authorship, contributions and significant modifications.

Relevant information may include:

- Original creator and contributors.
- Contribution dates.
- Changes to authorship or contributor information.
- Modifications to shared material.
- Adaptations and derived versions.
- Research participation.
- Relevant project associations.
- Licensing and attribution changes where applicable.

Users should be able to inspect contribution histories and identify how scientific material developed over time.

Attribution should remain connected to relevant datasets, analyses, workflows, models, educational resources and research outputs.

Historical contribution records should be preserved when material is reorganized, transferred between projects or exported.

Corrections to attribution should be possible, but significant changes should remain traceable.

The application should distinguish original contributions from later modifications, reproductions and independent evaluations.

## Future Possibilities

The following capabilities may be investigated as the application develops but are not initial implementation priorities.

### Detailed Contribution Statements

The application may eventually support more structured contribution records identifying individual responsibilities within collaborative investigations.

Potential categories include:

- Conceptualization and research design.
- Data collection and observation.
- Data processing and curation.
- Software development.
- Mathematical and computational modelling.
- Formal analysis.
- Scientific interpretation.
- Visualization.
- Documentation and writing.
- Scientific review and validation.
- Project coordination.

Standardized contributor taxonomies could be investigated where useful.

### Research Identity Integration

Potential integrations include:

- ORCID.
- External researcher profiles.
- Institutional research identities.
- Publication and citation databases.
- External research repositories.

Such integrations should remain optional and respect user privacy.

### Exportable Contribution Histories

Users may eventually be able to export records of their scientific contributions for:

- Academic portfolios.
- Research applications.
- Professional profiles.
- Publication documentation.
- Institutional reporting.
- Personal research records.

### Advanced Community Recognition

The application may eventually provide more extensive recognition of scientific contributions across the community.

Potential capabilities include:

- Recognition of independent reproduction.
- Recognition of scientific review.
- Recognition of observational contributions.
- Recognition of educational and technical contributions.
- Contribution histories across multiple research projects.
- Community-maintained scientific resources.

Recognition should remain evidence-based and should not become a popularity or activity ranking system.

### External Publication and Repository Integration

Future integration with publication platforms and research repositories may support more complete attribution and contribution records.

The application should preserve the distinction between internal research contributions, formal publication authorship and external repository records.

## Initial Implementation Priorities

The initial implementation should establish reliable attribution, ownership and contribution tracking without requiring an extensive researcher identity or recognition platform.

Priority capabilities include:

- Preserving original ownership and source information.
- Identifying creators and contributors.
- Supporting multiple contributors.
- Attributing external datasets and publications.
- Preserving attribution when material is adapted or derived.
- Maintaining basic contribution histories.
- Respecting licensing and usage permissions.
- Connecting contributions to user profiles.
- Supporting pseudonymous attribution.
- Recognizing different types of scientific and community contributions.
- Preserving attribution through projects, exports and shared resources.

Recognition should be based on identifiable contributions rather than popularity, activity levels or arbitrary numerical rankings.

## Implementation Considerations

Attribution and research recognition should integrate with the application's existing project, collaboration, provenance, resource-sharing and reproducibility systems.

Particular attention should be given to:

- Distinguishing ownership from attribution.
- Preserving original creators and subsequent contributors.
- Supporting interdisciplinary collaboration.
- Maintaining relationships between original and derived material.
- Respecting licensing and third-party rights.
- Supporting pseudonymous participation.
- Preserving historical attribution.
- Recognizing contributions beyond formal publication.
- Avoiding misleading measures of scientific value or credibility.

The application should establish a consistent attribution foundation that can accommodate more advanced contribution tracking and external researcher identity integration as it develops.

**General Principle:** Ownership, attribution and research recognition should preserve the origin and development of scientific contributions throughout the application. Users should receive appropriate recognition for their actual contributions, including research, observations, analytical methods, software, education, independent reproduction and scientific review. Recognition should be transparent and evidence-based rather than determined by popularity, activity levels or arbitrary rankings.
