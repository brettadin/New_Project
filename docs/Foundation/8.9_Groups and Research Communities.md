# 8.9 — Groups and Research Communities

## Purpose

Establishes how users can create, join and participate in organized scientific communities within the application.

The application should support groups formed around shared scientific interests, astronomical subjects, educational activities and collaborative research objectives.

Groups should accommodate both informal communities and more structured research organizations without requiring every group to follow the same organizational model.

They should provide spaces for discussion, knowledge exchange, shared resources and research activities while integrating with the application's existing project, community and collaboration systems.

## 8.9.1 — Scientific Groups and Communities

The application should support organized groups of users with shared scientific interests or research objectives.

Potential group types include:

- Astronomy and astrophysics communities.
- Exoplanet research groups.
- Planetary science communities.
- Stellar astronomy and observation groups.
- Spectroscopy and astrochemistry communities.
- Computational astronomy groups.
- Amateur astronomy communities.
- Citizen science initiatives.
- Educational and student groups.
- Specialized research collaborations.
- Communities dedicated to particular astronomical objects or systems.

Groups should be able to accommodate different levels of scientific knowledge and experience.

Some groups may exist primarily for informal discussion and shared interests, while others may organize substantial scientific investigations.

The group system should remain flexible enough to accommodate both.

## 8.9.2 — Group Creation and Management

Users should eventually be able to establish and manage their own scientific groups.

Group creators should be able to:

- Define the group's name and purpose.
- Provide a description of its scientific interests or objectives.
- Select relevant scientific subjects and categories.
- Establish membership and access requirements.
- Choose group visibility.
- Appoint additional administrators or moderators.
- Organize group discussions and resources.
- Manage membership and group settings.
- Update group information as its purpose evolves.

Group creation should not require professional scientific credentials or institutional affiliation.

Groups should be able to develop their organization over time without unnecessarily restricting their activities.

## 8.9.3 — Group Membership and Participation

The application should support different forms of group participation.

Users should be able to:

- Discover relevant groups.
- Join public groups.
- Request membership in restricted groups.
- Accept invitations to private groups.
- Leave groups.
- Review their existing memberships.
- Participate in discussions and group activities.
- Access resources according to their permissions.

Membership requirements should be determined by individual groups where appropriate.

Groups should be able to accommodate casual participation as well as more substantial research involvement.

Participation should not automatically require users to disclose their real identities, professional qualifications or institutional affiliations.

Membership should also remain separate from access to private research projects and datasets unless appropriate permissions have been explicitly granted.

## 8.9.4 — Subject-Specific and Discipline-Based Groups

Groups should be capable of organizing communities around particular scientific disciplines and specialized subjects.

Examples include:

- Exoplanetary science.
- Planetary atmospheres.
- Stellar characterization.
- Spectroscopy and molecular identification.
- Astrochemistry.
- Transit photometry.
- Atmospheric retrieval.
- Radiative transfer.
- Observational astronomy.
- Computational and theoretical astronomy.
- Galactic astronomy and cosmology.

Groups may focus on broad scientific disciplines or narrower specialized topics.

Users should be able to discover communities relevant to their research interests and experience.

The application should support interdisciplinary groups where multiple scientific areas contribute to a shared subject or investigation.

## 8.9.5 — Object-Focused Research Groups

The application should support groups dedicated to individual astronomical objects, systems or specific research subjects.

Potential examples include:

- Jupiter and its atmosphere.
- Saturn and its natural satellites.
- The Sun and solar observations.
- Particular stars and their planetary systems.
- Known exoplanets.
- Specific exoplanetary systems.
- Objects undergoing ongoing observational monitoring.

Object-focused groups could organize discussions, observations, publications, datasets, analyses and relevant research projects.

Users should be able to navigate between a group and its associated astronomical objects and research material.

These groups should complement the application's scientific workspaces and object-management capabilities rather than replacing them.

## 8.9.6 — Research and Collaboration Groups

Groups should be capable of supporting organized scientific research and collaborative activities.

Potential functionality includes:

- Organizing research objectives.
- Connecting members to shared investigations.
- Associating existing research projects.
- Organizing relevant datasets and observations.
- Sharing analytical methods and workflows.
- Discussing research findings.
- Coordinating observational activities.
- Exchanging scientific references.
- Preserving research documentation.

Research groups should integrate with the collaborative project system established in Section 8.4.

The group should provide the broader community and organizational environment, while individual research projects retain their own scientific structure, history, ownership and access permissions.

Groups should not automatically gain access to every project or research resource belonging to their members.

## 8.9.7 — Educational and Learning Groups

The application should support groups organized around scientific education and shared learning.

Potential examples include:

- Students studying astronomy or astrophysics.
- Beginners learning astronomical concepts.
- Amateur astronomers developing research skills.
- Users learning spectroscopy and photometry.
- Groups studying planetary and exoplanetary science.
- Communities working through educational courses.
- Researchers sharing knowledge about specialized analytical methods.

Educational groups should be able to organize:

- Discussions and questions.
- Reading lists and scientific references.
- Educational resources.
- Shared exercises and activities.
- Learning paths and course material.
- Practical demonstrations.
- Study activities and group projects.

These capabilities should complement the educational systems established in Section 7 rather than duplicating the scientific reference library or structured learning infrastructure.

## 8.9.8 — Group Resources and Shared Materials

Groups should be able to organize and access relevant scientific and educational resources.

Potential resources include:

- Discussions and announcements.
- Scientific publications and references.
- Shared datasets and observations.
- Research projects and investigations.
- Analytical workflows and methods.
- Models and simulations.
- Scientific visualizations.
- Educational material.
- Documentation and group guidelines.
- Links to relevant external resources.

Resources should retain their original ownership, provenance, attribution and access permissions.

Associating a resource with a group should not automatically transfer ownership or grant unrestricted access.

Groups should be able to organize resources without unnecessarily duplicating underlying datasets or research material.

The application should preserve relationships between shared group resources and their original scientific sources.

## 8.9.9 — Group Administration and Permissions

Groups should support understandable administrative roles and membership permissions.

Potential roles include:

- **Group owner:** Establishes the group and retains overall ownership and management authority.
- **Administrator:** Manages group settings, membership and organizational responsibilities.
- **Moderator:** Helps manage discussions and community conduct.
- **Member:** Participates in group activities and accesses permitted resources.
- **Guest or observer:** Accesses selected public or restricted material where permitted.

The exact permission structure should remain adaptable to different group types.

Group administrators should be able to:

- Manage membership.
- Assign or change administrative roles.
- Update group information.
- Manage group visibility.
- Establish participation requirements.
- Moderate group discussions.
- Organize shared resources.
- Remove members where justified.
- Transfer administrative responsibilities where appropriate.

Group permissions must remain consistent with the application's broader privacy and access-control requirements.

Membership should not automatically grant access to private projects, unpublished observations or other restricted research material.

Detailed moderation policies and enforcement mechanisms are addressed separately in Section 8.13.

## 8.9.10 — Group Discovery and Networking

Users should be able to discover scientific groups relevant to their interests and research activities.

Potential discovery criteria include:

- Scientific discipline.
- Astronomical subject or object.
- Research topic.
- Analytical method.
- Educational purpose.
- Group activity.
- Group visibility.
- Membership requirements.

Group descriptions should communicate their purpose, interests and participation expectations.

Users should be able to inspect relevant information before joining.

The application may eventually recommend groups based on users' declared scientific interests, research activities and selected preferences.

Recommendations should remain optional and understandable.

Group popularity should not automatically imply scientific credibility or research quality.

## 8.9.11 — Group Continuity and Organization

Groups should be able to maintain their identity, organization and useful resources over extended periods.

The application should support:

- Persistent group information.
- Historical discussions and shared resources.
- Membership and administrative history.
- Preservation of important announcements.
- Organization of ongoing research activities.
- Retrieval of previous educational material.
- Continuation of long-term observational or research initiatives.
- Appropriate handling of inactive groups.

Groups should remain useful even when individual members become inactive.

Where group ownership or administration changes, relevant history and attribution should be preserved.

The application should allow groups to evolve, reorganize or become inactive without automatically deleting their historical scientific contributions.

## Initial Implementation Priorities

The initial implementation should establish the foundations for scientific groups without requiring an extensive social networking or organizational platform.

Priority capabilities include:

- Creation and management of groups.
- Support for informal interest-based and structured research groups.
- Group descriptions and scientific categories.
- Public and private group visibility.
- Basic membership management.
- Basic administrative roles and permissions.
- Group discussions and shared resources.
- Links to relevant scientific subjects and astronomical objects.
- Associations with research projects and investigations.
- Basic group discovery and search.
- Preservation of group history and resources.
- Appropriate privacy and access controls.

The initial implementation should allow groups to organize scientific interaction while relying on the existing discussion, project, resource and collaboration systems.

A complex group-management or institutional collaboration platform is not required initially.

## Implementation Considerations

The group system should integrate with the application's existing community, research collaboration, educational and resource-sharing capabilities.

Particular attention should be given to:

- Supporting both informal communities and structured research groups.
- Avoiding unnecessary duplication of project-management functionality.
- Preserving ownership and attribution of shared resources.
- Maintaining independent project and group permissions.
- Supporting pseudonymous participation.
- Providing understandable administrative responsibilities.
- Preserving group history and continuity.
- Accommodating groups with different scientific purposes and organizational needs.

The underlying system should remain flexible enough to support more advanced research organizations and collaboration capabilities as the application develops.

**General Principle:** Scientific groups should provide organized environments for users to connect through shared interests, learning activities and research objectives. The system should accommodate informal communities and structured research collaborations while preserving individual privacy, research ownership, resource provenance and administrative control.
