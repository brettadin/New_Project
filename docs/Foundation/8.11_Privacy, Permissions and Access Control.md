# 8.11 — Privacy, Permissions and Access Control

## Purpose

Establishes how the application protects user information, research material and private interactions while allowing users to control what they share and with whom.

Privacy and access control should be fundamental application-wide requirements rather than isolated features implemented independently in individual workspaces or community systems.

Users should retain control over their personal information, research projects, datasets, observations, analyses, educational material and community participation.

The application should provide understandable permission systems, secure data handling, explicit sharing authorization and consistent access controls.

Privacy should be maintained across scientific workspaces, research projects, educational environments, community features, collaborative activities and external integrations.

## 8.11.1 — Privacy Principles and User Control

### Functional Requirements

- Treat user privacy and research confidentiality as fundamental application requirements.
- Give users control over their personal information and research material.
- Do not publicly expose private information or research without explicit authorization.
- Do not share user data with other users, groups or external services without appropriate authorization.
- Minimize the collection and storage of personal information.
- Collect only information necessary for the application's functionality.
- Make data handling and privacy practices understandable.
- Apply consistent privacy principles throughout the application.
- Allow users to inspect and manage relevant privacy settings.
- Avoid unnecessary exposure of information through indirect relationships, integrations or community features.
- Preserve user control when information moves between workspaces, projects and other application environments.

### Design Guidance

Privacy should not depend on users understanding complicated technical systems.

Important decisions about sharing, visibility and access should be presented clearly and at the appropriate point in a workflow.

The application should avoid unnecessary data collection and should not treat user participation in community or collaborative features as permission to expose unrelated personal or research information.

## 8.11.2 — Default Privacy and Visibility

### Functional Requirements

- Research projects must be private by default.
- Unpublished observations, datasets and analyses must remain private unless explicitly shared.
- Personal notes and private educational material must remain private by default.
- User profiles should have privacy controls, with private or minimally visible defaults where appropriate.
- Private discussions and communications must not become publicly accessible without authorization.
- Group membership must not automatically expose unrelated research material.
- Connecting an external service must not automatically authorize access to unrelated user information.
- Public sharing must require an explicit user action.
- Users must be able to understand the visibility of their material before sharing it.
- Changes to privacy defaults must not silently expose existing private material.

### Design Guidance

The application should distinguish between private, selectively shared and publicly accessible material.

Users should not have to repeatedly investigate whether their research is private.

Visibility should be identifiable through clear interface indicators and understandable settings.

## 8.11.3 — Research and Data Access Permissions

### Functional Requirements

- Support access permissions for research projects, datasets, observations, analyses, models, visualizations, documents and other scientific resources.
- Allow users to grant access to individual users or selected groups.
- Support different levels of access, such as viewing, contributing and managing.
- Allow users to share selected project components without necessarily exposing the entire project.
- Preserve the distinction between original data and derived or processed material.
- Allow independent permission management for different research resources where appropriate.
- Ensure access to a resource does not automatically grant access to unrelated resources.
- Preserve existing permissions when research material is reorganized where appropriate.
- Ensure permissions remain consistent when material is accessed through different workspaces or application features.
- Prevent unauthorized access through indirect resource relationships or shared references.

### Design Guidance

Permissions should accommodate different research arrangements without becoming unnecessarily complicated.

For example, a researcher might:

- Share an entire project with a collaborator.
- Allow a group to view selected observations.
- Give another researcher permission to contribute to a specific analysis.
- Share a visualization without exposing its underlying private dataset.
- Provide access to processed data while retaining control over original observations.

The application should make the scope of granted access clear.

## 8.11.4 — Sharing and Publication Permissions

### Functional Requirements

- Require explicit authorization before sharing research or personal material.
- Support private, selected-user, selected-group and public sharing.
- Allow users to choose which components of a research project are shared.
- Distinguish between sharing access to original material and sharing copies or derived versions.
- Make sharing permissions and applicable restrictions clear.
- Allow users to review sharing settings before confirming publication or distribution.
- Respect ownership, attribution and licensing requirements.
- Identify when shared material contains restricted or externally licensed resources.
- Avoid automatically publishing private research when creating public-facing outputs.
- Ensure exporting material does not unintentionally change its access permissions or expose unrelated private information.

### Design Guidance

Sharing and formal publication should remain distinct activities.

Users should be able to share preliminary research, selected findings or individual scientific resources without necessarily publishing an entire investigation.

The application should clearly communicate the difference between making material visible within a selected collaboration and making it publicly available.

## 8.11.5 — Collaborative Access and User Roles

### Functional Requirements

- Support different permission levels for collaborators and group members.
- Allow project and group administrators to assign appropriate roles.
- Distinguish ownership, administration, contribution and viewing permissions.
- Allow permissions to be assigned to individual users or groups.
- Ensure group membership does not automatically grant access to every associated research project.
- Allow project owners to maintain control over their research material.
- Support changes to collaborator roles and responsibilities.
- Preserve appropriate access restrictions when users leave groups or collaborative projects.
- Maintain consistent permissions across collaborative workspaces and associated resources.
- Ensure administrative privileges are limited to their intended scope.

### Design Guidance

Collaborative permissions should integrate with the systems established in Sections 8.4 and 8.9.

The application should avoid duplicating permission systems while ensuring that project ownership, group administration and individual resource permissions remain distinguishable.

A user may be an administrator of a group without automatically becoming an administrator of every research project associated with that group.

## 8.11.6 — Personal Information and Profile Privacy

### Functional Requirements

- Allow users to control the visibility of personal profile information.
- Support private, selected-user and public profile visibility where appropriate.
- Allow users to determine which profile fields are publicly displayed.
- Keep account credentials and sensitive account information private.
- Support pseudonymous participation.
- Avoid requiring real names, institutional affiliations or professional credentials unless specifically necessary for an external process.
- Prevent private account information from appearing in public research contributions.
- Allow users to modify their public identity without silently removing historical contribution attribution.
- Make relevant profile and privacy settings accessible.
- Avoid exposing personal information through community participation, research sharing or external integrations without authorization.

### Design Guidance

Account identity, public research identity and independently verified credentials should remain distinct.

Users should be able to participate in scientific communities and collaborative research without unnecessarily disclosing personal information.

These requirements should remain consistent with the profile and research identity provisions in Section 8.2.

## 8.11.7 — External Integrations and Account Security

### Functional Requirements

- Protect credentials, authentication tokens, API keys and other sensitive authentication information.
- Avoid hardcoding credentials or exposing them in application code, logs, error messages or exported files.
- Request only the permissions necessary for an external integration.
- Minimize information transmitted to external services.
- Make external data access and information exchange understandable to users.
- Do not send private research material to external services without appropriate authorization.
- Securely manage external authentication and authorization.
- Allow users to disconnect external integrations and revoke authorization.
- Identify relevant external account requirements, including API keys, registration and institutional access.
- Evaluate the privacy and security practices of external services before integration.
- Avoid storing unnecessary external account information.
- Protect sensitive information during transmission and storage.
- Handle authentication failures without exposing credentials or private information.

### Design Guidance

External integrations should follow the privacy and security principles established in Section 4.

The application should not assume that connecting an external scientific service grants unrestricted permission to access or transmit all user data.

Where external services require authentication, users should understand what information is accessed, why it is needed and how authorization can be withdrawn.

## 8.11.8 — Data Storage, Retention and Deletion

### Functional Requirements

- Give users appropriate control over stored personal information and research material.
- Support deletion of unwanted user-created material.
- Allow users to remove external integrations and associated authorization.
- Provide understandable information about what data is stored and retained.
- Avoid unnecessary retention of personal information.
- Preserve relevant scientific provenance and attribution when research material is retained or shared.
- Identify dependencies before deleting material used by other projects or collaborators.
- Communicate potential consequences of deleting shared or referenced resources.
- Support exporting relevant research material before account or project deletion.
- Handle backups, cached information and retained copies according to documented retention policies.
- Distinguish between deleting original material, removing access and deleting independently created copies.
- Respect applicable legal, licensing and retention requirements.

### Design Guidance

Deleting research material can affect other investigations, derived datasets and collaborative work.

The application should identify relevant dependencies and explain potential consequences before destructive actions.

Where material is shared or incorporated into another user's research, the application should distinguish between removing access to the original resource and deleting independent copies or derived material.

Historical research relationships should be handled carefully without silently corrupting other users' investigations.

## 8.11.9 — Permission Management and Access Revocation

### Functional Requirements

- Allow users to inspect who has access to their research material.
- Provide an accessible overview of active sharing permissions.
- Allow users to modify or revoke previously granted permissions.
- Support revoking individual access and group-level access where appropriate.
- Communicate the scope and consequences of permission changes.
- Ensure revoked permissions are no longer honored by application-controlled access mechanisms.
- Identify when access is inherited through group or project relationships.
- Avoid retaining unnecessary access after a user leaves a group or collaboration.
- Provide appropriate controls for project owners and group administrators.
- Preserve historical records of significant permission changes where appropriate.
- Ensure permission changes are applied consistently across relevant workspaces and resources.

### Design Guidance

Users should be able to understand and manage access without searching through unrelated settings.

The application should clearly distinguish between:

- Access to the original resource.
- Access through a project or group.
- Independently downloaded or exported copies.
- Derived material created by another user.

Revoking access to an original resource cannot necessarily retrieve copies already downloaded or exported by other users.

This limitation should be communicated clearly.

## 8.11.10 — Privacy Transparency and Activity Records

### Functional Requirements

- Make relevant data-handling practices understandable.
- Identify when research material is shared, published or made accessible to others.
- Provide visibility into active external integrations and their permissions.
- Record significant permission changes where appropriate.
- Identify relevant sharing and access events.
- Make it possible to review current sharing arrangements.
- Communicate when changes to permissions may affect collaborators or dependent research.
- Distinguish ordinary application activity from significant security or privacy events.
- Avoid unnecessary collection of detailed user activity.
- Protect activity records from unauthorized access.

### Design Guidance

Transparency should help users understand what is happening to their information without creating unnecessary surveillance or excessive notifications.

Activity records should serve security, accountability and research-continuity purposes rather than becoming a mechanism for monitoring every user action.

## 8.11.11 — Security and Privacy Incident Handling

### Functional Requirements

- Establish procedures for identifying and responding to security and privacy incidents.
- Identify unauthorized access, unintended data exposure and compromised authentication.
- Provide appropriate user communication when an incident affects their information or research.
- Support investigation and documentation of relevant incidents.
- Provide mechanisms for revoking compromised credentials or external authorizations.
- Support corrective actions and recovery where technically possible.
- Preserve relevant evidence needed to investigate incidents.
- Avoid exposing additional sensitive information during incident handling.
- Establish procedures for reporting suspected vulnerabilities.
- Maintain security practices as the application and its external integrations evolve.

### Design Guidance

Security and privacy should be considered throughout development, deployment and maintenance.

Incident handling should prioritize limiting exposure, protecting affected users, identifying causes and preventing recurrence.

Detailed response procedures and security architecture should be established during technical planning.

## Initial Implementation Priorities

The initial implementation should establish a consistent application-wide privacy and access-control foundation.

Priority capabilities include:

- Private-by-default research projects and personal material.
- Explicit authorization before sharing.
- Understandable visibility settings.
- Basic permissions for individual users and groups.
- Distinct viewing, contributing and management permissions.
- Protection of personal profile and account information.
- Consistent permissions across workspaces, projects and community resources.
- Secure handling of external credentials and integrations.
- Appropriate controls for stored data and deletion.
- Access review and revocation.
- Clear communication of sharing and privacy settings.
- Basic records of significant permission changes.
- Protection against unintended data exposure.

The initial implementation should prioritize reliable privacy and security foundations over extensive administrative complexity.

## Implementation Considerations

Privacy and access control should be established as shared application infrastructure rather than implemented independently within each feature.

The underlying permission system should accommodate:

- Individual users.
- Research projects.
- Scientific datasets and resources.
- Collaborative investigations.
- Community groups.
- Educational resources.
- Shared workflows and analyses.
- Public research contributions.
- External integrations.

Particular attention should be given to:

- Consistent permission enforcement across the application.
- Avoiding unintended access through indirect relationships.
- Maintaining separate ownership and access permissions.
- Preserving privacy when material moves between workspaces.
- Handling shared and derived resources correctly.
- Secure credential management.
- Minimizing personal information collection and retention.
- Clear access revocation and deletion behavior.
- Appropriate handling of external services and third-party resources.

The technical implementation should be investigated during architecture planning, with particular attention to security, scalability, maintainability and data protection.

**General Principle:** Users must retain meaningful control over their personal information, research material and interactions throughout the application. Privacy should be the default, sharing should require explicit authorization, and access permissions should be consistent, understandable and enforceable across every application environment. Security and data protection must be fundamental architectural requirements rather than features added after development.
