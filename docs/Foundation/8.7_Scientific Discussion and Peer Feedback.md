# 8.7 — Scientific Discussion and Peer Feedback

## Purpose

Establishes how users can discuss scientific subjects, exchange knowledge, question research findings and provide constructive feedback within the application community.

The application should support meaningful scientific discussions across different disciplines, experience levels and research interests.

Users should be able to discuss general scientific subjects, ask questions, examine research findings, challenge interpretations, compare analytical approaches and exchange evidence-based feedback.

Discussions should be capable of connecting directly to relevant astronomical objects, datasets, observations, analyses, publications, research projects and scientific workflows.

The system should encourage constructive scientific examination and knowledge exchange without requiring agreement between participants or treating differing scientific interpretations as misconduct.

## 8.7.1 — Scientific Discussions and Research Questions

The application should provide spaces for users to discuss scientific subjects and ask questions.

Discussions should accommodate different levels of scientific knowledge and experience, including beginners, students, amateur astronomers, educators and experienced researchers.

Potential discussion subjects include:

- General astronomy and astrophysics.
- Exoplanetary science.
- Planetary and stellar astronomy.
- Spectroscopy and astrochemistry.
- Atmospheric characterization.
- Photometry and transit observations.
- Observational techniques and instrumentation.
- Mathematical and computational methods.
- Scientific models and simulations.
- Published research and emerging discoveries.
- Open scientific questions and unresolved problems.

Users should be able to ask both introductory and technically advanced questions.

Discussion spaces should support explanations, clarification, follow-up questions, references and the exchange of relevant scientific information.

The application should accommodate both focused technical discussions and broader scientific conversations.

## 8.7.2 — Discussion of Shared Research

Users should be able to discuss research material shared through the application.

Potential discussion subjects include:

- Research findings and interpretations.
- Original and processed observations.
- Spectroscopic and photometric analyses.
- Atmospheric models and retrieval results.
- Scientific visualizations.
- Analytical methods and workflows.
- Research hypotheses and preliminary conclusions.
- Uncertainties and unresolved questions.
- Comparisons with published findings.

Discussions should be capable of linking directly to the relevant research material.

Users should be able to ask questions, request clarification, provide observations and discuss the scientific implications of shared work.

This functionality should accommodate both completed investigations and ongoing or preliminary research.

Discussion participants should be able to distinguish established findings from developing interpretations and unfinished work.

Access to discussions and associated research material should respect the permissions established by the relevant project or resource owner.

## 8.7.3 — Contextual Discussions and Scientific Relationships

Discussions should be connectable to specific scientific subjects and research materials rather than existing exclusively within general community forums.

Potential associations include:

- Astronomical objects and planetary or stellar systems.
- Datasets and observations.
- Spectra and photometric measurements.
- Individual spectral features or measurements.
- Scientific analyses and model outputs.
- Publications and scientific references.
- Research projects and investigations.
- Analytical methods and processing workflows.
- Scientific tools and application capabilities.

For example, a user investigating Jupiter should be able to discover discussions associated with Jupiter's atmosphere, spectroscopy, observational datasets, existing research and related scientific questions.

Users should be able to access relevant discussions from appropriate scientific contexts and navigate between the discussion and its associated research material.

The application should also support discovering discussions independently through community search and navigation.

Relationships between discussions and scientific resources should be preserved when relevant material is reorganized or updated.

This contextual approach should make scientific discussions useful as a long-term knowledge resource rather than allowing valuable exchanges to become buried in general conversations.

## 8.7.4 — Scientific Interpretations and Alternative Explanations

The application should encourage users to examine different scientific interpretations and consider alternative explanations for observations and research findings.

Users should be able to:

- Question existing interpretations.
- Propose alternative explanations.
- Identify potentially overlooked evidence.
- Challenge assumptions.
- Compare competing scientific models.
- Discuss possible observational or instrumental effects.
- Identify potential processing artifacts.
- Examine conflicting measurements.
- Suggest additional analyses or observations.
- Discuss unresolved scientific questions.

Discussions should support evidence-based examination rather than simply encouraging agreement with existing conclusions.

Participants should be able to explain the reasoning behind their interpretations and identify supporting or contradictory evidence.

The application should accommodate uncertainty and disagreement without requiring every discussion to reach a definitive conclusion.

Different interpretations should not automatically be treated as equally supported. Participants should be able to examine the available evidence and explain differences in scientific support.

These principles should remain consistent with the scientific interpretation and uncertainty requirements established in Section 5.4.

## 8.7.5 — Peer Feedback and Constructive Criticism

Users should be able to provide constructive feedback on research shared through the application.

Feedback may concern:

- Data quality and observational limitations.
- Processing decisions.
- Analytical methodologies.
- Model assumptions and configurations.
- Interpretation of scientific results.
- Statistical reasoning.
- Uncertainties and confidence.
- Scientific references.
- Reproducibility.
- Alternative analytical approaches.
- Potential errors or inconsistencies.

Users should be able to explain their feedback and provide supporting evidence or references where appropriate.

Feedback should encourage improvement and critical examination rather than personal criticism.

Researchers should retain control over whether and how they incorporate feedback into their investigations.

Providing feedback should not automatically modify another user's research material or conclusions.

Significant changes resulting from feedback should remain attributable to the user who made those changes.

Community feedback should be distinguished from formal scientific review, independent reproduction and scientific validation, which are addressed separately in Section 8.8.

## 8.7.6 — Evidence-Based Discussion and Scientific References

The application should support discussions grounded in scientific evidence and relevant research material.

Users should be able to reference:

- Peer-reviewed scientific publications.
- Preprints and research papers.
- Astronomical databases and catalogues.
- Observational datasets.
- Scientific measurements.
- Spectral and photometric reference material.
- Mathematical and physical models.
- Analytical methods and software.
- Relevant educational and technical documentation.

Where practical, users should be able to link references directly rather than relying exclusively on manually entered descriptions.

Discussions should support distinguishing between:

- Direct observational evidence.
- Processed measurements.
- Derived analytical results.
- Model predictions.
- Published interpretations.
- Preliminary findings.
- Personal hypotheses and opinions.

The presence of a reference should not automatically imply that a claim is correct or that the cited material supports the interpretation being discussed.

Users should be encouraged to examine original sources and evaluate the relevance and limitations of supporting evidence.

## 8.7.7 — Questions, Responses and Knowledge Exchange

The application should support ongoing exchanges between users.

Potential functionality includes:

- Questions and responses.
- Follow-up questions.
- Requests for clarification.
- Explanations of scientific concepts.
- Sharing relevant references.
- Suggestions for further investigation.
- Corrections and additional information.
- Discussion summaries where useful.
- Connections to related discussions.

Users should be able to revisit previous exchanges and continue discussions as new information becomes available.

Discussions should accommodate both straightforward questions and extended technical exchanges.

The application should avoid unnecessarily restricting scientific conversations to rigid question-and-answer formats.

Users should be able to exchange knowledge and develop discussions naturally while retaining sufficient organization for later discovery.

## 8.7.8 — Discussion Organization and Continuity

Scientific discussions should remain accessible and organized over time.

Potential functionality includes:

- Categorizing discussions by scientific discipline.
- Associating discussions with astronomical objects.
- Linking discussions to datasets and projects.
- Searching previous discussions.
- Filtering by topic, date or subject.
- Finding discussions associated with particular scientific methods or tools.
- Following discussions of interest.
- Revisiting previous questions and explanations.
- Identifying related discussions.
- Preserving discussion history.

Users should be able to discover existing discussions before creating new ones where relevant.

The application should support long-term discussion continuity, particularly when scientific questions develop as new observations, publications or analytical results become available.

Discussions should not disappear simply because a particular investigation has been completed or a user is no longer actively participating.

Where associated research material becomes unavailable or changes, the application should preserve the discussion history and communicate relevant limitations where practical.

## 8.7.9 — Discussion Attribution and Contribution History

The application should preserve authorship and relevant contribution history within scientific discussions.

Relevant information may include:

- Original discussion creator.
- Individual contributors.
- Dates of contributions.
- Edited or corrected responses.
- Added references and supporting material.
- Significant discussion updates.
- Relationships to associated research material.

Users should be able to identify who contributed particular explanations, interpretations or feedback.

Pseudonymous participation should remain supported in accordance with Section 8.2.

Discussion attribution should respect user privacy settings and should not require participants to reveal their real identities or professional affiliations.

Significant corrections or modifications should remain distinguishable from original contributions where appropriate.

## 8.7.10 — Responsible Scientific Disagreement

The application should support respectful disagreement and critical scientific discussion.

Scientific disagreement should not automatically be treated as inappropriate behaviour.

Users should be able to challenge interpretations, question methods, disagree with conclusions and propose competing explanations.

Community expectations should distinguish between:

- Legitimate scientific disagreement.
- Reasoned criticism and alternative interpretations.
- Unsupported scientific claims.
- Deliberate misrepresentation of evidence.
- Personal attacks and harassment.
- Inappropriate or abusive behaviour.

Discussions should prioritize scientific reasoning, supporting evidence and constructive communication.

Moderation should address inappropriate conduct without unnecessarily suppressing legitimate scientific debate.

Detailed moderation policies and enforcement mechanisms are addressed separately in Section 8.13.

## Future Possibilities

The following capabilities may be investigated as the application develops but are not initial implementation priorities.

### Advanced Discussion Communities

- More specialized discussion spaces for individual scientific subjects.
- Expert-led discussions and question sessions.
- Organized scientific debates.
- Research challenges and open scientific questions.
- Community-led problem-solving activities.
- Advanced topic and discussion subscriptions.

### Research and Knowledge Exchange

- Community-organized investigations into unresolved scientific questions.
- Collaborative examination of competing interpretations.
- Discussions associated with ongoing observational campaigns.
- More extensive integration with scientific publications and external research communities.
- Advanced discussion search and related-topic recommendations.
- Searchable collections of particularly useful scientific explanations and discussions.

### Mentorship and Learning

- Research mentorship and guidance.
- Student and amateur researcher support.
- Advanced scientific question sessions.
- Structured knowledge exchange between different experience levels.
- Connections between educational discussions and relevant learning resources.

### Additional Future Capabilities

- More advanced discussion organization and filtering.
- Optional discussion summaries.
- Contextual recommendations for related scientific discussions.
- Improved tools for identifying unanswered questions.
- More extensive discussion archiving and knowledge management.

These possibilities should be evaluated according to their scientific value, usefulness and practical demand.

## Initial Implementation Priorities

The initial implementation should establish useful scientific discussion and feedback functionality without requiring an extensive social networking or discussion platform.

Priority capabilities include:

- General scientific discussion spaces.
- Subject-based discussion organization.
- Questions and responses.
- Discussions associated with astronomical objects and research material.
- Links to datasets, observations, analyses, projects and publications.
- Constructive feedback on shared research.
- Discussion of alternative interpretations.
- Support for scientific references and evidence.
- Search and basic discussion navigation.
- Preservation of discussion history and attribution.
- Basic mechanisms for reporting inappropriate discussion content.

Discussions should be accessible both through the dedicated community environment and through relevant scientific contexts where appropriate.

The initial implementation should prioritize useful scientific exchange and contextual organization rather than engagement metrics or extensive social features.

## Implementation Considerations

Scientific discussion functionality should integrate with the application's community environment, research sharing, project management, educational resources and scientific reference systems.

Particular attention should be given to:

- Preserving relationships between discussions and associated research material.
- Respecting access permissions for private projects and datasets.
- Maintaining attribution and discussion history.
- Supporting pseudonymous participation.
- Distinguishing discussion and feedback from formal scientific validation.
- Accommodating legitimate scientific disagreement.
- Avoiding unnecessary duplication of educational and project-specific communication features.
- Making useful discussions discoverable without overwhelming scientific workspaces.

The application should establish a foundation for meaningful scientific discussion while allowing more advanced community interaction to develop later.

**General Principle:** Scientific discussion and peer feedback should encourage knowledge exchange, critical examination, alternative interpretations and constructive scientific disagreement. Discussions should remain connected to relevant research material and preserve their context and history, allowing the community to become a useful long-term resource for scientific understanding and investigation.
