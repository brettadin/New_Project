# 3.0 — Core Scientific Capabilities and Research Workflows

## Section Purpose

This section establishes the scientific capabilities of the application and the research workflows they should support.

The application should provide an integrated scientific environment in which users can examine astronomical observations, prepare data, apply appropriate analytical methods, develop and evaluate models, investigate astronomical systems, compare scientific evidence and produce meaningful research outputs.

Scientific capabilities should support the disciplines and research subjects established in Section 2 while remaining flexible enough to accommodate different observational methods, data types, scientific approaches and levels of investigation.

Capabilities should not exist as isolated tools. They should operate within a coherent research environment, allowing users to move between related scientific activities while preserving data relationships, methodological context, provenance and analytical continuity.

This section defines the intended scientific functionality of the application. Data discovery and external resources are addressed in Section 4, scientific integrity and analytical control in Section 5, and application workspaces and project organization in Section 6.

## 3.2 Observational Data Examination and Preparation

Defines the general capabilities required to inspect, understand and prepare astronomical observations and measurements before scientific analysis.

**Covers:**
- Examination of imported and retrieved observational data.
- Inspection of data structures, metadata, units and measurement conventions.
- Support for different scientific data formats and representations.
- Data selection, filtering, extraction and organization.
- Handling missing, invalid, incomplete or non-numeric measurements.
- Calibration, correction, normalization and other appropriate preparation procedures.
- Data quality assessment and identification of observational limitations.
- Preservation of original data and relationships with derived datasets.

- Comparison with reference spectra and theoretical predictions.
- Spectroscopic investigations across stellar, planetary, atmospheric and other astronomical research areas.

## General Principles

The application's scientific capabilities should:

- Support the scientific disciplines and research subjects established in Section 2.
- Provide both general-purpose research functionality and specialized scientific tools.
- Accommodate different observational data types, formats and measurement conventions.
- Support complete research workflows rather than isolated operations.
- Allow related capabilities to work together where scientifically appropriate.
- Preserve original data and distinguish it from processed or derived information.
- Maintain scientific context, units, metadata, uncertainty and provenance.
- Support exploratory investigation and advanced quantitative analysis.
- Preserve relationships between observations, datasets, analytical activities, models, outputs and results.
- Make scientific outputs accessible for further analysis, interpretation, visualization and reporting.
- Accommodate future methods and disciplines without unnecessary complexity.
- Maintain scientific integrity, transparency, reliability and reproducibility as capabilities expand.

## General Principle

Scientific capabilities should form an integrated and adaptable research environment in which users can move from observations and data to preparation, analysis, modelling, system investigation, comparison and scientific interpretation without losing the context or integrity of their work.

## Implementation Boundary

This section defines the intended scientific capabilities and research workflows of the application.

It does not establish specific implementation technologies, prescribe individual algorithms or require every capability to be developed simultaneously.

The order and priority of implementation should be determined through the broader development planning process, taking into account scientific value, technical feasibility, available resources, dependencies and validation requirements.

The application should maintain a coherent scientific foundation while allowing individual capabilities to develop as research needs and technical opportunities emerge.
