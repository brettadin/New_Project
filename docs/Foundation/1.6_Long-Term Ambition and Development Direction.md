# 1.6 — Long-Term Ambition and Development Direction

## Purpose

Establish the long-term ambition of developing the application into a comprehensive, adaptable and scientifically credible astronomy research platform.

The application should begin with a practical and achievable foundation while preserving the ability to expand its scientific capabilities, supporting functions and technical scope over time.

Its long-term direction should be guided by scientific value, user needs, technical feasibility and the continued development of astronomy and related scientific fields.

The initial implementation should not be treated as the permanent limit of what the application may eventually become. Equally, the broader vision should not be interpreted as a requirement to implement every proposed capability immediately.

## 1.6.1 Long-Term Scientific Ambition

The application should ultimately support a broad range of astronomical research and scientific investigation.

**Core requirements**

- Preserve the ability to support multiple astronomical disciplines and research subjects.
- Allow specialized scientific capabilities to become increasingly sophisticated over time.
- Support increasingly complex investigations involving multiple datasets, methods and scientific disciplines.
- Accommodate observational, theoretical, computational and interdisciplinary research.
- Allow new scientific methods and established research practices to be incorporated when appropriate.
- Support continued development of scientific analysis, modelling, visualization and interpretation capabilities.
- Maintain scientific credibility as the application's scope and complexity increase.

**Design guidance**

The long-term ambition is to provide a broad scientific research environment rather than a collection of narrowly focused tools.

The application should be capable of supporting both individual investigations and more complex research activities without requiring every capability to be developed at the same time.

## 1.6.2 Expansion Across Scientific Disciplines

The initial scientific scope should establish meaningful priorities without permanently restricting future development.

**Core requirements**

- Establish initial priority scientific disciplines according to research value and practical feasibility.
- Preserve the ability to introduce additional astronomical disciplines.
- Consider related scientific fields where they provide meaningful contributions to astronomical research.
- Allow new disciplines to develop specialized tools, datasets and workflows.
- Support scientifically meaningful connections between disciplines.
- Evaluate proposed expansions according to scientific relevance, technical requirements and available resources.
- Avoid treating the initial discipline list as an exhaustive definition of the application's permanent scope.

**Future possibilities**

Potential areas of expansion may include additional branches of astrophysics, planetary science, heliophysics, astrobiology, cosmology, computational science and other related fields.

These are possibilities for future consideration rather than commitments to implement every area.

Detailed scientific scope and priorities are established in Section 2.

## 1.6.3 Expansion of Scientific Capabilities

The application should be capable of developing more advanced and specialized scientific functionality as research needs evolve.

**Core requirements**

- Allow existing scientific tools and methods to be improved over time.
- Support the introduction of additional analytical and computational capabilities.
- Accommodate new data formats, observational methods and scientific resources.
- Allow specialized workflows to develop without unnecessarily disrupting existing functionality.
- Support increasingly advanced modelling, simulation and visualization where scientifically justified.
- Evaluate new capabilities according to their reliability, scientific usefulness and maintenance requirements.
- Preserve the ability to replace or improve outdated methods and dependencies.

**Future possibilities**

Potential areas of capability expansion include:

- More advanced scientific modelling and simulation.
- Additional observational and analytical methods.
- Expanded computational and statistical tools.
- More sophisticated data processing and interpretation.
- Additional integrations with established scientific software.
- Advanced research automation and intelligent assistance.
- Expanded support for large or complex scientific datasets.

These possibilities should be evaluated individually rather than treated as fixed implementation commitments.

## 1.6.4 Growth of the Research Environment

The application should evolve beyond its initial scientific tools into a more complete environment for conducting and managing research.

**Core requirements**

- Expand the relationships between scientific capabilities, research materials and supporting resources.
- Improve continuity across different stages of investigation.
- Allow projects to accommodate increasingly complex research activities.
- Develop educational and reference resources alongside scientific functionality.
- Preserve opportunities for additional collaboration and community capabilities.
- Support the organization, preservation and communication of increasingly substantial research records.
- Ensure supporting functions continue to strengthen the application's central scientific purpose.

**Design guidance**

The research environment should become more useful as scientific capabilities and supporting resources develop.

Expansion should improve the coherence and usefulness of the platform rather than simply increasing the number of available sections or features.

## 1.6.5 Technological and Architectural Evolution

The application should remain capable of adapting to changes in scientific computing, software development and research infrastructure.

**Core requirements**

- Preserve opportunities to improve the application's architecture as requirements evolve.
- Allow appropriate integration of new technologies, libraries, frameworks and scientific software.
- Evaluate technical changes according to reliability, compatibility, maintainability and practical value.
- Avoid unnecessary architectural complexity in anticipation of hypothetical future requirements.
- Support improvements to performance, data management and computational capabilities where justified.
- Consider evolving platform and deployment requirements.
- Preserve existing research materials and functionality during significant technical changes.
- Ensure technical evolution remains consistent with the application's scientific objectives.

**Design guidance**

The application should not become unnecessarily constrained by its initial implementation choices.

However, technical modernization should be driven by demonstrated needs and carefully evaluated benefits rather than adopting new technologies simply because they become available.

## 1.6.6 Incremental Development and Prioritization

The application should be developed through manageable stages that establish a reliable foundation before introducing increasingly complex capabilities.

**Core requirements**

- Divide development into clearly defined stages, milestones or work packages.
- Identify essential foundational capabilities and their dependencies.
- Prioritize functionality according to scientific value, practical usefulness, technical feasibility and available resources.
- Establish clear requirements before significant implementation work.
- Research unfamiliar scientific methods and technical approaches before committing to them.
- Validate implemented functionality before treating it as complete.
- Reassess priorities as new information and development experience become available.
- Preserve the broader vision when making short-term implementation decisions.
- Avoid allowing ambitious future possibilities to prevent practical progress.

**Design guidance**

Incremental development should not mean losing sight of the intended platform.

Each stage should contribute to a meaningful foundation for subsequent work, while maintaining a realistic understanding of what can be developed and validated at that time.

## 1.6.7 Research-Driven Development

Significant scientific and technical decisions should be informed by appropriate research rather than unsupported assumptions.

**Core requirements**

- Investigate scientific requirements before designing specialized capabilities.
- Evaluate established scientific methods and relevant literature.
- Research available datasets, archives, software and external resources.
- Compare potential technical approaches before making consequential architectural decisions.
- Identify uncertainties, limitations and unresolved questions.
- Document important findings and their influence on development decisions.
- Revisit previous decisions when new evidence or requirements justify doing so.
- Distinguish verified information from assumptions and proposed possibilities.

**Design guidance**

Research should be an ongoing part of development, not a preliminary activity that ends once implementation begins.

The application should evolve in response to improved scientific understanding, technical findings and demonstrated user needs.

## 1.6.8 User Needs and Community Influence

The application should remain responsive to the needs of its intended users and the wider scientific community.

**Core requirements**

- Consider user feedback when evaluating improvements and new capabilities.
- Identify recurring difficulties and unmet research needs.
- Consider the requirements of different scientific disciplines and experience levels.
- Evaluate community suggestions according to scientific relevance, feasibility and practical value.
- Avoid treating popularity or feature requests alone as sufficient justification for implementation.
- Preserve the application's scientific purpose when evaluating external suggestions.
- Allow the development direction to evolve when well-supported needs emerge.

**Design guidance**

User and community feedback should inform development without automatically determining priorities.

The application should remain guided by its documented vision, scientific requirements and responsible development practices.

## 1.6.9 Long-Term Maintainability and Sustainability

The application should remain practical to maintain, improve and operate as its capabilities expand.

**Core requirements**

- Consider long-term maintenance when introducing new functionality and dependencies.
- Avoid unnecessary complexity and duplication.
- Preserve documentation and development knowledge.
- Maintain appropriate testing and validation practices.
- Consider computational, storage and infrastructure requirements as the application grows.
- Evaluate the continued suitability of external services and dependencies.
- Support the preservation and portability of research materials.
- Plan for the eventual replacement or modernization of outdated components where necessary.
- Avoid allowing the application to become dependent on unsupported or unmaintainable technologies without appropriate consideration.

**Design principle**

Long-term success should be measured not only by the capabilities the application acquires, but also by its ability to remain reliable, understandable, maintainable and scientifically useful.

## General Principles

- Preserve a broad and ambitious scientific vision.
- Treat initial scientific priorities as starting points rather than permanent limitations.
- Expand scientific disciplines and capabilities according to meaningful research needs.
- Develop the research environment as an integrated whole.
- Allow technical architecture and implementation approaches to evolve when justified.
- Prioritize incremental, validated development.
- Base significant decisions on appropriate scientific and technical research.
- Consider user needs and community contributions without abandoning scientific priorities.
- Maintain reliability, documentation and sustainability as the application grows.
- Distinguish long-term possibilities from approved development commitments.
- Ensure short-term implementation decisions contribute to the broader vision.

**General principle:** The application should evolve into a comprehensive and adaptable astronomy research platform through research-driven, incremental development. Its long-term ambition should remain broad, while its actual development priorities should be determined by scientific value, technical feasibility, reliability and demonstrated usefulness.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes the application's long-term ambitions and general development direction.

It does not define a fixed implementation schedule, detailed development roadmap, final technical architecture, specific technology choices or commitments to implement every proposed future capability.

Development stages, research priorities, technical decisions and implementation boundaries are established through the relevant Foundations sections and the subsequent planning and approval processes.
