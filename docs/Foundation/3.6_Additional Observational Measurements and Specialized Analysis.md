

# 3.6 — Additional Observational Measurements and Specialized Analysis

## Purpose

The application should support observational measurements and scientific analysis techniques that are not adequately covered by spectroscopy, photometry or astronomical imaging alone.

Astronomical research uses a wide range of observational methods to investigate the properties, motion, structure and physical conditions of astronomical objects. Some methods produce specialized measurements or data representations that require dedicated analytical capabilities.

The application should accommodate these methods where scientifically relevant, allowing specialized observations to contribute to broader research investigations.

Capabilities should remain extensible so that additional observational techniques can be incorporated as scientific requirements develop.

## 3.6.1 Radial Velocity Measurements and Analysis

### Core Requirements

- Support examination of radial velocity measurements and associated observational data.
- Accommodate measurements derived from spectroscopic observations and other appropriate methods.
- Display radial velocity measurements with relevant units and reference conventions.
- Support examination of measurement uncertainties.
- Allow comparison of radial velocity measurements across multiple observations.
- Support identification and investigation of periodic radial velocity variations.
- Allow radial velocity measurements to contribute to orbital analysis.
- Support comparison with theoretical orbital predictions and fitted models.
- Preserve relevant instrumental, observational and calibration information.
- Distinguish measured radial velocities from values inferred through modelling.

### Design Guidance

Radial velocity measurements provide important evidence for investigating stellar motion, binary systems and exoplanetary companions.

The application should accommodate the relationship between spectroscopic measurements and derived velocity estimates without treating every reported value as directly equivalent.

Relevant reference frames, instrumental offsets, measurement uncertainties and observational limitations should remain accessible during analysis.

## 3.6.2 Astrometric Measurements and Analysis

### Core Requirements

- Support examination of astronomical positional measurements.
- Accommodate relevant celestial coordinate systems and reference frames.
- Support positional measurements and changes in position over time.
- Allow examination of proper motion and parallax measurements.
- Support angular separation and positional comparison.
- Accommodate astrometric uncertainties and covariance information where available.
- Support comparison of measurements from different observations and catalogs.
- Allow astrometric results to contribute to distance estimation, orbital analysis and object characterization.
- Preserve coordinate conventions, reference epochs and source metadata.
- Distinguish direct positional measurements from derived physical properties.

### Design Guidance

Astrometric measurements require careful treatment of coordinate systems, reference frames, epochs and measurement uncertainty.

The application should preserve these distinctions and avoid treating positional values from different reference systems as directly comparable without appropriate transformation.

## 3.6.3 Polarimetry and Polarimetric Analysis

### Core Requirements

- Support examination of astronomical polarimetric observations.
- Accommodate relevant Stokes parameters and polarization representations.
- Support analysis of linear and circular polarization where appropriate.
- Allow examination of polarization degree and polarization angle.
- Preserve relevant wavelength, instrument and observational information.
- Support comparison of polarimetric measurements across observations and wavelength regions.
- Accommodate associated uncertainties and calibration information.
- Allow polarimetric results to contribute to broader investigations of astronomical environments and physical processes.
- Distinguish measured quantities from derived polarization properties.

### Design Guidance

Polarimetry can provide information about magnetic fields, scattering environments, dust, radiation processes and other physical characteristics.

Different polarimetric methods may produce different measurement representations and require specialized calibration.

The application should preserve the underlying measurement conventions and support scientifically appropriate interpretation.

## 3.6.4 Occultation and Specialized Timing Measurements

### Core Requirements

- Support examination of astronomical occultation observations.
- Accommodate relevant timing and measurement information.
- Support investigation of occultation events involving stars, planets, moons and other astronomical objects.
- Allow examination of event duration, timing and associated measurement changes.
- Support comparison of observations from multiple locations or instruments where appropriate.
- Accommodate uncertainty in event timing and measurement geometry.
- Allow occultation measurements to contribute to investigations of object size, shape, atmosphere and orbital properties.
- Preserve observational conditions and relevant geometric information.
- Support integration with photometric, astrometric and orbital analysis.

### Design Guidance

Occultation observations can provide constraints on astronomical properties that may be difficult to measure through other methods.

Their interpretation may depend on observation geometry, timing precision, observer location and assumptions about the objects involved.

The application should preserve these conditions and distinguish observed event characteristics from properties inferred through modelling.

## 3.6.5 High-Energy and Specialized Detector Measurements

### Core Requirements

- Accommodate relevant observational measurements from X-ray and gamma-ray astronomy.
- Support appropriate representations of photon counts, energy distributions, event data and associated measurements.
- Allow examination of relevant timing and energy information.
- Preserve detector, instrument and observational metadata.
- Support analysis of energy-dependent measurements where appropriate.
- Accommodate relevant background, response and calibration information.
- Allow specialized observations to contribute to broader astrophysical investigations.
- Distinguish raw detector measurements from calibrated or derived scientific quantities.

### Design Guidance

High-energy astronomical observations may involve data structures and measurement conventions that differ substantially from optical and infrared observations.

The application should not assume that general-purpose image, spectral or photometric methods can be applied directly to every high-energy dataset.

Specialized analytical methods should be introduced according to scientific requirements and the availability of appropriate reference information.

## 3.6.6 Other Specialized Observational Techniques

### Core Requirements

Allow future support for additional observational techniques where scientifically relevant, including:

- Radio and microwave interferometry.
- Very Long Baseline Interferometry (VLBI).
- Interferometric imaging and measurements.
- Spectropolarimetry.
- Gravitational-wave observations.
- Neutrino astronomy.
- Solar and heliophysical measurements.
- Other specialized or emerging observational approaches.

Support appropriate integration of these observations with existing scientific capabilities.

Preserve the unique data structures, measurement conventions, metadata and limitations associated with each method.

### Design Guidance

Some observational techniques may require substantially different data handling, computational methods or specialized scientific expertise.

Their inclusion should be evaluated according to their scientific relevance, feasibility and relationship with the application's broader research environment.

The application should remain open to these methods without assuming that every technique must be supported during initial development.

## 3.6.7 Specialized Data Examination and Preparation

### Core Requirements

- Support examination of data produced by specialized observational methods.
- Preserve method-specific metadata and measurement conventions.
- Identify the scientific quantities represented by specialized measurements.
- Support appropriate selection, filtering and extraction procedures.
- Accommodate relevant calibration and correction requirements.
- Identify missing or incompatible information.
- Support preparation for specialized analytical operations.
- Preserve original observations and create identifiable derived outputs.
- Maintain relationships between specialized datasets and their subsequent analyses.

### Design Guidance

Specialized observational data may require preparation procedures that cannot be generalized across all scientific disciplines.

The application should provide common data-handling capabilities where appropriate while allowing individual methods to establish their own scientific requirements.

## 3.6.8 Specialized Measurement Analysis and Modelling

### Core Requirements

- Support appropriate quantitative analysis of specialized observational measurements.
- Allow relevant analytical parameters to be configured.
- Support comparison with suitable theoretical predictions and scientific models.
- Accommodate method-specific uncertainty and measurement limitations.
- Allow specialized analytical results to contribute to broader scientific investigations.
- Preserve relevant assumptions, methods and parameter settings.
- Distinguish observed measurements from derived properties and model-dependent interpretations.
- Allow new analytical methods to be introduced as scientific requirements develop.

### Design Guidance

Specialized observational methods may require dedicated analytical procedures.

The application should avoid forcing these methods into existing analytical frameworks when their scientific requirements differ.

Where shared methods are appropriate, they should be reused without compromising the validity of the specialized analysis.

## 3.6.9 Integration with Other Scientific Capabilities

### Core Requirements

- Allow specialized observational measurements to contribute to broader research workflows.
- Support integration with spectroscopy, photometry, imaging, modelling and orbital analysis where appropriate.
- Allow measurements from different observational techniques to be compared.
- Preserve units, coordinate systems, reference conventions and uncertainty information.
- Identify compatibility requirements before combining different measurements.
- Support multi-method and interdisciplinary investigations.
- Maintain relationships between specialized observations and the results derived from them.

### Design Guidance

Specialized observations should not become isolated datasets simply because they use different measurement techniques.

For example, radial velocity measurements may contribute to orbital analysis, astrometry may provide complementary positional constraints, and polarimetry may contribute information about physical environments.

Integration should preserve the distinct scientific meaning of each measurement rather than reducing different observational methods to superficially similar numerical inputs.

## 3.6.10 Specialized Outputs and Future Expansion

### Core Requirements

- Allow specialized analytical results to be saved and revisited.
- Support export of relevant measurements and derived outputs.
- Preserve associated metadata, units, provenance and uncertainty.
- Allow results to be reused in compatible scientific capabilities.
- Support the introduction of additional observational techniques and analytical methods.
- Maintain compatibility with existing research workflows where practical.
- Distinguish established capabilities from experimental or planned functionality.

### Design Guidance

Specialized observational capabilities should expand incrementally.

Their outputs should follow the same general expectations for scientific traceability and usability as other analytical results while retaining any method-specific information required for interpretation.

## General Principles

Additional observational measurements and specialized analysis should:

- Accommodate observational methods beyond spectroscopy, photometry and imaging.
- Preserve method-specific measurement conventions and scientific context.
- Support appropriate specialized data examination and preparation.
- Provide suitable analytical methods for different observational techniques.
- Maintain uncertainty, calibration and provenance information.
- Distinguish observations from derived measurements and model interpretations.
- Allow specialized results to contribute to broader research workflows.
- Avoid forcing fundamentally different methods into unsuitable analytical frameworks.
- Remain open to emerging observational techniques.
- Expand according to scientific relevance and practical feasibility.

## General Principle

The application should accommodate the diversity of astronomical observational science without restricting research to a small number of established measurement types.

Specialized observational methods should be supported as meaningful parts of the wider research environment and integrated with other scientific capabilities wherever appropriate.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes the intended support for additional observational measurements and specialized analytical methods.

It does not require every listed observational technique to be implemented initially, nor does it prescribe specific algorithms, instruments, data archives, software libraries or implementation technologies.

General data examination and preparation are addressed in Section 3.2. Spectroscopy, photometry and imaging are addressed in Sections 3.3–3.5. Broader modelling, comparative analysis and scientific integration are addressed in Sections 3.7–3.9. Data discovery and external resources are addressed in Section 4, while scientific validation and reproducibility are addressed in Section 5.
