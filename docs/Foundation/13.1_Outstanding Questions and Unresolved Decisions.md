# 13.1 — Outstanding Questions and Unresolved Decisions

## Purpose

Establish a consistent approach to identifying, recording and resolving questions that remain unanswered during the application's planning, research and development.

The application is expected to evolve as scientific requirements become clearer, technical research progresses and practical development reveals new information.

Unresolved questions should remain visible and traceable without unnecessarily delaying unrelated work or being treated as established requirements.

## 13.1.1 Identification of Outstanding Questions

**Core requirements**

- Identify scientific, technical, architectural, functional and organizational questions that remain unresolved.
- Identify requirements that are ambiguous, incomplete or dependent on further investigation.
- Record questions arising from scientific research, technical evaluations and implementation experience.
- Identify decisions that depend on unavailable information or external developments.
- Distinguish questions that affect immediate development from those relevant only to future possibilities.
- Identify questions that can be investigated independently and those requiring user input or approval.
- Avoid repeatedly raising questions that have already been resolved unless new evidence justifies reconsideration.

## 13.1.2 Classification and Prioritization

Outstanding questions should be classified according to their significance and when they need to be addressed.

**Suggested classifications**

- **Blocking:** Must be resolved before a particular development task or consequential decision can proceed.
- **Important:** Requires investigation or a decision before a relevant development stage.
- **Deferred:** Can remain unresolved until a later stage without preventing current work.
- **Future consideration:** Relevant to possible future capabilities or application direction.
- **Exploratory:** An interesting question or opportunity that may warrant investigation but has no established development commitment.

**Core requirements**

- Record why a question remains unresolved.
- Identify its affected requirements, capabilities or decisions.
- Document relevant dependencies and consequences.
- Establish when or under what conditions it should be revisited.
- Update its status when new information becomes available.
- Avoid treating every unanswered question as an immediate development priority.

## 13.1.3 Independent Investigation and Resolution

**Core requirements**

- The AI development partner should independently investigate questions that can be resolved through scientific or technical research.
- Evaluate available evidence and relevant alternatives.
- Identify when additional experimentation, testing or practical implementation is necessary.
- Present recommendations when sufficient evidence supports a conclusion.
- Distinguish confirmed findings from assumptions and unresolved uncertainty.
- Ask the user for input when a decision depends on personal preferences, project direction or authorization.
- Avoid transferring routine technical research decisions to the user unnecessarily.
- Preserve previous findings and reasoning when a question is resolved or superseded.

Not every question requires a formal user decision. The AI should resolve ordinary scientific and technical uncertainties independently where appropriate.

## 13.1.4 User-Dependent Decisions

Some questions cannot be resolved through technical or scientific research alone.

These may involve:

- Application identity, branding and presentation preferences.
- Prioritization of competing development objectives.
- Decisions involving acceptable costs or external commitments.
- Significant changes to application scope or direction.
- Choices involving user privacy, permissions or data ownership.
- Preferences where multiple technically suitable approaches exist.

**Core requirements**

- Identify when a decision genuinely requires user input.
- Explain the relevant context and implications.
- Present practical alternatives where appropriate.
- Provide a recommendation when sufficient information is available.
- Clearly identify the decision or approval being requested.
- Record the user's response and any conditions attached to it.
- Never treat silence or an ambiguous response as approval.

These requirements must remain consistent with the approval and communication procedures established in Section 10.9.

## 13.1.5 Documentation and Continuity

**Core requirements**

- Preserve significant outstanding questions in the appropriate project records.
- Link questions to relevant requirements, research, decisions and development tasks.
- Record investigation findings and unsuccessful approaches where useful.
- Update records when questions are resolved, deferred, rejected or superseded.
- Preserve the reasoning behind significant decisions.
- Ensure unresolved questions remain accessible to future development sessions and contributors.
- Avoid maintaining duplicate or conflicting lists of outstanding decisions.

The Master Development Roadmap should reference unresolved questions that affect development priorities, dependencies or implementation readiness.

Detailed investigations and consequential decisions should remain in their appropriate documentation records.

## General Principles

- Unresolved questions must remain visible rather than being silently converted into assumptions.
- Investigate scientific and technical questions independently where possible.
- Distinguish blocking decisions from matters that can safely wait.
- Avoid unnecessary interruptions to unrelated development work.
- Preserve previous findings and decision history.
- Revisit questions when new evidence or requirements justify doing so.
- Maintain clear ownership of decisions requiring user approval.

**General principle:** Unresolved questions should be managed as part of the application's continuing development knowledge. They must remain visible, appropriately prioritized and traceable without unnecessarily preventing progress.
