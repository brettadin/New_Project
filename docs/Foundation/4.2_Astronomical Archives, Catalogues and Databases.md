

# 4.2 — Astronomical Archives, Catalogues and Databases

## Purpose

The application should support access to external astronomical archives, catalogues and scientific databases that provide observations, object information, measurements and other research resources.

Astronomical information is distributed across numerous organizations and specialized repositories. These resources differ in scientific purpose, data coverage, access methods, metadata, quality standards and availability.

The application should provide a structured approach to identifying, evaluating and integrating relevant sources without assuming that every resource serves the same purpose or provides interchangeable information.

The initial collection of supported sources should be selected through research and evaluation rather than treated as a fixed list. The application should also remain adaptable as new resources become available and existing services change.

## 4.2.1 Types of External Scientific Resources

### Core Requirements

Recognize and accommodate different categories of external scientific resources, including:

- **Astronomical observation archives:** Repositories providing observational data from ground-based and space-based facilities.
- **Object and system catalogues:** Databases containing information about astronomical objects, systems and their measured or derived properties.
- **Survey databases:** Collections of observations and measurements produced by astronomical surveys.
- **Specialized scientific databases:** Resources providing information for particular disciplines, objects, measurements or research methods.
- **Planetary and Solar System databases:** Resources containing observations, measurements and reference information for the Sun, planets, moons and other Solar System bodies.
- **Stellar and exoplanet databases:** Resources containing stellar properties, exoplanet discoveries, planetary system parameters and related observations.
- **Scientific reference databases:** Collections of laboratory measurements, molecular and atomic information, spectral references and other scientific reference material.
- **Literature and publication databases:** Resources providing access to scientific publications, abstracts, citations and associated research information.
- **Specialized observational repositories:** Resources supporting particular instruments, wavelength regions, measurement techniques or scientific disciplines.

Maintain clear distinctions between resources that provide original observations, processed data products, catalogue measurements, derived properties, reference information, scientific literature and other forms of research material.

### Design Guidance

Different resource types may contain information about the same astronomical object while serving very different scientific purposes.

For example, an observation archive may provide an original or processed telescope observation, an object catalogue may provide measured or derived physical properties, and a scientific reference database may provide laboratory measurements used to interpret that observation.

The application should preserve these distinctions when presenting and integrating external resources.

The application should make these differences understandable so users can identify the most appropriate sources for their research.

## 4.2.3 Source Evaluation and Selection

### Core Requirements

Evaluate potential external resources before selecting them for integration.

Selection criteria should include:

- **Scientific relevance:** Whether the resource supports the application's research areas and intended capabilities.
- **Scientific authority:** The credibility of the organization, institution or scientific collaboration responsible for the resource.
- **Data coverage:** The range of astronomical objects, observations, measurements and scientific subjects represented.
- **Data quality:** The reliability, completeness and scientific suitability of the available information.
- **Metadata availability:** Whether sufficient information is provided to understand and interpret the data.
- **Accessibility:** Whether the resource is publicly accessible, restricted or dependent on authentication.
- **Technical accessibility:** Availability and suitability of APIs, query interfaces, downloadable products and other access methods.
- **Licensing and usage conditions:** Restrictions affecting retrieval, storage, modification, redistribution and citation.
- **Integration complexity:** The technical and scientific effort required to access and interpret the resource correctly.
- **Maintenance requirements:** Expected effort to maintain compatibility with the resource and its access methods.
- **Long-term sustainability:** The likelihood that the resource will remain available and useful.
- **Overlap and complementary value:** Whether the resource duplicates existing coverage or provides meaningful additional capabilities.

Document significant evaluation findings and the reasons for selecting or rejecting a resource for integration.

### Design Guidance

A resource should not be selected solely because it is well known, widely used or technically easy to access.

Scientific suitability, coverage, reliability and long-term value should take precedence over the number of available integrations.

Some resources may be valuable for particular research activities even if they are not appropriate for broad initial integration.

## 4.2.4 Initial Resource Collection and Prioritization

### Core Requirements

- Establish an initial collection of external sources based on the application's scientific priorities.
- Prioritize resources that provide meaningful coverage for exoplanet science, planetary science, stellar astronomy and observational astronomy.
- Consider resources that support multiple scientific disciplines and research workflows.
- Identify essential sources and additional resources that can be introduced later.
- Avoid treating every potentially useful source as an initial implementation requirement.
- Preserve the reasoning behind major integration priorities.
- Allow priorities to change as research, user requirements and technical evaluations develop.
- Treat broad coverage across relevant astronomical archives, catalogues and scientific databases as a major priority.
- Avoid unnecessary dependence on a single provider or archive when other scientifically relevant sources are available.
- Identify and support multiple complementary sources for important research areas, including sources that provide overlapping observations or information where comparison and independent verification are valuable.
- Where appropriate, allow research involving an astronomical object or scientific question to draw upon relevant information from multiple supported sources.
- Preserve distinctions between independent observations, duplicate or mirrored records, processed products and derived information obtained through different providers.
- Balance broad source coverage against scientific reliability, data quality, integration feasibility, licensing and ongoing maintenance requirements.

### Design Guidance

The initial resource collection should provide a useful foundation without creating an unnecessarily large integration and maintenance burden.

A smaller collection of well-evaluated and reliably integrated resources may provide greater scientific value than a much larger collection with inconsistent support.

However, minimizing the number of integrations should not become a reason to unnecessarily restrict scientific coverage.

The application should make a deliberate effort to identify and accommodate multiple relevant sources, particularly where different archives provide complementary observations, independent measurements or information needed for comprehensive research.

The objective is not to integrate every available resource indiscriminately, but to provide broad and useful scientific coverage through reliable, appropriately evaluated sources.

The development process should independently research and recommend suitable initial sources, explaining the scientific and practical reasoning behind those recommendations.

Final integration priorities should remain subject to review and approval through the established development process.

## 4.2.5 Resource Identity and Provider Information

### Core Requirements

- Preserve the identity of each external archive, catalogue or database.
- Identify the organization or institution responsible for the resource where known.
- Provide relevant source descriptions and scientific scope.
- Preserve resource identifiers and original record references where available.
- Distinguish between the original provider and any intermediary service used to access its data.
- Identify relevant versions, releases or catalogue editions.
- Communicate when a resource is maintained by a collaboration or multiple organizations.
- Allow users to access available source documentation and provider information.

### Design Guidance

The application should not obscure the origin of scientific information behind a unified interface.

Users should be able to identify where information originated, who maintains the resource and which version or release was consulted.

Where data passes through an intermediary service, the application should preserve the distinction between the original scientific source and the service used to retrieve it.

## 4.2.6 Resource-Specific Capabilities and Limitations

### Core Requirements

- Recognize that external resources provide different types of information and access capabilities.
- Identify supported query methods and available data products.
- Document relevant search, retrieval and filtering limitations.
- Identify restrictions on bulk downloads, request sizes, query complexity or service usage.
- Accommodate resources that provide metadata without direct data retrieval.
- Accommodate resources that require users to access an external website or specialized service.
- Communicate when a resource cannot provide a particular type of information.
- Avoid presenting unsupported functionality as available through an integration.

### Design Guidance

Not every archive or database provides the same capabilities.

Some may support advanced queries and direct retrieval, while others may provide only catalogue records, downloadable files or external links.

The application should adapt its interaction with each source according to its actual capabilities rather than forcing every integration into an identical workflow.

## 4.2.7 Resource Integration and Maintenance Records

### Core Requirements

Maintain documentation for supported external resources and their integrations.

Records should include, where applicable:

- Resource name and provider.
- Scientific purpose and coverage.
- Resource type.
- Integration status.
- Supported access methods.
- Relevant API or service documentation.
- Applicable versions or releases.
- Authentication requirements.
- Licensing and usage conditions.
- Known limitations.
- Maintenance requirements.
- Last relevant evaluation or verification.
- Known compatibility issues.
- Relevant alternatives or fallback resources.

Maintain records of significant integration decisions and changes.

Identify resources that are planned, under evaluation, supported, temporarily unavailable, deprecated or no longer maintained.

### Design Guidance

The resource catalogue should provide a reliable overview of which external resources the application supports and what users can expect from each integration.

It should distinguish between a resource that has merely been identified, one that has been evaluated and one that has an operational integration.

Detailed resource catalogue management is addressed in Section 4.12.

## 4.2.8 Unsuccessful Integrations and Alternative Sources

### Core Requirements

When a resource cannot be integrated, becomes unsuitable or presents significant technical or scientific problems, document the investigation.

Records should identify:

1. **What:** The resource and the specific issue encountered.
2. **How:** How the issue was discovered, investigated or reproduced.
3. **Why:** The identified cause, or what remains uncertain.
4. **When:** The investigation date and relevant software, API or resource versions.
5. **Impact:** The functionality, data coverage or research capability affected.
6. **Next choice:** An alternative source or integration approach, including the reasoning behind the recommendation.

Preserve unsuccessful integration findings as part of the development history.

Avoid repeating investigations unnecessarily when previous findings remain relevant.

Identify alternative sources where practical and document any differences in scientific coverage or functionality.

### Design Guidance

An unsuccessful integration attempt should not simply disappear from the project history.

A resource may be unsuitable because of technical restrictions, insufficient metadata, licensing limitations, poor coverage, discontinued services or other scientific and practical concerns.

Documenting these findings helps future development make informed decisions and prevents previously encountered problems from being overlooked.

## 4.2.9 Ongoing Resource Evaluation

### Core Requirements

- Periodically review the external resource collection.
- Identify new archives, catalogues and scientific databases that may provide additional value.
- Monitor significant changes to existing resources.
- Identify discontinued services, deprecated APIs and changes in access requirements.
- Review whether existing integrations remain scientifically and technically suitable.
- Identify improved or alternative resources where appropriate.
- Update relevant resource and integration records.
- Preserve historical information about significant changes and previous integration decisions.

### Design Guidance

External scientific resources are not static.

New surveys, missions, catalogues and specialized databases become available, while existing services may change their interfaces, data products, access conditions or maintenance status.

The application should maintain an ongoing process for reviewing its resource collection rather than treating initial integration decisions as permanent.

## General Principles

External archives, catalogues and databases should:

- Provide access to scientifically relevant astronomical observations and information.
- Represent different resource types according to their actual scientific purpose.
- Support the application's initial research priorities while allowing future expansion.
- Be selected through documented scientific and technical evaluation.
- Preserve provider identity, resource versions and relevant source information.
- Communicate resource-specific capabilities and limitations.
- Maintain records of supported integrations and their status.
- Document unsuccessful integration attempts and alternative approaches.
- Be reviewed periodically for changes, new opportunities and continued suitability.
- Remain adaptable as astronomical research resources develop.

## General Principle

The application should connect users with reliable and scientifically relevant external resources through carefully evaluated integrations.

The value of the resource environment should be measured by the quality, suitability and usefulness of the information it provides, rather than simply by the number of connected archives and databases.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes the types of external archives, catalogues and databases the application should accommodate and the principles for evaluating, selecting, documenting and maintaining them.

It does not establish a finalized list of integrations, require every example to be supported initially or prescribe specific APIs, software libraries or implementation technologies.

Search behaviour and query refinement are addressed in Section 4.3. Retrieval and user-provided data import are addressed in Section 4.5. Scientific reference databases and specialized knowledge resources are covered within this subsection, while their evaluation and integration into the supported resource catalogue are addressed in Section 4.12. Resource catalogue management and user-suggested resources are addressed in Section 4.12.
