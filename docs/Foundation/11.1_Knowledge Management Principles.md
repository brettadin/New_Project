# 11.1 — Knowledge Management Principles

## Purpose

Establish a reliable system for preserving and organizing the scientific, technical, architectural and development knowledge accumulated throughout the application's lifecycle.

The knowledge-management system must allow the user and AI development partner to understand the application's purpose, requirements, current state, previous decisions and future direction without depending on conversational memory.

## Core Requirements

- Maintain persistent documentation for important scientific, technical, architectural and development information.
- Treat project documentation and designated records as the authoritative sources of established knowledge.
- Preserve important research findings, requirements, decisions, assumptions, implementation details, limitations and unresolved questions.
- Keep documentation consistent with the actual application and its current development status.
- Distinguish established facts, verified findings, assumptions, recommendations, proposed changes and approved decisions.
- Preserve the reasoning behind significant decisions so they can be understood and reassessed later.
- Retain unsuccessful investigations and approaches when they may prevent repeated work.
- Organize information so it can be located and retrieved without reviewing every previous conversation.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication, conflicting records and excessive documentation.
- Maintain knowledge in a format that can be understood by future AI development partners and human contributors.

## Knowledge Categories

The documentation system should accommodate the following major categories:

- **Application requirements:** Overall vision, scientific scope, functional requirements and design principles.
- **Scientific knowledge:** Research findings, scientific methods, mathematical foundations, references and limitations.
- **Technical knowledge:** Technologies, libraries, dependencies, integrations, compatibility and implementation considerations.
- **Architecture and design:** System structure, component responsibilities, interfaces and architectural decisions.
- **Development history:** Work performed, implementation progress, milestones and outstanding tasks.
- **Decisions and approvals:** Important user decisions, approved changes, alternatives and reasoning.
- **Problems and investigations:** Defects, blockers, attempted solutions, findings and resolutions.
- **Testing and validation:** Test reports, verification evidence, scientific validation and known limitations.
- **Operational knowledge:** Configuration, environments, deployment, maintenance and recovery procedures.
- **Future opportunities:** Proposed capabilities, improvements, deferred work and unresolved possibilities.

## Knowledge Reliability and Status

- Identify the source and status of important information.
- Distinguish verified information from assumptions or interpretations.
- Preserve references and supporting evidence where appropriate.
- Identify outdated, incomplete or potentially conflicting information.
- Reassess important knowledge when scientific developments, technical changes or new evidence make it necessary.
- Never silently replace historical findings or decisions with newer information.
- Ensure recommendations and proposed changes are not mistaken for approved requirements.

## Knowledge Accessibility and Continuity

- Organize documentation using consistent categories, naming and locations.
- Make important information discoverable by both users and AI development partners.
- Maintain relationships between requirements, implementation, decisions, problems, tests and development records.
- Provide sufficient context for another development partner to understand and continue the project.
- Preserve important knowledge across conversations, development sessions, application versions and changes in development personnel.
- Avoid making critical knowledge dependent on one individual or AI system.

## General Principles

- Documentation should be authoritative, organized, accessible and maintainable.
- Important knowledge must be preserved rather than relying on memory.
- Information should remain traceable to its source where appropriate.
- Historical context and decision reasoning should not be lost.
- Documentation should reflect actual project status.
- The system should be comprehensive without becoming unnecessarily complicated.

**General principle:** The project must maintain a persistent and trustworthy body of knowledge that allows scientific research and application development to continue consistently, transparently and reliably over the long term.
