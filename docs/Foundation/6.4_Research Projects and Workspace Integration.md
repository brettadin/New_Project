# 6.4 Research Projects and Workspace Integration

## Purpose

Research projects provide persistent, organized environments for conducting, managing and preserving scientific investigations.

An important distinction exists between scientific workspaces and research projects:

- **Workspaces** provide specialized scientific environments for particular disciplines or research activities.
- **Projects** provide persistent environments for organizing and managing investigations.

Projects should exist independently of individual scientific workspaces while remaining accessible through all relevant areas of the application.

The project system must accommodate different research approaches, multiple scientific disciplines, shared datasets and resources, long-term investigations and flexible user-defined organization.

It must also preserve scientific relationships, provenance, reproducibility, research history and user control.

## 6.4.1 Project and Workspace Relationship

### Functional Requirements

- Support object-focused projects, such as an investigation dedicated to Jupiter.
- Support research-question-focused projects involving multiple objects, datasets or scientific disciplines.
- Allow projects to contain multiple related investigations.
- Allow projects to span multiple scientific workspaces.
- Do not permanently associate a project with the workspace or discipline in which it was created.
- Allow users to create projects without having to select one permanent scientific workspace.
- Allow users to access projects from any relevant workspace or application area.
- Allow workspace-specific tools and capabilities to operate on appropriate project materials.
- Preserve relationships between objects, datasets, observations, analyses, models and results when moving between workspaces.
- Allow users to move between different scientific activities while retaining the overall project context.
- Support connections between results and materials originating from different scientific disciplines.

### Multi-disciplinary Integration

Projects must support investigations that combine multiple scientific disciplines without requiring users to divide related research into separate projects.

For example, an investigation of an exoplanet's atmosphere might involve:

- Stellar characterization from a stellar astronomy workspace.
- Transit observations from an observational astronomy workspace.
- Spectral analysis from a spectroscopy environment.
- Atmospheric retrieval from an exoplanet science workspace.
- Comparative modelling using shared scientific tools.

These activities should be able to belong to one persistent research project despite involving different workspaces, tools and scientific methodologies.

Relevant project materials should be accessible from each applicable workspace without unnecessarily duplicating them.

### Design Guidance

- Projects should remain independently organized while being accessible through relevant scientific environments.
- Workspace transitions should preserve research context and continuity.
- Each workspace should present relevant project materials and capabilities without unnecessarily exposing unrelated functionality.
- Cross-disciplinary integration should be natural and scientifically appropriate rather than forced.
- Projects should not become restricted by the discipline or workspace in which they were originally created.

## 6.4.2 Project Organization

### Functional Requirements

Projects should support a flexible internal structure containing, where relevant:

- Astronomical objects and systems.
- Research questions and objectives.
- Observations and datasets.
- Imported and retrieved reference data.
- Processing workflows.
- Scientific analyses and models.
- Visualizations, graphs and comparisons.
- Literature, references and citations.
- Notes, annotations and research decisions.
- Reports and conclusions.
- Research history and activity records.
- Other relevant research materials.

Additional requirements:

- Provide standard organizational elements for common research materials.
- Allow users to create custom categories, folders and groupings.
- Allow users to organize project materials according to their own research approach.
- Support different organizational structures for different types of investigations.
- Do not require every project to follow an identical structure.
- Allow users to reorganize project contents without losing relationships, provenance or traceability.
- Allow users to find and retrieve project materials without needing to remember exactly where they placed them.
- Do not require every available organizational category to be populated.

### Design Guidance

Distinguish between the user's preferred organizational structure and the underlying scientific relationships.

For example, moving a spectrum into a custom folder must not break its relationships with:

- Its original observation.
- Its source and instrument.
- Its processing history.
- Analyses that use it.
- Models and results derived from it.

Visible organization should remain flexible without compromising the integrity of the research record.

## 6.4.3 Shared Data and Resources

### Functional Requirements

- Allow multiple projects to reference and use the same dataset, observation or scientific resource.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of large datasets and files.
- Maintain a clear distinction between original resources and their use within individual projects.
- Preserve provenance, metadata, source information and processing history.
- Allow projects to perform independent analyses using the same underlying data.
- Allow shared scientific resources, such as reference databases, molecular and atomic data, line lists, models and other relevant resources, to be used across projects and workspaces.
- Ensure changes to one project's analysis, processing or organization do not unintentionally alter another project's work.
- Where data is processed or transformed, maintain separate derived versions and preserve the original.
- Track relationships between shared resources, projects and dependent analyses.
- Identify when shared resources become unavailable, outdated, modified or potentially incompatible.
- Communicate potential impacts on projects without silently changing existing research results.
- Preserve the identity and provenance of shared resources when they are used in different research contexts.

### Design Guidance

Sharing a dataset does not mean sharing every subsequent change made to it.

Projects should be able to independently process and analyse the same source data without compromising one another.

Shared resources should remain distinguishable from project-specific analyses, configurations and derived results.

## 6.4.4 Project Continuity

### Functional Requirements

- Support long-term research and maintain project accessibility over extended periods.
- Preserve research progress, datasets, analyses, models, configurations, visualizations and documentation.
- Maintain chronological records of significant project activities.
- Support ongoing investigations as new observations, data and scientific findings become available.
- Allow users to revisit, extend and revise previous analyses without losing historical results.
- Support unfinished, ongoing, completed and inconclusive investigations.
- Preserve project relationships when users reorganize or expand their work.
- Identify outdated, unavailable or modified external resources that may affect historical research.
- Identify changes to relevant software, libraries, databases, scientific methods and other dependencies.
- Never silently replace historical data, methods or results with newer versions.
- Preserve sufficient information to understand and reproduce historical work wherever technically and legally possible.
- Allow investigations to continue even when their original external resources or dependencies are no longer available, where sufficient information remains.

### Design Guidance

Projects should function as persistent research environments rather than temporary collections of files.

Historical research must remain distinguishable from subsequent developments. Updating an investigation should not erase the state of earlier work.

This requirement should align with the reproducibility and traceability principles established in Section 5.5.

## 6.4.5 Project-Specific Configurations

### Functional Requirements

- Allow projects to maintain independent configurations alongside global application settings.
- Support project-specific units and measurement conventions.
- Support default graphing and visualization settings.
- Support scientific constants and reference values.
- Support preferred datasets, databases and reference libraries.
- Support analysis and processing configurations.
- Support modelling parameters and assumptions.
- Support project-specific automation preferences.
- Support reporting and documentation preferences.
- Allow project configurations to override global defaults.
- Preserve configurations with relevant analyses and reproducibility records.
- Allow configurations to be saved, revisited, modified and reused.
- Maintain configuration history where changes could affect scientific results.
- Make active project settings clear to users.

### Design Guidance

Global settings should provide sensible defaults, while individual projects should be able to override them.

This allows different investigations to use different scientific conventions and analytical configurations without repeatedly changing application-wide settings.

## 6.4.6 Project History and Activity Records

### Functional Requirements

- Maintain a unified chronological history of significant project activity.
- Record data imports and retrievals.
- Record new observations and datasets.
- Record processing and analysis operations.
- Record model and parameter changes.
- Record new results and visualizations.
- Record research notes and decisions.
- Record changes to project organization and configurations.
- Link recorded activities to their relevant datasets, analyses, outputs and project components.
- Provide an accessible overview with the ability to inspect detailed analysis-level records.
- Preserve history when projects are reorganized, expanded, duplicated or continued.
- Integrate with the detailed analysis-level activity and reproducibility records defined in Section 5.5.

### Design Guidance

Project history should provide an overview of the investigation rather than duplicate the detailed activity-recording system established in Section 5.5.

The project-level history should connect to detailed analysis records so users can move from an overview of project activity to the underlying methods, parameters, datasets and results.

## 6.4.7 Project Portability, Export and Recovery

### Functional Requirements

- Support exporting and importing complete projects.
- Support project backups, transfers and long-term archival.
- Preserve project organization, datasets, metadata, provenance, analyses, configurations, visualizations, references and history.
- Preserve relationships between original data, processed data, analyses and results.
- Identify external dependencies and resources that cannot be included in an export.
- Clearly identify missing software, libraries, datasets or other dependencies when importing.
- Preserve licensing restrictions and respect data redistribution limitations.
- Allow users to export selected project components as well as entire projects.
- Make exported research understandable outside the application wherever practical.
- Support recovery from project loss or corruption through appropriate backup and restoration capabilities.
- Preserve project integrity when transferring between installations or supported platforms.

### Design Guidance

Portability, backup and recovery should be considered from the beginning, even if exact export formats and implementation methods are determined later.

This functionality should align with the portable analysis package requirements established in Section 5.5.

## 6.4.8 Project Duplication and Templates

### Functional Requirements — Project Duplication

- Allow users to create independent copies of existing projects.
- Support creating new versions of investigations involving the same astronomical target but different observations, datasets, methods or research objectives.
- Allow straightforward project copying for backup, experimentation or alternative approaches.
- Preserve the original project unchanged.
- Allow users to choose which components are copied, where practical.
- Maintain clear relationships between duplicated projects and their source projects.
- Ensure subsequent changes to either project do not unintentionally affect the other.
- Preserve relevant provenance, configurations, documentation and research history.
- Allow duplicated projects to evolve independently.

### Functional Requirements — Reusable Project Templates

- Allow users to create reusable templates from existing projects or define them independently.
- Include organizational structures, categories, configurations, workflows and documentation arrangements.
- Support different templates for different research disciplines and investigation types.
- Allow templates to be modified and reused across multiple projects.
- Avoid automatically copying large datasets or treating previous scientific conclusions as applicable to new investigations.
- Identify required resources, dependencies and configurations when creating a project from a template.
- Allow templates to provide a consistent starting structure without restricting subsequent project organization.

### Design Guidance

Project duplication and templates serve different purposes:

- **Duplication:** Creates an independent copy of an existing investigation. This is particularly useful for alternative versions of research involving the same target.
- **Template:** Provides a reusable starting structure for a new investigation.

Neither operation should unintentionally alter the original project or compromise the provenance of its contents.

## 6.4.9 Project Access and Permissions

### Functional Requirements

- Projects are private by default.
- Users retain control over who can access their projects.
- Access permissions must be explicit and understandable.
- No project data, research materials or results may be shared without user authorization.
- Support different permission levels, such as viewing, contributing and managing a project.
- Allow users to review and change access permissions.
- Preserve project ownership and attribution.
- Design the project system to accommodate future collaboration and sharing features.
- Ensure project access controls apply consistently to associated materials and resources.

### Design Guidance

- Keep project access controls straightforward.
- Avoid exposing private research through shared resources or external integrations.
- Ensure permissions are respected across project materials, datasets and associated resources.
- Coordinate detailed collaboration, group and public-sharing functionality with Section 8.
- Do not assume that access to a shared scientific resource grants access to the private projects using it.

## 6.4.10 Project Status and Lifecycle

### Functional Requirements

- Provide an overview of the user's projects.
- Support statuses such as Active, Paused, Completed and Archived.
- Allow users to change project status.
- Preserve project contents, history and accessibility regardless of status.
- Allow archived projects to be retrieved and reactivated.
- Archiving must not delete or invalidate research materials.
- Support searching, filtering and organizing projects by status.
- Allow projects to evolve between statuses as research progresses.

### Design Guidance

Project status should help users manage and navigate their research without imposing unnecessary restrictions on how investigations progress.

A completed or archived project should remain available for reference, reproduction, comparison and further investigation.

## 6.4.11 Exploratory Workspaces and Project Transfer

### Purpose

Scientific exploration should not require users to create a formal research project before they can begin investigating data.

Users should be able to retrieve, inspect, compare and analyze scientific resources within exploratory workspaces. When an investigation develops into a more substantial research effort, users should be able to transfer selected work into an existing or newly created project.

Exploratory workspaces and research projects serve different purposes. They should remain distinct while supporting a continuous research workflow.

### Functional Requirements — Exploratory Workspaces

- Allow users to conduct exploratory scientific work without first creating a formal project.
- Support temporary and ongoing exploration of datasets, observations, references, analyses and visualizations.
- Allow users to deliberately save exploratory workspace contents independently of formal projects.
- Preserve saved exploratory work across application sessions.
- Allow users to reopen and continue previously saved exploratory work.
- Support organization of saved exploratory materials without requiring the full organizational structure of a formal project.
- Preserve relevant datasets, metadata, provenance, processing history, analytical configurations, visualizations, annotations and notes.
- Distinguish unsaved temporary workspace activity from deliberately saved exploratory work.
- Allow users to discard temporary exploratory activity without affecting saved research.
- Do not automatically convert every exploratory workspace into a formal project.

### Functional Requirements — Transfer to Research Projects

- Allow users to transfer selected exploratory workspace materials into an existing project or use them to establish a new project.
- Allow users to select which datasets, observations, analyses, models, visualizations, references, configurations, annotations, notes and other materials are transferred.
- Preserve relationships between transferred materials.
- Preserve original data, derived-data relationships, metadata, provenance, processing history and relevant analytical configurations.
- Preserve relevant annotations, research notes, assumptions and decisions.
- Maintain links to original source resources and external dependencies.
- Identify missing or unavailable dependencies that may affect the transferred work.
- Ensure transferred materials become properly associated with the destination project.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of large datasets when compatible shared resources can be referenced safely.
- Preserve the original saved exploratory workspace unless the user explicitly chooses to remove it.
- Ensure subsequent changes to the project do not unintentionally modify the original saved exploratory workspace.
- Allow users to revisit the original exploratory work and understand its relationship to the resulting project.
- Record significant transfer activity where appropriate for research history and traceability.

### Design Guidance

Exploratory workspaces should provide a lower-commitment environment for investigating scientific resources and developing possible research directions.

For example, a user may retrieve several astronomical observations, inspect their contents, compare spectra and test preliminary processing methods before deciding whether the investigation warrants a formal project.

The user should be able to save that exploratory work without creating a project. If the investigation develops into a more substantial study, selected materials can then be transferred into a project while retaining their scientific relationships and history.

The application should preserve the distinction between:

- **Temporary exploration:** Work that has not been deliberately saved and may be discarded.
- **Saved exploratory work:** Independently retained research activity that does not yet belong to a formal project.
- **Project-associated research:** Persistent investigations organized and managed through the project system.

Exploration should not be unnecessarily restricted by project-management requirements. Transferring work into a project should not compromise the continuity, independence or provenance of the original investigation.

Coordinate exploratory retrieval and import workflows with Section 4.5, local resource retention and cache protection with Section 4.9, and analysis reproducibility and traceability with Section 5.5.

## Future Functional Possibilities

The following possibilities may be considered as the project system develops. They are not additional initial requirements:

- More advanced project organization and customizable project structures.
- Advanced project dashboards and research-progress summaries.
- More sophisticated project relationship visualizations.
- Additional project-management and collaboration capabilities developed in coordination with Section 8.
- Advanced project comparison and management across large collections of investigations.

These possibilities should be evaluated according to actual user needs, scientific value, technical complexity and their relationship with the rest of the application.

## Implementation Status

All functional requirements documented in this subsection are agreed design requirements for the application. They have not yet been implemented or technically validated.

The following remain for later research and planning:

- Exact project and workspace interface arrangements.
- Internal project data structures and storage architecture.
- Methods for referencing and sharing underlying datasets without unnecessary duplication.
- Project export, import, backup and recovery formats.
- Implementation of project duplication and reusable templates.
- Project permission and access-control architecture.
- Integration with the analysis history and reproducibility systems in Section 5.5.
- Detailed project-management and navigation interfaces.
- Storage and persistence arrangements for saved exploratory workspaces.
- Transfer mechanisms for moving selected exploratory materials into existing or new projects while preserving relationships and provenance.

These decisions should be informed by scientific, technical, architectural and usability research rather than fixed prematurely.

## General Principles

The project system should:

- Accommodate both object-focused and research-question-focused investigations.
- Support multi-disciplinary research across scientific workspaces.
- Allow flexible user-defined organization.
- Preserve shared data relationships and scientific provenance.
- Support long-term research continuity.
- Maintain project-specific configurations and comprehensive history.
- Support portability, backup, recovery, duplication and reusable templates.
- Protect private research through explicit access controls.
- Integrate with shared scientific tools and resources.
- Remain adaptable as the application expands.
- Distinguish temporary exploration, saved exploratory work and formal project research.
- Allow users to transfer exploratory work into projects without losing scientific context, relationships or provenance.

The project system should function as a persistent foundation for scientific research rather than simply a file-management feature.
