# 7.9 — Educational Content Quality and Validation

## 7.9.1 Scientific Accuracy and Reliability

Educational material must accurately represent established scientific knowledge, mathematical relationships and scientific methodologies.

Content should be developed using reliable scientific sources and appropriate subject knowledge.

This applies to:

- Astronomy and astrophysics explanations.
- Physical and chemical principles.
- Mathematical equations and derivations.
- Scientific models and theoretical descriptions.
- Observational techniques and methodologies.
- Spectroscopic and photometric explanations.
- Atmospheric science and astrochemistry.
- Computational and numerical methods.
- Educational demonstrations and simulations.
- Explanations of scientific results and interpretations.

Educational material should not simplify scientific concepts to the point of introducing inaccuracies or misleading explanations.

Where simplifications are necessary for introductory material, their purpose and limitations should be communicated appropriately.

The application should distinguish between established scientific principles, approximations, theoretical models, empirical relationships and developing areas of research.

Scientific accuracy should take priority over making an explanation appear simpler or more definitive than the evidence supports.

These requirements should align with the scientific integrity principles established in Section 5.

## 7.9.2 Source Quality and References

Official educational content should be supported by appropriate and reliable sources.

Potential source types include:

- Peer-reviewed scientific publications.
- Established scientific textbooks.
- University educational material.
- Official scientific documentation.
- Established astronomical databases and reference resources.
- Original publications describing scientific methods and discoveries.
- Authoritative mathematical, physical and chemical references.
- Reputable educational lectures and learning resources.

Sources should be selected according to their relevance, scientific authority, accuracy, completeness and suitability for the subject being explained.

Where appropriate, educational material should retain references to the sources used in its development.

The application should distinguish between:

- Sources directly supporting the explanation.
- Original research or publications establishing particular findings or methods.
- Additional references recommended for further study.
- General educational material used to explain background concepts.

Source information should be sufficiently detailed to allow users to locate and independently inspect the original material.

Where sources are outdated, inaccessible, superseded or subject to significant corrections, these circumstances should be considered when maintaining the associated educational content.

Detailed external resource evaluation and licensing requirements should follow Section 4, while publication discovery and bibliographic management should follow Section 7.6.

## 7.9.3 Fact, Theory, Hypothesis and Scientific Uncertainty

Educational content should communicate the scientific status of the information being presented.

Users should be able to distinguish between:

- Established scientific facts and principles.
- Widely accepted scientific theories.
- Theoretical models and predictions.
- Empirical relationships and approximations.
- Scientific hypotheses.
- Proposed explanations and interpretations.
- Candidate discoveries and preliminary findings.
- Unresolved scientific questions.
- Areas where competing interpretations remain.

The application should avoid presenting developing or uncertain scientific interpretations as universally established knowledge.

Where scientific understanding is incomplete, educational explanations should communicate relevant uncertainties and limitations.

Where different scientific models or interpretations exist, the application should explain their differences and the evidence supporting them where appropriate.

Scientific explanations should also communicate when conclusions depend on particular observational conditions, model assumptions, datasets or analytical methods.

The objective is to teach users how scientific knowledge is established and developed, not simply present conclusions without their supporting context.

These distinctions should remain consistent with the scientific interpretation and uncertainty requirements in Section 5.

## 7.9.4 Mathematical and Technical Accuracy

Mathematical, physical and computational explanations require particular attention to correctness.

Educational content involving equations, calculations, units and scientific methods should be checked for accuracy and appropriate application.

This should include:

- Correct mathematical expressions and notation.
- Accurate definitions of variables and constants.
- Appropriate units and dimensional consistency.
- Correct mathematical derivations.
- Valid assumptions and applicable conditions.
- Appropriate numerical methods.
- Accurate descriptions of physical and chemical relationships.
- Correct statistical and uncertainty explanations.
- Appropriate treatment of approximations and limitations.
- Consistency between educational explanations and implemented application functionality.

Where equations or methods are presented, their original sources should be identified where practical.

Worked examples should be checked for calculation errors, incorrect units, misleading precision and inappropriate assumptions.

Where mathematical methods have multiple formulations or areas of applicability, educational material should explain relevant distinctions.

Computational demonstrations should also be checked to ensure that their behaviour and outputs are consistent with the scientific principles they are intended to illustrate.

These requirements should complement the mathematical education framework established in Section 7.5 and the technical validation requirements in Section 5.

## 7.9.5 Educational Quality and Clarity

Scientific correctness alone does not guarantee that educational material is effective.

Official educational content should also be evaluated for clarity, structure, accessibility and suitability for its intended audience.

This should include:

- Clear explanations of scientific terminology.
- Logical organization and progression of subjects.
- Appropriate introduction of new concepts.
- Clear definitions of mathematical symbols and scientific quantities.
- Relevant examples and practical applications.
- Appropriate use of equations, diagrams, graphs and visualizations.
- Connections between related scientific concepts.
- Identification of relevant prerequisites.
- Appropriate explanations of assumptions and limitations.
- Avoidance of unnecessary repetition and confusing terminology.

Material should be suitable for its intended learning level without unnecessarily restricting access to advanced detail.

Introductory explanations should remain understandable without removing important scientific meaning.

Advanced material should preserve mathematical and theoretical depth without assuming that every user has the same background knowledge.

Where practical, explanations should connect theoretical concepts to actual astronomical observations, scientific methods and application functionality.

Educational content should also be maintained consistently across the knowledge library, learning paths, interactive exercises and contextual assistance.

The application should avoid situations where different educational components provide contradictory explanations of the same scientific concept.

## 7.9.6 Review and Validation of Educational Content

Official educational material should undergo appropriate review before being treated as established application educational content.

The depth of review should be proportionate to the subject, complexity and scientific significance of the material.

Potential review activities include:

**Source verification**
- Checking that supporting references are genuine and relevant.
- Confirming that scientific claims are supported by the cited material.
- Identifying outdated or superseded sources.

**Scientific review**
- Checking scientific principles, terminology and interpretations.
- Evaluating assumptions and limitations.
- Identifying unsupported or misleading conclusions.

**Mathematical and technical review**
- Checking equations, derivations, calculations and units.
- Verifying numerical examples.
- Checking descriptions of scientific methods and computational procedures.

**Educational review**
- Evaluating clarity, structure and progression.
- Checking suitability for the intended learning level.
- Identifying confusing explanations or unnecessary complexity.
- Ensuring examples genuinely support the intended learning objectives.

**Practical review**
- Checking interactive demonstrations and examples.
- Confirming that educational material reflects actual application functionality.
- Identifying discrepancies between written explanations and implemented tools.

The application should maintain appropriate distinctions between content that is drafted, under review, reviewed and approved for official educational use.

Where practical, significant review decisions and revisions should be recorded.

The level of review required should depend on the material. A simple terminology explanation may not require the same depth of review as a specialist explanation of radiative transfer or atmospheric retrieval methodology.

Scientific review and educational review should be treated as related but distinct activities.

## 7.9.7 AI-Generated and AI-Assisted Educational Content

AI may be used to assist with developing, explaining, summarizing and maintaining educational material.

Potential applications include:

- Drafting scientific explanations.
- Simplifying complex concepts for introductory learners.
- Providing more detailed explanations for advanced users.
- Explaining equations and mathematical derivations.
- Creating worked examples.
- Summarizing scientific publications.
- Generating educational exercises.
- Providing contextual explanations within application workflows.
- Identifying possible inconsistencies or outdated information.
- Helping maintain educational material as application capabilities develop.

However, AI-generated content must not automatically be treated as scientifically accurate or approved educational material.

Appropriate verification should take place before AI-generated content is incorporated into official educational resources.

AI-assisted material should be evaluated against reliable sources and the relevant scientific and mathematical requirements.

The application should distinguish between:

- AI-generated explanations.
- Human-authored educational material.
- AI-assisted material that has undergone human review.
- Official educational material that has been reviewed and approved.

AI-generated content must not fabricate scientific publications, references, equations, measurements or observational findings.

Where AI provides an explanation or summary of scientific material, users should be able to identify its source information and inspect the underlying scientific material where available.

AI assistance should support educational understanding without replacing independent verification or scientific judgment.

These requirements should align with the future AI-assisted capabilities described in Section 5.7 and the scientific integrity requirements in Section 5.1.

## 7.9.8 Community-Created Content Quality

Community-created educational material should remain distinguishable from official application educational content.

Users should be able to contribute educational resources while retaining the ability to identify their origin and review status.

Potential community content categories include:

- Personal notes and explanations.
- User-created tutorials.
- Educational exercises.
- Scientific demonstrations.
- Custom learning paths.
- Shared reference material.
- Educational guides for application tools.
- Community-created examples and datasets.

Community resources should not automatically receive the same review or approval status as official educational material.

Where community review or feedback is available, its nature and limitations should be communicated.

The application may support different levels of community resource assessment, including:

- Creator-provided material.
- Community feedback received.
- Tested or used by other users.
- Independently reviewed.
- Scientifically evaluated where appropriate.

These classifications should accurately describe what has taken place.

A resource should not be presented as scientifically validated simply because it has been downloaded, positively rated or widely shared.

Community review and resource-sharing procedures should follow Section 7.8.

## 7.9.9 Content Updates and Scientific Developments

Educational material should be maintained as scientific knowledge, methodologies and application capabilities evolve.

The application should provide a means of identifying content that may require review or revision.

Potential reasons for updates include:

- New scientific discoveries.
- Changes in established scientific understanding.
- New observational evidence.
- Revised theoretical models.
- Corrections to previously published research.
- Improved mathematical or computational methods.
- Changes to scientific reference databases.
- Changes to external resources or scientific documentation.
- Changes to application functionality.
- Identification of errors or outdated explanations.

Where an educational resource is updated, relevant changes should be recorded where appropriate.

The application should avoid silently replacing important scientific explanations when doing so could affect the user's understanding or previously saved material.

Where a scientific interpretation has changed, the educational content should communicate the development and explain relevant differences where useful.

Historical material may remain accessible when it provides important scientific or educational context.

Content maintenance should also consider relationships between educational resources. Updating an underlying scientific explanation may require checking related tutorials, courses, exercises, equations and contextual guidance.

## 7.9.10 Error Reporting and Corrections

Users should have a straightforward way to report potential errors or problems in educational content.

Reports may concern:

- Incorrect scientific explanations.
- Mathematical or calculation errors.
- Incorrect units or notation.
- Missing or misleading references.
- Outdated scientific information.
- Confusing or incomplete explanations.
- Incorrect descriptions of application functionality.
- Errors in educational demonstrations or examples.
- Inappropriate scientific simplifications.

Reported issues should be reviewable by the relevant content maintainers.

The application should distinguish between:

- Confirmed errors.
- Potential inaccuracies requiring investigation.
- Differences in scientific interpretation.
- Suggestions for improved clarity.
- Requests for additional educational material.

Where an error is confirmed, the relevant material should be corrected.

Significant corrections should be documented sufficiently to maintain an understandable record of the change.

Where an issue cannot immediately be resolved, the application should be able to record that it is under investigation rather than silently treating the existing material as unquestionably correct.

Users who report issues may be informed of relevant outcomes where practical.

The correction process should encourage users to contribute to improving educational quality without treating every disagreement as evidence of an error.

## Future Possibilities

The following capabilities may be considered as the educational system develops:

- More extensive scientific peer-review processes.
- Independent expert review of specialist educational material.
- Community-assisted educational review.
- Advanced automated mathematical and reference checking.
- Automated identification of potentially outdated educational content.
- More extensive content versioning and review histories.
- Formal educational quality assessments.
- Educational content certification or approval systems.
- Advanced consistency checking across the entire educational library.
- Automated identification of conflicting explanations or duplicated material.
- More extensive verification of interactive demonstrations and scientific examples.
- Review workflows involving external educators and scientific specialists.

These possibilities should be evaluated according to scientific value, educational benefit, technical feasibility and the resources required to maintain them.

## Initial Implementation Scope

Initial educational quality and validation capabilities should prioritize:

- Reliable scientific sources for official educational material.
- Accurate scientific and mathematical explanations.
- Clear identification of uncertainty and scientific limitations.
- Appropriate educational structure and readability.
- Verification of important equations, calculations and units.
- Review of official educational content before approval.
- Clear distinction between official and community-created material.
- Identification of AI-generated or AI-assisted educational content.
- A basic process for reporting educational errors.
- Correction and maintenance of outdated or inaccurate material.
- Consistency between educational explanations and implemented application functionality.

More extensive expert review, automated validation, community review systems and advanced content-quality monitoring can be developed later.

## Implementation Considerations

- Educational quality should be maintained throughout the application's development rather than treated as a final documentation task.
- Official educational material should have identifiable sources and an appropriate review status.
- Review depth should be proportionate to scientific complexity and educational importance.
- Scientific correctness and educational effectiveness should be evaluated separately.
- AI-generated content should not automatically become official educational material.
- Community contributions should retain their origin and review status.
- Significant corrections and revisions should be traceable.
- Educational material should remain consistent with actual application capabilities and scientific methods.
- Quality and validation requirements should reuse the wider scientific integrity and validation framework established in Section 5.
- External source evaluation and licensing should follow Section 4.
- Educational resource organization and maintenance should align with Sections 7.1–7.8.

## General Principle

The application's educational system should be scientifically trustworthy, educationally effective, transparent about uncertainty and limitations, and maintained as scientific knowledge develops.

Educational material should be supported by reliable sources, reviewed at an appropriate level and presented in a manner that accommodates different levels of prior knowledge without compromising scientific depth.

Official, AI-assisted, external and community-created content should have clearly identifiable origins and appropriate review status.

The objective is to establish an educational environment users can rely on for learning and scientific development while encouraging independent verification, critical thinking and an accurate understanding of how scientific knowledge is established and revised.
