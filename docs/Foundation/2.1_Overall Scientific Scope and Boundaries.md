# 2.1 — Overall Scientific Scope and Boundaries

## Purpose

Establish the overall scientific scope of the application and define the principles governing which areas of astronomy and related scientific research it should accommodate.

The application should provide a broad foundation for astronomical investigation rather than being restricted to a single object type, scientific discipline, observational technique or research methodology.

Its initial development should concentrate on a manageable set of priority scientific areas while preserving the ability to expand into additional disciplines and research subjects as the application matures.

The scientific scope should be broad in ambition but deliberate in implementation. Not every scientific area needs to receive equal development priority, and inclusion within the long-term vision does not automatically establish a requirement for immediate implementation.

## 2.1.1 Broad Scientific Scope

**Core requirements**

- Establish astronomy as the application's central scientific domain.
- Support research involving astronomical objects, systems, phenomena and scientific questions.
- Accommodate multiple astronomical disciplines and related areas of scientific investigation.
- Support observational, theoretical, computational and interdisciplinary research.
- Allow scientific investigations to involve individual objects, multiple related objects, larger systems and broader astronomical phenomena.
- Preserve opportunities to incorporate additional scientific subjects as research requirements evolve.
- Avoid permanently restricting the application to its initial scientific disciplines or research methods.
- Maintain scientific relevance when considering areas of research beyond traditional astronomy.

**Design guidance**

The application should be broad enough to accommodate the diversity of modern astronomical research without attempting to implement every possible discipline or capability at the beginning.

Its scientific scope should establish a coherent direction while allowing individual research areas to develop according to their own requirements.

## 2.1.2 Initial Scientific Priorities

The application should establish a defined set of scientific priorities to guide its initial development.

**Core requirements**

- Identify initial priority disciplines according to their scientific value, practical relevance and development feasibility.
- Establish exoplanet science, planetary science, stellar astronomy and observational astronomy as initial priority areas.
- Recognize the relationships between these disciplines and their shared scientific requirements.
- Allow other astronomical disciplines to remain within the broader application vision without requiring immediate implementation.
- Avoid treating initial priorities as permanent restrictions on future development.
- Reevaluate priorities as scientific, technical and user requirements become clearer.

**Design guidance**

Initial priorities should provide a practical starting point for development rather than an exhaustive definition of the application's scientific identity.

The selection of priority disciplines should also consider opportunities for shared scientific capabilities, datasets, methods and research workflows.

The detailed priority disciplines are established in Sections 2.2–2.6.

## 2.1.3 Astronomy and Related Scientific Fields

The application should primarily focus on astronomy while accommodating related scientific fields when they contribute meaningfully to astronomical research.

**Core requirements**

- Support scientific areas that directly investigate astronomical objects, systems and phenomena.
- Consider related disciplines when their methods or subject matter provide meaningful contributions to astronomical investigations.
- Accommodate relevant connections with physics, chemistry, geology, planetary science, atmospheric science, mathematics, statistics and computational science.
- Distinguish between the application's central scientific focus and supporting scientific disciplines.
- Evaluate additional fields according to their relevance to the application's research objectives.
- Avoid expanding the scientific scope indiscriminately into unrelated areas.

**Design guidance**

Astronomy is inherently interdisciplinary. Investigating astronomical environments and phenomena can require methods and knowledge from several scientific fields.

The application should accommodate these relationships without becoming an unrestricted general-purpose scientific software platform.

Related disciplines should be included where they provide clear scientific value to astronomical research.

## 2.1.4 Scientific Disciplines, Objects, Methods and Workspaces

The application must distinguish between four related but conceptually different categories.

**Scientific disciplines**

Areas of scientific study, such as exoplanet science, planetary science, stellar astronomy, observational astronomy and astrophysics.

**Astronomical objects and research subjects**

The objects, systems and phenomena being investigated, such as stars, planets, moons, planetary systems, galaxies, nebulae and black holes.

**Research methods**

The scientific approaches used to investigate research subjects, such as spectroscopy, photometry, imaging, time-series analysis, modelling, simulation and statistical analysis.

**Application workspaces**

The organizational environments through which users access scientific capabilities and conduct research activities.

**Core requirements**

- Maintain these distinctions throughout the application's scientific organization and documentation.
- Avoid treating an astronomical object as a scientific discipline simply because it has a dedicated research area.
- Avoid assuming that a particular research method belongs exclusively to one scientific discipline.
- Allow multiple disciplines to investigate the same astronomical object or phenomenon.
- Allow individual research methods to be used across different scientific areas where appropriate.
- Ensure workspace organization reflects application usability and research requirements rather than defining the scientific scope itself.

**Design guidance**

These categories should inform one another without being treated as interchangeable.

For example, spectroscopy is a research method that may be used in stellar astronomy, exoplanet science and planetary science. A planet is an astronomical object that may be investigated through several disciplines and methods.

The application's organization should accommodate these relationships without creating unnecessary restrictions.

## 2.1.5 Scientific Breadth and Specialized Depth

The application should support a broad range of scientific subjects while allowing individual disciplines to retain the specialized capabilities they require.

**Core requirements**

- Provide a consistent scientific foundation across the application.
- Allow different disciplines to maintain specialized terminology, methods, data requirements and research approaches.
- Avoid forcing scientifically different disciplines into identical analytical workflows.
- Support shared capabilities where they are scientifically and technically appropriate.
- Allow specialized scientific functionality to develop independently when necessary.
- Preserve opportunities for interdisciplinary research.
- Evaluate scientific capabilities according to the requirements of the disciplines they support.

**Design guidance**

A broad scientific platform should not attempt to make every research area function identically.

Consistency should exist where it improves usability, data handling and research continuity. Specialized differences should remain where they reflect genuine scientific requirements.

## 2.1.6 Scientific Scope and Development Priorities

The inclusion of a scientific discipline or research subject within the application's scope should be distinguished from its development status.

**Core requirements**

- Distinguish initial development priorities from longer-term scientific possibilities.
- Identify scientific areas that are supported, planned, under investigation or reserved for future consideration.
- Avoid implying that every subject within the broader scope already has dedicated functionality.
- Evaluate new scientific areas according to research relevance, scientific requirements, technical feasibility and available resources.
- Consider shared capabilities that may support multiple disciplines.
- Avoid implementing incomplete scientific functionality merely to claim broader subject coverage.
- Preserve the ability to revise development priorities when new evidence or requirements emerge.

**Design guidance**

A scientific area may belong within the application's intended scope long before its specialized tools or workflows are implemented.

The application should communicate this distinction clearly so that its documented ambitions are not mistaken for existing functionality.

## 2.1.7 Scientific Scope Boundaries

The application should maintain a clear scientific identity while remaining open to meaningful expansion.

**Core requirements**

- Keep astronomy and directly related scientific investigation at the center of the application.
- Evaluate proposed additions according to their contribution to astronomical research.
- Avoid expanding into unrelated scientific or general-purpose software functionality without a clear justification.
- Distinguish supporting scientific functionality from the primary research subjects of the application.
- Consider whether proposed additions belong within the application itself, an external integration or a separate specialized tool.
- Preserve the ability to use external scientific resources without requiring the application to reproduce every external capability.
- Reevaluate scope boundaries when new scientific requirements justify doing so.

**Design guidance**

The application should not attempt to replace every scientific software package or provide every conceivable research capability.

Its purpose is to provide a coherent astronomy research environment that integrates appropriate capabilities and resources while recognizing when specialized external tools are more suitable.

## General Principles

- Astronomy is the application's central scientific domain.
- The scientific scope should be broad, coherent and adaptable.
- Initial development should concentrate on clearly defined priority disciplines.
- Initial priorities must not become permanent scientific restrictions.
- Related scientific fields should be accommodated when they contribute meaningfully to astronomical research.
- Scientific disciplines, astronomical objects, research methods and application workspaces must remain conceptually distinct.
- Broad scientific coverage should coexist with specialized methodological depth.
- Inclusion within the scientific scope does not imply immediate implementation.
- New scientific areas should be evaluated according to their relevance, value and feasibility.
- The application should maintain a clear scientific identity rather than becoming an unrestricted general-purpose platform.

**General principle:** The application should establish a broad and adaptable scientific foundation centered on astronomy, with clearly defined initial priorities and the flexibility to accommodate additional research disciplines and subjects as its capabilities develop.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes the overall scientific scope and its boundaries.

It does not define the detailed requirements of individual scientific disciplines, specific astronomical object catalogues, analytical methods, scientific tools, data sources or application workspaces.

These matters are addressed in the remaining subsections of Section 2 and the relevant sections of the Foundations documentation.
