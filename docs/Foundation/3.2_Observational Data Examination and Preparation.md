

# 3.2 — Observational Data Examination and Preparation

## Purpose

The application should provide comprehensive capabilities for examining, understanding and preparing observational data for scientific analysis.

Astronomical observations may originate from different instruments, facilities, archives and research environments. They may use different file formats, measurement conventions, coordinate systems, units, metadata structures and levels of completeness.

Users should be able to inspect these observations, understand their contents and determine whether they are suitable for particular analytical operations.

The application should support appropriate data preparation while preserving original measurements, documenting transformations and maintaining the scientific context required for reliable interpretation and reproducibility.

These capabilities should provide a common foundation for specialized analytical tools throughout the application.

## 3.2.1 Data Inspection and Examination

### Core Requirements

- Allow users to inspect observational datasets before applying analytical methods.
- Display relevant data structures, measurements, columns, dimensions and metadata.
- Support inspection of both numerical and non-numerical information where appropriate.
- Allow users to examine individual measurements, selected regions and larger portions of a dataset.
- Provide appropriate tabular, graphical or other visual representations.
- Identify missing, invalid, incomplete or non-finite values.
- Distinguish between measured values, derived values, metadata and unavailable information.
- Allow users to inspect relevant information without modifying the underlying dataset.
- Support examination of datasets that contain multiple measurements, observations or data components.

### Design Guidance

Data inspection should help users understand what a dataset contains before deciding how to use it.

The application should not assume that imported data is immediately suitable for analysis. Users should be able to examine its structure, identify potential problems and understand its limitations before proceeding.

## 3.2.2 Scientific Data Formats and Representations

### Core Requirements

- Support a broad range of astronomical observational data formats as scientific capabilities develop.
- Accommodate common structured and unstructured scientific data representations where appropriate.
- Support tabular measurements, numerical arrays, time-series data, spectral data and image-based data.
- Accommodate established astronomical formats, including FITS and relevant extensions.
- Support commonly encountered text-based and delimited data formats.
- Preserve relevant information when reading different data representations.
- Distinguish between a file's physical format and the scientific meaning of its contents.
- Identify unsupported or partially supported formats clearly.
- Avoid assuming that datasets with similar file extensions necessarily have equivalent structures or scientific meaning.

### Design Guidance

Astronomical research uses a wide variety of data formats. The application should not be permanently restricted to a small number of formats or assume that all observations can be represented as simple tables.

Support for individual formats should be introduced according to scientific relevance, availability of reliable parsing methods and practical development requirements.

Where possible, users should be able to examine supported datasets without first converting them manually into a single application-specific format.

## 3.2.3 Metadata and Scientific Context

### Core Requirements

- Provide access to relevant observational metadata.
- Preserve information about the originating instrument, facility, observation and data source where available.
- Support metadata describing observation time, location, wavelength coverage, spectral resolution, exposure information and other relevant parameters.
- Accommodate coordinate systems, reference frames and measurement conventions.
- Preserve units and descriptions associated with scientific measurements.
- Distinguish between metadata supplied by the original source and information added during subsequent processing.
- Identify missing or incomplete metadata that may affect analysis.
- Allow users to inspect metadata relevant to a selected analytical operation.
- Preserve relationships between datasets and their associated metadata.

### Design Guidance

Metadata is essential to understanding and interpreting astronomical observations.

Two datasets containing apparently similar measurements may require different analytical treatment because they were collected using different instruments, configurations, calibration procedures or observational conditions.

The application should preserve available context rather than reducing observations to numerical values without their associated scientific information.

## 3.2.4 Measurement Units and Data Conventions

### Core Requirements

- Identify and preserve known measurement units.
- Support appropriate scientific unit conversions.
- Accommodate different representations of equivalent physical quantities.
- Support relevant coordinate, wavelength, frequency, time and measurement conventions.
- Distinguish between known units, inferred units and unknown units.
- Avoid silently assigning units when the original information does not establish them.
- Communicate when a conversion requires additional information or assumptions.
- Preserve original values when conversions or transformations are performed.
- Make the units and conventions used in analytical operations clear to users.

### Design Guidance

Astronomical datasets may express equivalent physical quantities using different units or conventions.

For example, spectral observations may use wavelength, frequency or wavenumber representations. These may be convertible, but the conversion must preserve the physical meaning and account for the relevant conventions.

The application should make such transformations traceable and avoid treating numerical similarity as evidence of scientific equivalence.

## 3.2.5 Data Selection, Filtering and Extraction

### Core Requirements

- Allow users to select relevant portions of observational datasets.
- Support filtering according to appropriate measurement values, metadata, coordinates, time intervals and other available properties.
- Support extraction of selected measurements, regions, wavelength intervals or time ranges where appropriate.
- Allow users to examine selected subsets without unintentionally modifying the original dataset.
- Support selection of multiple regions or groups of measurements where scientifically appropriate.
- Preserve relationships between extracted data and their original sources.
- Make selection criteria understandable and reviewable.
- Allow selected data to be used in subsequent analytical operations.
- Distinguish between temporary selections and separately saved derived datasets.

### Design Guidance

Research frequently requires examination of only part of an observation.

Users may need to isolate a wavelength interval, select a time range, examine a particular image region or exclude measurements that do not meet specified criteria.

Selection and extraction should therefore be available as general capabilities while respecting the scientific structure of each data type.

## 3.2.6 Data Preparation and Transformation

### Core Requirements

Support appropriate data preparation operations, including where scientifically applicable:

- Data type and structure conversion.
- Unit conversion.
- Coordinate and reference-frame transformations.
- Resampling and interpolation.
- Normalization and scaling.
- Background or baseline correction.
- Calibration and instrument-related corrections.
- Noise reduction and filtering.
- Removal or masking of unsuitable measurements.
- Alignment and registration of compatible datasets.
- Preparation of data for specific analytical methods.

For applicable operations:

- Identify the purpose and assumptions of the transformation.
- Allow users to review relevant settings and parameters.
- Preserve the original dataset.
- Create identifiable derived outputs.
- Record the relationship between original and transformed data.
- Communicate when an operation may affect scientific interpretation.
- Avoid presenting an optional preparation procedure as universally necessary.

### Design Guidance

Data preparation should be driven by the requirements of the intended scientific analysis.

Not every dataset requires every preparation operation. Some transformations may be inappropriate for particular instruments, measurement types or research questions.

The application should avoid automatically applying generic processing steps without establishing their suitability.

## 3.2.7 Missing, Invalid and Incomplete Data

### Core Requirements

- Identify missing measurements and unavailable values.
- Detect non-numeric values where numerical measurements are required.
- Identify non-finite numerical values, including `NaN` and positive or negative infinity.
- Detect malformed or structurally inconsistent data where possible.
- Distinguish between missing observations and measurements that are genuinely zero.
- Preserve available information when only part of a dataset is invalid.
- Communicate relevant data-quality problems to users.
- Allow appropriate exclusion, masking or handling of invalid values.
- Avoid silently replacing missing or invalid measurements with assumed values.
- Preserve records of any changes made to address data-quality problems.

### Design Guidance

Missing or invalid data should not automatically make an entire dataset unusable.

The application should identify the extent and location of problems and allow scientifically appropriate handling.

Where an operation cannot proceed reliably, the application should explain why rather than silently discarding information or generating misleading results.

## 3.2.8 Data Quality and Observational Limitations

### Core Requirements

- Support examination of observational quality and completeness.
- Identify relevant quality indicators supplied by data sources.
- Accommodate measurement uncertainty and associated error information.
- Support identification of outliers, artifacts and potentially unreliable measurements.
- Preserve quality flags and source-provided warnings.
- Allow users to examine limitations associated with observational resolution, sensitivity, coverage and sampling.
- Distinguish between source-provided quality assessments and application-generated assessments.
- Communicate when data limitations may affect the reliability of subsequent analysis.
- Avoid treating every available measurement as equally reliable.

### Design Guidance

Data quality should be considered in relation to the intended scientific operation.

A dataset may be adequate for exploratory visualization but unsuitable for a particular quantitative measurement.

The application should help users understand these distinctions without automatically declaring observations scientifically valid or invalid based on a single general-purpose quality measure.

Detailed validation, quality-control procedures and scientific safeguards are addressed in Section 5.

## 3.2.9 Original Data and Derived Dataset Management

### Core Requirements

- Preserve original observational data independently from subsequent preparation and processing.
- Distinguish original datasets from modified, transformed or derived datasets.
- Maintain relationships between source data and its derived versions.
- Allow multiple derived datasets to originate from the same source.
- Support independent preparation workflows using shared original observations.
- Preserve relevant metadata, units, source information and processing history.
- Allow users to compare original and derived data.
- Avoid overwriting original measurements during routine preparation.
- Make the origin and processing state of a dataset understandable.

### Design Guidance

Different research questions may require different preparation methods applied to the same original observation.

The application should allow these approaches to coexist without requiring users to create unnecessary copies of the original source or risk losing previous work.

The relationship between original data and derived datasets should remain clear throughout subsequent analysis.

## 3.2.10 Preparation for Specialized Scientific Analysis

### Core Requirements

- Allow general data-preparation capabilities to support specialized analytical tools.
- Identify the input requirements of individual scientific methods.
- Check whether a dataset contains the necessary measurements, metadata and conventions.
- Communicate additional preparation requirements before an operation is performed.
- Support method-specific preparation procedures where appropriate.
- Preserve the relationship between prepared data and the analytical operation that uses it.
- Avoid assuming that a dataset prepared for one method is automatically suitable for another.
- Allow users to review and reuse compatible prepared datasets.

### Design Guidance

Different analytical methods may require different forms of preparation.

A spectral fitting operation, for example, may require a particular axis representation, appropriate calibration and a defined treatment of uncertainties. An image-analysis operation may require different preparation steps.

The application should provide a common foundation for these activities while allowing specialized tools to establish their own scientific requirements.

## 3.2.11 Data Preparation Review and User Control

### Core Requirements

- Allow users to review preparation operations before applying them where appropriate.
- Make significant transformations and their relevant parameters understandable.
- Allow users to compare original and prepared data.
- Communicate warnings and potential consequences of preparation procedures.
- Support cancellation of operations before completion where technically possible.
- Preserve previous data states when a new preparation operation produces a derived result.
- Avoid silently applying consequential transformations.
- Allow users to decide whether prepared data is suitable for subsequent analysis.

### Design Guidance

Preparation should remain a scientifically meaningful activity rather than an invisible prerequisite performed without user awareness.

Users should be able to understand what has changed, why it changed and how the resulting dataset differs from its source.

The degree of automation and approval required should remain consistent with the controls established in Section 5.

## General Principles

Observational data examination and preparation should:

- Accommodate diverse astronomical data types and formats.
- Preserve original measurements and scientific context.
- Make metadata, units and conventions accessible.
- Support inspection before analysis.
- Provide appropriate selection, filtering and extraction capabilities.
- Support scientifically justified preparation and transformation.
- Identify missing, invalid and incomplete information.
- Preserve uncertainty and observational quality information.
- Maintain clear relationships between original and derived datasets.
- Evaluate compatibility with intended analytical methods.
- Keep users informed and in control of consequential operations.

## General Principle

Observational data should be treated as scientific evidence with associated context, limitations and provenance, not simply as numerical information to be converted and processed.

The application should make data easier to understand and prepare without obscuring its original meaning or compromising the integrity of subsequent analysis.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes general capabilities for examining and preparing observational data.

It does not define the detailed algorithms, instrument-specific calibration procedures, specialized analytical methods or implementation technologies required for individual operations.

Detailed spectral, photometric, imaging and other analytical capabilities are addressed in the remaining subsections of Section 3. Data discovery, retrieval, importing and external resource integration are addressed in Section 4. Scientific validation, quality control, uncertainty management and reproducibility are addressed in Section 5.
