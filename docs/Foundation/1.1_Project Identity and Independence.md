# 1.1 — Project Identity and Independence

## Purpose

Establish the application's identity as an entirely new and independent software project, developed with its own purpose, direction, architecture, codebase and development history.

The application must be treated as a distinct project rather than a continuation, rebuild, successor or extension of an existing application.

Its identity should develop around its own scientific objectives, intended users and long-term vision.

## 1.1.1 Independent Project Identity

**Core requirements**

- Establish the application as an entirely new and independent project.
- Maintain its own identity, purpose, development direction and long-term objectives.
- Maintain a separate codebase, architecture, documentation and development history.
- Establish independent project organization and development records.
- Avoid treating the requirements, limitations or design decisions of unrelated existing applications as inherited requirements.
- Evaluate existing software independently rather than assuming its architecture or functionality should be reproduced.
- Ensure the application's identity and direction are determined by its own documented vision and requirements.

**Design principle**

The application should be developed according to what best serves its intended scientific purpose, rather than being constrained by the history or design of another project.

## 1.1.2 Separation from Existing Applications

**Core requirements**

- Maintain clear independence from previous or existing software projects.
- Do not present the application as a successor, replacement, continuation or rebranding of another application.
- Do not inherit another application's name, acronym, branding or project identity.
- Do not automatically carry over previous application requirements, architecture, implementation decisions or development plans.
- Evaluate potentially useful existing functionality and approaches on their own merits.
- Preserve the distinction between independently developed application functionality and functionality provided by external software.
- Ensure documentation and development records clearly identify this application as its own project.

Existing scientific knowledge, general programming experience, established scientific methods and appropriately licensed external resources may still inform development. Independence does not require ignoring useful prior work or established scientific software.

However, any reuse of existing code, components, resources or documentation must be evaluated for technical suitability, licensing, attribution and compatibility. It must not compromise the application's independent identity or development direction.

## 1.1.3 Application Name and Acronym

**Core requirements**

- Establish a distinct and recognizable application name.
- Ensure the name appropriately reflects the application's scientific purpose and intended identity.
- Consider an acronym or abbreviated name where useful.
- Ensure the full name and acronym are consistent throughout application documentation, development records and eventual interface elements.
- Avoid names that unnecessarily restrict the application to one scientific discipline when its intended scope is broader.
- Evaluate potential names for clarity, memorability, suitability and potential conflicts with existing software or organizations.
- Maintain consistent naming once a final name has been approved.

**Current status**

The application's name and acronym have not yet been selected.

Naming should be considered after its identity and broader purpose are sufficiently established. No provisional name should be treated as an official commitment.

## 1.1.4 Branding and Visual Identity

**Core requirements**

- Establish an independent visual identity appropriate for professional scientific software.
- Develop branding that reflects astronomy, scientific investigation and the application's broader purpose.
- Maintain consistency between the application, its documentation and related project materials.
- Ensure branding remains recognizable as the application expands into additional scientific disciplines.
- Avoid visual identities that make the application appear limited to one particular astronomical subject.
- Distinguish application branding from scientific visualization conventions and data presentation.

**Future possibilities**

- Application logo and icon.
- Distinctive typography and visual identity.
- Branded application themes.
- Consistent documentation and presentation materials.
- Additional visual identity elements for future application-related resources.

Exact branding decisions remain open for later design work under Section 6.5.

## 1.1.5 Project Ownership and Development Independence

**Core requirements**

- Maintain the application as a distinct project with its own development records and source-control history.
- Preserve independent control over its requirements, architecture, implementation and future direction.
- Ensure development decisions are based on the application's documented objectives and approved requirements.
- Maintain clear ownership and attribution for original application code, documentation and other project materials.
- Identify external dependencies, integrations and reused components appropriately.
- Preserve the ability to modify or replace external dependencies without compromising the application's identity or unnecessarily restricting its development.
- Avoid unnecessary reliance on another application's infrastructure, development process or project organization.

The application should remain understandable and maintainable as an independent software project, even when developed with external libraries, tools or contributions.

## 1.1.6 Consistency of Identity and Documentation

**Core requirements**

- Use the approved application name and terminology consistently once established.
- Maintain consistent references across requirements, architecture, technical documentation, development records and user-facing materials.
- Identify the application clearly in its project documentation.
- Avoid conflicting names, acronyms or descriptions across different files.
- Update relevant references when an approved identity change occurs.
- Preserve historical documentation where appropriate while clearly identifying superseded names or terminology.
- Ensure future AI development partners and contributors understand the application's independent identity and purpose.

The application's identity should be documented in a central authoritative location and referenced consistently throughout the project.

## General Principles

- The application is an entirely independent project.
- It has its own scientific purpose, identity and development direction.
- It is not a continuation, successor or rebranding of an existing application.
- Existing scientific knowledge and appropriately evaluated external resources may inform development.
- Reuse must be deliberate, technically justified and appropriately attributed.
- The application name and branding remain undecided until formally selected.
- Its identity should reflect its broad scientific ambitions rather than unnecessarily limiting its scope.
- Naming, branding and project terminology should remain consistent throughout development.
- The application must retain independent ownership and control over its development direction.

**General principle:** The application should establish and maintain its own recognizable identity as an independent scientific astronomy platform. Its purpose, development and long-term direction must be determined by its own vision and requirements, informed by established scientific and technical knowledge rather than inherited from another project.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes project identity, independence, naming and branding principles.

It does not select a final application name, acronym, logo, visual branding system, programming language, architecture or development technology.

These decisions remain open for appropriate research, design and approval during later planning and development stages.
