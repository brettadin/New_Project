# 13.2 — Future Scientific Capabilities and Disciplines

## Purpose

Establish the application's flexibility to expand into additional scientific disciplines, astronomical subjects and research capabilities as development progresses.

The application should not be permanently restricted to its initial scientific priorities. Future expansion should be guided by scientific value, user needs, technical feasibility and opportunities for meaningful integration with existing capabilities.

Potential future disciplines and capabilities should remain open to investigation without automatically becoming approved development requirements.

## 13.2.1 Expansion Beyond Initial Scientific Priorities

**Core requirements**

- Do not treat the initial scientific scope as a permanent limitation on the application.
- Allow additional astronomical disciplines and related scientific fields to be considered as the application develops.
- Evaluate new areas according to scientific relevance, practical usefulness and technical feasibility.
- Identify relationships between new capabilities and existing scientific workspaces.
- Consider whether new functionality can reuse established application infrastructure.
- Preserve the ability to introduce specialized tools, datasets, models and workflows.
- Avoid unnecessary architectural restrictions that would prevent reasonable scientific expansion.

**Design guidance**

The application should have a clear initial scientific focus while remaining adaptable to the broader needs of astronomy and related sciences.

## 13.2.2 Additional Astronomical Disciplines

Potential future scientific areas include, but are not limited to:

- **Galactic astronomy:** Galactic structure, stellar populations, interstellar matter, galactic dynamics and evolution.
- **Extragalactic astronomy:** Galaxies, galaxy groups and clusters, active galactic nuclei and large-scale structures.
- **Cosmology:** Cosmic expansion, large-scale structure, cosmic evolution, observational cosmology and cosmological models.
- **High-energy astrophysics:** Black holes, neutron stars, pulsars, quasars, X-ray sources and energetic astrophysical phenomena.
- **Astroparticle physics:** Cosmic rays, neutrinos, dark matter and other interactions between astrophysics and particle physics.
- **Solar physics:** Solar activity, magnetic fields, flares, coronal mass ejections and solar observations.
- **Heliophysics and space weather:** Solar-terrestrial interactions, planetary magnetospheres and space-weather phenomena.
- **Astrobiology:** Conditions relevant to life, planetary habitability, biosignature research and the relationship between planetary environments and biological processes.
- **Small Solar System bodies:** Asteroids, comets, dwarf planets, trans-Neptunian objects and other minor bodies.
- **Planetary geology and geophysics:** Planetary surfaces, interiors, geological processes, magnetic fields and internal structure.
- **Planetary formation and evolution:** Formation mechanisms, migration, system evolution and the development of planetary environments.

These are examples of potential expansion areas rather than a fixed or exhaustive list.

## 13.2.3 Additional Scientific Research Capabilities

Future functionality may include:

- More advanced astronomical image processing and analysis.
- Radio astronomy and interferometric data analysis.
- High-resolution and multi-instrument observational comparisons.
- Advanced time-domain astronomy and transient event analysis.
- Stellar evolution and population modelling.
- Galactic and extragalactic data analysis.
- Cosmological modelling and simulation.
- Advanced orbital dynamics and multi-body simulations.
- Planetary interior and geophysical modelling.
- Advanced atmospheric chemistry and photochemical modelling.
- Astrobiological and biosignature investigations.
- Computational astrophysics and numerical simulations.
- Large-scale statistical and population analysis.
- Multi-messenger astronomical data analysis.

The relevance and implementation requirements of each capability should be investigated before it is added to the development plan.

## 13.2.4 Expansion of Existing Scientific Disciplines

Expansion does not need to involve introducing entirely new disciplines.

Existing scientific areas may develop additional depth through:

- More advanced analytical methods.
- Additional observational techniques and data types.
- New instruments, archives and reference databases.
- Improved physical and chemical models.
- More comprehensive numerical simulations.
- Advanced statistical and computational approaches.
- Greater integration between observational and theoretical research.
- Improved uncertainty analysis and validation.
- Additional specialized workflows.
- More extensive comparative investigations.

The application should support increasing scientific depth without requiring unnecessary restructuring of established functionality.

## 13.2.5 Cross-Disciplinary Scientific Expansion

**Core requirements**

- Support investigations that combine multiple scientific disciplines where scientifically appropriate.
- Identify opportunities to reuse existing data, models, methods and reference resources.
- Allow new disciplines to interact with established workspaces and shared capabilities.
- Preserve scientific context and provenance when combining information from different areas.
- Evaluate compatibility between different methods, datasets and scientific assumptions.
- Avoid forcing unrelated disciplines into a single workflow.
- Support research questions that extend across multiple scientific areas.

**Examples**

- Combining stellar, planetary and atmospheric research when investigating exoplanet systems.
- Connecting solar physics with planetary magnetospheric investigations.
- Combining spectroscopy, photometry and imaging in multi-observational studies.
- Connecting galactic stellar populations with stellar evolution models.
- Comparing planetary formation models with observed exoplanet populations.
- Combining astronomical observations with particle physics or cosmological datasets where relevant.

## 13.2.6 New and Emerging Scientific Methods

**Core requirements**

- Maintain awareness of relevant developments in astronomical research and scientific methodologies.
- Investigate emerging methods that may improve existing capabilities or enable new research.
- Evaluate scientific maturity, supporting evidence, limitations and practical requirements.
- Distinguish established methodologies from experimental or developing approaches.
- Consider opportunities to support new methods without compromising existing validated functionality.
- Preserve alternative approaches when scientific understanding is still developing.
- Reassess experimental methods as additional evidence becomes available.

New scientific developments should be considered on their merits rather than automatically adopted because they are recent or technically interesting.

## 13.2.7 Evaluation of Future Scientific Expansion

**Core requirements**

Before adding a new discipline or major scientific capability, evaluate:

- Scientific relevance and potential research value.
- User needs and practical applications.
- Required scientific knowledge and methodologies.
- Available observational data and reference resources.
- Technical and computational requirements.
- Compatibility with existing architecture and workspaces.
- Opportunities for shared functionality.
- Development complexity and maintenance requirements.
- Validation and scientific reliability.
- Licensing, accessibility and external dependencies.
- Long-term usefulness and expansion potential.

Record significant opportunities in the appropriate research documentation and Improvement Register.

Future scientific capabilities must not automatically become initial implementation priorities.

## General Principles

- Preserve an open and expandable scientific scope.
- Maintain a manageable initial focus.
- Allow existing disciplines to develop greater scientific depth.
- Consider additional disciplines and research capabilities as opportunities arise.
- Encourage scientifically meaningful cross-disciplinary integration.
- Evaluate emerging methods carefully.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication and architectural restrictions.
- Base expansion on scientific value, feasibility and practical usefulness.
- Distinguish potential capabilities from approved development commitments.

**General principle:** The application should begin with a coherent scientific focus while remaining capable of growing into a much broader astronomy and related-science platform as research needs, technologies and opportunities evolve.
