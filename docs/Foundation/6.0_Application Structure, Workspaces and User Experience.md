# 6.0 — Application Structure, Workspaces and User Experience

## Section Purpose

This section establishes how the application's scientific capabilities, workspaces, research projects, interface and user-facing functionality should be organized and experienced.

The application should provide a coherent environment in which users can access scientific disciplines, specialized tools, shared capabilities and research materials without creating unnecessary separation between related activities.

Its structure should accommodate both specialized scientific work and interdisciplinary investigations. Users should be able to move between workspaces, tools and research activities while maintaining relevant context, project relationships and continuity.

The application should also provide an accessible and adaptable user experience, accommodating different levels of scientific and technical experience without limiting the depth of its scientific functionality.

This section establishes the requirements governing application organization, workspace integration, shared tools, project access, interface design, scientific data presentation, customization and contextual assistance.

## Section Overview

Section 6 addresses nine interconnected areas:

1. **Overall Application Structure and Navigation:** Establishes how scientific disciplines, research areas, tools and application capabilities should be organized and made accessible.
2. **Scientific Workspaces and Specialized Environments:** Defines the role of dedicated workspaces for different astronomical disciplines and research activities.
3. **Shared Tools and Cross-Disciplinary Workflows:** Establishes how common capabilities should be made available across relevant workspaces and combined into interdisciplinary workflows.
4. **Research Projects and Workspace Integration:** Defines how persistent research projects, exploratory workspaces and specialized scientific environments should interact while preserving research context and continuity.
5. **Interface Design and Visual Identity:** Establishes the desired visual identity and general design principles governing the application's interface.
6. **User Experience and Accessibility:** Defines how the application should accommodate different users, levels of experience and degrees of scientific complexity.
7. **Scientific Data Presentation and Interaction:** Establishes how scientific datasets, measurements, analyses and results should be presented, explored and compared.
8. **Customization and User Preferences:** Defines how users should be able to configure application appearance, workspace arrangements, scientific preferences and general application behaviour.
9. **Contextual Guidance and Workflow Assistance:** Establishes how explanations, educational guidance and workflow assistance should be made available within the application.

Covers:

- Organization of scientific disciplines, research areas, tools and application capabilities.
- Discoverability and accessibility of functionality.
- Multiple appropriate methods of navigation and access.
- Consistency across different areas of the application.
- Adaptability of navigation and organizational structures as the application expands.
- Avoidance of unnecessary complexity, clutter and disconnected functionality.

Covers:

- Specialized scientific workspaces.

- Consistency across workspaces while accommodating discipline-specific requirements.
- Flexible workspace organization as scientific capabilities develop.

### 6.3 — Shared Tools and Cross-Disciplinary Workflows

Establishes how common application capabilities should be accessible across relevant workspaces and integrated into broader research activities.

Covers:

- Shared tools and capabilities available across multiple workspaces.

### 6.4 — Research Projects and Workspace Integration

Defines how persistent research projects should interact with scientific workspaces, exploratory environments and shared research materials.

Covers:

- Integration between specialized workspaces and persistent research projects.
- Object-focused and research-question-focused projects.
- Investigations spanning multiple objects, datasets and scientific disciplines.
- Flexible project organization and project-specific configurations.

- Simple and understandable default experiences.
- Availability of advanced controls and functionality when needed.
- Clear workflows and understandable interactions.
- Avoidance of unnecessary steps, confusing controls and frustrating navigation.
- Usability across different levels of scientific knowledge and technical experience.

Covers:

- Application-level customization and persistent user preferences.
- Appearance, themes and visual presentation settings.

- Default settings and reusable configurations for scientific tools and workflows.
- Automation preferences and appropriate workspace or project-specific overrides.

- Preference management, restoration and portability.

### 6.9 — Contextual Guidance and Workflow Assistance

Establishes how the application should provide relevant explanations and assistance within scientific tools and research workflows.

Covers:

- Context-sensitive explanations within scientific tools.
- Guidance explaining what functions do and when they should be used.
- Scientific explanations of methods, equations, assumptions and limitations.
- Guided workflows for users who need assistance.
- Independent operation for users who prefer to work without guidance.
- Links to relevant educational resources and documentation.
- Clear explanations of errors, warnings and processing decisions.
- Assistance that improves usability without unnecessarily interrupting workflows.

## General Principles

The application's structure, workspaces and user experience should:

- Remain adaptable as the application and its scientific capabilities develop.
- Accommodate new scientific disciplines, workspaces, tools and functionality without requiring unnecessary restructuring.
- Maintain consistency across shared functionality and different areas of the application.
- Allow specialized capabilities to integrate into the wider application without unnecessary dependencies or duplication.
- Remain manageable as the number of features, datasets, workspaces and scientific disciplines increases.
- Combine professional scientific depth with accessibility and intuitive operation.
- Preserve relevant research context, relationships and continuity when users move between workspaces and projects.
- Support both exploratory activities and organized long-term research.
- Present scientific information accurately and clearly.
- Allow customization without compromising scientific meaning, data integrity or essential functionality.
- Improve through research, implementation experience and usability testing.
- Avoid prematurely restricting future interface, navigation or organizational decisions.

## Implementation Boundary

This section establishes the intended application organization, workspace relationships and user experience requirements.

It does not prescribe a permanent interface layout, navigation hierarchy, individual screen arrangement or specific implementation technology.

The following decisions should be addressed through later design and technical planning:

- Exact application layout and screen arrangements.
- Navigation structure and hierarchy.
- Workspace hierarchy and individual workspace layouts.
- Feature-specific interface designs.
- Visual themes, styling and detailed appearance.
- Specific interface technologies and implementation methods.
- Responsive behaviour across supported platforms.

These decisions should be informed by the application's evolving scientific capabilities, technical architecture, platform requirements, user needs and usability testing.

## General Principle

The application should provide a coherent, adaptable and accessible environment for scientific research.

Its workspaces, projects, shared capabilities and interface should function as parts of one integrated application, allowing users to conduct specialized and interdisciplinary research without losing access to relevant tools, information or scientific context.
