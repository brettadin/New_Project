# 5.5 — Analysis Reproducibility and Traceability

## Purpose

The application should support reproducible scientific research by preserving sufficient information to understand how analytical results were produced and, where practical, repeat the work.

Scientific investigations frequently involve multiple datasets, processing operations, analytical methods, model configurations, parameter choices and interpretations. These elements may change as an investigation develops.

The application should maintain meaningful records of this activity so users can revisit previous analyses, understand the decisions that produced particular results, compare alternative approaches and continue research without losing historical context.

Reproducibility should be treated as an ongoing property of the research environment rather than a final documentation step.

The application should preserve the relationships between original data, derived datasets, analytical procedures, configurations, decisions and resulting outputs. It should also identify when exact reproduction is no longer possible because of missing resources, changed external dependencies, unavailable software or other limitations.

The application should also support portable analysis packages, allowing complete investigations to be preserved, transferred, reopened and reproduced across compatible installations and research environments wherever technically and legally possible.

## 5.5.1 Analysis Records and Documentation

### Core Requirements

- Maintain records of significant scientific analyses performed within the application.
- Identify the purpose and scope of each analysis where available.
- Record the datasets and resources used as inputs.
- Identify the analytical methods and operations performed.
- Preserve relevant configurations, parameters and assumptions.
- Record significant processing and analytical decisions.
- Preserve resulting measurements, calculations, models, visualizations and interpretations.
- Identify the date and relevant execution context of analytical activities.
- Link analysis records to their associated projects, workspaces and source materials.
- Allow users to inspect and revisit previous analysis records.
- Support additional user documentation, notes and explanations.
- Distinguish automatically generated records from user-authored documentation.
- Preserve enough information to identify which datasets, methods and configurations produced a particular result.
- Support the preservation of complete investigations rather than recording only isolated analytical operations.

### Design Guidance

An analysis record should provide a meaningful account of how a result was produced.

For example, an analysis involving an exoplanet transmission spectrum might include:

- The original observation and its source.
- Any derived or prepared datasets used.
- Processing and calibration procedures.
- Reference spectra or scientific databases consulted.
- Selected analytical methods.
- Model configurations and fitted parameters.
- Relevant uncertainty information.
- Resulting visualizations and analytical outputs.
- The user's interpretation and conclusions.

The level of detail should reflect the scientific significance and complexity of the analysis.

## 5.5.2 Inputs, Dependencies and Source Relationships

### Core Requirements

- Record the datasets, observations, reference resources and other inputs contributing to an analysis.
- Preserve links to original source materials wherever possible.
- Identify derived datasets and their relationships to original inputs.
- Record relevant external references, databases, models and software dependencies.
- Identify the versions or releases of external resources when available.
- Preserve the relationships between inputs and the analytical outputs that depend on them.
- Identify missing, modified, unavailable or outdated dependencies.
- Avoid silently substituting newer or different source data when revisiting historical analyses.
- Support analyses that depend on multiple datasets or resources.
- Identify when an output cannot be fully reproduced because a required input is unavailable.
- Preserve relevant source identifiers, retrieval dates, citations and attribution information.
- Identify whether required resources are included in an analysis package, referenced externally or available only through the original research environment.

### Design Guidance

An analysis may depend on more than one dataset or a single external source.

For example, a spectral comparison may use an astronomical observation, laboratory reference measurements, molecular databases and a model configuration.

The application should preserve these relationships so users can identify the complete set of resources that contributed to a result.

Where external resources change or become unavailable, historical analysis records should retain their original references and identify the resulting limitations.

## 5.5.3 Methods, Parameters and Configurations

### Core Requirements

- Record the scientific methods used during an analysis.
- Preserve relevant parameters, settings and configurations.
- Identify important assumptions and constraints.
- Record method and configuration versions where available.
- Distinguish default settings from user-modified settings.
- Preserve relevant numerical precision and unit conventions.
- Record significant changes to analytical configurations.
- Identify when methods or parameters are selected automatically.
- Preserve information about manual adjustments and user interventions.
- Allow users to inspect the configurations associated with previous results.
- Support reuse of compatible configurations without changing historical analysis records.
- Preserve sufficient configuration information to support reproduction of the original analysis.
- Identify when a configuration is incomplete, incompatible or dependent on unavailable functionality.

### Design Guidance

Reproducing an analysis requires more than knowing which tool was used.

Different parameter values, assumptions or method versions may produce different results even when the same dataset is analyzed.

The application should preserve enough configuration information to understand the analytical procedure and identify meaningful differences between successive analyses.

## 5.5.4 Analysis Execution Records

### Core Requirements

- Record significant analytical operations and their execution order.
- Identify when an operation was initiated and whether it completed successfully.
- Distinguish completed, failed, interrupted and partially completed operations.
- Preserve relevant execution parameters and configurations.
- Record significant warnings, errors and deviations from expected procedures.
- Identify manual and automated operations.
- Record the automation level active when relevant.
- Preserve significant user approvals and interventions.
- Link execution records to their resulting outputs.
- Allow users to inspect the sequence of operations contributing to an analysis.
- Avoid presenting incomplete or failed operations as successfully completed work.
- Preserve relevant execution information needed to reproduce significant operations.
- Record when an operation cannot be repeated because its original inputs, dependencies or execution requirements are unavailable.

### Design Guidance

An analysis may contain numerous connected operations.

A record of the final output alone may not explain how the application produced it.

The execution history should therefore provide a meaningful sequence of the operations that contributed to the result, including important failures, adjustments and manual interventions.

The degree of execution detail should be proportionate to the operation's scientific significance and reproducibility requirements.

## 5.5.5 Result Preservation and Historical Versions

### Core Requirements

- Preserve significant analytical results and their associated configurations.
- Allow users to revisit previous analytical outputs.
- Maintain historical versions when results are revised or recalculated.
- Distinguish previous results from current results.
- Preserve the inputs, methods and parameters associated with each historical result.
- Avoid silently replacing historical outputs with newer calculations.
- Support comparison between different versions of an analysis.
- Identify when a result has been superseded, revised or invalidated.
- Preserve relevant explanations for significant changes to results.
- Allow users to continue research from a previous valid analytical state.
- Preserve original results when reproduction attempts or alternative analytical approaches produce different outputs.
- Distinguish original results, reproduced results, modified analyses and independently validated results.

### Design Guidance

Scientific results may change as users correct errors, obtain additional observations, refine methods or adopt different assumptions.

The application should preserve meaningful historical states rather than treating the latest result as the only result that matters.

For example, a user may revise an exoplanet atmospheric analysis after obtaining additional observations. The earlier result should remain accessible with its original inputs and configurations, allowing the user to understand why the interpretation changed.

## 5.5.6 Branching Analyses and Alternative Approaches

### Core Requirements

- Support branching analytical workflows from common source datasets or previous analysis states.
- Allow users to investigate alternative methods, parameters, models and assumptions independently.
- Preserve the common source relationships between branches.
- Maintain separate records and outputs for each analytical path.
- Allow users to compare alternative analytical approaches and their results.
- Prevent changes in one branch from unintentionally modifying another.
- Identify the point at which an analytical branch diverged from its source.
- Support continuation of multiple research approaches within the same investigation.
- Allow users to identify preferred, provisional, abandoned or completed analytical paths.
- Preserve previous branches even when they are no longer actively used.
- Allow users to create further branches from previous versions or existing branches.
- Preserve the provenance and history of all branches.
- Distinguish independent reproductions from modifications of an existing analysis.

### Design Guidance

Scientific investigation is not always a single linear sequence of operations.

A researcher may need to compare different normalization procedures, test alternative models, investigate competing hypotheses or evaluate different parameter constraints.

The application should support these approaches without requiring users to duplicate entire projects or overwrite previous analyses.

Branching should preserve independent analytical histories while maintaining clear relationships to their shared source data and earlier work.

## 5.5.7 Analytical Decisions and Research Rationale

### Core Requirements

- Allow users to document significant analytical decisions.
- Preserve explanations for important methodological choices.
- Record the reasons for selecting or rejecting relevant methods, parameters, models or datasets.
- Support documentation of assumptions, constraints and alternative approaches.
- Allow users to record unresolved questions and decisions requiring further investigation.
- Link decision records to relevant datasets, analyses, configurations and outputs.
- Distinguish user-provided rationale from automatically generated recommendations.
- Preserve significant decision history when an investigation changes direction.
- Allow users to revisit previous decisions and their supporting context.
- Avoid requiring users to document every routine operation manually.
- Allow users to add or revise explanations after an analytical operation has been completed.
- Preserve decision documentation when analyses are branched, exported, imported or reproduced.

### Design Guidance

A reproducible investigation should explain not only what was done but also why important choices were made.

For example, a researcher may select one atmospheric retrieval model over another because the available observations do not adequately constrain additional parameters.

Recording that rationale helps explain the analytical approach and provides context when the investigation is revisited.

Decision documentation should focus on scientifically meaningful choices rather than creating unnecessary administrative work.

## 5.5.8 Reproducibility of Processing and Analysis Workflows

### Core Requirements

- Support repeating previously recorded processing and analytical workflows where practical.
- Preserve the sequence of operations and relevant configurations.
- Identify required inputs and dependencies.
- Check whether required resources remain available.
- Identify compatibility changes that may affect reproduction.
- Distinguish exact reproduction from approximate or partial reproduction.
- Communicate when current software, methods or external resources differ from those used originally.
- Avoid silently substituting different inputs or methods during reproduction.
- Preserve the original result when a reproduction attempt produces a different output.
- Record significant differences between the historical and reproduced results.
- Support reproduction of individual workflow stages where full reproduction is unavailable.
- Allow users to revisit and continue earlier investigations without reconstructing the complete workflow manually.
- Identify which portions of a workflow can be reproduced and which remain unavailable.

### Design Guidance

Reproducibility does not always mean that an old analysis can be repeated under identical conditions.

External databases may change, software dependencies may become unavailable, and computational methods may evolve.

The application should preserve the original analytical record while identifying which aspects can still be reproduced and where differences or limitations exist.

## 5.5.9 Software, Method and Environment Information

### Core Requirements

- Preserve relevant information about software and tools used during an analysis.
- Record application and component versions where appropriate.
- Identify relevant external software, libraries and scientific dependencies.
- Preserve information about computational methods and execution environments when necessary for reproduction.
- Record relevant model or reference-data versions.
- Identify when a software or dependency update may affect previous analytical results.
- Distinguish changes in software execution from changes in scientific methodology.
- Avoid silently recalculating historical results using updated methods.
- Communicate when the original execution environment cannot be reconstructed.
- Support appropriate documentation of environment-specific limitations.
- Record relevant operating system, hardware or computational configuration information where these may affect reproduction.
- Preserve dependency manifests or equivalent environment descriptions for portable analyses.
- Identify missing or incompatible dependencies when an analysis is transferred to another environment.

### Design Guidance

Some analyses depend on specific software versions, numerical libraries, external tools or computational environments.

These details may affect numerical results or the behavior of scientific methods.

The application should preserve relevant environment information without requiring every routine analysis to generate an unnecessarily extensive technical record.

The amount of recorded detail should reflect the complexity and reproducibility requirements of the operation.

## 5.5.10 Automated Analysis Records

### Core Requirements

- Record significant automated operations contributing to scientific results.
- Identify when analytical operations or decisions were initiated automatically.
- Distinguish automatically generated suggestions from user-approved methods and conclusions.
- Preserve relevant instructions, configurations or model identifiers where appropriate and available.
- Record significant automated decisions and user interventions.
- Identify when automated outputs were modified, accepted or rejected by the user.
- Preserve relevant supporting evidence and source references.
- Avoid treating automatically generated outputs as independently verified scientific findings.
- Identify when automated systems cannot provide sufficient information to reproduce an output.
- Preserve the automation level active when relevant.
- Coordinate automation records with the user-control requirements in Section 5.2.

### Design Guidance

Automated operations may contribute to scientific workflows by identifying patterns, suggesting analytical methods, executing processing sequences or assisting with model configuration.

Their contributions should remain distinguishable from direct observations and independently validated results.

The application should preserve enough information to understand what was performed automatically, what required user intervention and how the resulting outputs were produced.

This subsection establishes general requirements for recording automated operations. It does not establish a requirement to introduce AI-assisted scientific analysis.

## 5.5.11 Analysis Comparison and Historical Review

### Core Requirements

- Allow users to compare different versions of an analysis.
- Support comparison of analytical methods, configurations, assumptions and outputs.
- Identify meaningful changes between historical and current results.
- Preserve the context required to understand why results differ.
- Support comparison between alternative analytical branches.
- Identify changes caused by new data, revised methods or modified assumptions.
- Distinguish numerical differences from changes in scientific interpretation.
- Allow users to revisit previous results without replacing current work.
- Preserve relevant annotations and explanations associated with comparisons.
- Support historical review of completed, ongoing and inconclusive investigations.
- Allow users to compare results from independent reproductions where appropriate.
- Communicate when comparisons are limited by incompatible methods, inputs or configurations.

### Design Guidance

Comparing analytical results can be as important as producing them.

For example, a researcher may need to determine whether a revised atmospheric abundance estimate resulted from additional observations, a different retrieval model or a change in the assumed temperature profile.

The application should make these differences understandable rather than presenting two numerical results without their methodological context.

## 5.5.12 Reproducibility Limitations and Incomplete Records

### Core Requirements

- Identify when analysis records lack information required for full reproduction.
- Communicate missing inputs, unavailable dependencies and incomplete metadata.
- Distinguish known reproducibility limitations from unknown or unrecorded information.
- Identify when external resources have changed or become unavailable.
- Preserve historical results even when exact reproduction is no longer possible.
- Identify which workflow stages remain reproducible.
- Communicate when reproduction is approximate or dependent on alternative resources.
- Avoid claiming that an analysis is fully reproducible when essential information is missing.
- Allow users to supplement incomplete records where appropriate.
- Preserve explanations for known limitations and unresolved dependencies.
- Identify when differences between the original and reproduced environments prevent meaningful comparison.
- Clearly distinguish successful reproduction, partial reproduction, unsuccessful reproduction and reproduction that could not be attempted.

### Design Guidance

The application should be transparent about the limits of reproducibility.

An analysis may remain scientifically useful even when its original environment or some external resources are unavailable.

The goal is to preserve as much meaningful research context as possible and clearly identify what can and cannot be reconstructed.

## 5.5.13 Research Continuity and Long-Term Accessibility

### Core Requirements

- Preserve significant analysis records for long-term research continuity.
- Allow users to revisit previous investigations and continue unfinished work.
- Maintain relationships between analysis records, projects, datasets and outputs.
- Support retrieval of historical methods, configurations and decisions.
- Identify dependencies that may prevent future reproduction.
- Preserve historical results when scientific methods or reference data change.
- Support continuation of investigations using new observations or additional evidence.
- Allow users to revise previous analyses without losing their historical states.
- Maintain access to relevant documentation and supporting materials.
- Coordinate analysis continuity with the project history and archival requirements in Section 6.4 and Section 9.
- Support retrieval of previous analyses across increasingly large research histories.
- Preserve relevant records when an investigation is reorganized, transferred or archived.

### Design Guidance

Scientific investigations may extend over months or years and may be revisited after substantial changes in available data, scientific understanding or analytical methods.

The application should preserve enough context for users to understand where an investigation left off and how previous results were obtained.

Research continuity should not depend on a user's ability to remember undocumented decisions or reconstruct an entire workflow from memory.

## 5.5.14 Analysis Export and Portable Reproducibility Packages

### Core Requirements

- Support exporting and importing complete investigations as portable analysis packages.
- Allow users to save complete investigations as reusable, repeatable analysis packages.
- Preserve relevant datasets, processing sequences, analytical methods, parameters, models, visualizations and report configurations.
- Include relevant metadata, provenance, citations and source information.
- Include relevant software and dependency manifests.
- Preserve configurations, assumptions and analytical decisions.
- Include relevant analysis history, versions, branches and associated outputs.
- Support reopening saved packages and continuing previous investigations.
- Support reproducing analyses using their original datasets and configurations.
- Allow compatible packages to be applied to alternative datasets.
- Identify compatibility problems, missing dependencies and unsuitable inputs before attempting reproduction.
- Check whether alternative datasets satisfy the scientific and technical requirements of the package.
- Communicate when differences in data structure, units, instruments, observational conditions or scientific context may affect compatibility.
- Preserve previous results and configurations when a package is applied to different data.
- Maintain traceability between the package, its source data and resulting outputs.
- Support exporting selected analyses or components of larger investigations.
- Include required datasets where redistribution and licensing permit.
- Preserve references to external resources that cannot legally or practically be included.
- Identify anything that cannot be included and explain what is required to reproduce the investigation.
- Ensure imported packages can be inspected before use.
- Maintain relationships between package components so the investigation can be understood outside its original installation wherever feasible.
- Preserve modification and contribution history where available and appropriate.
- Distinguish the original analysis, an imported copy, a modified version and an independent reproduction.

### Design Guidance

Portable analysis packages should allow research to be reviewed, transferred, archived and continued without depending entirely on continued access to the original application installation.

A complete package should preserve the scientific context of an investigation rather than simply producing a collection of disconnected output files.

For example, a package containing an exoplanet atmospheric analysis should preserve the observation, relevant reference data, processing history, retrieval configuration, model parameters, software dependencies, results and associated visualizations.

Packages should also support reuse with alternative datasets where scientifically and technically appropriate. Applying an existing analysis configuration to a different observation must not imply that the alternative dataset is automatically suitable or that previous scientific conclusions remain valid.

The application should inspect package contents and identify compatibility limitations before attempting to reproduce or apply an analysis.

Where a required dataset or dependency cannot be included, the package should preserve its source reference and clearly identify what remains necessary.

Exact package formats and export mechanisms can be determined during technical planning.

## 5.5.15 Analysis History, Activity Logs and User Inspection

### Core Requirements

- Provide users with accessible views of significant analytical history.
- Maintain a chronological record of significant activities performed during an analysis.
- Record dataset imports, processing operations, parameter changes, model executions, interpretation changes and report generation.
- Record relevant timestamps, methods, configurations and associated results.
- Link activities to the datasets, analysis versions and outputs they affect.
- Allow inspection of individual operations and their associated records.
- Support navigation between inputs, processing stages, decisions and outputs.
- Identify relationships between historical results and their contributing analyses.
- Distinguish current, historical, superseded and incomplete analytical states.
- Allow users to add notes, comments and annotations to specific datasets, graphs, results and stages of an investigation.
- Support marking particular features, measurements or results for later attention.
- Allow annotations to include explanations, questions, observations and reminders.
- Where appropriate, support visual annotations directly on graphs and other scientific visualizations.
- Keep annotations associated with the relevant data or analysis elements.
- Allow users to edit, organize and revisit annotations.
- Preserve annotations when analyses are saved, exported, imported, shared or branched.
- Keep general annotations distinct from formal scientific conclusions and decision records.
- Allow users to search their analysis histories across research projects.
- Support filtering by datasets, astronomical objects, molecules, analytical methods, parameters, dates and analysis statuses.
- Allow users to search analysis versions, branches, annotations and recorded scientific decisions.
- Support locating specific processing operations, model configurations and previous results.
- Provide links between search results and their associated analyses, datasets and projects.
- Allow users to navigate between related analyses and their histories.
- Preserve activity history when analyses are modified, branched, exported or transferred.
- Distinguish automatically recorded activities from manually added notes or explanations.
- Avoid silently modifying or deleting historical records.
- Avoid overwhelming users with unnecessary technical detail during routine work.
- Provide access to more detailed records when required for scientific review or reproduction.

### Design Guidance

Analysis history should be useful to both users who need a quick understanding of previous work and those who need to inspect detailed methodological information.

The application should provide an accessible overview while allowing users to examine the underlying records when necessary.

Annotations, activity logs and decision records serve related but distinct purposes. Activity logs record what happened, decision records explain important choices, and annotations allow users to attach contextual information to relevant research materials.

Search and navigation should make these records useful across increasingly large investigations and research histories.

## 5.5.16 Cross-Environment Reproducibility and Compatibility

### Core Requirements

- Analysis packages should identify the software, libraries, dependencies, versions and configurations required to reproduce an analysis.
- The application should check whether the receiving environment meets those requirements.
- Identify missing, incompatible or unavailable dependencies before reproduction where practical.
- Where possible, help recreate the original analytical environment.
- Support reproducing analyses on different computers or installations.
- Consider differences in operating systems, software versions, computational environments and available resources.
- Identify relevant differences in hardware or computational execution where they may affect results.
- Communicate when an analysis requires software or computational capabilities unavailable in the receiving environment.
- Identify whether exact, approximate or partial reproduction is possible.
- Where exact reproduction is not possible, explain why and identify potential differences in the resulting outputs.
- Preserve the original analysis records and results even when reproduction attempts produce different results.
- Support appropriate reproduction using compatible alternative environments where exact environment reconstruction is unavailable.
- Preserve relevant environment and compatibility information in portable analysis packages.

### Design Guidance

An analysis should not be assumed reproducible simply because its package can be opened on another computer.

Different installations may have different software versions, numerical libraries, computational resources or operating systems.

The application should identify these differences and communicate their potential effects before or during reproduction.

Where possible, the application should help recreate the original environment or establish a compatible alternative.

Cross-environment reproduction should preserve the distinction between exact reproduction and reproduction under modified conditions.

## 5.5.17 Reproducibility Verification and Reports

### Core Requirements — Automated Verification

- Support automated verification of whether a reproduced analysis produces results consistent with its original recorded results.
- Compare reproduced analytical outputs against the original results.
- Identify differences in numerical results, generated data, model outputs and other relevant analytical products.
- Use scientifically appropriate comparison methods and tolerance criteria for each type of analysis.
- Distinguish expected numerical variation from discrepancies that may indicate a problem.
- Communicate the extent and significance of differences rather than simply reporting whether two outputs match.
- Where possible, help identify causes of discrepancies, including software versions, dependencies, numerical precision, computational environments and input-data differences.
- Preserve both original and reproduced results.
- Record verification outcomes as part of the analysis history.
- Identify when automated comparison is not scientifically appropriate or cannot be performed reliably.
- Avoid claiming successful reproduction solely because an operation completed without errors.
- Distinguish successful execution from successful scientific reproduction.

### Core Requirements — Reproducibility Reports

- Generate reports documenting attempts to reproduce an analysis.
- Include the original analysis configuration and relevant methodology.
- Record the reproduction environment, software versions and dependencies.
- Document dataset and dependency comparisons.
- Identify differences between original and reproduced outputs.
- Include identified causes of discrepancies where possible.
- Provide an overall reproducibility assessment supported by verification results.
- Document unresolved issues, limitations and uncertainties.
- Distinguish exact, approximate, partial, unsuccessful and unattempted reproduction.
- Support exporting reports for research documentation, collaboration and publication.
- Preserve reports within the associated research project and analysis history.
- Ensure reports accurately reflect the actual reproduction and verification records.

### Design Guidance

Reproducing an analysis should include an evaluation of whether the reproduced results agree with the original results to a scientifically acceptable degree.

A simple exact-match comparison is not appropriate for every scientific operation. Numerical calculations, iterative models and computational methods may produce small differences even when the underlying analysis is successfully reproduced.

Comparison methods and tolerances should therefore be appropriate to the scientific procedure and output being evaluated.

For example, reproducing a spectral fitting workflow may require comparison of fitted parameters, uncertainties, residuals and resulting model spectra rather than relying on a single exact numerical match.

Reproducibility reports should communicate the evidence supporting their assessment. They should not imply that a scientifically valid analysis has been independently confirmed merely because its outputs can be reproduced.

## 5.5.18 Analysis Status and Completion Tracking

### Core Requirements

- Provide identifiable statuses for scientific analyses.
- Support statuses appropriate to the progress and outcome of an investigation, including:
  - In progress.
  - Completed.
  - Needs further investigation.
  - Inconclusive.
  - Superseded by a newer analysis.
- Allow users to update analysis statuses as investigations progress.
- Where appropriate, support application-generated status suggestions based on analysis activity or results while retaining user control.
- Clearly distinguish completed analyses from preliminary or unresolved investigations.
- Preserve status history and relevant explanations for status changes.
- Allow users to revisit and continue analyses regardless of their current status.
- Avoid treating completion status as evidence of scientific confirmation or validation.
- Support different analysis types and research objectives without requiring every investigation to follow an identical status progression.

### Design Guidance

Analysis statuses should help users manage investigations and understand their current state.

An analysis marked as completed may still be scientifically inconclusive, while an investigation marked as needing further work may contain valuable and valid results.

The application should distinguish administrative progress from scientific validity.

Status changes should help users organize research without imposing unnecessary restrictions on continuing or revising an investigation.

## 5.5.19 User-Defined Analysis Completion Criteria

### Core Requirements

- Allow users to define completion criteria for individual analyses.
- Support different criteria depending on the scientific objective and type of investigation.
- Allow users to identify required analytical steps, evaluations, comparisons and documentation.
- Track which criteria have been satisfied and which remain outstanding.
- Allow criteria to be modified as an investigation develops while preserving relevant history.
- Support optional application-generated recommendations for additional steps that may be necessary.
- Allow users to review completion criteria alongside the analysis status.
- Preserve completion criteria when analyses are exported, imported or branched.
- Avoid imposing identical completion criteria on different scientific investigations.
- Never imply that satisfying completion criteria automatically establishes scientific validity or confirms a finding.

### Design Guidance

Different investigations require different definitions of completion.

For example, a spectral identification investigation may require comparison with reference spectra, evaluation of alternative identifications and documentation of uncertainty.

An atmospheric retrieval investigation may require model evaluation, parameter uncertainty assessment and comparison of alternative configurations.

Users should be able to define what constitutes sufficient completion for their particular research objective.

Completion criteria should support research organization and transparency rather than function as automatic declarations of scientific validity.

## 5.5.20 Reusable Analysis Templates

### Core Requirements

- Allow users to create reusable templates from existing analyses.
- Support templates containing preferred processing methods, analytical workflows, model configurations, parameters, visualization settings and reporting structures.
- Allow users to apply templates when starting new investigations.
- Allow users to modify templates independently of the analyses created from them.
- Preserve template versions and modification histories.
- Identify required inputs, dependencies and compatibility requirements.
- Check whether template methods and configurations are appropriate for new datasets before applying them.
- Warn users when differences in data, instruments, units or scientific conditions may make a template unsuitable.
- Preserve relationships between templates and the analyses created from them.
- Avoid automatically changing the original analysis when a template is modified.
- Allow users to inspect templates before applying them.
- Never assume that a previously successful analytical procedure is automatically appropriate for another investigation.
- Preserve relevant template documentation, assumptions and known limitations.

### Design Guidance

Reusable templates can reduce repetitive configuration and support consistent analytical practices.

For example, a user may create a template for processing and analyzing a particular type of transmission spectrum.

The template could preserve preferred processing operations, fitting configurations, visualization settings and reporting arrangements.

Applying the template to a new observation should still involve compatibility checks. Differences in instruments, data quality, spectral coverage or scientific conditions may make some configurations unsuitable.

Templates should provide a reusable starting point rather than automatically transfer previous scientific conclusions to new investigations.

## 5.5.21 Outdated Analysis Identification and Reassessment

### Core Requirements

- Identify previous analyses that may warrant reassessment because of changes to external resources, scientific methods or software.
- Monitor relevant changes to external databases, reference libraries, scientific methods, software and dependencies used in previous analyses where reliable update information is available.
- Identify analyses that may be affected by significant updates.
- Notify users when previously used resources become outdated, unavailable or superseded.
- Explain which parts of an analysis may be affected and why.
- Where possible, identify newer resources, methods or versions that could warrant reassessment.
- Allow users to review flagged analyses and decide whether to repeat or update them.
- Preserve original analyses, configurations and results.
- Never automatically modify, replace or invalidate historical results.
- Record reassessments and resulting changes as new analysis versions.
- Distinguish a potentially outdated analysis from one that has been demonstrated to be scientifically incorrect.
- Avoid flagging analyses solely because a newer resource exists when the change is not relevant to the original research.

### Design Guidance

Scientific resources, methods and software can change over time.

A previously completed analysis may warrant reassessment if a reference database is updated, an important calibration error is identified or a scientific method is substantially revised.

The application should help users identify potentially affected investigations without automatically treating historical results as invalid.

A reassessment should be an explicit research activity that preserves the original analysis and records any resulting changes.

## 5.5.22 Project Integration

### Core Requirements

- Associate analysis records with their relevant research projects where applicable.
- Allow users to navigate from a result to the analysis and source data that produced it.
- Keep analyses accessible when users return to projects after extended periods.
- Preserve the history of investigations as new observations, analyses and interpretations are added.
- Keep relevant analysis records, annotations, activity logs, configurations and reproducibility reports connected to their associated projects.
- Preserve relationships between shared datasets, project-specific analyses and derived outputs.
- Support independent analyses of shared source data without unintentionally changing another project's results.
- Preserve analysis relationships when projects are exported, transferred, duplicated or archived.
- Allow exploratory analyses to exist independently of formal projects where supported by the workspace system.
- Support transferring selected exploratory analysis materials into projects without losing relevant history, provenance or dependencies.

### Design Guidance

Analysis records should remain connected to the wider research environment without requiring every analytical activity to begin inside a formal project.

A user may investigate an observation in an exploratory workspace before deciding whether it belongs in a persistent research project.

When analysis materials are transferred into a project, their relevant inputs, configurations, outputs and relationships should remain intact.

Project organization and long-term research continuity are further addressed in Section 6.4.

## 5.5.23 Future Scope

The following capabilities are desirable future extensions. They are not mandatory initial implementation requirements. Their feasibility and priority should be evaluated during development according to scientific value, technical complexity, dependencies and available resources.

### Selective Merging of Analysis Branches

Investigate allowing users to selectively incorporate useful changes from different analysis branches into a new investigation.

Potential capabilities include:

- Incorporating methods, processing steps, parameters or other useful changes from one branch into another.
- Creating a new analysis version when changes are combined.
- Preserving the histories and original configurations of all contributing branches.
- Identifying conflicts or incompatible methods, parameters and dependencies before merging.
- Allowing users to review proposed changes before accepting them.
- Recording which changes originated from each branch.
- Ensuring merged analyses remain traceable and reproducible.
- Preserving the original branches unchanged.

### Community Sharing of Analysis Templates

Investigate allowing users to publish and share analysis templates with the wider community.

Potential capabilities include:

- Sharing templates for different scientific disciplines and analytical procedures, such as stellar spectroscopy, exoplanet transit analysis and atmospheric retrieval.
- Preserving creator attribution and template version histories.
- Including documentation explaining the template's purpose, methods, assumptions and limitations.
- Identifying the data types and scientific conditions for which a template was designed.
- Allowing users to inspect templates before adopting them.
- Supporting community feedback, improvements and revised versions.
- Distinguishing community-created templates from independently validated scientific methods.
- Avoiding the automatic treatment of shared templates as scientifically validated.

### Community Template Evaluation and Validation

Investigate a system for evaluating and reviewing community-shared analysis templates.

Potential evaluation statuses could include:

- Community-created.
- Tested by other users.
- Independently reproduced.
- Scientifically reviewed.
- Validated against established reference cases.

Additional possibilities include:

- Recording who performed evaluations and what methods or reference cases were used.
- Preserving review histories and validation evidence.
- Communicating known limitations, unresolved issues and applicable scientific conditions.
- Allowing templates to receive feedback and undergo further evaluation as they develop.
- Keeping validation status distinct from the original creator's claims.
- Avoiding the treatment of popularity, user ratings or positive feedback as evidence of scientific validity.

## 5.5.24 Acceptance Criteria

Analysis reproducibility and traceability capabilities should not be considered complete simply because their interfaces or basic functionality have been implemented. They must demonstrate that scientific investigations can be recorded, revisited, reproduced and evaluated reliably.

The following acceptance criteria apply to the core requirements.

### Complete Analysis Records

- Analysis records contain relevant input datasets, methods, parameters, processing operations, models and outputs.
- Software and dependency versions are recorded where applicable.
- Relationships between original data, processed data, derived results and interpretations are preserved.
- Users can trace a particular result back to the data and configurations that produced it.
- Tests verify that analysis records remain complete after saving, closing and reopening an investigation.

### Reproduction of Previous Analyses

- Previously recorded analyses can be reopened and reproduced using their original data and configurations where the required resources remain available.
- Users can continue previous investigations without reconstructing their workflows manually.
- Reproduction failures are identified and explained.
- Original analysis records and results remain unchanged during reproduction.
- Tests demonstrate repeatability using appropriate reference datasets and known analysis configurations.

### Analysis Packages and Portability

- Complete analysis packages can be exported and imported successfully.
- Required components, metadata and provenance are preserved.
- Processing sequences, analytical configurations, models, visualizations and relevant reports remain associated with their original investigation.
- Missing or incompatible dependencies are identified.
- Imported packages can be inspected before use.
- Compatible packages can be applied to alternative datasets following appropriate compatibility checks.
- Applying a package to alternative data does not overwrite its source analysis or automatically transfer previous conclusions.
- Packages remain understandable outside their original installation wherever technically feasible.
- Tests verify package integrity and component relationships after export and import.

### Cross-Environment Reproducibility

- The application identifies relevant software versions, libraries, dependencies and environment requirements.
- Compatibility checks correctly identify missing or incompatible requirements.
- Where supported, the original analytical environment can be recreated.
- Differences between computational environments are communicated.
- Tests verify reproduction across supported environments.
- Exact, approximate and partial reproduction are distinguished appropriately.

### Reproducibility Verification

- Original and reproduced outputs can be compared using scientifically appropriate methods.
- Differences are correctly identified and quantified where possible.
- Comparison tolerances are appropriate to the relevant scientific procedure.
- Expected numerical variation is distinguished from potentially significant discrepancies.
- Original and reproduced results remain separately preserved.
- Verification outcomes are associated with the relevant analysis records.
- Tests include known reproducible cases and cases where differences are expected.

### Reproducibility Reports

- Reports accurately reflect the original analysis and reproduction attempt.
- Software versions, dependencies, datasets and relevant configurations are documented.
- Identified discrepancies and unresolved limitations are included.
- Reports distinguish successful, approximate, partial, unsuccessful and unattempted reproduction.
- Reports can be exported and retained within their associated projects.
- Tests verify that report contents correspond to the actual analysis and reproduction records.

### External Resource and Long-Term Traceability

- External resources can be identified through recorded names, identifiers, versions and retrieval dates where available.
- Resource changes and unavailability can be identified when reliable update information exists.
- Original dataset and resource references remain preserved.
- Local copies are retained only where permitted.
- Missing or discontinued dependencies are communicated accurately.
- Tests verify that resource references and provenance survive export, import and long-term storage operations.

### Versioning and Analysis Branching

- Modifications create identifiable new versions or branches without overwriting original analyses.
- Changes to relevant components are recorded.
- Version comparisons correctly identify differences.
- Branches retain their relationships to the original investigation.
- Previous versions can be revisited.
- Tests verify that branching and modification do not corrupt or disconnect existing analysis records.

### Analysis Status and Completion Criteria

- Analysis statuses can be assigned, modified and retained.
- Status history is preserved.
- User-defined completion criteria can be created and tracked.
- Changes to completion criteria are recorded.
- Completion status does not incorrectly imply scientific confirmation or validation.
- Tests verify status tracking and completion criteria across different analysis types.

### Activity Logs, Decision Records and Annotations

- Significant analysis activities are recorded chronologically.
- Activity records link to the relevant datasets, configurations, versions and outputs.
- Users can add optional scientific reasoning without being forced to document every operation.
- Annotations remain associated with their relevant data and analysis elements.
- Logs, decision records and annotations survive saving, exporting, importing and branching.
- Tests verify the accuracy and persistence of these records.

### Analysis Search and Navigation

- Users can locate previous analyses using relevant search and filtering criteria.
- Search results link to the correct analyses, datasets, versions and projects.
- Users can locate relevant branches, annotations and historical configurations.
- Tests verify search accuracy and navigation across increasingly large analysis histories.

### Analysis Templates

- Templates can be created from existing analyses and reused.
- Template configurations, versions and dependencies are preserved.
- Compatibility checks identify unsuitable inputs or configurations.
- Applying a template does not overwrite its source analysis.
- Tests verify that templates reproduce their intended configurations on compatible datasets.

### Outdated Analysis Identification

- Relevant changes to monitored external resources and dependencies can be identified.
- Potentially affected analyses are correctly flagged.
- Notifications explain the reason an analysis may warrant reassessment.
- Original results remain unchanged.
- Tests verify that reassessment creates new analysis records rather than silently modifying historical work.

### Scientific Integrity and Validation

- Analysis records accurately distinguish original results, reproduced results, modified analyses and independently validated results.
- Reproducibility checks use scientifically appropriate comparison methods.
- Known limitations and unsupported scenarios are documented.
- Tests cover normal operation, edge cases, missing dependencies, changed resources and incompatible environments.
- Scientific correctness is assessed separately from basic software functionality.
- A capability is not considered scientifically validated merely because an analysis can be executed repeatedly or produces plausible-looking results.

Acceptance criteria should be adapted to the scientific capability being implemented. Not every analysis type will require identical reproduction methods, comparison tolerances or validation procedures. The scientific research phase must establish appropriate standards for each capability.

Future-scope capabilities should receive their own acceptance criteria if and when they are approved for implementation.

## General Principles

Scientific reproducibility and traceability must be maintained throughout the application.

The application should ensure that:

- Scientific investigations remain understandable and traceable from their original inputs through to their final outputs and interpretations.
- Original observations and historical results are preserved.
- Modifications, reproductions and alternative approaches are recorded without destroying previous work.
- Complete analysis packages remain as portable and reproducible as technically and legally possible.
- Compatible packages can be reused with alternative datasets without silently transferring previous scientific conclusions.
- Cross-environment reproduction accounts for relevant differences in software, dependencies and computational conditions.
- Reproducibility verification uses scientifically appropriate comparisons and tolerances.
- Reproducibility reports accurately communicate results and limitations.
- Analysis status and completion criteria remain distinct from scientific validation.
- Reusable templates are checked for suitability before application to new investigations.
- Potentially outdated analyses are identified without automatically changing historical results.
- Limitations affecting reproducibility are communicated honestly.
- Scientific conclusions are not automatically treated as validated simply because an analysis can be reproduced.
- Users retain control over their investigations and can inspect the history of their work.

The objective is to make scientific analyses persistent, transparent, reproducible and reusable, supporting both individual research and collaborative scientific work.

## General Principle

Scientific research should remain understandable beyond the moment an analysis is performed.

The application should preserve not only the results of an investigation but also the evidence, methods, decisions and context needed to understand how those results were obtained and how the research can be continued.

Reproducibility should extend beyond recording what happened. It should support the practical ability to revisit, transfer, reproduce, compare and evaluate complete scientific investigations.
