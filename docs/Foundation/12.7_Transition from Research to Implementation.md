# 12.7 — Transition from Research to Implementation

## Purpose

Establish the process for transitioning from the initial research and planning phase into active application development.

Implementation should begin only after sufficient scientific and technical understanding has been established, an initial development scope has been defined, relevant dependencies have been investigated and the necessary approvals have been obtained.

The transition should produce a clear, documented starting point for development while preserving flexibility to adapt as new information emerges.

## 12.7.1 Completion of Initial Research

**Core requirements**

Before transitioning into implementation:

- Review the findings from the initial scientific and technical research phases.
- Confirm that the application's major scientific capabilities are sufficiently understood.
- Identify established scientific methodologies and relevant limitations.
- Confirm that relevant external resources and software have been evaluated.
- Identify major technical requirements and architectural considerations.
- Document significant dependencies, risks and unresolved questions.
- Identify areas requiring further investigation during later development stages.
- Ensure research findings are available in the appropriate project documentation.
- Distinguish established findings from recommendations, assumptions and unresolved questions.

Initial research does not need to resolve every future scientific or technical question.

The objective is to establish sufficient understanding to begin practical development without relying on unsupported assumptions.

## 12.7.2 Initial Development Strategy

**Core requirements**

- Establish a recommended initial development strategy based on the completed research.
- Identify the foundational application capabilities that should be developed first.
- Determine the logical sequence of development stages.
- Identify dependencies between foundational infrastructure and scientific functionality.
- Establish an appropriate initial platform based on the technical evaluation and user approval.
- Identify shared functionality that should be developed before specialized implementations.
- Consider the intended hybrid web-and-desktop direction.
- Preserve opportunities for future scientific and technical expansion.
- Avoid attempting to implement the entire long-term application vision immediately.

The initial strategy should prioritize a functional, coherent and scientifically useful application foundation.

## 12.7.3 Initial Development Plan

**Core requirements**

Prepare a documented development plan containing:

- Initial development objectives.
- Approved initial application scope.
- Foundational capabilities.
- Initial scientific functionality.
- Major implementation stages.
- Dependencies and prerequisites.
- Technical and architectural recommendations.
- Relevant external resources and software.
- Scientific and technical risks.
- Testing and verification expectations.
- Acceptance criteria.
- Outstanding questions and deferred work.
- Future capabilities requiring additional research.
- Required user approvals.

The plan must distinguish proposed recommendations from approved development commitments.

The Master Development Roadmap must contain the authoritative overview of development stages, priorities, dependencies and status.

Detailed implementation plans and technical decisions should remain in their appropriate documentation locations.

## 12.7.4 Architectural and Technical Approval

**Core requirements**

- Present significant architectural and technical recommendations for review where approval is required.
- Explain the reasoning behind major technology and platform choices.
- Identify relevant alternatives and their advantages and disadvantages.
- Explain significant development, performance, maintenance and compatibility implications.
- Identify licensing, security, privacy and financial considerations.
- Confirm the intended relationship between shared functionality and specialized scientific workspaces.
- Confirm that the proposed architecture can support the approved initial scope.
- Document consequential decisions and user approvals.

The AI development partner should make ordinary technical recommendations independently but must respect the approval boundaries established in Section 10.

Research completion does not automatically authorize major architectural commitments or implementation.

## 12.7.5 Establishing the Initial Development Baseline

**Core requirements**

Before substantial implementation begins, establish a documented baseline for the project.

This should identify:

- Approved application requirements.
- Current development scope.
- Approved architectural direction.
- Selected initial platform and relevant technologies.
- Development environment requirements.
- Initial dependencies.
- Project documentation structure.
- Testing and verification expectations.
- Data management and preservation requirements.
- Initial development priorities.
- Known limitations and unresolved questions.

The baseline should provide a reference point against which subsequent development changes can be evaluated.

Changes to the baseline must be documented when they materially affect requirements, architecture, implementation scope or development direction.

## 12.7.6 Formal Transition to Active Development

**Core requirements**

The transition into active implementation should occur when:

- Initial research has reached an appropriate level of completion.
- The initial application scope has been established.
- The development strategy has been documented.
- Major technical and architectural decisions have been addressed.
- Required approvals have been obtained.
- Foundational dependencies and prerequisites are understood.
- The development environment can be established.
- Testing and acceptance criteria are defined.
- The Master Development Roadmap has been updated.
- The project documentation provides sufficient context for implementation.

Once these conditions are satisfied, the AI development partner may begin approved implementation work.

The transition should be recorded in the development history, including the approved starting scope, relevant decisions and outstanding limitations.

## 12.7.7 Continued Research During Implementation

**Core requirements**

The transition into implementation does not end scientific or technical research.

The AI development partner must continue investigating relevant subjects throughout development.

This includes:

- Research required by upcoming scientific capabilities.
- Investigation of newly identified technical constraints.
- Evaluation of emerging scientific methods and resources.
- Reassessment of external dependencies.
- Investigation of problems discovered during implementation.
- Review of relevant scientific and technological developments.
- Identification of improvements and opportunities.
- Reassessment of previous decisions when new evidence justifies it.

Research findings must be incorporated into the appropriate documentation and development records.

Significant findings that affect requirements, priorities, architecture or implementation scope must be evaluated and handled through the approval procedures established in Section 10.

Development should remain responsive to new knowledge without becoming directionless or continuously expanding its scope.

## 12.7.8 Initial Development Review and Reassessment

**Core requirements**

After the first meaningful implementation stage:

- Evaluate whether the application foundation satisfies its intended objectives.
- Review architectural and technical decisions against actual development experience.
- Assess whether scientific functionality has been implemented appropriately.
- Evaluate maintainability, performance and integration.
- Review testing and verification results.
- Identify shortcomings, unexpected complexity and missing foundations.
- Reassess development priorities and dependencies.
- Identify whether the initial strategy requires adjustment.
- Document significant findings and recommendations.
- Update the Master Development Roadmap accordingly.

The initial development strategy should be treated as an informed starting point rather than an unchangeable commitment.

Reassessment should be evidence-based and should not trigger unnecessary redesign or scope expansion.

## General Principles

- Research must establish the foundation for implementation.
- Initial development scope must be manageable and meaningful.
- Significant technical and architectural decisions must be appropriately reviewed and approved.
- Development must begin with documented objectives and acceptance criteria.
- The Master Development Roadmap must establish the authoritative development overview.
- The initial project baseline must preserve important decisions and assumptions.
- Research must continue throughout implementation.
- Development priorities may evolve as practical experience and new evidence become available.
- Significant changes must be documented and handled through established approval procedures.
- The transition should avoid both premature implementation and unnecessary delays caused by excessive planning.

**General principle:** The application should transition into implementation through a deliberate, documented and approved process that establishes a scientifically informed and technically practical foundation while retaining the flexibility to adapt as development progresses.
