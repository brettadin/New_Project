# 11.6 — Knowledge Retrieval and Development Continuity

## Purpose

Establish consistent procedures for locating, reviewing and applying existing project knowledge throughout development.

The AI development partner must be able to retrieve relevant requirements, scientific research, technical documentation, decisions, implementation records and previous investigations before beginning work.

Knowledge retrieval must support informed development decisions, reduce unnecessary repetition and preserve continuity across sessions and changes in development personnel.

## 11.6.1 Documentation Retrieval Before Development

**Core requirements**

- Consult relevant project documentation before beginning significant research, planning or implementation.
- Review the Master Development Roadmap to understand current priorities, status and dependencies.
- Consult the rolling development record for recent work and outstanding context.
- Review relevant requirements and approved decisions.
- Inspect relevant architecture and technical documentation.
- Check existing problem records, test reports and research findings where applicable.
- Identify previous investigations that may already answer the current question.
- Verify important assumptions against the actual codebase and current project state.
- Avoid relying exclusively on previous conversations or remembered information.

**Design principle:** Existing project knowledge must be consulted before new work begins, rather than reconstructed through repeated investigation.

## 11.6.2 Contextual Knowledge Retrieval

**Core requirements**

- Retrieve information according to the current task, research question or development objective.
- Identify related requirements, scientific methods, technical dependencies and previous decisions.
- Follow relevant cross-references between documentation categories.
- Retrieve detailed records when summaries are insufficient.
- Distinguish authoritative information from historical context, recommendations and unresolved proposals.
- Identify missing information that could materially affect the work.
- Avoid reviewing unrelated documentation unnecessarily.

**Examples**

- Before implementing spectroscopy functionality, review relevant scientific requirements, methods, data formats, architecture, dependencies and previous investigations.
- Before changing a database structure, review the data model, storage requirements, existing architecture and relevant decisions.
- Before resolving a recurring defect, inspect previous problem records and attempted solutions.
- Before modifying an analytical method, review its scientific basis, validation evidence, assumptions and limitations.

## 11.6.3 Search and Knowledge Discovery

**Core requirements**

- Make project documentation searchable and reasonably easy to navigate.
- Support retrieval by subject, requirement, capability, component, dependency, decision, problem and record identifier.
- Allow related records to be located through cross-references.
- Preserve the ability to retrieve historical and superseded information.
- Identify relevant documents even when their filenames do not directly match the current task.
- Avoid treating keyword matches as sufficient evidence that a document is relevant or authoritative.

**Future possibilities**

- More advanced semantic documentation search.
- Automated discovery of related requirements and technical dependencies.
- Search across research records, development history and architecture documentation.
- Identification of potentially duplicated or conflicting information.
- Knowledge relationship maps.

## 11.6.4 Reuse of Existing Research and Findings

**Core requirements**

- Reuse relevant previous research rather than unnecessarily repeating it.
- Check the date, source, scope and reliability of previous findings before relying on them.
- Reassess findings when scientific developments, technical changes or new evidence may affect their validity.
- Identify gaps in existing investigations.
- Extend previous research where additional information is required.
- Preserve the distinction between previously established findings and newly obtained evidence.
- Record significant updates and changes to previous conclusions.
- Avoid treating previously rejected approaches as permanently unsuitable when circumstances have changed.

**Design principle:** Previous work should provide a foundation for further investigation, not prevent reconsideration when new evidence justifies it.

## 11.6.5 Knowledge Application and Traceability

**Core requirements**

- Use retrieved information when developing requirements, recommendations, architecture and implementation.
- Identify relevant supporting records when making consequential recommendations.
- Link significant implementation work to the requirements and decisions that justify it.
- Preserve relationships between research findings, technical decisions, implementation and verification.
- Identify when existing documentation does not adequately support a proposed approach.
- Record new findings in the appropriate authoritative location.
- Avoid introducing contradictory assumptions or undocumented changes.
- Make it possible to trace significant development work back to its supporting knowledge.

**Design principle:** Knowledge retrieval should actively inform development rather than serve as a passive document-search exercise.

## 11.6.6 Session Startup and Work Resumption

**Core requirements**

At the beginning of a new development session, the AI development partner must:

1. Locate and consult the Master Development Roadmap.
2. Review the rolling development record and recent handoff information.
3. Identify the current development stage, priorities and outstanding work.
4. Review relevant requirements, architecture and technical documentation.
5. Check unresolved problems, dependencies and approval requirements.
6. Inspect the actual codebase when implementation work is involved.
7. Identify the next appropriate task based on current project conditions.
8. Determine whether additional research or user approval is required before proceeding.

When resuming previously interrupted work:

- Review the relevant problem, decision, test and development records.
- Verify the current implementation state.
- Identify changes made since the previous session.
- Reassess whether the original approach remains appropriate.
- Avoid repeating completed or unsuccessful work without a reason.
- Preserve outstanding questions and unresolved limitations.

These procedures are mandatory and must remain consistent with Section 10.11.

## 11.6.7 Knowledge Gaps and Missing Records

**Core requirements**

- Identify missing, incomplete or inaccessible documentation when it affects development.
- Search existing records before concluding that information is unavailable.
- Distinguish genuinely missing information from information that has not yet been retrieved.
- Investigate missing information where possible.
- Record significant knowledge gaps and their potential consequences.
- Ask the user for clarification when necessary information cannot be established independently.
- Avoid inventing previous decisions, research findings or project history.
- Create new records when an investigation produces information that should be preserved.

**Design principle:** Missing knowledge should be identified and addressed transparently rather than concealed through assumptions.

## 11.6.8 Knowledge Continuity Between Development Partners

**Core requirements**

- Maintain documentation so another AI development partner or human contributor can continue work without depending on the original developer.
- Preserve project objectives, current state, architectural context, approved decisions and outstanding work.
- Make important development procedures understandable independently.
- Maintain consistent terminology and documentation conventions.
- Preserve relevant source references, dependencies and verification evidence.
- Provide sufficient handoff information to identify where work stopped and what should happen next.
- Avoid storing essential project knowledge exclusively in conversation history or individual AI memory.

**Design principle:** Development continuity should belong to the project itself, not to a particular conversation, AI model or individual contributor.

## 11.6.9 Knowledge Retrieval and Documentation Efficiency

**Core requirements**

- Retrieve information proportionately to the task.
- Prioritize authoritative and directly relevant records.
- Use summaries and indexes to locate detailed documentation efficiently.
- Avoid repeatedly reading large collections of unrelated material.
- Follow references when additional detail is necessary.
- Avoid duplicating information when an authoritative record can be referenced.
- Keep retrieval procedures straightforward and maintainable.

**Future possibilities**

- Automated document relevance suggestions.
- Context-aware retrieval based on the active development task.
- Dependency-aware documentation discovery.
- Automated identification of missing supporting records.

## General Principles

- Consult existing knowledge before significant new work.
- Use authoritative documentation rather than relying on conversational memory.
- Retrieve information relevant to the task without unnecessary review.
- Reuse previous research while reassessing its continued validity.
- Preserve traceability between knowledge, decisions, implementation and verification.
- Identify missing information and uncertainty transparently.
- Maintain continuity across sessions and development partners.
- Avoid repeating work without a clear reason.

**General principle:** The AI development partner must understand the project's existing knowledge before extending it. Research and development should build upon preserved evidence, decisions and experience rather than repeatedly starting from the beginning.
