

# 4.11 — Access, Licensing, Privacy and Responsible Resource Use

## Purpose

The application should support responsible access to external scientific resources while respecting licensing conditions, usage restrictions, authentication requirements, privacy and the policies of external providers.

Astronomical research resources vary considerably in how they may be accessed and used. Some datasets are openly available, while others require registration, institutional access, subscriptions, specific permissions or compliance with redistribution restrictions.

The application should help users understand these conditions before accessing or using external resources. It should not assume that publicly discoverable information is unrestricted, that accessible data may be redistributed or that an authenticated account grants unlimited usage rights.

Users should retain control over their accounts, credentials, personal information and research materials.

## 4.11.1 Resource Access Conditions

### Core Requirements

- Support discovery and use of resources with different access conditions.
- Distinguish openly accessible resources from restricted, registration-based, subscription-based and permission-controlled resources.
- Communicate relevant access requirements before attempting restricted operations.
- Identify when access depends on an institutional account, individual registration, subscription or other authorization.
- Distinguish resources that can be discovered from those that can actually be retrieved or used.
- Identify access limitations that affect available data, services or functionality.
- Avoid implying that a resource is freely available simply because its metadata or description is publicly visible.
- Allow users to access external resources through their own authorized accounts where supported.
- Preserve information about relevant access restrictions when resources are retained or incorporated into research.
- Avoid attempting to bypass access controls or restrictions imposed by external providers.

### Design Guidance

Access conditions should be visible and understandable throughout the discovery and retrieval process.

For example, a catalogue may provide publicly accessible observation metadata while requiring an authenticated account or approved proposal to retrieve the underlying observations.

The application should distinguish these different access levels rather than presenting the entire resource as either freely available or completely inaccessible.

## 4.11.2 Licensing and Usage Rights

### Core Requirements

- Preserve available licensing information for external datasets, reference resources, software and other scientific materials.
- Identify relevant usage conditions before users download, retain, process, export or redistribute restricted resources.
- Distinguish between access permission and permission to reuse or redistribute material.
- Support different licensing models, including open licenses, restricted licenses, institutional agreements and provider-specific usage conditions.
- Preserve attribution requirements and relevant license information.
- Identify when licensing conditions are unknown, incomplete or difficult to interpret.
- Avoid assuming that publicly accessible resources may be freely redistributed.
- Communicate relevant restrictions when users export or share materials.
- Avoid removing licensing or attribution information during data conversion, processing or transfer.
- Allow licensing information to remain associated with relevant resources and research records.

### Design Guidance

Licensing conditions can apply differently to original datasets, derived products, software, reference information and supporting documentation.

The application should preserve these distinctions rather than treating all materials associated with a resource as having identical usage rights.

Where conditions are unclear, the application should communicate the uncertainty rather than presenting an unsupported interpretation as established permission.

## 4.11.3 Attribution and Citation Requirements

### Core Requirements

- Preserve citation and attribution requirements associated with external resources.
- Identify relevant data providers, archives, observatories, missions, instruments, software developers and scientific contributors where information is available.
- Retain recommended citation formats and bibliographic references.
- Support attribution of datasets and external tools used in research.
- Preserve citation information when resources are processed, reorganized, exported or incorporated into projects.
- Distinguish mandatory attribution from recommended acknowledgment where the source provides that distinction.
- Make relevant citation information accessible when preparing research reports or sharing results.
- Avoid presenting externally sourced materials as original application-generated resources.
- Communicate when required citation information is missing or unavailable.

### Design Guidance

Attribution should remain connected to the scientific materials and operations that depend on external contributions.

Users should not need to reconstruct the sources of their research manually when preparing a report, publication or shared investigation.

Coordinate these requirements with the source attribution and provenance requirements in Section 4.7.

## 4.11.4 External Accounts and Authentication

### Core Requirements

- Support authenticated access to external scientific resources where appropriate.
- Accommodate different authentication methods supported by external providers.
- Allow users to connect and disconnect supported external accounts.
- Clearly identify which external account or authorization is being used for a particular operation.
- Communicate when authentication is required, expired, invalid or insufficient.
- Avoid requesting account credentials when authentication is not necessary.
- Support reauthorization when external access permissions expire or change.
- Preserve appropriate account connection preferences between sessions.
- Allow users to review and revoke external account connections.
- Avoid requiring users to create unnecessary external accounts solely to use unrelated application functionality.
- Clearly distinguish application accounts from accounts maintained by external providers.

### Design Guidance

Some scientific archives and services require authentication for particular operations, even when other functionality is publicly accessible.

The application should accommodate these requirements without making external account management unnecessarily complicated.

Users should understand which provider they are connecting to, why authorization is needed and what functionality the connection enables.

## 4.11.5 Credentials and Authentication Security

### Core Requirements

- Handle external credentials and authentication tokens securely.
- Avoid storing credentials in ordinary application configuration files, research records or exported project documents.
- Use appropriate secure storage mechanisms where credentials must be retained.
- Avoid exposing authentication information through logs, error messages, reports or diagnostic records.
- Avoid transmitting credentials to unrelated external services.
- Limit credential use to the operations and providers for which authorization was granted.
- Support credential expiration, revocation and renewal.
- Identify when a stored authorization is no longer valid.
- Avoid requesting or retaining unnecessary authentication information.
- Respect the security requirements of external authentication providers.

### Design Guidance

External credentials are sensitive information and should be treated separately from scientific datasets and research records.

Project portability must not inadvertently transfer private credentials or authentication tokens to another installation or user.

The application should retain only the information necessary to maintain supported connections and should make account authorization understandable and controllable.

## 4.11.6 Subscriptions, Institutional Access and Usage Limits

### Core Requirements

- Support resources that require subscriptions, institutional access or other forms of authorized entitlement.
- Identify when access depends on an active subscription, institutional affiliation or approved authorization.
- Communicate known access limitations and relevant usage restrictions.
- Distinguish subscription requirements from ordinary registration requirements.
- Identify when an external service imposes usage limits, quotas or request restrictions.
- Communicate when an operation may be restricted by those limits.
- Avoid initiating paid subscriptions or purchasing access on the user's behalf without explicit authorization.
- Avoid assuming that an institutional subscription is available to every user.
- Allow users to continue using unrelated application capabilities when a particular external resource is inaccessible.
- Preserve relevant access information without storing unnecessary institutional or personal details.

### Design Guidance

Scientific resources may provide different access rights depending on the user's institution, subscription or approved research status.

The application should make these requirements visible without attempting to manage institutional entitlements or commercial subscriptions beyond the functionality explicitly supported by an external provider.

## 4.11.7 Privacy and Personal Information

### Core Requirements

- Protect personal information associated with application use and external resource access.
- Minimize the personal information collected or transmitted during external operations.
- Clearly identify when information must be provided to an external service.
- Distinguish personal account information from scientific data and research materials.
- Avoid transmitting project contents, research notes or private datasets to external providers without appropriate user authorization.
- Support user control over connected accounts and relevant personal information.
- Avoid exposing private information through public resource searches, shared research materials or exported records.
- Communicate when external services may collect or retain information submitted through an integration.
- Respect relevant privacy requirements and provider policies.
- Avoid using private research materials for unrelated purposes without authorization.

### Design Guidance

Scientific research may involve unpublished observations, preliminary findings, personal notes, institutional information and other materials that users do not intend to share.

External integration should not imply permission to transmit or disclose these materials.

The application should distinguish information necessary to perform an explicitly requested external operation from unrelated private research content.

## 4.11.8 External Data Transmission and User Authorization

### Core Requirements

- Make external data transmission understandable to users.
- Identify when datasets, queries, metadata or other research information will be transmitted to an external provider.
- Communicate the purpose of the transmission where appropriate.
- Avoid transmitting unnecessary project information or unrelated research materials.
- Require appropriate authorization before transmitting private, sensitive or restricted research materials.
- Distinguish ordinary public queries from operations involving private or authenticated resources.
- Make external processing and data-sharing behavior clear.
- Avoid silently submitting research materials to third-party services.
- Preserve user control over whether externally processed results are returned to the application.
- Communicate relevant external processing or retention conditions when known.

### Design Guidance

An operation may involve more than retrieving information from an external source.

For example, an external service may require a dataset to be uploaded before performing a calculation or analysis.

The application should make this distinction clear so that users understand when information leaves their local environment.

## 4.11.9 Responsible Resource Use

### Core Requirements

- Respect the usage policies and access conditions of external providers.
- Avoid excessive or unnecessary requests to external services.
- Accommodate provider-defined request limits, quotas and rate restrictions.
- Avoid automatically repeating failed requests in ways that create unnecessary service load.
- Support appropriate handling of temporary restrictions and service-imposed delays.
- Avoid bypassing access restrictions, request limits or authentication requirements.
- Preserve relevant information about restricted or unsuccessful operations.
- Identify when external usage conditions may affect automated retrieval or background operations.
- Avoid using external resources in ways that conflict with their stated scientific, legal or technical restrictions.

### Design Guidance

Responsible access helps preserve the availability of scientific resources for the wider research community.

The application should use external services appropriately and avoid treating public scientific infrastructure as an unlimited resource.

## 4.11.10 Restricted and Unavailable Resources

### Core Requirements

- Identify when a resource is restricted, inaccessible or unavailable because of access conditions.
- Distinguish access denial from temporary service failure, missing resources and unsupported functionality.
- Communicate when users may need to obtain authorization directly from the provider.
- Avoid repeatedly attempting operations that are clearly unauthorized.
- Preserve relevant information about access restrictions.
- Allow users to continue with alternative accessible resources where appropriate.
- Avoid silently substituting restricted resources with unrelated or scientifically different alternatives.
- Preserve research records that depend on previously accessible materials.
- Identify when changes to access conditions may affect existing research activities.

### Design Guidance

A resource may become inaccessible because of expired credentials, subscription changes, institutional restrictions or revised provider policies.

The application should communicate the reason where possible and avoid treating every access failure as a generic retrieval error.

## 4.11.11 Exporting, Sharing and Redistribution Restrictions

### Core Requirements

- Preserve relevant licensing and access restrictions when exporting research materials.
- Identify when exported content includes resources that may not be redistributed.
- Distinguish between sharing research conclusions, metadata, derived results and original source data.
- Communicate when a project or dataset contains materials with different usage conditions.
- Avoid automatically including restricted datasets in project exports or shared research packages when redistribution is not permitted.
- Support appropriate export of permitted metadata and research records.
- Preserve attribution and citation information in exported materials.
- Warn users when intended sharing may conflict with known licensing or access conditions.
- Avoid representing the ability to export a resource as evidence that redistribution is permitted.

### Design Guidance

A project may contain a mixture of user-generated material, openly licensed datasets, restricted observations and derived results.

Export and sharing operations should account for these differences rather than applying a single unrestricted assumption to the entire project.

Coordinate these requirements with project portability in Section 6.4 and collaboration and sharing requirements in Section 8.

## 4.11.12 Changes to Licensing and Access Conditions

### Core Requirements

- Preserve the access and licensing information associated with previously acquired resources.
- Identify when known external access conditions or licensing information change.
- Distinguish the conditions that applied when a resource was acquired from those currently published by its provider.
- Communicate when a change may affect future retrieval, reuse, retention or redistribution.
- Avoid silently changing the recorded licensing or access information associated with historical research.
- Preserve available information about the source and timing of relevant changes.
- Identify when current usage conditions cannot be verified.
- Allow users to review potentially affected resources and research activities.

### Design Guidance

External providers may revise licensing terms, access policies or authentication requirements over time.

Historical research should retain information about the resource and conditions under which it was originally acquired.

The application should not assume that a later policy change automatically alters the historical circumstances of previous research, nor should it assume that previous access guarantees continued permission for future use.

## General Principles

Access, licensing, privacy and responsible resource use should:

- Respect external access conditions and licensing requirements.
- Distinguish discovery, access, retrieval, reuse and redistribution permissions.
- Preserve source attribution and citation requirements.
- Support authorized external account access without unnecessary credential collection.
- Protect authentication information and personal data.
- Keep private research materials under user control.
- Make external data transmission understandable.
- Respect institutional access, subscriptions and provider usage limits.
- Handle restricted resources transparently.
- Preserve relevant licensing and access information in research records.
- Account for restrictions when exporting or sharing research materials.
- Avoid bypassing external provider policies or access controls.

## General Principle

Access to scientific resources carries responsibilities beyond simply retrieving their contents.

The application should help users use external resources appropriately while protecting their privacy, respecting provider conditions and preserving the scientific and legal context of acquired materials.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes requirements for external resource access, licensing, attribution, authentication, subscriptions, privacy, data transmission and responsible usage.

It does not prescribe specific authentication protocols, credential storage technologies, legal interpretations, subscription systems or privacy compliance implementations.

Resource provenance and citation information are addressed in Section 4.7. External software and service integrations are addressed in Section 4.10. Resource reliability and availability are addressed in Section 4.8. Resource evaluation and provider management are addressed in Section 4.12. Project portability and access permissions are addressed in Section 6.4. Broader collaboration and sharing requirements are addressed in Section 8.
