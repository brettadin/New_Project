# 2.2 — Initial Priority Scientific Disciplines

## Purpose

Establish the scientific disciplines that should form the initial foundation of the application's research capabilities.

The application is intended to support a broad range of astronomical research, but its initial development must concentrate on a manageable set of scientifically valuable and interconnected areas.

The initial priorities should provide a strong foundation for developing a coherent research environment while establishing opportunities for shared tools, datasets, methods and workflows.

The initial priority disciplines are:

1. Exoplanet Science
2. Planetary Science and Solar System Research
3. Stellar Astronomy
4. Observational Astronomy

These areas provide a practical starting point because they encompass important astronomical research subjects, involve complementary scientific approaches and offer opportunities for meaningful integration.

Their selection establishes development priorities rather than permanent restrictions on the application's scientific scope.

## 2.2.1 Selection Principles

Initial scientific disciplines should be selected according to their scientific importance, practical usefulness and suitability for establishing the broader application.

**Core requirements**

- Prioritize disciplines with meaningful opportunities for scientific investigation.
- Consider the availability and accessibility of relevant observational data and scientific resources.
- Consider whether established scientific methods and reference materials are available.
- Evaluate the potential for developing useful analytical and research capabilities.
- Consider opportunities for shared functionality between disciplines.
- Account for the complexity and technical requirements of supporting each area.
- Prefer disciplines that can contribute to a coherent initial research environment.
- Preserve opportunities for additional disciplines to be introduced as development progresses.

**Selection considerations**

The initial disciplines should collectively demonstrate the application's ability to support different forms of astronomical research, including:

- Investigations of individual astronomical objects and planetary systems.
- Analysis of observational measurements and datasets.
- Spectroscopic and photometric research.
- Characterization of astronomical properties and environments.
- Comparison between observations, theoretical expectations and scientific models.
- Research involving multiple related astronomical objects.

The objective is not to select disciplines solely because they are easy to implement. Their selection should reflect both scientific value and their contribution to establishing the wider platform.

## 2.2.2 Exoplanet Science

<badge color="success">Initial priority</badge>

Exoplanet science should be one of the application's central initial research areas.

It provides opportunities to investigate planets outside the Solar System, their host stars, planetary systems, physical properties and atmospheric characteristics.

**Research subjects include**

- Exoplanet discovery and identification.
- Planetary properties and characterization.
- Planetary systems and orbital configurations.
- Exoplanet atmospheres and atmospheric composition.
- Transmission and emission spectroscopy.
- Transit and secondary eclipse observations.
- Atmospheric structure and temperature.
- Planetary formation and evolution.
- Comparative exoplanet studies.
- Habitability-related investigations.

**Scientific direction**

The application should accommodate investigations using observational evidence, established planetary models and appropriate computational methods.

Exoplanet research should also provide opportunities to connect stellar astronomy, spectroscopy, photometry, planetary science and atmospheric chemistry.

Detailed scientific scope is established in Section 2.3.

## 2.2.3 Planetary Science and Solar System Research

<badge color="success">Initial priority</badge>

Planetary science should form another major initial research area, with a distinct emphasis on the Solar System.

The Solar System provides accessible and scientifically valuable subjects for investigation, including the Sun, planets and their natural satellites.

It also provides opportunities for studying planetary environments and comparing nearby planetary bodies with exoplanets.

**Research subjects include**

- The Sun and solar phenomena.
- The eight planets.
- Natural satellites and moons.
- Planetary atmospheres and atmospheric composition.
- Planetary surfaces and geological characteristics.
- Planetary environments and physical properties.
- Planetary systems and orbital characteristics.
- Comparative planetary science.
- Relationships between Solar System research and exoplanet investigations.

**Scientific direction**

The application should provide a distinct Solar System research area that accommodates the different planets and their associated research subjects.

The initial scope should concentrate on the Sun, eight planets and their natural satellites, while preserving opportunities to incorporate dwarf planets, asteroids, comets and other small bodies in the future.

Detailed scientific scope is established in Section 2.4.

## 2.2.4 Stellar Astronomy

<badge color="success">Initial priority</badge>

Stellar astronomy should provide the scientific foundation for investigating stars and their properties, including their physical characteristics, atmospheres, spectra and variability.

It is particularly important because stars are central to many astronomical investigations, including exoplanet characterization and planetary system research.

**Research subjects include**

- Stellar properties and classification.
- Stellar spectra and spectral characteristics.
- Stellar atmospheres and composition.
- Stellar temperature, luminosity and other physical properties.
- Stellar variability and brightness changes.
- Stellar activity and evolution.
- Binary and multiple-star systems.
- Stellar environments and relationships with planetary systems.
- Characterization of stars hosting exoplanets.

**Scientific direction**

Stellar astronomy should be developed as an independent research area while supporting meaningful connections with exoplanet science, observational astronomy and other relevant disciplines.

Detailed scientific scope is established in Section 2.5.

## 2.2.5 Observational Astronomy

<badge color="success">Initial priority</badge>

Observational astronomy should provide a foundational area of research across the application.

It concerns the acquisition, examination and interpretation of astronomical observations and provides evidence used by many other scientific disciplines.

**Research subjects and observational areas include**

- Optical and infrared observations.
- Radio and microwave observations.
- Ultraviolet observations.
- X-ray and gamma-ray observations.
- Astronomical spectroscopy.
- Astronomical photometry.
- Imaging and image-based observations.
- Time-series observations.
- Multi-wavelength investigations.
- Observations obtained through ground-based and space-based facilities.
- Comparison of observations from different instruments and sources.

**Scientific direction**

Observational astronomy should not be treated as a discipline isolated from the other initial priorities.

Its capabilities and research approaches should support investigations throughout the application, including exoplanet science, planetary science and stellar astronomy.

Detailed scientific scope is established in Section 2.6.

## 2.2.6 Relationships Between Initial Priority Disciplines

The initial disciplines should be considered complementary areas of research rather than isolated development targets.

**Core requirements**

- Support meaningful scientific relationships between the priority disciplines.
- Allow compatible observations and datasets to contribute to investigations across multiple areas.
- Preserve the scientific context and requirements of each discipline.
- Support investigations involving multiple astronomical objects or systems.
- Allow shared scientific capabilities to serve different disciplines where appropriate.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of common functionality.
- Preserve the independence of specialized scientific methods and analytical approaches.

**Examples of interdisciplinary relationships**

- **Exoplanet science and stellar astronomy:** Investigating host stars alongside their planets, including stellar properties that influence planetary characterization.
- **Exoplanet science and planetary science:** Comparing exoplanet characteristics and atmospheric environments with those of Solar System planets.
- **Planetary science and observational astronomy:** Using observations across different wavelengths to investigate planetary atmospheres, surfaces and environments.
- **Stellar astronomy and observational astronomy:** Using spectroscopy, photometry and other observations to characterize stars and investigate their variability.
- **Planetary science and stellar astronomy:** Investigating solar activity and its influence on planetary environments.

These relationships should help guide the development of shared capabilities and integrated research workflows.

## 2.2.7 Initial Priority Boundaries

Establishing initial priority disciplines does not mean that all scientific subjects within them must be implemented simultaneously.

**Core requirements**

- Allow each priority discipline to develop incrementally.
- Identify foundational capabilities before introducing more advanced specialized functionality.
- Distinguish initial implementation requirements from future possibilities within each discipline.
- Avoid treating the initial priority list as a commitment to complete every possible research capability.
- Allow development priorities to evolve according to scientific research and technical findings.
- Preserve opportunities for other disciplines to receive priority when justified.
- Ensure the initial application remains useful even while some planned scientific areas are still developing.

**Design guidance**

The priority disciplines establish the initial direction of scientific development. Their detailed capabilities, workflows and implementation requirements should be defined separately through the appropriate sections of the Foundations documentation.

## General Principles

- Exoplanet science, planetary science, stellar astronomy and observational astronomy establish the initial scientific priorities.
- These disciplines should provide a strong and interconnected foundation for the application.
- Each discipline should retain its own scientific identity and specialized research requirements.
- Shared capabilities should support meaningful connections between disciplines.
- Observational astronomy should provide a foundation for evidence-based research across the application.
- Solar System research should have a distinct and substantial place within planetary science.
- Initial priorities should guide development without permanently restricting scientific scope.
- Scientific value, feasibility, data availability and opportunities for integration should inform development priorities.
- Individual disciplines should be developed incrementally rather than treated as complete requirements from the outset.

**General principle:** The initial scientific disciplines should establish a practical and scientifically valuable foundation for the application, providing interconnected areas of research while preserving the flexibility to expand into additional astronomical subjects and disciplines.
