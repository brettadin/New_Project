# 11.4 — Development Records and Documentation Templates

## Purpose

Establish standardized documentation templates for recording important research, decisions, problems, testing, recommendations and development activities.

Records must preserve enough context to understand what happened, why it happened, what evidence supports the outcome and what remains to be done.

Templates should provide consistency without imposing unnecessary documentation requirements on routine development work.

## 11.4.1 Documentation Template Principles

**Core requirements**

- Maintain reusable templates for recurring development records.
- Use consistent structures and terminology.
- Capture the information necessary to understand and revisit each activity.
- Allow records to contain additional relevant information when circumstances require it.
- Avoid requiring irrelevant fields or excessive documentation for minor activities.
- Use unique identifiers for individual records where appropriate.
- Include dates, status and relevant project references.
- Link records to affected requirements, roadmap entries, components and related records.
- Preserve unsuccessful investigations and important intermediate findings.
- Distinguish confirmed outcomes from assumptions, recommendations and unresolved questions.

Templates should support consistent documentation rather than become rigid forms that interfere with useful investigation.

## 11.4.2 Decision Records

Maintain individual records for consequential scientific, technical, architectural and development decisions.

**Required information**

- Decision identifier and title.
- Date and relevant development stage.
- Context and problem being addressed.
- Relevant requirements and constraints.
- Options investigated.
- Advantages, disadvantages and trade-offs.
- Evidence and research supporting the decision.
- AI recommendation, where applicable.
- User's decision and any conditions or restrictions.
- Final outcome and reasoning.
- Affected components, requirements and documentation.
- Dependencies and consequences.
- Related decisions and records.
- Status: Proposed, Pending Approval, Approved, Rejected, Deferred, Modified or Superseded.

**Additional considerations**

- Distinguish recommendations from approved decisions.
- Preserve rejected and deferred alternatives when they may be relevant later.
- Record when a previous decision is reconsidered or replaced.
- Never interpret silence or an ambiguous response as approval.

## 11.4.3 Problem and Investigation Records

Maintain individual records for significant technical, scientific, architectural and development problems.

Use the standardized Problem Record template established alongside Section 10.7.

**Required information**

- Problem identifier and title.
- Date, affected components and development stage.
- Description of the problem and its impact.
- Expected and actual behaviour.
- Conditions under which it occurs.
- Investigation performed and evidence collected.
- Confirmed and suspected causes.
- Attempted solutions and their outcomes.
- Alternative approaches.
- Recommended or selected resolution.
- Approval requirements.
- Implementation and recovery details.
- Testing and verification results.
- Remaining limitations and risks.
- Development impact, blockers and outstanding work.
- Related roadmap entries and documentation.
- Record history and final status.

**Core principles**

- Preserve unsuccessful attempts where they provide useful information.
- Update existing records as investigations progress.
- Do not create unnecessary separate records for repeated observations of the same underlying problem.
- Keep unresolved and deferred problems accessible.
- Consult previous problem records before repeating an investigation.

## 11.4.4 Mandatory Test Reports

Every relevant implementation and testing task must produce a standardized test report.

This requirement must be followed alongside the testing responsibilities and test-scope limits established in Section 10.6.

**Required test-report information**

1. **Identification**
   - Report identifier and title.
   - Date of execution.
   - Development stage or task.
   - Related roadmap entry.
   - Relevant problem, decision or implementation records.

2. **Purpose and scope**
   - What change or functionality was tested.
   - Objectives of the testing.
   - Components and workflows included.
   - Reason for the selected testing scope.
   - Relevant regression risks.

3. **Test environment**
   - Operating system and relevant platform.
   - Programming language and version.
   - Relevant frameworks, libraries and dependency versions.
   - Configuration and environment details affecting results.

4. **Tests performed**
   - Test categories and individual tests.
   - Exact commands or procedures where appropriate.
   - Relevant test files or references.
   - Manual and automated testing performed.
   - Functional and integration checks.

5. **Results**
   - Exact test results.
   - Passed tests.
   - Failed tests.
   - Skipped tests.
   - Tests not executed.
   - Relevant output, logs or other evidence.

6. **Failure investigation**
   - Identified failures and unexpected behaviour.
   - Investigation performed.
   - Confirmed or suspected causes.
   - Corrective changes.
   - Results of subsequent test executions.

7. **Scientific verification**
   - Scientific methods or calculations examined.
   - Reference datasets, published methods or benchmarks used.
   - Numerical comparisons and tolerances where relevant.
   - Scientific verification outcomes.
   - Limitations of the verification.

8. **Limitations and residual risks**
   - Functionality not tested.
   - Tests unavailable or deferred.
   - Environmental restrictions.
   - Unverified integrations or dependencies.
   - Remaining concerns and potential consequences.

9. **Verification status**
   - Implemented.
   - Tested.
   - Functionally verified.
   - Scientifically verified.
   - Scientifically validated.
   - Fully verified.

   Only applicable statuses should be assigned, and each must be supported by evidence.

10. **Conclusion and follow-up**
    - Overall outcome.
    - Outstanding issues.
    - Required follow-up work.
    - Related roadmap updates.
    - Date of completion or last update.

**Test scope requirements**

Testing must be thorough but proportionate to the changes and associated risks.

- Always test the functionality directly affected by an implementation change.
- Test relevant integration points and dependent workflows where regressions are reasonably possible.
- Use broader or full test suites when changes affect shared infrastructure, major architecture, data structures, core scientific methods or substantial application functionality.
- Do not automatically run every available test for every minor change when doing so provides little additional confidence.
- Do not modify unrelated functionality or tests merely because an issue is discovered during testing.
- Use isolated test data and environments wherever practical.
- Never run destructive tests against real user research data or production systems without appropriate authorization and safeguards.
- External service tests must respect costs, access restrictions, rate limits, privacy and authorization requirements.
- Clearly identify tests that were skipped, unavailable, deferred or outside the selected scope, including the reason.
- Scope limitations must never be used to justify omitting necessary verification.

**Mandatory reporting principles**

- The detailed test report is the authoritative record of test execution.
- The Master Development Roadmap and rolling development record should link to or summarize the report rather than duplicate it.
- Report exact results rather than simply stating that tests passed.
- Never claim scientific validation when only software functionality has been tested.
- Never weaken legitimate tests or alter expected results merely to produce a successful outcome.
- Preserve relevant evidence and failure history.

## 11.4.5 Scientific and Technological Research Records

Maintain research records for significant investigations into scientific methods, technologies, software, external resources and technical approaches.

**Required information**

- Research identifier and title.
- Date and subject of investigation.
- Research question or objective.
- Relevant application capabilities and requirements.
- Sources consulted, including links and publication or access dates where available.
- Scientific or technical findings.
- Evidence supporting important conclusions.
- Alternatives investigated.
- Advantages, limitations and unresolved questions.
- Compatibility, dependencies and integration considerations.
- Licensing, privacy, security and cost considerations where relevant.
- Recommendations and their rationale.
- Whether further investigation or approval is required.
- Related requirements, roadmap entries and decisions.

**Core principles**

- Distinguish established findings from interpretations and assumptions.
- Preserve important unsuccessful investigations.
- Record why a resource or approach was rejected or deferred when that information may prevent repeated work.
- Keep research records distinguishable from approved implementation decisions.
- Update research records when significant new evidence changes their conclusions.

## 11.4.6 Development Recommendations and Improvement Records

Maintain an Improvement Register and supporting records for significant development opportunities.

Use the requirements established in Section 10.8.

**Required information**

- Improvement identifier and description.
- Date identified and source.
- Problem or opportunity being addressed.
- Expected scientific, technical or practical benefit.
- Estimated complexity and effort.
- Risks, dependencies and maintenance implications.
- Alternatives and supporting evidence.
- Recommended priority.
- AI recommendation and user decision where applicable.
- Current status and reason for that status.
- Related roadmap entries and records.

**Standard status categories**

- Proposed.
- Under Investigation.
- Recommended.
- Approved.
- Deferred.
- Rejected.
- Implemented.

The Improvement Register tracks opportunities and their evaluation. It does not replace the Master Development Roadmap or authorize implementation.

## 11.4.7 Rolling Development Records

Maintain a chronological record of significant development activity.

**Required information**

- Date and development session.
- Active stage, objective or roadmap item.
- Significant work performed.
- Files, components and documents affected.
- Research findings and discoveries.
- Decisions and approvals.
- Problems encountered and investigations performed.
- Tests executed and results.
- Changes to development status.
- Outstanding tasks, limitations and blockers.
- Deferred work and reasons.
- Next steps and relevant record references.

**Core principles**

- Update the record throughout development rather than reconstructing everything at the end.
- Preserve enough context for another development partner to understand the work.
- Link detailed investigations, decisions and test reports rather than duplicating their contents.
- Distinguish completed work from proposed or unfinished work.
- Maintain a clear chronological history.

The rolling development record is the detailed chronological development history. The Master Development Roadmap remains the authoritative overview of development status and priorities.

## 11.4.8 Session Handoff Records

Maintain structured handoff summaries when development is paused, transferred or continued in another session.

**Required information**

- Application and project context.
- Current development stage.
- Overall project status.
- Work completed during the relevant period.
- Files and documentation changed.
- Tests performed and results.
- Verified functionality and remaining limitations.
- Current blockers and unresolved problems.
- Decisions and approvals relevant to continuation.
- Deferred tasks and outstanding requirements.
- Relevant roadmap and development-record references.
- Recommended next steps.

Handoffs must describe the actual project state rather than relying on assumptions about what the next development partner already knows.

## 11.4.9 Documentation Templates and Maintenance

**Core requirements**

- Store reusable documentation templates in `docs/templates/`.
- Maintain templates for:
  - Decision records.
  - Problem records.
  - Test reports.
  - Scientific and technological research.
  - Improvement records.
  - Session handoffs.
- Introduce additional templates when recurring documentation needs justify them.
- Keep templates consistent with the requirements established in this section.
- Update templates when the documentation system changes.
- Avoid creating multiple competing templates for the same record type.
- Ensure records created using older templates remain understandable.

Templates should be practical starting points. Additional fields may be added when a particular investigation requires them.

## General Principles

- Maintain consistent records for significant development activities.
- Use individual records for detailed investigations and decisions.
- Preserve evidence, reasoning, outcomes and historical context.
- Make test reports mandatory for relevant implementation and testing tasks.
- Apply testing scope proportionately without weakening verification.
- Link detailed records to the roadmap and rolling development history.
- Keep templates reusable, understandable and maintainable.
- Avoid excessive documentation of routine activities that do not require permanent records.

**General principle:** Important development work must leave behind enough reliable documentation that its purpose, methods, outcomes and remaining limitations can be understood and revisited without repeating the original investigation.
