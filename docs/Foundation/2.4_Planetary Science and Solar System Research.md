

# 2.4 — Planetary Science and Solar System Research

## Purpose

Planetary science is a major scientific discipline within the application, encompassing the study of planets, moons and other planetary bodies, their physical properties, environments, composition, formation and evolution.

The Solar System provides an important foundation for planetary research. Its objects offer opportunities to investigate planetary processes, compare different planetary environments and develop scientific understanding that can also inform exoplanet research.

The application should provide a dedicated area for Solar System research while allowing planetary science to connect naturally with other scientific disciplines and research subjects.

## 2.4.1 Solar System Research Scope

### Core Requirements

- Include the Solar System as a major area of scientific research.
- Support investigations involving the Sun, the eight recognized planets and their natural satellites.
- Accommodate research into individual Solar System objects and relationships between multiple objects.
- Support comparative studies of planetary properties, environments and evolutionary histories.
- Recognize the Solar System as a connected system rather than treating its objects exclusively as independent research subjects.
- Allow Solar System research to connect with broader planetary science, stellar astronomy, observational astronomy and astrophysics.

### Design Guidance

Solar System research should have a recognizable place within the application rather than being treated as a collection of unrelated object records.

The dedicated research area should provide a natural starting point for investigating Solar System objects while allowing users to access relevant scientific tools, data and resources from across the application.

## 2.4.2 The Sun and Solar Research

### Core Requirements

Include the Sun as an important research subject within the Solar System.

Relevant scientific subjects include:

- Solar structure and physical properties.
- Solar atmosphere and atmospheric layers.
- Solar radiation and electromagnetic emission.
- Solar magnetic fields and activity.
- Sunspots and other observable solar phenomena.
- Solar flares and coronal mass ejections.
- Solar wind and interactions with planetary environments.
- Solar variability and its influence on the Solar System.
- Relationships between solar activity and planetary conditions.

### Design Guidance

The Sun should be recognized both as a research subject in its own right and as the central star influencing the Solar System.

Solar research naturally overlaps with stellar astronomy and heliophysics. The application should preserve these relationships without unnecessarily duplicating scientific subject areas.

## 2.4.3 Planetary Research

### Core Requirements

Support scientific investigations of the eight recognized planets:

- Mercury.
- Venus.
- Earth.
- Mars.
- Jupiter.
- Saturn.
- Uranus.
- Neptune.

Relevant research subjects include:

- Planetary physical properties and dimensions.
- Mass, density and gravitational characteristics.
- Orbital properties and rotation.
- Internal structure and composition.
- Surface characteristics and geological processes.
- Planetary atmospheres and atmospheric chemistry.
- Temperature, climate and energy balance.
- Magnetic fields and magnetospheric environments.
- Planetary weather and atmospheric dynamics.
- Formation, evolution and long-term changes.
- Interactions with moons, rings and other surrounding objects.

### Design Guidance

Planetary investigations should accommodate substantial differences between terrestrial planets, gas giants, ice giants and other planetary classifications.

The application should not impose a single scientific model or organizational structure that assumes all planets have equivalent physical characteristics or research requirements.

## 2.4.4 Natural Satellites and Moons

### Core Requirements

Include natural satellites as important planetary research subjects.

Support scientific topics such as:

- Physical properties, dimensions and composition.
- Orbital characteristics and relationships with their parent bodies.
- Surface geology and geological activity.
- Internal structure and possible subsurface environments.
- Atmospheres and exospheres.
- Temperature and environmental conditions.
- Tidal interactions and tidal heating.
- Magnetic and plasma interactions.
- Possible subsurface oceans and other scientifically relevant environments.
- Formation, evolution and relationships with their parent planetary systems.

Recognize moons as independent research subjects while preserving their relationships with the planets and other objects they orbit.

### Design Guidance

Natural satellites should not be treated merely as subordinate information attached to planetary records.

Many moons have substantial scientific importance in their own right, including environments, geological processes and possible astrobiological relevance.

The application should allow research to move naturally between a moon and its parent planet while preserving their distinct scientific identities.

## 2.4.5 Planetary Atmospheres and Environments

### Core Requirements

Support research into planetary atmospheres and the broader environments surrounding Solar System objects.

Relevant subjects include:

- Atmospheric composition and chemical processes.
- Atmospheric pressure and temperature structure.
- Clouds, aerosols and atmospheric particles.
- Atmospheric circulation and dynamics.
- Weather systems and seasonal variation.
- Atmospheric escape and interaction with space.
- Solar radiation and atmospheric responses.
- Magnetospheres and charged-particle environments.
- Radiation environments.
- Interactions between planetary surfaces, interiors and atmospheres.

Recognize that planetary environments differ substantially across the Solar System and may require different scientific approaches.

### Design Guidance

Atmospheric and environmental research should connect with spectroscopy, chemistry, physics, observational astronomy and planetary modelling.

The application should accommodate comparisons between Solar System atmospheres and exoplanetary atmospheres where scientifically appropriate.

## 2.4.6 Planetary Surfaces, Geology and Interiors

### Core Requirements

Include the study of planetary surfaces, geological processes and internal structures.

Relevant subjects include:

- Surface composition and mineralogy.
- Geological formations and terrain.
- Impact cratering.
- Volcanism and tectonic activity.
- Erosion and surface modification.
- Ice, frost and volatile materials.
- Internal layering and differentiation.
- Core, mantle and crust characteristics.
- Thermal evolution and internal energy.
- Relationships between internal processes and observable surface features.

Accommodate equivalent research involving moons and other planetary bodies where relevant.

### Design Guidance

Planetary geology and geophysics should be recognized as important components of planetary science rather than being limited to visual examination of planetary surfaces.

Research should be able to connect surface observations with geological interpretations, physical models and other relevant evidence.

## 2.4.7 Planetary Orbits, Dynamics and System Relationships

### Core Requirements

Support research into the orbital and dynamical characteristics of Solar System objects.

Relevant subjects include:

- Planetary orbits and orbital periods.
- Orbital eccentricity and inclination.
- Rotational periods and axial orientation.
- Gravitational interactions.
- Orbital resonances.
- Planetary and satellite system dynamics.
- Tidal interactions.
- Long-term orbital evolution.
- Relationships between planetary motion and observable phenomena.

Include relevant investigations of the Solar System's overall structure and organization.

### Design Guidance

Orbital and dynamical research should accommodate both individual-object studies and investigations involving multiple interacting bodies.

The application should preserve the context of the physical system being studied, particularly where gravitational or orbital relationships influence scientific interpretation.

## 2.4.8 Additional Solar System Bodies

### Core Requirements

Allow the broader scientific scope to accommodate additional Solar System objects beyond the eight planets and their natural satellites.

Relevant subjects include:

- Dwarf planets.
- Asteroids.
- Comets.
- Trans-Neptunian objects.
- Kuiper Belt objects.
- Centaurs.
- Meteoroids and related small bodies.
- Rings and ring systems.
- Interplanetary dust and other small-scale Solar System components.

Recognize that these objects may contribute to research into Solar System formation, composition, dynamics and evolution.

### Design Guidance

The initial research emphasis should remain on the Sun, eight planets and natural satellites.

Additional object categories should remain within the broader scientific scope without requiring every category to receive dedicated functionality during initial development.

The application should avoid an unnecessarily restrictive object classification system that would prevent future inclusion of scientifically relevant bodies.

## 2.4.9 Comparative Planetary Science

### Core Requirements

Support comparative investigations involving Solar System objects and, where appropriate, exoplanets.

Relevant subjects include:

- Comparisons of planetary size, mass and density.
- Differences in atmospheric composition and structure.
- Comparisons of surface and geological processes.
- Temperature and energy balance.
- Magnetic fields and planetary environments.
- Planetary formation and evolutionary histories.
- Differences between terrestrial planets, gas giants and ice giants.
- Comparisons between natural satellites.
- Relationships between planetary characteristics and their host stars.

### Design Guidance

Comparative planetary science should help users investigate shared physical processes as well as important differences between planetary environments.

Comparisons should preserve relevant differences in measurement methods, observational conditions, uncertainty and scientific context.

Solar System research can provide valuable reference points for exoplanet investigations, but comparisons should not assume that Solar System conditions represent every possible planetary environment.

## 2.4.10 Interdisciplinary Relationships

### Core Requirements

Recognize the relationships between planetary science and other scientific disciplines, including:

- Exoplanet science.
- Stellar astronomy and solar physics.
- Observational astronomy.
- Spectroscopy and molecular science.
- Chemistry and atmospheric chemistry.
- Planetary geology and geophysics.
- Astrophysics.
- Heliophysics and space physics.
- Astrobiology.
- Computational and theoretical science.

Allow Solar System investigations to draw upon relevant scientific resources, observations, concepts and methods from these disciplines.

### Design Guidance

Planetary research should remain connected to the wider scientific environment of the application.

For example, an investigation of a planetary atmosphere may involve spectroscopy, chemical composition, atmospheric dynamics, solar interactions and comparative exoplanet research.

These relationships should be supported without requiring users to organize every investigation within only one scientific discipline.

## General Principles

Planetary science and Solar System research should:

- Provide a dedicated scientific area for Solar System investigations.
- Support research involving the Sun, eight planets and natural satellites.
- Recognize moons and other planetary bodies as important research subjects in their own right.
- Accommodate planetary atmospheres, surfaces, interiors and environments.
- Support orbital and dynamical research.
- Allow future expansion into additional Solar System object categories.
- Encourage comparative planetary research.
- Preserve relationships between objects and their parent systems.
- Connect naturally with exoplanet science and other scientific disciplines.
- Accommodate different scientific approaches according to the physical characteristics of each object.

## General Principle

Solar System research should provide a connected scientific environment for investigating planetary bodies, their environments and their relationships, while also serving as an important foundation for comparative planetary and exoplanet science.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes the scientific subjects and research areas that fall within planetary science and Solar System research.

It does not require every Solar System object category, observational dataset, planetary model or analytical capability to be supported in the initial implementation.

Specific tools, data workflows, modelling capabilities, visualization and research functionality are defined elsewhere in the Foundations documentation, particularly Section 3.
