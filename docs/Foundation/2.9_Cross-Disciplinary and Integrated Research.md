

# 2.9 — Cross-Disciplinary and Integrated Research

## Purpose

Astronomical research frequently involves multiple scientific disciplines, research methods and types of evidence. Many important research questions cannot be adequately investigated through a single scientific subject area.

The application should support integrated research that allows users to combine relevant scientific knowledge, observations, datasets and analytical approaches across disciplinary boundaries.

Cross-disciplinary research should be a natural part of the application's scientific environment rather than requiring users to organize related investigations independently within isolated workspaces or subject categories.

The application should preserve the scientific context, relationships and methodological requirements of each contributing discipline while allowing them to contribute to a shared research objective.

## 2.9.1 Cross-Disciplinary Research Principles

### Core Requirements

- Support investigations involving multiple scientific disciplines.
- Allow research questions to draw upon different astronomical objects, systems and phenomena.
- Accommodate combinations of observational, theoretical, computational and analytical approaches.
- Preserve the scientific identity and context of contributing research materials.
- Allow users to connect related observations, datasets, analyses and results across disciplines.
- Avoid requiring investigations to belong exclusively to one scientific category.
- Support both straightforward interdisciplinary comparisons and complex integrated investigations.
- Maintain scientific accuracy when combining information from different disciplines.

### Design Guidance

Cross-disciplinary research should be supported without removing the distinctions between the scientific fields involved.

Combining several disciplines does not mean their methods, assumptions or standards of evidence become interchangeable.

The application should allow different scientific approaches to contribute to a shared investigation while preserving the context necessary to interpret each contribution correctly.

## 2.9.2 Integration Across Scientific Disciplines

### Core Requirements

Support meaningful relationships between the scientific disciplines established in Section 2.

Relevant combinations include:

- Exoplanet science and planetary science.
- Exoplanet science and stellar astronomy.
- Planetary science and observational astronomy.
- Stellar astronomy and astrophysics.
- Spectroscopy and astrochemistry.
- Planetary science and atmospheric chemistry.
- Observational astronomy and theoretical astrophysics.
- Astrobiology and planetary or exoplanetary research.
- Galactic astronomy and stellar population studies.
- Solar physics and planetary environmental research.

Allow investigations to incorporate additional scientific disciplines as the application's scope expands.

### Design Guidance

Disciplinary integration should be based on scientific relevance rather than arbitrary organizational relationships.

For example, an investigation into an exoplanet atmosphere may involve:

- Planetary science to understand the planet's physical properties.
- Stellar astronomy to characterize its host star.
- Observational astronomy to examine the available measurements.
- Spectroscopy to investigate observed spectral features.
- Chemistry to evaluate possible molecular constituents.
- Atmospheric physics to interpret atmospheric structure.
- Computational science to compare observations with theoretical models.

These disciplines contribute different forms of scientific understanding to the same research objective.

## 2.9.3 Integration Across Astronomical Objects and Systems

### Core Requirements

- Allow investigations to involve multiple related astronomical objects.
- Preserve relationships between objects and their parent systems.
- Support comparisons between objects belonging to different systems.
- Accommodate investigations involving stars, planets, moons and other associated bodies.
- Allow research to extend from individual objects to broader populations and environments.
- Preserve the distinction between individual objects, connected systems and larger astronomical structures.
- Support investigations involving objects that participate in several scientific relationships.

### Design Guidance

Research questions often extend beyond the primary object being investigated.

For example, understanding an exoplanet may require examining its host star, other planets in the system, orbital relationships and surrounding environment.

Similarly, investigating a planetary atmosphere may require comparing it with other planetary atmospheres or examining the influence of solar radiation and planetary magnetic conditions.

The application should allow users to follow these scientific relationships without unnecessarily duplicating objects or their associated research materials.

## 2.9.4 Integration of Different Observational Methods

### Core Requirements

Support research that combines or compares different observational methods.

Relevant combinations include:

- Spectroscopy and photometry.
- Imaging and spectroscopic measurements.
- Time-series observations and individual measurements.
- Transmission and emission spectroscopy.
- Multi-wavelength observations.
- Ground-based and space-based observations.
- Observations from different instruments and facilities.
- Direct observations and independently derived measurements.

Allow different observational methods to contribute to the same research question when scientifically appropriate.

### Design Guidance

Different observational methods may provide complementary information about the same object or phenomenon.

For example, photometric observations may constrain planetary transit properties, while spectroscopic observations may provide information about atmospheric composition.

Combining these observations should preserve their individual measurement characteristics, observational conditions, uncertainties and limitations.

The application should not assume that different types of observational evidence can be combined directly without considering their scientific compatibility.

## 2.9.5 Integration of Observations, Models and Theoretical Research

### Core Requirements

- Support investigations combining observational evidence with theoretical or computational models.
- Allow model predictions to be compared with relevant observations.
- Preserve distinctions between measured data, calculated quantities, model outputs and scientific interpretations.
- Accommodate multiple models or competing theoretical explanations.
- Allow research conclusions to reference the evidence and assumptions supporting them.
- Preserve the relationship between original observations and subsequent model-based investigations.
- Support revisions to interpretations without losing previous results.

### Design Guidance

Observational and theoretical research provide different but complementary forms of scientific understanding.

Models may help interpret observations, evaluate possible explanations and predict physical behaviour. However, model agreement should not automatically be treated as proof that a particular interpretation is correct.

The application should preserve the distinction between what has been observed, what has been calculated and what has been predicted or inferred.

## 2.9.6 Integration of Scientific Data and Evidence

### Core Requirements

- Allow relevant datasets and observations from different scientific areas to contribute to shared investigations.
- Preserve the original identity and provenance of contributing data.
- Maintain relevant metadata, units, measurement conventions and uncertainty.
- Support comparisons between datasets obtained through different methods or sources.
- Identify important differences in data quality, coverage and compatibility.
- Allow shared source data to support multiple independent investigations.
- Preserve relationships between original observations, derived datasets and analytical results.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of scientific resources.

### Design Guidance

Integrated research may involve data from different archives, instruments, observational campaigns and scientific disciplines.

These resources should remain identifiable and traceable to their original sources.

Combining data for a shared investigation must not erase important differences in their measurement methods, calibration, processing history or scientific limitations.

Data compatibility should be evaluated according to the research question rather than assumed from superficial similarities in format or subject matter.

## 2.9.7 Comparative and Population-Level Research

### Core Requirements

Support investigations that compare multiple astronomical objects, systems, observations or populations across scientific disciplines.

Relevant research may include:

- Comparisons between Solar System planets and exoplanets.
- Comparisons between planetary and stellar atmospheres.
- Relationships between host-star properties and exoplanet characteristics.
- Comparisons between observed and theoretically predicted planetary properties.
- Studies of atmospheric composition across different planetary populations.
- Comparisons between different stellar populations.
- Investigations of shared physical or chemical processes across astronomical environments.
- Relationships between individual observations and broader population trends.

### Design Guidance

Comparative research should preserve the scientific context of each contributing object and dataset.

Differences in observational coverage, measurement methods, uncertainties and selection effects should be considered when interpreting apparent similarities or differences.

The application should accommodate both exploratory comparisons and more structured scientific investigations.

## 2.9.8 Integrated Research Questions and Investigations

### Core Requirements

- Allow research to be organized around scientific questions that span multiple disciplines.
- Support investigations that develop from an initial question into related sub-investigations.
- Allow related analyses and research materials to contribute to a broader objective.
- Preserve relationships between the primary research question and its supporting investigations.
- Allow users to organize interdisciplinary work according to their preferred research structure.
- Support investigations that evolve as new observations, evidence or interpretations become available.
- Avoid imposing a single organizational structure on all interdisciplinary research.

### Design Guidance

A research question should not need to fit neatly within a predefined scientific discipline to be supported by the application.

For example, a question about the relationship between stellar metallicity and exoplanet atmospheric composition may involve stellar characterization, planetary science, spectroscopy, chemistry and population-level analysis.

The investigation should be able to maintain a coherent research objective while accommodating its different scientific components.

## 2.9.9 Scientific Compatibility and Methodological Consistency

### Core Requirements

- Preserve the scientific requirements and assumptions of individual analytical methods.
- Recognize when datasets, measurements or models are unsuitable for direct comparison.
- Identify incompatible units, conventions, measurement systems or scientific assumptions where relevant.
- Maintain the distinction between compatible and merely related scientific information.
- Preserve uncertainty and limitations when combining evidence.
- Avoid automatically applying methods from one discipline to another without evaluating their suitability.
- Make important methodological differences understandable to users.

### Design Guidance

Cross-disciplinary research should not compromise scientific validity for the sake of integration.

A method that is appropriate for one type of astronomical object or dataset may not be suitable for another.

The application should support scientific integration while retaining discipline-specific methodological requirements and allowing users to evaluate whether a particular combination is justified.

## 2.9.10 Future Interdisciplinary Expansion

### Core Requirements

- Allow new scientific disciplines to connect with existing research areas.
- Accommodate emerging research questions that cross established disciplinary boundaries.
- Support additional combinations of astronomical objects, observations and analytical approaches.
- Preserve existing scientific relationships as new disciplines and capabilities are introduced.
- Avoid requiring major restructuring whenever a new interdisciplinary research area is added.
- Allow the application to evolve alongside developments in astronomical research.

### Design Guidance

Interdisciplinary research will likely become increasingly important as new observational facilities, datasets and scientific methods become available.

The application should be designed to accommodate these developments without attempting to predict every future research combination in advance.

New integrations should be introduced according to their scientific relevance, feasibility and contribution to the overall research environment.

## General Principles

Cross-disciplinary and integrated research should:

- Allow multiple scientific disciplines to contribute to shared investigations.
- Preserve relationships between astronomical objects, systems and research questions.
- Support the integration of different observational methods.
- Accommodate combinations of observational, theoretical and computational research.
- Preserve the identity, provenance and scientific context of contributing data.
- Maintain methodological consistency and scientific compatibility.
- Distinguish observations, calculations, models and interpretations.
- Support comparative and population-level investigations.
- Allow interdisciplinary research structures to evolve over time.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication and artificial disciplinary restrictions.

## General Principle

The application should treat interdisciplinary research as a natural part of astronomy, allowing different scientific disciplines, objects, observations and methods to contribute to coherent investigations without compromising their individual scientific context or validity.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes the scientific and organizational principles for interdisciplinary research.

It does not define specific integration mechanisms, workspace arrangements, project structures, analytical workflows or technical implementation methods.

Those requirements are addressed elsewhere in the Foundations documentation, particularly Sections 3, 4 and 6.

Cross-disciplinary integration should be introduced incrementally, with scientific compatibility and practical research value guiding development priorities.
