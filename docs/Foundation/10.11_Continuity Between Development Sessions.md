# 10.11 — Continuity Between Development Sessions

## Purpose

Ensure the AI development partner can maintain consistent, informed and reliable development across multiple conversations, sessions and stages of the application's lifecycle.

The AI must preserve important project knowledge, development progress, decisions, problems, research findings and outstanding work so that development can resume without repeatedly reconstructing previous discussions or losing important context.

Continuity must be supported by persistent documentation and records rather than relying on conversational memory.

## 10.11.1 Persistent Development Knowledge

**Core requirements**

- Maintain accurate, accessible records of important development knowledge.
- Preserve established requirements, scientific findings, technical research, architectural decisions and implementation details.
- Record important assumptions, limitations, dependencies and unresolved questions.
- Maintain consistency between requirements, implementation, testing and development documentation.
- Update relevant records when significant information changes.
- Avoid relying exclusively on previous conversations for important project information.
- Preserve unsuccessful investigations and decisions when they may prevent repeated work.
- Ensure new development sessions can recover the context necessary to continue responsibly.

**Design guidance**

The AI should treat project documentation as the authoritative source of persistent development knowledge.

Conversational history may provide additional context, but it must not replace properly maintained project records.

## 10.11.2 Master Development Roadmap

The application must maintain one authoritative Master Development Roadmap as the central overview of development.

**Core requirements**

- Maintain the roadmap at its established canonical location: `docs/MASTER_DEVELOPMENT_ROADMAP.md`.
- Consult it when beginning a new development session.
- Consult it before planning or implementing new work.
- Use it to understand current priorities, completed work, dependencies, blockers and upcoming development stages.
- Update it whenever significant development status or priorities change.
- Preserve a clear distinction between planned, approved, in-progress, completed, deferred, cancelled and blocked work.
- Identify dependencies, milestones, risks, outstanding questions and unresolved requirements.
- Distinguish implemented functionality from tested, functionally verified and scientifically validated functionality.
- Link to detailed documentation and records rather than duplicating extensive information.
- Maintain consistency with actual application and documentation status.

**Required update occasions**

Update the roadmap when:

- New work is approved.
- Development begins or reaches a significant milestone.
- A task is completed and appropriately verified.
- Testing identifies defects or unresolved limitations.
- Dependencies or blockers affect development.
- Requirements or priorities change.
- Work is deferred, cancelled or replaced.
- New research identifies important opportunities or constraints.

**Design guidance**

The roadmap is the mandatory central development overview, not a substitute for detailed technical documentation, individual problem records or chronological development history.

There must not be competing documents presenting conflicting development status.

The roadmap's location should remain stable. If relocation becomes necessary, update all relevant references and preserve a clear transition.

## 10.11.3 Rolling Development Record

Maintain a persistent chronological development record that captures meaningful development activity and provides continuity between sessions.

**Core requirements**

Record significant activities, including:

- Development objectives and work undertaken.
- Implementation changes and affected components.
- Research and technical investigations.
- Important findings and discoveries.
- Decisions and approvals.
- Testing and verification outcomes.
- Problems, unsuccessful approaches and resolutions.
- Changes to requirements, priorities and development direction.
- Outstanding tasks, blockers and deferred work.
- Relevant documentation and roadmap updates.

Each significant entry should communicate:

- What happened.
- Why it happened.
- What was investigated or changed.
- How the work was performed.
- The outcome and verification status.
- What remains outstanding.
- Relevant files, decisions, problems and documentation.

**Design guidance**

The rolling development record should provide chronological context without becoming a duplicate of every technical document or test report.

Detailed information should remain in its designated records, with links or references maintained where appropriate.

The record must be updated throughout development rather than reconstructed only when a session ends.

## 10.11.4 Problem Records and Investigation History

Maintain individual records for significant development problems, investigations and unresolved blockers.

**Core requirements**

- Create a problem record for each distinct significant problem.
- Preserve confirmed findings, suspected causes, assumptions and relevant evidence.
- Record attempted solutions, including unsuccessful approaches.
- Document the selected resolution or recommended alternatives.
- Record implementation, recovery and verification outcomes.
- Identify affected components, dependencies, tasks and roadmap items.
- Preserve unresolved problems until appropriately resolved or formally closed.
- Update existing records when investigations continue rather than creating unnecessary duplicates.
- Consult previous problem records before resuming work on a known issue.

**Design guidance**

Problem records should follow the structure established in Section 10.7.

They must preserve enough information to prevent repeated troubleshooting, explain previous decisions and support responsible continuation.

## 10.11.5 Decision and Approval Continuity

The AI must preserve consequential decisions and user approvals across development sessions.

**Core requirements**

- Maintain records of important user decisions and approvals.
- Preserve the reasoning, alternatives and conditions associated with consequential decisions.
- Identify whether decisions were approved, rejected, deferred, modified or remain pending.
- Preserve changes to established requirements and preferences.
- Identify decisions that have been superseded or require reassessment.
- Consult relevant decision records before proposing changes to established requirements or architecture.
- Never treat an unapproved recommendation as an established decision.
- Avoid repeatedly requesting approval for decisions that have already been resolved unless circumstances materially change.

**Design guidance**

Decision records should remain authoritative and distinguish user-approved decisions from AI recommendations, assumptions and temporary implementation choices.

Their detailed format and organization should be established in Section 11.

## 10.11.6 Session Startup and Development Resumption

The AI must follow a consistent process when beginning a new development session.

**Core requirements**

At the beginning of a new session, the AI should:

1. Locate and consult the Master Development Roadmap.
2. Review the latest rolling development record.
3. Identify the current development stage and active priorities.
4. Review relevant requirements and technical documentation.
5. Check outstanding problems, blockers and unresolved decisions.
6. Review relevant approvals and conditions.
7. Inspect the actual codebase and implementation state before making assumptions about what currently exists.
8. Identify the appropriate next task and its dependencies.
9. Determine whether additional research, clarification or approval is required before proceeding.

Before resuming interrupted work, the AI must establish:

- What was previously being attempted.
- What was completed.
- What remains incomplete.
- Whether the underlying conditions or dependencies have changed.
- Whether previous approaches failed and why.
- Whether the work remains consistent with current requirements and priorities.

**Design guidance**

The AI should not automatically assume that the next task mentioned in an earlier conversation remains the highest priority.

It must evaluate the current project state and roadmap before continuing.

## 10.11.7 Session Handoffs and Continuation Summaries

At the end of a development session, the AI should provide a useful handoff summary.

**Core requirements**

Summaries should identify:

- Current development stage.
- Work completed during the session.
- Files, components and documentation changed.
- Tests performed and their results.
- Verification status and remaining limitations.
- Outstanding tasks and incomplete implementation.
- Active problems and blockers.
- Decisions or approvals still required.
- Deferred work and dependencies.
- Recommended next steps.
- Relevant roadmap entries and development records.

Handoff summaries must:

- Reflect the actual state of the project.
- Avoid claiming incomplete work is finished.
- Identify anything that must be checked before further implementation.
- Provide enough context for another development session to resume without repeating completed work.
- Refer to persistent documentation rather than relying on the summary as the only record.

**Design guidance**

A handoff summary should be concise enough to be useful while retaining the information necessary for continuity.

The underlying project records must remain authoritative.

## 10.11.8 Documentation Organization and Record Relationships

Development records must remain organized so the AI can locate and maintain them reliably.

**Core requirements**

- Maintain clearly defined locations for requirements, architecture, technical documentation, decisions, development history, problems, testing and roadmap records.
- Establish authoritative documents for each major type of information.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication and conflicting records.
- Use consistent naming and organization.
- Link related records where appropriate.
- Preserve historical information when documents are revised or reorganized.
- Ensure documentation remains consistent with actual implementation.
- Make it possible to identify which records require updating after significant changes.

**Design guidance**

The detailed documentation structure, directory organization, templates and relationships between records will be established in Section 11.

The AI should not independently invent competing documentation systems when an established structure already exists.

## 10.11.9 Mandatory Documentation and Reporting Templates

The AI should use consistent templates for recurring development records where standardized documentation improves clarity and continuity.

**Core requirements**

Establish and maintain standardized templates for:

- Development and session handoffs.
- Individual problem records.
- Consequential decision and approval records.
- Test reports.
- Scientific and technical research reviews.
- Significant development recommendations.
- Other recurring records where consistency provides meaningful value.

Templates must capture the information necessary for their intended purpose without requiring unnecessary documentation.

**Mandatory test-report requirements**

Every relevant implementation or testing task must use the standardized test-report format established in Section 10.6.

The report must capture:

- Test scope and purpose.
- Date and test environment.
- Changes or functionality being tested.
- Tests executed and commands or references.
- Exact results, including passed, failed, skipped and unexecuted tests.
- Relevant evidence and outputs.
- Failures and investigations.
- Scientific verification or validation, reported separately.
- Limitations, residual risks and verification status.

Detailed test reports should remain authoritative. The roadmap and rolling development record should reference or summarize them rather than duplicating the complete report.

**Design guidance**

Templates should improve consistency and completeness, not become unnecessary administrative overhead.

## 10.11.10 Knowledge Preservation and Long-Term Continuity

The AI must preserve important development knowledge even when individual sessions, conversations, implementation approaches or technical dependencies change.

**Core requirements**

- Preserve important scientific and technical research findings.
- Retain the reasoning behind consequential architectural and implementation decisions.
- Preserve known limitations, failed approaches and unresolved questions.
- Maintain relevant dependency and compatibility information.
- Document significant changes to scientific methods, external resources and software.
- Preserve enough information to understand historical development and research decisions.
- Avoid silently replacing historical findings or decisions with newer interpretations.
- Update documentation when important knowledge becomes outdated.
- Ensure the application can continue development even if a particular AI session or development partner is no longer available.

**Design guidance**

The project's accumulated knowledge should belong to the project documentation, not to a particular conversation or AI instance.

Documentation should allow a new development session or another capable development partner to understand the application and continue work responsibly.

## General Principles

The AI development partner must:

- Treat persistent documentation as the authoritative source of development continuity.
- Consult the Master Development Roadmap at the beginning of new sessions and whenever development direction is evaluated.
- Maintain an accurate chronological development record.
- Preserve individual problem investigations and unsuccessful approaches.
- Record consequential decisions and approvals.
- Inspect actual implementation state before resuming work.
- Provide accurate session handoffs.
- Maintain organized, authoritative documentation.
- Use standardized records and mandatory test reports.
- Preserve important knowledge across conversations, sessions and changes in development personnel.
- Avoid repeated investigations, conflicting decisions and unnecessary reconstruction of previous work.

## Implementation Considerations

- The Master Development Roadmap is mandatory and must remain at `docs/MASTER_DEVELOPMENT_ROADMAP.md`, as established in Section 10.3.
- Problem records and investigation procedures must align with Section 10.7.
- Test-report requirements must align with Section 10.6.
- Decision and approval records must align with Sections 10.4 and 10.9.
- Development documentation must remain consistent with actual implementation and Section 10.5.
- The detailed documentation directory structure, record templates, naming conventions and cross-references must be established in Section 11.
- The AI must preserve continuity without relying exclusively on conversational memory or summaries.
- Documentation requirements should remain proportionate to the significance of the work.

**General principle:** Development knowledge, decisions and progress must remain part of the application project rather than being lost between conversations. The AI should be able to resume work with an accurate understanding of the application's current state, previous reasoning and outstanding responsibilities, without repeatedly starting over.
