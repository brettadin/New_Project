# 1.5 — Core Philosophy and Guiding Principles

## Purpose

Establish the fundamental philosophy governing the application and the principles that should guide its development and operation.

The application should be built around scientific credibility, meaningful functionality, usability, transparency and long-term adaptability. Its purpose is not simply to accumulate scientific tools or provide the greatest possible number of features, but to create a coherent environment that genuinely supports astronomical research.

Scientific integrity must remain central to every aspect of the application. Convenience, automation, accessibility and visual presentation should strengthen scientific work without compromising the reliability, traceability or understandability of its results.

## 1.5.1 Scientific Integrity and Credibility

Scientific integrity must be a fundamental requirement throughout the application.

**Core requirements**

- Prioritize scientifically valid methods, reliable data and appropriate analytical procedures.
- Use established scientific methods and references where applicable.
- Distinguish established scientific knowledge from hypotheses, assumptions, approximations and exploratory interpretations.
- Communicate methodological limitations, uncertainties and relevant sources of error.
- Avoid presenting uncertain or unsupported conclusions as established scientific results.
- Preserve the distinction between measured observations, processed data, model outputs and interpreted results.
- Make scientific methods and analytical decisions sufficiently transparent for users to understand and evaluate them.
- Support appropriate validation and verification of scientific functionality.
- Avoid sacrificing scientific accuracy for convenience, presentation or simplified workflows.

**Design principle**

The application should earn scientific credibility through the reliability, transparency and traceability of its functionality and results rather than relying on presentation or claims of sophistication.

## 1.5.2 Transparency and User Understanding

Users should be able to understand what the application is doing, why an operation is being performed and how its results were produced.

**Core requirements**

- Explain important analytical operations and methodological choices.
- Communicate relevant assumptions, parameters and limitations.
- Identify the sources of data and external resources.
- Make processing and analysis steps inspectable where appropriate.
- Distinguish automated operations from user-directed actions.
- Communicate warnings, errors and potential problems clearly.
- Avoid concealing significant scientific decisions behind automated processes.
- Provide access to supporting information when results require further examination.
- Make uncertainty and limitations understandable without unnecessarily obscuring scientific detail.

**Design principle**

The application should make scientific processes understandable and inspectable rather than functioning as an opaque system that simply produces answers.

## 1.5.3 Reproducibility and Research Traceability

Scientific investigations should remain understandable and reproducible wherever technically and legally possible.

**Core requirements**

- Preserve relevant data sources, metadata, configurations and analytical methods.
- Maintain records of significant processing and analytical activities.
- Connect results to the observations, datasets and methods that produced them.
- Support revisiting, extending and comparing previous investigations.
- Preserve historical results when methods, data or configurations change.
- Distinguish original data from processed and derived materials.
- Make important changes and analytical decisions traceable.
- Support appropriate export and preservation of research materials.

**Design principle**

Research results should not exist as isolated outputs. Their supporting data, methods, assumptions and processing history should remain accessible so users can understand and evaluate how they were obtained.

## 1.5.4 Usability and Scientific Accessibility

The application should make scientific work approachable without reducing the depth or rigor of its capabilities.

**Core requirements**

- Maintain clear and understandable navigation and interaction.
- Provide appropriate explanations and contextual guidance.
- Support different levels of scientific and technical experience.
- Avoid unnecessary interface complexity and repetitive operations.
- Present scientific information clearly and accurately.
- Provide access to advanced functionality without unnecessarily burdening ordinary workflows.
- Make important scientific information available rather than hiding it for the sake of simplicity.
- Consider accessibility throughout design and development.

**Design principle**

Usability should make scientific capabilities easier to understand and operate, not simplify them to the point that they lose their scientific value.

## 1.5.5 Modularity and Meaningful Integration

The application should be organized so that its scientific capabilities can develop independently while contributing to a coherent overall environment.

**Core requirements**

- Organize functionality into understandable scientific and functional components.
- Allow specialized capabilities to retain their own methodological requirements.
- Support meaningful interaction between compatible components.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of functionality.
- Minimize unnecessary dependencies between unrelated capabilities.
- Allow individual components to be improved or replaced without requiring unrelated parts of the application to be redesigned.
- Preserve consistency across shared functionality.
- Evaluate integration according to scientific and practical value.

**Design principle**

Modularity should support maintainability and scientific specialization, while integration should provide meaningful connections between related capabilities.

Neither should be pursued at the expense of the other.

## 1.5.6 User Control and Scientific Responsibility

Users should retain appropriate control over their research, data, analytical decisions and application behaviour.

**Core requirements**

- Keep users informed about significant operations and changes.
- Provide appropriate control over automated processes.
- Make important analytical parameters and assumptions accessible.
- Allow users to inspect and question results.
- Avoid silently changing original data or historical research records.
- Require appropriate authorization for consequential actions.
- Provide clear ways to review, revise or reject proposed operations.
- Preserve user ownership and control over private research materials.

**Design principle**

The application should assist scientific investigation without unnecessarily removing the user's ability to understand, direct or evaluate the work being performed.

## 1.5.7 Responsible Automation and Assistance

Automation and intelligent assistance should improve efficiency while preserving scientific judgment and user oversight.

**Core requirements**

- Automate appropriate routine operations where doing so is reliable and useful.
- Make automated actions and their outcomes understandable.
- Preserve user control over significant scientific decisions.
- Communicate uncertainty, limitations and potential errors.
- Avoid presenting automated interpretations as independently verified scientific conclusions.
- Allow users to review and modify automated results where appropriate.
- Ensure automation follows the scientific requirements and safeguards of the relevant operation.
- Accommodate different levels of automation according to user needs and task requirements.

**Design principle**

Automation should reduce unnecessary work and support scientific investigation, not replace critical scientific judgment or conceal important decisions.

Detailed automation requirements are established in Section 5.2.

## 1.5.8 Reliability, Maintainability and Long-Term Sustainability

The application should be developed with long-term reliability and continued improvement in mind.

**Core requirements**

- Prioritize reliable and maintainable functionality.
- Avoid unnecessary technical complexity and fragile dependencies.
- Support appropriate testing and validation.
- Document significant design and implementation decisions.
- Preserve important research data and historical records.
- Evaluate dependencies and external integrations for ongoing suitability.
- Allow scientific methods and capabilities to be improved as requirements evolve.
- Consider long-term maintenance and resource requirements when evaluating new functionality.

**Design principle**

A capability should not be considered successful merely because it works once. It should be understandable, maintainable, appropriately validated and suitable for continued use.

## 1.5.9 Extensibility and Scientific Adaptability

The application should be capable of accommodating new scientific requirements and changing research practices.

**Core requirements**

- Avoid unnecessarily restricting the application to its initial scientific scope.
- Allow additional disciplines, methods, datasets and tools to be considered.
- Preserve opportunities to incorporate improved scientific methods and external resources.
- Evaluate new capabilities according to their scientific value and practical suitability.
- Avoid introducing complexity solely to anticipate hypothetical future requirements.
- Maintain a development foundation that can evolve without unnecessary disruption to existing functionality.

**Design principle**

The application should remain open to meaningful scientific expansion without becoming overengineered in anticipation of possibilities that have not yet been established.

## 1.5.10 Purposeful Development and Feature Prioritization

Development should remain guided by the application's central purpose rather than the accumulation of features.

**Core requirements**

- Evaluate proposed functionality according to its contribution to scientific research.
- Consider scientific value, user benefit, reliability, feasibility and maintenance requirements.
- Prioritize foundational capabilities that support multiple research activities.
- Avoid duplicating existing functionality without a clear justification.
- Distinguish essential initial requirements from optional future possibilities.
- Develop complex capabilities incrementally.
- Reconsider priorities when new research or technical information becomes available.
- Preserve the overall vision while allowing implementation priorities to evolve.

**Design principle**

The application should prioritize meaningful scientific capability over feature quantity. A smaller number of reliable, well-integrated tools is preferable to a larger collection of poorly connected or insufficiently validated features.

## 1.5.11 Scientific Independence and External Resources

The application should benefit from established scientific knowledge, methods, software and resources while maintaining its own identity and development direction.

**Core requirements**

- Evaluate external scientific resources according to their reliability and relevance.
- Recognize and attribute external data, methods, software and contributions appropriately.
- Preserve awareness of licensing, access conditions and usage restrictions.
- Avoid unnecessary dependence on individual external services or providers.
- Distinguish the application's own capabilities from those supplied by external tools.
- Maintain control over the application's scientific direction and development priorities.
- Evaluate external integrations according to their long-term suitability.

**Design principle**

The application should build upon the wider scientific and software ecosystem without surrendering its independence or relying uncritically on external resources.

## General Principles

- Scientific integrity and credibility take priority over convenience or presentation.
- Scientific methods, data sources and analytical decisions should remain transparent.
- Research materials and results should be traceable and reproducible wherever possible.
- Usability should improve access to scientific capabilities without sacrificing depth.
- Modularity and integration should support one another.
- Users should retain appropriate control over their research and analytical decisions.
- Automation should assist rather than replace scientific judgment.
- Reliability, validation and maintainability should guide development.
- The application should remain adaptable without becoming unnecessarily complex.
- Development priorities should reflect meaningful scientific and practical value.
- External resources should be evaluated and attributed appropriately.
- The application should function as a coherent scientific environment rather than an accumulation of disconnected features.

**General principle:** Every aspect of the application should contribute to a scientifically credible, understandable, reliable and maintainable research environment. Scientific value, transparency and user control should guide decisions throughout its development and continued operation.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes the fundamental philosophy and guiding principles of the application.

It does not prescribe specific scientific methods, validation procedures, software architecture, interface designs, automation mechanisms or development technologies.

Detailed requirements are established in the relevant sections of the Foundations documentation and should be evaluated through the appropriate scientific research, technical planning and approval processes.
