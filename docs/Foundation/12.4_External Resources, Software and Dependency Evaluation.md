# 12.4 — External Resources, Software and Dependency Evaluation

## Purpose

Establish a systematic approach to identifying, evaluating and recommending external scientific resources, software, libraries, databases and services that may support the application.

The AI development partner must independently investigate available resources and determine their scientific value, technical suitability, accessibility, reliability and long-term practicality before recommending integration.

The application should benefit from established scientific infrastructure wherever appropriate rather than unnecessarily recreating existing capabilities.

## 12.4.1 External Resource Discovery

**Core requirements**

- Independently identify scientific databases, archives, catalogues, reference libraries, software, frameworks and computational resources relevant to the application.
- Investigate resources beyond those already identified in the requirements.
- Consider resources supporting multiple scientific disciplines and research activities.
- Identify specialized resources that may provide capabilities unavailable through general-purpose services.
- Investigate alternative resources where existing services are unavailable, unsuitable or restricted.
- Maintain awareness of relevant scientific and technical developments.
- Document discoveries and their potential applications.

Potential resource categories include:

- Astronomical observation archives.
- Exoplanet and planetary databases.
- Stellar catalogues and astronomical object databases.
- Spectroscopic and molecular reference databases.
- Laboratory measurement collections.
- Scientific literature and publication databases.
- Numerical and statistical libraries.
- Astronomy-specific software and computational tools.
- Modelling and simulation packages.
- Scientific visualization libraries.
- External computational services.

## 12.4.2 Scientific Databases and Reference Resources

**Core requirements**

Investigate the scientific suitability of external databases and reference resources.

Evaluate:

- Scientific authority and reliability.
- Data coverage and completeness.
- Relevant objects, observations and scientific disciplines.
- Measurement quality and available uncertainties.
- Metadata, identifiers and provenance.
- Data formats, units and conventions.
- Update frequency and historical availability.
- Accessibility and retrieval methods.
- Licensing and permitted use.
- Compatibility with intended application workflows.

Identify whether resources provide original observations, processed measurements, reference data, theoretical predictions, catalogued properties or published interpretations.

Do not treat different resource types as interchangeable.

## 12.4.3 Scientific Software and Libraries

**Core requirements**

Investigate established software and libraries that may provide or support scientific functionality.

Potential areas include:

- Astronomy and astrophysics.
- Spectroscopy and signal processing.
- Photometry and time-series analysis.
- Atmospheric modelling and radiative transfer.
- Numerical computation and statistics.
- Scientific data formats and conversion.
- Graphing and visualization.
- Database access and data retrieval.
- Machine learning and computational modelling where scientifically justified.

Evaluate:

- Scientific credibility and methodological accuracy.
- Available functionality and limitations.
- Documentation and examples.
- Compatibility with candidate application technologies.
- Dependency requirements.
- Performance and resource consumption.
- Licensing and redistribution restrictions.
- Maintenance activity and long-term sustainability.
- Testing and validation evidence.
- Opportunities for reuse across scientific workspaces.

Prefer established and appropriately maintained solutions when they satisfy the application's requirements.

## 12.4.4 External Applications and Computational Tools

**Core requirements**

- Investigate standalone scientific applications that may complement the application.
- Identify capabilities that can be accessed through libraries, APIs, command-line interfaces, file exchange or other supported interfaces.
- Evaluate whether functionality should be integrated directly, accessed externally or provided through a combination of approaches.
- Consider compatibility with existing workflows and data structures.
- Investigate how inputs, outputs, configurations and provenance can be preserved.
- Identify external software that may be useful without requiring permanent integration.
- Assess the maintenance and operational implications of depending on external applications.

Do not assume that every useful scientific application needs to be embedded within the platform.

An external tool may remain independently operated while exchanging compatible scientific data with the application.

## 12.4.5 External Resource and Dependency Evaluation

**Core requirements**

Evaluate potential resources and dependencies using consistent criteria.

Consider:

1. **Scientific value:** Relevance, authority, accuracy and usefulness.
2. **Coverage:** Data availability, completeness and unique capabilities.
3. **Technical accessibility:** APIs, interfaces, formats, documentation and authentication requirements.
4. **Compatibility:** Integration with existing architecture, technologies and scientific workflows.
5. **Data quality:** Metadata, uncertainty, provenance, units and validation.
6. **Performance:** Processing requirements, transfer speeds, memory and computational demands.
7. **Licensing:** Usage, modification, redistribution and commercial restrictions.
8. **Security and privacy:** Credential handling, data transmission and external access.
9. **Maintenance:** Development activity, dependency stability, support and availability.
10. **Practical cost:** Development effort, hosting, storage, subscriptions and ongoing maintenance.
11. **Long-term value:** Future usefulness, interoperability and contribution to application expansion.

Identify overlapping functionality and simpler alternatives before introducing additional dependencies.

## 12.4.6 Integration Strategy and Prioritization

**Core requirements**

- Recommend integration only when it provides meaningful scientific or practical value.
- Identify whether a resource should be integrated initially, considered later, used externally or rejected.
- Prioritize resources according to scientific importance, technical feasibility, dependencies and maintenance requirements.
- Consider opportunities for one resource or library to support multiple workspaces.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of existing application capabilities.
- Identify whether a resource requires additional infrastructure or architectural changes.
- Consider the consequences of relying on a single external provider.
- Preserve alternatives where resource availability or compatibility may change.

**Priority classifications**

- **High:** Important to foundational scientific capabilities or essential workflows.
- **Medium:** Valuable resource that supports important functionality but is not an immediate prerequisite.
- **Low:** Useful additional capability with limited initial importance.
- **Deferred:** Potentially valuable but currently impractical, inaccessible or dependent on future development.
- **Rejected:** Unsuitable due to scientific, technical, licensing, security, maintenance or practical concerns.

Priorities should be revisited when requirements, available technologies or resource conditions change.

## 12.4.7 Dependency Management and Sustainability

**Core requirements**

- Maintain awareness of external dependencies and their relationships to application functionality.
- Identify which capabilities rely on particular libraries, services or databases.
- Investigate dependency compatibility and version requirements.
- Evaluate security vulnerabilities and maintenance concerns.
- Identify abandoned, deprecated or unstable dependencies.
- Consider replacement options and fallback approaches.
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies that increase development or maintenance complexity.
- Preserve relevant dependency versions and configurations for reproducibility.
- Identify the impact of dependency updates on existing functionality and scientific results.
- Document significant dependency decisions.

An external dependency should not be adopted solely because it provides convenient functionality. Its long-term maintenance implications must also be considered.

## 12.4.8 Resource Availability, Licensing and Access Restrictions

**Core requirements**

- Investigate whether resources are publicly accessible, registration-based, subscription-based, institutionally restricted or otherwise limited.
- Identify API keys, authentication and account requirements.
- Determine whether resources permit downloading, local retention, modification and redistribution.
- Identify restrictions affecting project exports and community sharing.
- Assess whether external resources introduce costs or contractual obligations.
- Identify unavailable, discontinued or restricted resources.
- Investigate alternatives when access limitations prevent intended use.
- Document unresolved licensing, security or access concerns before recommending adoption.

Public accessibility must not be interpreted as unrestricted permission to use or redistribute scientific material.

Any financial commitments, restricted access, external account creation or transmission of private research remain subject to Section 10.2 approval requirements.

## 12.4.9 Resource Redundancy and Existing Capability Review

**Core requirements**

- Check whether the application or an existing dependency already provides the required functionality.
- Identify overlapping libraries, databases and external services.
- Compare alternatives before introducing additional resources.
- Determine whether existing functionality can be extended or reused.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of scientific methods and data sources.
- Evaluate whether combining resources provides meaningful additional coverage or reliability.
- Identify dependencies that could be consolidated or removed when appropriate.
- Preserve functionality and compatibility when recommending replacements.

**Design principle**

The application should use a coherent collection of complementary scientific resources rather than accumulating multiple tools that perform substantially identical functions without meaningful benefit.

## 12.4.10 External Resource Documentation

**Core requirements**

Maintain records for significant external resources and dependencies.

Records should identify:

- Resource or software name.
- Provider, developer or responsible organization.
- Scientific or technical purpose.
- Relevant application capabilities and workspaces.
- Integration approach and current status.
- Scientific reliability and known limitations.
- Data coverage and compatibility.
- Dependencies and version requirements.
- Authentication and access restrictions.
- Licensing and usage conditions.
- Maintenance and availability.
- Known problems and alternatives.
- Evaluation findings and prioritization.
- Last verification or review date.

Maintain relationships between resource records, architecture documentation, implementation dependencies and the Master Development Roadmap.

## 12.4.11 Integration Approval and Implementation Boundaries

**Core requirements**

- Research and evaluation of publicly available resources may be conducted independently.
- Recommendations must identify significant costs, dependencies, licensing, privacy and security implications.
- Researching or recommending a resource does not authorize its integration.
- New dependencies may be introduced independently only within previously approved implementation scope and applicable approval boundaries.
- Significant architectural changes, major dependencies, paid services and external commitments require appropriate approval.
- Private research materials and credentials must not be transmitted to external services without authorization.
- Unfamiliar external software, scripts and tools must not be installed or executed without the required inspection and approval.
- Preserve the ability to reconsider or replace external resources without silently changing historical research results.

These requirements must remain consistent with Sections 4, 9 and 10.

## General Principles

- Prefer scientifically credible and established resources.
- Evaluate resources before adopting them.
- Reuse existing scientific software where appropriate.
- Prioritize complementary functionality rather than unnecessary duplication.
- Consider accessibility, licensing, security and long-term maintenance.
- Avoid excessive dependencies and external service commitments.
- Preserve resource provenance, versions and limitations.
- Maintain alternative approaches where practical.
- Distinguish research recommendations from approved integration decisions.

**General principle:** The application should develop a carefully evaluated ecosystem of scientific databases, software, libraries and external resources that meaningfully support its capabilities without introducing unnecessary dependencies, costs or maintenance burdens.
