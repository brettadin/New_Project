# 8.1 — Community Environment and Participation

## Purpose

Establishes the overall structure, purpose and participation principles of the application's community environment. Defines how users with different levels of scientific knowledge, experience and research involvement can interact, exchange information, contribute resources and participate in the wider astronomy community.

The community should provide opportunities for scientific discussion, collaboration, knowledge exchange and research sharing while remaining accessible to users who may have different goals and levels of involvement.

The application should support both a dedicated community environment and appropriate community-related functionality integrated into scientific workspaces. These should complement one another without allowing social features to unnecessarily interfere with scientific work.

## 8.1.1 — Overall Community Environment

The application should provide an integrated community environment that allows users to interact around astronomy, scientific research, educational interests and related activities.

The community should support:

- General astronomical and scientific discussions.
- Questions and knowledge exchange.
- Research discussions and collaboration.
- Sharing scientific findings, observations and resources.
- Discussion of astronomical objects, systems and phenomena.
- Exchange of analytical methods, workflows and scientific approaches.
- Feedback and constructive criticism.
- Educational and research-related contributions.
- Formation of groups around shared scientific interests.
- Discovery of other users, research activities and community resources.

The community should accommodate both casual participation and more substantial scientific involvement.

Users should be able to participate in discussions and explore shared material without necessarily conducting formal research or collaborating on projects.

Similarly, users who primarily use the application for independent scientific research should not be required to participate in community activities.

## 8.1.2 — Dedicated Community Environment

The application should provide a dedicated area for broader community interaction, separate from the primary scientific research environments.

This area should accommodate activities that are not necessarily tied to an individual research project or scientific workspace.

Potential functionality includes:

- General community discussions.
- Subject-specific discussion areas.
- Scientific questions and answers.
- User-created discussions and contributions.
- Research and educational resource discovery.
- Community groups and shared interests.
- Researcher and contributor profiles.
- Community announcements and relevant updates.
- Feedback and discussion around shared scientific material.
- Discovery of ongoing community activities.

The dedicated community environment should provide a recognizable place for users to interact without requiring them to enter a scientific workspace.

Its organization should remain adaptable as the community grows and develops.

## 8.1.3 — Community Integration Within Scientific Workspaces

Although broader community interaction should take place in a dedicated environment, relevant community functionality should also be accessible within scientific workspaces.

This may include:

- Accessing discussions related to the current astronomical subject or research area.
- Sharing selected datasets, observations, analyses and visualizations.
- Discussing research findings and interpretations.
- Accessing relevant community-created resources.
- Receiving feedback on shared scientific work.
- Connecting with users working on related scientific investigations.
- Accessing collaborative research functionality where appropriate.

Community features integrated into scientific workspaces should remain relevant to the user's current activity.

They should not unnecessarily introduce general social content, unrelated discussions or distracting notifications into research environments.

The intention is to make collaboration and scientific exchange accessible when useful without making social interaction a requirement of the research experience.

## 8.1.4 — Separation Between Community and Research Activities

The community environment and scientific research environments should have distinct purposes while remaining connected.

The dedicated community environment should primarily support:

- General interaction.
- Scientific and educational discussions.
- Community participation.
- Resource discovery and sharing.
- Networking and shared interests.
- Broader collaboration opportunities.

Scientific workspaces should primarily support:

- Scientific investigation.
- Data analysis and interpretation.
- Modelling and visualization.
- Research project management.
- Scientific workflows.
- Research-specific collaboration.

Users should be able to move between these environments without losing relevant context or access to associated material.

Community interaction should not be necessary for completing scientific workflows, and scientific workspaces should not become overloaded with social functionality.

The degree of community integration may differ between workspaces depending on their scientific purpose and the functionality available.

## 8.1.5 — Intended Community Participants

The community should accommodate a broad range of users, including:

- Astronomy enthusiasts and beginners.
- Amateur astronomers.
- Students and university researchers.
- Educators and science communicators.
- Citizen scientists.
- Independent researchers.
- Professional astronomers and scientists.
- Research groups and scientific institutions.

Participation should not require formal academic qualifications, professional affiliation or a particular level of scientific experience.

Users should be able to contribute according to their knowledge, interests and capabilities.

The application should encourage meaningful interaction between different experience levels while maintaining appropriate distinctions between personal opinions, educational contributions, research findings and scientifically evaluated work.

## 8.1.6 — Different Levels of Community Participation

The community should support different levels of involvement rather than assuming that every user wants to participate in the same way.

Examples include:

- Browsing discussions and shared resources without contributing.
- Asking questions or requesting explanations.
- Participating in existing discussions.
- Sharing educational or scientific material.
- Providing feedback on another user's work.
- Joining groups around particular interests.
- Participating in collaborative investigations.
- Sharing independent research findings.
- Contributing to broader scientific discussions and resource collections.

Users should be able to participate selectively without being pressured into greater involvement.

The application should not make public participation, profile completion or social interaction prerequisites for using its scientific capabilities.

## 8.1.7 — Community Discovery and Accessibility

Users should be able to discover relevant community activity without needing to know where specific discussions, groups or resources are located.

Potential discovery areas include:

- Scientific disciplines and research subjects.
- Astronomical objects and systems.
- Scientific questions and discussions.
- Research projects and shared investigations.
- Educational resources.
- Shared datasets and observations.
- Analytical workflows and scientific methods.
- Groups and research interests.
- Individual contributors and their public contributions.

Discovery should support the broader community environment while allowing relevant material to be accessed from scientific workspaces.

The application should avoid presenting all community content indiscriminately. Relevant categories, filters and contextual organization should help users find material appropriate to their interests and activities.

## 8.1.8 — Community Growth and Adaptability

The community structure should be capable of expanding as the application attracts more users and supports additional scientific disciplines.

It should accommodate:

- New scientific subject areas.
- Additional discussion categories.
- New forms of community contribution.
- More specialized research communities.
- Educational and professional groups.
- Expanded collaboration capabilities.
- Additional forms of scientific resource sharing.

The initial community structure should remain manageable rather than attempting to establish an unnecessarily complicated social platform before sufficient participation exists.

The application should prioritize useful scientific interaction and meaningful contributions over maximizing social activity or engagement.

## Overall Principles

- Provide both a dedicated community environment and relevant community functionality within scientific workspaces.
- Keep broader social interaction separate from the primary scientific research experience.
- Allow users to participate independently, collaboratively or not at all.
- Accommodate users with different levels of scientific knowledge and experience.
- Support meaningful scientific discussion, knowledge exchange and research interaction.
- Make relevant community resources discoverable throughout the application.
- Avoid unnecessary social features, distractions and clutter within scientific environments.
- Maintain clear connections between community contributions and their associated scientific material.
- Allow the community structure to expand alongside the application's scientific capabilities.

**General Principle:** The community should provide a connected environment for scientific interaction, knowledge exchange and collaboration while preserving the independence and focus of the application's research environments. Community participation should enrich the scientific platform without becoming an obstacle to using it for research.
