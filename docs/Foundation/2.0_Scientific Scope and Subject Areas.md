# 2. Scientific Scope and Subject Areas

## Purpose

This section establishes the scientific scope of the application and identifies the astronomical disciplines, research subjects and areas of scientific investigation it is intended to support.

The application should provide a broad foundation for astronomy research, with initial priorities established around selected scientific disciplines and meaningful opportunities for expansion into additional areas.

Its scientific scope should accommodate different astronomical objects, research questions, observational approaches and theoretical investigations. It should also support meaningful connections between disciplines where scientific questions require multiple areas of expertise.

The section defines what scientific subjects the application is intended to cover. It does not prescribe the specific tools, analytical methods, data resources or workspace arrangements used to investigate them.

## Overall Principles

- The application should support a broad and adaptable range of astronomical research.
- Initial scientific priorities should establish a practical starting point without permanently restricting the application's scope.
- Exoplanet science, planetary science, stellar astronomy and observational astronomy should receive initial priority.
- Solar System research should have a distinct and meaningful place within the application's scientific scope.
- The application should accommodate both established and developing areas of astronomical research.
- Scientific disciplines, astronomical objects, research methods and application workspaces must remain conceptually distinct.
- Cross-disciplinary research should be supported where scientifically meaningful.
- New scientific areas should be evaluated according to their research value, relevance and practical feasibility.
- The scientific scope should remain adaptable as astronomy and related sciences develop.

**General principle:** The application should provide a broad scientific foundation for astronomy research, beginning with clearly defined priority disciplines while remaining capable of expanding into additional astronomical subjects and related scientific fields.

## Implementation Boundary

Section 2 establishes the scientific subjects and disciplines the application is intended to support.

It does not define the specific scientific tools, analytical methods, data acquisition procedures, external resources, technical architecture or workspace arrangements required to support those subjects.

These matters are addressed in subsequent sections:

- **Section 3:** Core scientific capabilities and research workflows.
- **Section 4:** Data discovery, databases and external resources.
- **Section 5:** Scientific integrity, automation and analysis control.
- **Section 6:** Application structure, workspaces and user experience.

The scientific scope established here should inform those requirements without prematurely determining their implementation.
