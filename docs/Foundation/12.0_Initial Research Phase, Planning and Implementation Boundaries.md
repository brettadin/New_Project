# 12. Initial Research Phase, Planning and Implementation Boundaries

This section establishes the process for moving from the application's documented vision and requirements into scientific and technical research, development planning and eventual implementation.

It defines how the AI development partner investigates scientific capabilities, evaluates technologies and external resources, determines initial development priorities, prepares implementation stages and establishes when development is ready to begin.

The central principle is that implementation should be guided by sufficient scientific understanding, technical feasibility, documented requirements and appropriate user approval. Research and planning should establish a reliable foundation without creating unnecessary delays or prematurely committing to implementation decisions.

### 12.1 — Initial Research Objectives and Scope

Establishes the objectives and boundaries of the initial research phase. Requires comprehensive review of application requirements, independent scientific and technical investigation, identification of knowledge gaps and risks, and documentation of findings. Defines when research is sufficiently complete to support detailed development planning without requiring every future question to be resolved.

### 12.2 — Scientific Research and Capability Investigation

Defines how the AI investigates the scientific foundations of proposed application capabilities. Covers scientific principles, mathematical foundations, methodologies, observational data, instrumentation, reference resources, complete workflows, validation, limitations and cross-disciplinary relationships. Requires scientific understanding before implementing significant scientific functionality.

### 12.3 — Technical Research and Feasibility Assessment

Establishes how the AI evaluates programming languages, frameworks, development technologies, architecture, scientific computing, platform compatibility, data management and interoperability. Requires comparison of alternatives based on scientific suitability, performance, development complexity, maintainability, security and long-term practicality. Avoids premature technology commitments and unnecessary architectural complexity.

### 12.4 — External Resources, Software and Dependency Evaluation

Defines how the AI discovers and evaluates external scientific databases, archives, reference resources, software, libraries and computational tools. Establishes evaluation criteria covering scientific value, reliability, accessibility, compatibility, licensing, security, cost and maintenance. Requires meaningful justification before adopting dependencies and preserves the distinction between researching a resource and approving its integration.

### 12.5 — Initial Application Scope and Development Priorities

Establishes how the initial application scope is determined and how capabilities are prioritized. Distinguishes foundational infrastructure, initial scientific functionality, later development stages and future possibilities. Requires manageable incremental development, scientific usefulness, dependency awareness and periodic reassessment without unnecessarily restricting long-term expansion.

### 12.6 — Development Planning and Implementation Readiness

Defines the preparation required before an approved development stage begins. Covers objectives, scope, scientific and technical preparation, implementation design, task breakdown, acceptance criteria, testing, dependencies, risks, environment preparation, documentation and user authorization. Establishes readiness criteria without requiring every minor implementation detail to be predetermined.

### 12.7 — Transition from Research to Implementation

Establishes the formal transition from initial research and planning into active development. Requires a documented initial development strategy, approved scope, technical and architectural decisions, development baseline and readiness assessment. Confirms that scientific and technical research continues throughout implementation and that development priorities may be reassessed as new evidence and practical experience become available.

## Overall Principles

- Scientific and technical research must inform development decisions.
- Investigate complete scientific capabilities and workflows rather than isolated features.
- Evaluate technologies, software and external resources against actual requirements.
- Prioritize foundational infrastructure and meaningful scientific functionality.
- Keep the initial development scope manageable without compromising the long-term vision.
- Establish objectives, dependencies, acceptance criteria and testing requirements before significant implementation.
- Obtain appropriate approval for consequential decisions and implementation commitments.
- Maintain the Master Development Roadmap as the authoritative development overview.
- Continue research and reassessment throughout implementation.
- Preserve flexibility without allowing uncontrolled scope expansion or unnecessary planning delays.

**General principle:** The application should move from research into implementation through a deliberate, scientifically informed and technically practical process. Development should begin with a reliable foundation, clear priorities and appropriate authorization while retaining the flexibility to evolve as the project progresses.

## Implementation Boundary

Section 12 establishes the research, planning, prioritization and readiness requirements governing the transition into implementation.

It does not prescribe a final technology stack, detailed architecture, initial feature list or fixed development schedule. These must be determined through the research and evaluation processes established in this section, in coordination with the wider application requirements and Section 10.

The Master Development Roadmap must record the approved development strategy, priorities, dependencies, milestones and status.

Implementation must remain within the scope and approval boundaries established by the user and documented in Section 10.
