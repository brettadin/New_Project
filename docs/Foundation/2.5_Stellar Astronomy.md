

# 2.5 — Stellar Astronomy

## Purpose

Stellar astronomy is a major scientific discipline within the application, encompassing the study of stars, their physical properties, composition, structure, behaviour, formation and evolution.

Stars are important research subjects in their own right and provide essential context for understanding planetary systems, exoplanets and broader astronomical phenomena.

The application should accommodate investigations of individual stars, stellar populations and relationships between stars and other astronomical objects. Stellar research should connect naturally with spectroscopy, observational astronomy, astrophysics, planetary science and other relevant disciplines.

## 2.5.1 Stellar Properties and Physical Characteristics

### Core Requirements

Support research into the fundamental physical properties of stars, including:

- Mass and radius.
- Luminosity and brightness.
- Effective temperature.
- Surface gravity.
- Density.
- Rotation and rotational period.
- Age and evolutionary state.
- Distance and spatial position.
- Spectral energy distribution.
- Other relevant physical characteristics.

Accommodate properties obtained through direct observation, derived calculations and model-dependent estimates.

### Design Guidance

Stellar properties should retain their scientific context, including measurement methods, assumptions, uncertainties and reference information.

Distinguish observed quantities from calculated or inferred properties. Values derived through different methods should not automatically be treated as directly equivalent.

## 2.5.2 Stellar Classification and Types

### Core Requirements

Include the classification and categorization of stars according to their physical and observational characteristics.

Relevant subjects include:

- Spectral classification.
- Stellar temperature classes.
- Luminosity classes.
- Main-sequence stars.
- Giant and supergiant stars.
- White dwarfs.
- Brown dwarfs and substellar objects where relevant.
- Variable and chemically peculiar stars.
- Stellar populations and other recognized classifications.

Recognize the importance of established stellar classification systems and their scientific conventions.

### Design Guidance

Classification should help organize and interpret stellar information without imposing unnecessarily rigid categories.

The application should accommodate different classification systems and recognize that a star's classification may depend on the observational evidence and scientific context available.

Brown dwarfs and other objects near the boundary between stellar and planetary science should be accommodated where relevant, with their distinct scientific classification preserved.

## 2.5.3 Stellar Spectra and Atmospheric Composition

### Core Requirements

Stellar spectroscopy and atmospheric characterization should be important research subjects.

Include investigations involving:

- Stellar spectral features and absorption lines.
- Emission lines and other spectral phenomena.
- Atomic and molecular species.
- Elemental abundances.
- Metallicity and chemical composition.
- Effective temperature and atmospheric structure.
- Surface gravity and pressure-sensitive features.
- Stellar winds and atmospheric outflows.
- Spectral classification and interpretation.
- Chemical peculiarities and abundance variations.

Recognize the importance of spectroscopy across different wavelength regions and observational conditions.

### Design Guidance

Stellar spectra provide information about stellar composition, temperature, physical conditions and activity.

The application should preserve distinctions between observed spectral features, proposed identifications, derived stellar parameters and model-dependent interpretations.

Spectral measurements and inferred properties should retain their relevant observational and analytical context.

## 2.5.4 Stellar Structure and Internal Processes

### Core Requirements

Include scientific research into the internal structure and physical processes of stars.

Relevant subjects include:

- Stellar interiors and internal structure.
- Nuclear fusion and energy generation.
- Energy transport through radiation and convection.
- Core composition and evolutionary changes.
- Stellar equilibrium and stability.
- Internal mixing and chemical evolution.
- Mass loss and changes in stellar structure.
- Stellar pulsations and internal oscillations.
- Asteroseismology and related investigations.

### Design Guidance

Stellar structure research should accommodate both observationally constrained investigations and theoretical or computational studies.

The application should recognize that internal stellar properties are frequently inferred through indirect evidence and models rather than observed directly.

## 2.5.5 Stellar Activity and Variability

### Core Requirements

Support research into changes in stellar brightness, spectra, radiation and physical activity over time.

Relevant subjects include:

- Stellar variability and brightness changes.
- Starspots and surface activity.
- Stellar flares.
- Magnetic activity.
- Stellar rotation and activity cycles.
- Pulsations and oscillations.
- Eruptive and transient stellar phenomena.
- Changes in spectral features over time.
- Stellar winds and mass-loss activity.
- Long-term and periodic variations.

Accommodate research involving both periodic and irregular stellar behaviour.

### Design Guidance

Stellar activity and variability may influence observations of other astronomical objects, particularly exoplanets orbiting active stars.

The application should preserve the temporal and observational context of stellar measurements so that changes in stellar behaviour can be distinguished from signals originating elsewhere.

## 2.5.6 Stellar Formation and Evolution

### Core Requirements

Include research into the formation, development and eventual evolutionary outcomes of stars.

Relevant subjects include:

- Star-forming regions.
- Protostars and early stellar development.
- Pre-main-sequence evolution.
- Main-sequence evolution.
- Post-main-sequence development.
- Red giant and supergiant phases.
- Stellar mass loss.
- White dwarf formation.
- Supernovae and other relevant stellar end stages.
- Neutron stars and black holes as related compact-object research subjects.
- Stellar lifetimes and evolutionary pathways.

Support investigations into how stellar mass, composition and initial conditions influence stellar evolution.

### Design Guidance

Stellar evolution should be understood as a connected physical process rather than a collection of unrelated classifications.

The application should accommodate comparisons between observed stars at different evolutionary stages and scientific models describing their development.

Compact objects and explosive stellar phenomena may also connect with other areas of astrophysics, as described in Section 2.7.

## 2.5.7 Binary and Multiple Star Systems

### Core Requirements

Include research involving systems containing two or more gravitationally associated stars.

Relevant subjects include:

- Binary star systems.
- Multiple star systems.
- Orbital periods and configurations.
- Stellar masses and mass ratios.
- Eclipsing binaries.
- Spectroscopic binaries.
- Astrometric binaries.
- Mass transfer and interacting systems.
- Tidal interactions.
- Stellar mergers and related evolutionary outcomes.

Recognize that stellar system properties may differ substantially from those of isolated stars.

### Design Guidance

The application should preserve relationships between individual stars and their shared system.

Binary and multiple systems should be treated as interconnected research subjects, allowing users to investigate both individual stellar properties and system-level behaviour.

## 2.5.8 Host Stars and Planetary System Relationships

### Core Requirements

Recognize the importance of stellar astronomy to planetary and exoplanet research.

Include investigations into:

- Host-star mass, radius and temperature.
- Stellar composition and metallicity.
- Stellar age and evolutionary state.
- Stellar activity and variability.
- Stellar radiation and its influence on planetary environments.
- Star–planet interactions.
- Relationships between stellar properties and planetary formation.
- Relationships between stellar characteristics and observed planetary populations.
- The influence of stellar activity on planetary observations and atmospheric characterization.

### Design Guidance

Host-star characterization should be closely integrated with exoplanet research.

A planet's observed properties and inferred characteristics may depend on the accuracy of the information available about its host star.

The application should preserve these relationships without duplicating the exoplanet science scope defined in Section 2.3.

## 2.5.9 Stellar Populations and Comparative Research

### Core Requirements

Support research involving groups of stars and comparisons between different stellar populations.

Relevant subjects include:

- Stellar populations and distributions.
- Star clusters and associations.
- Open and globular clusters.
- Stellar populations within galaxies.
- Relationships between stellar properties and age.
- Chemical abundance patterns.
- Stellar formation histories.
- Population-level evolutionary trends.
- Comparisons between different stellar types and evolutionary stages.

### Design Guidance

Stellar research should accommodate both individual-object investigations and broader population-level studies.

Comparisons should account for observational selection effects, measurement uncertainty, differences in data quality and the scientific context of each population.

## 2.5.10 Interdisciplinary Relationships

### Core Requirements

Recognize the relationships between stellar astronomy and other scientific disciplines, including:

- Exoplanet science.
- Planetary science.
- Observational astronomy.
- Spectroscopy and molecular science.
- Chemistry and nuclear physics.
- Astrophysics.
- Solar physics and heliophysics.
- Galactic astronomy.
- Computational and theoretical science.

Allow stellar investigations to draw upon relevant scientific observations, datasets, methods and concepts from these disciplines.

### Design Guidance

Stellar astronomy should remain connected to the wider scientific environment of the application.

For example, an investigation of a host star may involve spectroscopy, chemical abundance analysis, stellar activity, planetary system characterization and atmospheric observations of orbiting exoplanets.

These relationships should be preserved without forcing every investigation into a single scientific category.

## General Principles

Stellar astronomy should:

- Support research into individual stars and stellar populations.
- Accommodate stellar properties, classification, spectra and composition.
- Include stellar structure, internal processes and evolution.
- Support investigations of stellar activity and variability.
- Recognize binary and multiple star systems.
- Integrate host-star research with exoplanet science.
- Accommodate both observational and theoretical investigations.
- Preserve distinctions between measurements, derived properties and model-dependent interpretations.
- Maintain relevant uncertainty, assumptions and observational context.
- Connect naturally with other scientific disciplines.

## General Principle

Stellar astronomy should provide a connected scientific environment for understanding stars as individual objects, evolving physical systems and important influences on the broader astronomical environments in which they exist.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes the scientific subjects and research questions that fall within stellar astronomy.

It does not require every stellar classification, observational technique, physical model or analytical capability to be supported in the initial implementation.

Specific scientific tools, analytical workflows, modelling capabilities, data handling and visualization requirements are defined elsewhere in the Foundations documentation, particularly Section 3.
