

# 3.1 — Scientific Capability Framework and Research Workflows

## Purpose

The application should provide a coherent framework for organizing and connecting its scientific capabilities.

Scientific research rarely consists of a single isolated operation. An investigation may involve discovering or importing observations, examining data, preparing measurements, applying analytical methods, evaluating models, comparing results and developing scientific interpretations.

The application should support these activities as connected parts of a broader research process while allowing users to choose their own methods, workflows and level of analytical complexity.

The capability framework should accommodate both general research activities shared across scientific disciplines and specialized tools designed for particular types of astronomical investigation.

## 3.1.1 General and Specialized Scientific Capabilities

### Core Requirements

- Distinguish between general scientific capabilities and specialized analytical tools.
- Provide common capabilities that can be reused across multiple scientific disciplines.
- Support specialized capabilities for particular research subjects, observational methods and analytical requirements.
- Allow specialized tools to use appropriate general capabilities where applicable.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of functionality across scientific disciplines.
- Ensure that shared capabilities remain suitable for the scientific contexts in which they are used.
- Allow new capabilities to be introduced as scientific requirements develop.

### Design Guidance

General capabilities may include data examination, unit handling, visualization, measurement inspection and comparison.

Specialized capabilities may include spectral line analysis, transit fitting, orbital calculations, atmospheric modelling or astronomical source extraction.

A shared capability should not force different scientific disciplines to use identical methods when their requirements differ. Reuse should improve consistency and reduce duplication without compromising scientific suitability.

## 3.1.2 Research Workflow Structure

### Core Requirements

Support research workflows that can incorporate activities such as:

- Identifying a scientific question or research objective.
- Selecting an astronomical object, system or research subject.
- Finding, retrieving or importing relevant observations and datasets.
- Examining the available data and associated metadata.
- Preparing data for appropriate scientific analysis.
- Selecting and applying relevant analytical methods.
- Performing measurements, calculations or modelling.
- Comparing observations, reference information and model outputs.
- Evaluating uncertainty, limitations and alternative interpretations.
- Producing and examining scientific results.
- Recording findings and preparing outputs for further research.

Allow workflows to be adapted to different scientific disciplines and investigation types.

Do not require every investigation to follow the same sequence or include every activity.

### Design Guidance

A workflow should represent a useful structure for conducting research, not a rigid sequence of mandatory steps.

For example, an investigation may begin with an existing dataset, a theoretical model, a previously published result or a newly identified research question.

Users should be able to revisit earlier stages, repeat analyses, compare alternative methods and incorporate additional observations as an investigation develops.

## 3.1.3 Flexible and Non-Linear Research

### Core Requirements

- Allow users to move between research activities without requiring a fixed sequence.
- Support exploratory analysis before a complete research objective has been established.
- Allow users to revisit and revise previous analytical steps.
- Support multiple analytical approaches using the same underlying observations.
- Allow investigations to branch into alternative methods or research questions.
- Support the incorporation of additional observations and datasets during an investigation.
- Preserve relationships between related activities and their resulting outputs.
- Avoid requiring users to restart an investigation when its direction changes.

### Design Guidance

Scientific research is frequently iterative and exploratory.

An initial observation may lead to several possible analytical approaches, and the results of one analysis may create new research questions.

The application should accommodate this naturally rather than treating research as a linear process in which each step permanently follows the previous one.

## 3.1.4 Integration Between Scientific Capabilities

### Core Requirements

- Allow compatible scientific tools to exchange relevant data and analytical outputs.
- Support the use of results from one capability as inputs to another where scientifically appropriate.
- Preserve relevant metadata, units, uncertainty and provenance when transferring information.
- Identify compatibility requirements before applying data or results to another capability.
- Communicate when conversion, preprocessing or additional information is required.
- Avoid silently discarding information during transfers between capabilities.
- Allow users to retain access to original data and previous analytical outputs.
- Support integrated workflows involving multiple scientific disciplines and methods.

### Design Guidance

Scientific capabilities should operate as connected parts of the application rather than as unrelated tools.

For example, spectral measurements may contribute to atmospheric characterization, while photometric observations may help constrain orbital or planetary properties.

Integration must preserve scientific meaning. Data should not be treated as interchangeable simply because two tools can technically accept the same numerical values.

## 3.1.5 Data, Methods and Capability Compatibility

### Core Requirements

- Evaluate whether a capability is suitable for the data provided.
- Consider data format, structure, units, measurement type and required metadata.
- Identify missing information that may prevent an operation from being performed reliably.
- Distinguish between unsupported data, incompatible data and data that requires preparation.
- Communicate relevant limitations before or during an operation.
- Avoid silently applying methods to unsuitable data.
- Support appropriate conversions and transformations where their scientific validity can be established.
- Preserve information about any transformations performed.

### Design Guidance

Compatibility should be determined by scientific requirements rather than file extensions or superficial similarities in data structure.

For example, two datasets may contain numerical values and an independent measurement axis but represent different physical quantities or use different conventions.

The application should identify these distinctions and avoid presenting technically executable operations as scientifically valid when their assumptions are not satisfied.

## 3.1.6 User-Directed Workflow Control

### Core Requirements

- Allow users to select the scientific activities appropriate to their investigations.
- Support both guided workflows and independent use of individual capabilities.
- Allow users to determine which analytical methods are applied.
- Provide relevant explanations and guidance when appropriate.
- Make significant processing and analytical operations understandable.
- Allow users to review inputs, settings and outputs.
- Avoid automatically advancing through scientifically consequential steps without appropriate user control.
- Support different levels of user experience and analytical complexity.

### Design Guidance

Some users may prefer structured guidance through an unfamiliar research process, while experienced users may want direct access to individual tools.

The application should support both approaches without making guided workflows mandatory or restricting advanced users to simplified procedures.

Automation levels, approval requirements and analytical safeguards are addressed in Section 5.

## 3.1.7 Reusable Scientific Operations

### Core Requirements

- Allow appropriate scientific operations to be reused across different investigations.
- Support reusable methods, configurations and analytical settings where applicable.
- Allow compatible outputs to be used in subsequent research activities.
- Avoid unnecessary repetition of equivalent processing operations.
- Preserve the settings and assumptions associated with reusable operations.
- Check compatibility before applying previously established configurations to new data.
- Allow users to modify or replace configurations when scientific requirements differ.

### Design Guidance

Reusable operations can improve efficiency and consistency, particularly when users perform similar analyses across multiple observations or astronomical objects.

However, reuse must not imply that the same method or configuration is appropriate for every dataset.

The application should make relevant assumptions and compatibility requirements clear.

## 3.1.8 Capability Availability Across Scientific Workspaces

### Core Requirements

- Make scientific capabilities accessible from relevant scientific workspaces.
- Allow shared capabilities to be used across multiple disciplines where appropriate.
- Support specialized capabilities within the research contexts for which they are intended.
- Avoid permanently restricting a capability to one scientific discipline when it has broader applications.
- Preserve access to relevant project materials and analytical outputs when users move between workspaces.
- Make unavailable or incompatible capabilities understandable to users.
- Maintain consistent behavior for shared capabilities across the application.

### Design Guidance

Scientific workspaces should provide relevant environments for particular research activities without creating isolated collections of tools.

A capability may be accessible from multiple workspaces while retaining appropriate scientific requirements and context.

Workspace organization and navigation are defined in Section 6.

## 3.1.9 Capability Expansion and Maintainability

### Core Requirements

- Allow the scientific capability framework to expand over time.
- Support the addition of new analytical methods and specialized tools.
- Encourage appropriate reuse of established functionality.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication when introducing new capabilities.
- Preserve compatibility with existing research workflows where practical.
- Evaluate the effect of new capabilities on established scientific operations.
- Allow specialized capabilities to develop without requiring unrelated parts of the application to change.
- Maintain a clear distinction between available, planned, experimental and unavailable capabilities.

### Design Guidance

The framework should support long-term scientific development without requiring an unnecessarily complicated system from the beginning.

New capabilities should be introduced according to demonstrated scientific requirements and evaluated for their relationships with existing functionality.

## General Principles

The scientific capability framework should:

- Connect individual tools into coherent research workflows.
- Support both general and specialized scientific functionality.
- Accommodate linear, iterative and exploratory research.
- Allow users to control their analytical approach.
- Preserve scientific meaning when transferring data between capabilities.
- Evaluate compatibility before applying methods or configurations.
- Encourage appropriate reuse without assuming universal suitability.
- Make relevant capabilities accessible across scientific workspaces.
- Support future expansion while avoiding unnecessary duplication.
- Preserve scientific context throughout the research process.

## General Principle

Scientific capabilities should work together as an adaptable research environment, allowing users to conduct investigations in different ways while maintaining continuity, compatibility and scientific integrity.

## Implementation Boundary

This subsection establishes the overall framework for scientific capabilities and research workflows.

It does not prescribe specific algorithms, software architectures, interface layouts or implementation technologies.

Detailed requirements for individual scientific capabilities are established in the remaining subsections of Section 3. Data discovery and external resource integration are addressed in Section 4, while scientific integrity, automation, validation and reproducibility are addressed in Section 5.
