# 11.7 — Historical Records, Archiving and Long-Term Preservation

## Purpose

Establish procedures for preserving important project information throughout the application's development and long-term lifecycle.

The project must retain sufficient historical context to understand how its requirements, scientific knowledge, architecture, implementation and development decisions have evolved.

Historical records should remain accessible, traceable and useful without unnecessarily cluttering current documentation or interfering with ongoing development.

## 11.7.1 Preservation of Development History

**Core requirements**

- Preserve significant development activity throughout the application's lifecycle.
- Maintain chronological records of research, planning, implementation, testing and maintenance.
- Preserve important decisions, approvals, recommendations and changes in direction.
- Retain records of significant problems, unsuccessful approaches and their outcomes.
- Preserve development milestones, completed work and deferred or cancelled tasks.
- Maintain relationships between historical records and the relevant requirements, components and roadmap entries.
- Ensure historical records remain accessible when development priorities or personnel change.

## 11.7.2 Historical Requirements and Decisions

**Core requirements**

- Preserve previous versions of significant requirements and approved decisions.
- Record when requirements are modified, replaced, deferred or withdrawn.
- Preserve the reasoning behind consequential changes.
- Distinguish current requirements from historical or superseded requirements.
- Maintain references between original decisions and subsequent revisions.
- Preserve rejected alternatives when they provide useful context for future decisions.
- Allow previous decisions to be reconsidered when new evidence or circumstances justify it.
- Never silently rewrite historical decisions to make previous development appear consistent with current direction.

**Design principle**

Historical decisions should remain understandable even when they are no longer applicable. Previous approval does not prevent future reassessment, but reassessment must preserve the original context.

## 11.7.3 Historical Research and Scientific Knowledge

**Core requirements**

- Preserve significant scientific and technical research findings.
- Retain source references, publication information and relevant research dates.
- Preserve assumptions, limitations and unresolved questions associated with previous findings.
- Identify when scientific knowledge has been superseded, corrected or substantially revised.
- Preserve historical scientific interpretations when they explain previous development decisions or research results.
- Distinguish historical scientific understanding from current accepted knowledge.
- Avoid silently replacing previous research findings with newer information.
- Preserve unsuccessful investigations when they provide useful methodological or technical knowledge.

**Design principle**

Scientific knowledge evolves. Historical records should make that evolution understandable rather than erase earlier interpretations.

## 11.7.4 Historical Implementation and Version Records

**Core requirements**

- Preserve development history through version control where available.
- Maintain identifiable application versions and significant release milestones.
- Preserve records of major implementation changes, architectural revisions and dependency changes.
- Identify when functionality is introduced, modified, replaced or removed.
- Maintain relationships between implementation changes, requirements, decisions and test reports.
- Preserve information about incomplete, experimental or temporary implementations.
- Allow previous implementation states to be investigated when necessary.
- Avoid relying exclusively on version-control history to explain the purpose or reasoning behind significant changes.

**Design guidance**

Version control preserves code changes. Development records explain their purpose, context and consequences. Both are necessary for meaningful historical continuity.

## 11.7.5 Historical Testing and Validation Records

**Core requirements**

- Preserve significant test reports and verification evidence.
- Retain relevant test environments, commands, configurations and results.
- Preserve scientific verification and validation records separately from ordinary software testing.
- Identify the implementation version and relevant datasets associated with historical results.
- Preserve failed tests and significant corrective investigations.
- Maintain references to superseded or repeated test reports.
- Allow previous results to be compared with subsequent testing.
- Distinguish historical successful tests from current verification status.

**Design principle**

A feature passing tests in an earlier version does not automatically establish that it remains correctly implemented or verified in a later version.

## 11.7.6 Archiving Completed, Deferred and Abandoned Work

**Core requirements**

- Support archiving completed development stages, investigations and significant records.
- Preserve deferred and abandoned work with explanations where available.
- Identify why work was completed, paused, rejected, cancelled or superseded.
- Retain relevant dependencies, findings, limitations and unresolved questions.
- Allow archived work to be retrieved and reconsidered.
- Avoid treating archived work as automatically approved for future implementation.
- Preserve links to related roadmap entries and subsequent work.

**Design guidance**

Archiving should remove inactive material from routine attention without deleting useful project knowledge.

## 11.7.7 Long-Term Documentation Accessibility

**Core requirements**

- Prefer widely supported, readable documentation formats where practical.
- Preserve source references and relevant metadata.
- Avoid unnecessary dependence on proprietary documentation systems.
- Keep document relationships understandable outside the original development environment.
- Maintain useful indexes and references for archived material.
- Identify unavailable external resources and preserve available descriptions or citations.
- Avoid storing essential historical knowledge exclusively in external services or individual AI systems.
- Review important archival material when format compatibility or accessibility becomes a concern.

**Future possibilities**

- Automated archival checks.
- Long-term documentation format migration.
- More extensive offline documentation packages.
- Preservation of historical dependency and computational environments.
- Dedicated archival exports of development records.

## 11.7.8 Backup and Recovery of Development Records

**Core requirements**

- Include important project documentation and development records in appropriate backup procedures.
- Protect the Master Development Roadmap, rolling development record, decision records, problem records and test reports from accidental loss.
- Preserve relationships and directory structures during backup and recovery.
- Support recovery of individual documents or records where practical.
- Verify that backups can be restored.
- Avoid treating version control as the only backup mechanism.
- Protect backups from unauthorized access and exposure of sensitive information.
- Coordinate documentation backup with the broader project backup and recovery requirements in Section 9.4.

## 11.7.9 Historical Record Retention and Cleanup

**Core requirements**

- Retain significant historical records for as long as they remain useful to scientific, technical or development continuity.
- Avoid deleting records solely because they are old or no longer active.
- Identify genuinely redundant, obsolete or irrelevant material.
- Preserve historical context when records are consolidated or reorganized.
- Clearly identify retained historical documents that are no longer authoritative.
- Ensure cleanup does not remove important evidence, decisions, failed investigations or development context.
- Apply appropriate privacy, licensing and retention restrictions.

**Design principle**

Historical preservation should be deliberate rather than unlimited. Records may be reorganized or consolidated when their information remains preserved and traceable.

## 11.7.10 Continuity Across Application Versions and Development Partners

**Core requirements**

- Preserve enough historical information to understand the application across major versions and development stages.
- Maintain current and historical architecture and technical decision records.
- Preserve unresolved problems, deferred work and outstanding requirements.
- Allow new AI development partners and human contributors to understand previous work.
- Maintain consistent documentation terminology and record relationships.
- Provide handoff information that identifies the current state and relevant history.
- Preserve the distinction between historical implementation and current application behaviour.
- Avoid requiring previous conversations or individual developer memory to reconstruct project history.

## General Principles

- Preserve significant research and development history.
- Retain previous decisions, requirements and their reasoning.
- Keep historical scientific knowledge distinguishable from current understanding.
- Preserve implementation and testing evidence.
- Archive inactive work without losing its context.
- Maintain accessible and recoverable documentation.
- Protect important records through appropriate backups.
- Avoid unnecessary deletion or duplication.
- Support continuity across application versions and development partners.

**General principle:** The project must preserve its scientific and development history so that previous work remains understandable, traceable and recoverable. Historical knowledge should support future development without being mistaken for current requirements, implementation or scientific conclusions.
