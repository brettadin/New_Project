# 3.12 — Extensible Scientific Capabilities and Future Methods

## Purpose

The application should be designed to accommodate the continuing development of astronomy and related scientific disciplines.

Scientific research methods, observational technologies, instruments, datasets, analytical approaches and computational techniques evolve over time. The application should therefore support the introduction of additional scientific capabilities without requiring fundamental restructuring whenever new requirements emerge.

Extensibility should allow the application to develop beyond its initial scientific capabilities while maintaining scientific integrity, consistency, compatibility and usability.

The application should accommodate both improvements to existing capabilities and the introduction of entirely new scientific methods.

This subsection establishes the requirements and principles governing future scientific expansion. It does not require every potential capability to be implemented or prescribe specific technologies, software architectures or development priorities.

## 3.12.1 Scientific Capability Extensibility

### Core Requirements

- Design scientific capabilities so that additional methods and functionality can be introduced as requirements develop.
- Support the expansion of existing scientific tools without unnecessarily disrupting established functionality.
- Allow new scientific capabilities to address additional research questions, observational methods and analytical requirements.
- Accommodate scientific methods from different astronomical disciplines.
- Support capabilities that operate independently as well as those that contribute to integrated research workflows.
- Allow new capabilities to make appropriate use of existing datasets, observations, metadata, analytical outputs and scientific resources.
- Preserve compatibility with the application's established scientific data and provenance requirements.
- Avoid requiring every new capability to follow an identical internal workflow.
- Ensure that introducing additional capabilities does not compromise existing scientific operations.

### Design Guidance

The application should be capable of expanding as scientific requirements become clearer.

For example, an initial implementation may support established spectral analysis methods while future development introduces additional atmospheric retrieval approaches, more advanced radiative-transfer calculations or specialized spectroscopic techniques.

Similarly, additional capabilities may be introduced for planetary imaging, stellar characterization, orbital dynamics or other scientific disciplines.

The goal is not to anticipate and implement every possible scientific method from the beginning. It is to avoid unnecessary restrictions that would make future expansion difficult.

## 3.12.2 Expansion of Scientific Disciplines

### Core Requirements

- Support the introduction of additional astronomical and related scientific disciplines.
- Allow existing scientific workspaces and capabilities to expand as new research requirements emerge.
- Accommodate disciplines that require specialized datasets, observational methods, analytical procedures or scientific conventions.
- Support interdisciplinary capabilities that draw on methods from multiple scientific fields.
- Allow new scientific disciplines to make appropriate use of shared application resources.
- Preserve the distinction between general scientific functionality and discipline-specific requirements.
- Avoid restricting future development to the initial scientific priorities established in Section 2.

### Design Guidance

The initial scientific scope provides direction for development, not a permanent boundary on the application's capabilities.

Future scientific expansion may include additional areas of astronomy, planetary science, astrophysics, astrochemistry and related disciplines.

The application should also accommodate interdisciplinary research where methods and datasets from different fields contribute to the same investigation.

Expansion should be guided by identifiable scientific requirements rather than the assumption that every possible discipline needs a dedicated workspace or independent toolset.

The scientific scope and discipline relationships are established in Section 2.

## 3.12.3 New Observational Methods and Data Sources

### Core Requirements

- Accommodate new observational techniques and measurement methods.
- Support additional instrument types and observational facilities as their data becomes relevant.
- Allow new scientific data products and measurement types to be introduced.
- Accommodate evolving data formats, standards and metadata conventions.
- Support observations from ground-based, airborne, space-based and future observational platforms.
- Allow new data sources to integrate with existing discovery, retrieval, import and examination capabilities.
- Preserve the original scientific meaning, metadata, provenance and limitations of newly supported data.
- Avoid assuming that all future observations will follow existing data structures or measurement conventions.

### Design Guidance

New observational technologies may introduce data types and measurement approaches that are not fully accommodated by the application's initial capabilities.

The application should therefore be capable of evaluating and incorporating additional data sources and observational methods without requiring them to be forced into inappropriate existing representations.

Where new data formats or measurement conventions are introduced, their scientific meaning and compatibility should be established before they are used in analytical workflows.

Data discovery, standards, retrieval, provenance and resource integration are addressed in Section 4.

## 3.12.4 Expansion of Analytical and Computational Methods

### Core Requirements

- Allow additional scientific analytical methods to be introduced over time.
- Support improvements and alternatives to existing analytical procedures.
- Accommodate new mathematical, statistical, numerical and computational methods.
- Support additional modelling, simulation, optimization and prediction approaches.
- Allow specialized methods to be introduced for particular scientific disciplines or data types.
- Support alternative methods that address similar scientific questions.
- Preserve the ability to compare outputs from different analytical approaches.
- Ensure that new methods identify their assumptions, required inputs, limitations and applicable conditions.
- Maintain appropriate validation and quality-control requirements for newly introduced methods.

### Design Guidance

Scientific methods should be introduced according to their scientific purpose and suitability.

For example, future capabilities might include more advanced atmospheric retrieval methods, additional statistical approaches, specialized image-processing techniques, improved orbital calculations, population-level analyses or more sophisticated physical simulations.

The application should not assume that a newly introduced method is automatically more accurate or appropriate than an established alternative.

Methods should be evaluated according to their scientific validity, intended application, assumptions, limitations and performance.

Relevant modelling requirements are established in Section 3.7, while comparative and integrated analytical requirements are established in Section 3.9.

## 3.12.5 Integration of New Scientific Capabilities

### Core Requirements

- Allow new capabilities to integrate with appropriate existing scientific workspaces and research workflows.
- Support the use of existing observations, datasets, metadata and analytical outputs where compatible.
- Allow new capabilities to produce outputs that can be examined, stored, visualized and reused through established application functionality.
- Preserve relationships between new analytical operations and their inputs and outputs.
- Allow compatible capabilities to contribute to broader investigations.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of existing scientific functionality.
- Maintain consistent handling of units, uncertainties, provenance and scientific metadata.
- Identify dependencies and compatibility requirements associated with new capabilities.
- Ensure that new integrations do not silently alter existing datasets or research results.

### Design Guidance

New capabilities should integrate with the wider scientific environment where doing so provides meaningful value.

For example, a newly introduced modelling method should be able to use compatible observational data, produce identifiable scientific outputs, support appropriate visualization and contribute to existing research investigations.

However, integration should not mean that every capability must be directly connected to every other tool.

Connections should be established according to genuine scientific relationships, compatible inputs and outputs, and useful research workflows.

The application should favor meaningful integration over unnecessary interconnectedness.

## 3.12.6 Modular Scientific Capabilities

### Core Requirements

- Organize scientific functionality so that capabilities can be developed and maintained without unnecessary dependence on unrelated tools.
- Allow individual capabilities to be expanded, improved or replaced without requiring widespread changes to the application.
- Support shared scientific resources and common functionality where appropriate.
- Allow specialized capabilities to retain the requirements and behavior specific to their scientific purpose.
- Preserve consistent interfaces for relevant shared operations.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of common scientific functionality.
- Identify dependencies between capabilities where they affect operation or compatibility.
- Ensure that changes to one capability do not silently invalidate unrelated scientific functionality.

### Design Guidance

Scientific capabilities should be sufficiently modular to support continued development and maintenance.

Shared functionality may include data access, unit handling, uncertainty representation, metadata management, visualization, validation and provenance.

Specialized scientific tools may require different methods, assumptions, parameters and data structures.

The application should balance shared functionality with the flexibility needed for scientifically distinct operations.

Modularity should serve maintainability, scientific clarity and meaningful expansion. It should not become an objective that introduces unnecessary abstraction or architectural complexity.

Detailed application architecture and modularity principles are addressed in Section 9.

## 3.12.7 Evaluation of New Scientific Methods

### Core Requirements

Before a new scientific method or capability is incorporated into the application, evaluate its suitability for the intended scientific purpose.

Evaluation should consider:

- The scientific problem it is intended to address.
- Its underlying scientific principles and methodology.
- Its mathematical and physical assumptions.
- Its required observations, datasets and metadata.
- Its applicability to different types of scientific measurements.
- Its limitations and known sources of uncertainty.
- Its validation against appropriate reference data or established methods.
- Its computational and storage requirements.
- Its dependencies on external software, services or resources.
- Its compatibility with existing application functionality.
- Its maintenance requirements and long-term viability.
- Its relevance to the application's scientific priorities.

Allow methods with different levels of maturity to be evaluated without automatically treating them as equally established.

### Design Guidance

A method should not be incorporated solely because it is available, popular or technically straightforward to integrate.

Scientific suitability should remain the primary consideration.

Established methods, emerging approaches and experimental techniques may require different levels of evaluation and validation.

Where scientific evidence is limited, the application should communicate that limitation rather than presenting an experimental capability as an established method.

The application should also consider whether an existing capability can be improved or extended before introducing an entirely separate tool.

## 3.12.8 Validation and Scientific Reliability of New Capabilities

### Core Requirements

- Apply appropriate scientific validation to newly introduced analytical methods.
- Establish suitable reference cases, benchmark datasets or comparison methods where available.
- Evaluate accuracy, reliability, uncertainty and limitations.
- Identify conditions under which a method is valid or unsuitable.
- Test compatibility with relevant data formats, units and measurement conventions.
- Preserve validation information and relevant method documentation.
- Distinguish verified capabilities from experimental or insufficiently validated functionality.
- Reevaluate existing validation when significant methodological changes are introduced.
- Avoid presenting unvalidated outputs as scientifically established results.

### Design Guidance

New scientific functionality should be subject to validation appropriate to its intended purpose.

The degree of validation required may differ between a visualization feature, a numerical calculation, a statistical procedure and a complex physical model.

Where established reference results are available, they should be considered as part of the evaluation.

Where suitable reference data does not exist, the application should identify the limitations of available validation rather than implying that a capability has been comprehensively verified.

Scientific integrity, validation and quality-control requirements are established in Section 5.

## 3.12.9 Experimental and Emerging Scientific Capabilities

### Core Requirements

- Allow emerging scientific methods to be investigated and evaluated before becoming established application capabilities.
- Distinguish experimental functionality from established scientific operations.
- Clearly communicate when a method is incomplete, experimental or insufficiently validated.
- Preserve appropriate user control over experimental operations.
- Avoid allowing experimental capabilities to silently replace established analytical methods.
- Support evaluation of new methods using suitable test data and reference cases.
- Allow experimental capabilities to be revised, expanded or discontinued as evidence develops.
- Preserve the ability to distinguish experimental outputs from outputs produced by established methods.

### Design Guidance

Astronomy and related disciplines continue to develop new observational and analytical approaches.

The application should be capable of exploring these developments without automatically treating every emerging technique as a production-ready scientific capability.

Experimental functionality may be useful for research and evaluation, but its status, limitations and validation requirements should remain clear.

The introduction of experimental methods should not compromise established research workflows or the reliability of previously generated results.

## 3.12.10 External Scientific Software and Method Integration

### Core Requirements

- Allow the application to evaluate and integrate relevant external scientific software and methods.
- Support external tools where they provide capabilities that would be impractical or scientifically inappropriate to reproduce internally.
- Evaluate external tools according to their scientific suitability, reliability, compatibility, licensing and maintenance requirements.
- Preserve the distinction between internally developed capabilities and externally provided functionality.
- Maintain information about relevant external dependencies.
- Preserve input, output, method and version information where available.
- Allow external methods to contribute to broader application workflows when appropriate.
- Avoid unnecessary dependence on external software for essential application functionality.
- Provide appropriate handling when external tools or services become unavailable or incompatible.

### Design Guidance

The application should not attempt to independently recreate every scientific method or computational tool.

Established scientific software may provide specialized functionality that would be difficult to develop or maintain internally.

External integration should be considered when it provides meaningful scientific value and can be incorporated without compromising reliability, transparency or user control.

The application should also consider long-term maintenance, dependency management and the possibility that external software may change or become unavailable.

External software and service integration requirements are addressed in Section 4.10, with broader architectural considerations in Section 9.

## 3.12.11 Capability Compatibility and Evolution

### Core Requirements

- Maintain compatibility between existing application functionality and newly introduced capabilities where practical.
- Identify changes that may affect existing data, configurations, analyses or research projects.
- Preserve historical analytical outputs when methods or capabilities are updated.
- Avoid silently changing previously generated scientific results when an analytical method is modified.
- Identify when existing configurations are incompatible with updated methods.
- Allow users to evaluate or migrate existing configurations where appropriate.
- Preserve relevant method and version information.
- Communicate significant changes to scientific methods and their potential effects.
- Support continued access to historical results even when the original method is no longer available.

### Design Guidance

Scientific capabilities will change as methods improve and new requirements emerge.

However, improving a method should not silently alter the meaning of existing results.

Where a new method produces different outputs, users should be able to distinguish the new results from those produced by earlier versions.

Historical analyses should retain sufficient information to understand the methods and configurations originally used, subject to the availability of required dependencies and resources.

Detailed historical analysis preservation and reproducibility requirements are established in Section 5.5.

## 3.12.12 User and Research Community Contributions

### Core Requirements

- Allow future consideration of scientific capabilities proposed by users and research communities.
- Provide a means to identify scientific needs that are not currently supported.
- Allow proposed methods, datasets and external tools to be evaluated before adoption.
- Preserve scientific review and validation requirements for contributed capabilities.
- Avoid automatically treating user-submitted methods or resources as established scientific functionality.
- Maintain appropriate attribution and licensing information.
- Support the possibility of community-developed scientific extensions where suitable safeguards can be established.

### Design Guidance

Researchers and users may identify useful scientific methods, tools and data sources that are not yet supported.

The application should remain open to these contributions while maintaining appropriate evaluation, security, licensing and scientific validation.

Community contribution should provide a route for identifying and evaluating new capabilities, not an automatic mechanism for incorporating unverified scientific methods.

Community participation and sharing requirements are addressed in Section 8.

## 3.12.13 Prioritization of Future Scientific Capabilities

### Core Requirements

Evaluate potential additions according to their scientific value and practical suitability.

Consider:

- Relevance to the application's established scientific priorities.
- Research needs identified by users.
- Scientific maturity and validation.
- Availability and quality of required data.
- Compatibility with existing capabilities.
- Expected computational and storage requirements.
- Development and maintenance complexity.
- Availability of suitable external tools.
- Potential benefits to multiple research disciplines.
- Whether the capability addresses a genuine gap or duplicates existing functionality.

Allow future capabilities to be categorized according to their development status, such as:

- Established application capability.
- Planned capability.
- Capability under investigation.
- Experimental capability.
- Long-term possibility.
- Not currently prioritized.

### Design Guidance

The application should maintain a distinction between its established scientific capabilities and possible future additions.

Not every scientifically interesting method needs to become an application feature.

Development priorities should reflect scientific usefulness, available resources, validation requirements and the broader direction of the application.

This approach should prevent the long-term vision from becoming an unmanageable list of mandatory features.

## 3.12.14 Long-Term Scientific Development

### Core Requirements

- Preserve flexibility for continued scientific development.
- Allow existing capabilities to evolve as observational methods, scientific understanding and computational techniques improve.
- Support the introduction of new research disciplines and analytical methods.
- Maintain compatibility and continuity wherever practical.
- Preserve scientific integrity and reproducibility as capabilities expand.
- Reevaluate the scientific scope and capability priorities as the application matures.
- Allow future development to be guided by scientific research, user needs and technological feasibility.
- Avoid treating the initial application scope as a permanent limit on scientific ambition.

### Design Guidance

The application should be developed as a long-term scientific environment rather than a fixed collection of tools.

Its scientific capabilities should be able to evolve alongside astronomy and related disciplines.

This does not require implementing every potential capability or predicting the complete future direction of the application.

Instead, the application should retain a clear scientific foundation and a sufficiently adaptable structure to support meaningful expansion when justified.

## General Principles

The application should:

- Remain open to additional scientific disciplines, observational methods and analytical approaches.
- Allow existing capabilities to improve without unnecessarily disrupting established functionality.
- Support meaningful integration between new and existing scientific tools.
- Preserve scientific data, metadata, provenance and analytical relationships.
- Evaluate new methods according to scientific suitability and validation.
- Distinguish established, experimental and proposed capabilities.
- Maintain compatibility and historical research continuity.
- Consider external scientific software where it provides meaningful value.
- Support appropriate user and research community contributions.
- Prioritize scientific usefulness over feature quantity.
- Avoid unnecessary architectural complexity and duplication.
- Preserve scientific integrity, transparency, reproducibility and user control.

## General Principle

The application should be capable of growing alongside astronomy and related scientific disciplines. Its scientific foundation should remain stable and reliable while allowing new methods, observational technologies, analytical capabilities and research requirements to be introduced when scientifically justified.

The application should remain adaptable without committing prematurely to particular technical solutions.
